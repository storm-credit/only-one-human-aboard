# 0. Core judgment

The previous target:
**272 / 410 = 66.3% protagonist-owned**

was too low for the intended reading experience.
At that level, the project risked reading as an equal-share ensemble rather than a protagonist-led long-form mystery/survival novel.

New target:
# **319 / 410 = 77.8% protagonist-owned**

Separate presence target:
# **protagonist appears in 386 / 410 = 94.1% of episodes**

Full episodes with no protagonist appearance:
# **24 / 410 = 5.9%**

Hard distinction:
- `protagonist-owned` = the episode's dominant dramatic question, hook, decision or emotional spine belongs to the protagonist.
- `secondary-owned mixed` = another character owns the episode's main local decision, but the protagonist appears in a meaningful opening/closing/parallel scene.
- `full protagonist-absent` = the protagonist does not appear; use only when absence itself strengthens scale, suspense, consequence or independent causality.

# 2. Full protagonist-absence budget

Only **24 episodes** are planned as fully protagonist-absent by default.

These are listed and controlled in:
`docs/prewriting-v3/POV-OWNERSHIP-REALLOCATION-v0.2.md`

Series-wide ceiling:
**<= 6% default / hard warning above 8%.**

A fully absent episode must satisfy at least 2 of:
1. the protagonist cannot plausibly witness the causal event,
2. another character makes an irreversible choice independently,
3. the episode shows consequence of a protagonist decision from the other side,
4. absence itself increases suspense or scale,
5. the event is a long-plant whose later relevance should not be protagonist-mediated,
6. a person's independent current life must be proven rather than merely stated.

If none apply, convert to a mixed episode.

---

# 3. Mixed-secondary rule

Most former full secondary-POV episodes are **not deleted**.
Instead, they become one of these forms:

## M1 — Protagonist frame / secondary center / protagonist consequence
Example:
protagonist encounters a problem → cut to Mira making the allocation decision → return to protagonist experiencing the result.

## M2 — Protagonist A-plot / independent secondary B-plot
The protagonist owns the episode hook and turn, while a cleanly separated secondary scene advances causality elsewhere.

## M3 — Secondary opening / protagonist convergence
Start where the independent decision occurs; protagonist enters later because the consequence intersects his work/life.

Hard:
Do not head-hop inside a scene. Scene breaks remain explicit.

---

# 4. Why this does not make the protagonist omnipresent

The world-independence requirement remains unchanged:
- at least 2 meaningful causal developments per Act occur outside protagonist presence,
- at least 1 problem per Act is solved or worsened independently,
- at least 1 relationship/institutional state change per Act occurs beyond protagonist control,
- Act2 and Act6 maintain multi-location concurrency ledgers,
- the Act6 neutral-corridor causal chain still bypasses the protagonist.

The change is **reader attachment**, not causal monopoly.

---

# 5. Scene-presence target

Target reading experience:
- protagonist is visible in ~90–95% of episodes,
- protagonist owns ~75–80% of episodes,
- secondary characters still receive scene-level autonomy,
- fully protagonist-absent episodes feel intentional and memorable rather than routine.

Do NOT measure sentence count or screen-time mechanically.
These are drift-detection targets.

## Act2

## Act4

## Act5

## Act6
Widest ensemble allowance.
This is the only Act where protagonist ownership may sit below 70% without warning.

## Act7
Scale revelation returns strongly to protagonist because identity, Arun and technical maintenance inference are his emotional/professional spine.

## Act8
Affected D3 persons speak for themselves, but title mystery and operational investigation remain protagonist-anchored.

## Act9
Final settlement remains plural, but emotional closure and final physical inference stay visibly protagonist-led.

---

# 7. Failure conditions

FAIL if:
- protagonist disappears for 3+ consecutive full episodes without a major deliberate reason,
- secondary scenes exist only to explain information to the reader,
- protagonist is inserted into decisions he should not own merely to increase percentage,
- all secondary causal chains report to protagonist before acting,
- Act6 neutral corridor becomes protagonist's idea,
- D3 affected people lose their own voice in Act8,
- protagonist's mere physical appearance replaces actual narrative ownership.
