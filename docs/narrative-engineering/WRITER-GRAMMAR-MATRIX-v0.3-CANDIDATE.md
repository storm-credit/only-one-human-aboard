# 0. Narrative identity

**주인공 중심 다중인과형 장편.**

Not:
- pure single-POV hero story,
- equal-share ensemble,
- anthology,
- omniscient civilization chronicle.

The protagonist is the dominant emotional/mystery anchor.
The world still acts without him.

Working diagnostic:
- protagonist-centered scenes/POV: ~65~75% overall,
- core/major secondary: ~15~25%,
- ordinary/emergent/institutional: ~5~15%.

Not quota.

---

# 1. Base POV mode

Selected:
**close third-person limited, one clear POV owner per scene.**

Default POV = protagonist.

POV transition:
- scene break,
- chapter/episode break,
- unmistakable spatial/temporal reset.

Avoid:
- head hopping within continuous scene,
- omniscient summary of several minds,
- switching POV only to disclose secret information.

---

# 2. Secondary POV eligibility rule

A non-protagonist POV earns direct access only if the scene has:
1. immediate personal goal,
2. causal action or irreversible consequence,
3. information that character plausibly owns,
4. value beyond explaining lore.

If removing exposition makes the scene pointless: CUT or move information elsewhere.

# 5. Author-only vocabulary discipline

Terms such as:
- D0/D1/D2/D3/D4,
- C1/C2/C3/C4,
- lineage seed,
- ultradense substrate field,
- continuity failover misbinding,
are **author tools first**.

Even after reveal, characters need not use exact labels if normal speech is clearer.

Bad dialogue:
`당신은 C4 무결성 오류로 D3 계보에 잘못 바인딩됐어요.`

Better late technical speech:
`죽기 전에 보존 절차가 시작됐고, 그 과정에서 다른 계보의 주소로 붙었습니다.`

---

# 6. Scene priority stack

Every scene prioritizes:
1. immediate goal,
2. obstacle/person conflict,
3. concrete action,
4. consequence,
5. clue/recontextualization if earned,
6. ontology explanation last.

A scene with only item 6 fails.

---

# 7. Exposition rule

World explanation must attach to current need:
- fix something,
- decide access,
- evaluate evidence,
- treat patient,
- allocate resource,
- argue legal right,
- survive danger.

Avoid long `as you know` dialogues.

If information can be shown through:
- form,
- queue,
- commute,
- injury,
- argument,
- error message,
- ordinary purchase,
prefer that over lecture.

---

# 8. Other-history morality grammar

Use:
`current ordinary interaction`
→ `disproportionate reaction`
→ `testimony/expectation`
→ `current person contradicts it through behavior`
→ `later evidence partly supports/refutes`
→ `current relationship chooses its own future`.

Rotate contradiction types:
- intimacy,
- authority,
- guilt/debt,
- professional competence,
- family role,
- rescue/abandonment,
- truth/secrecy,
- convergent trust.

Do not use `good there / evil here` repeatedly.

---

# 9. H-A/H-B relationship writing

H-B is not a replacement stage.

Rules:
- protagonist may project H-A intimacy/guilt,
- H-B person can reject that projection,
- current H-B relationship gets more long-term scene weight,
- other-history spouse/child memories do not create automatic current claims,
- protagonist learns boundaries through action, not a philosophy speech.

---

# 10. Protagonist-absent causality grammar

World motion does not require direct POV every time.

Three methods:

### A. Direct secondary POV
Use when decision itself deserves experience.

### B. Later consequence
Protagonist returns and discovers:
- relationship changed,
- route changed,
- decision already made,
- policy passed,
- person moved/injured.

### C. Brief external evidence
News, message, work handoff, conversation, physical result.

Prefer B/C for minor offscreen changes.
This prevents anthology drift.

---

# 11. Secondary scene hard gate

Every non-protagonist scene must satisfy at least one:
- changes current incident,
- changes relationship,
- changes institution/resource state,
- seeds/pays later causal pivot,
- changes reader judgment with concrete consequence.

`shows the world is big` alone is not enough.

---

# 12. Concurrent-crisis writing

For Act2/6:
- keep location named/recognizable at transition,
- do not show all fronts every episode,
- hand off causality instead of montage spam,
- return to protagonist before emotional spine disappears.

Reader should track:
`where / who wants what / what changed`.
Not necessarily exact clock every scene.

---

# 13. Protagonist competence grammar

He is strongest at:
- damage chronology,
- structure/material failure,
- local safety consequence.

When problem becomes:
- policing → Sora,
- command → Selene,
- logistics → Mira,
- clinical medicine → Leila/Rafi,
- archive provenance → Hana,
he needs them.

Do not let protagonist suddenly know another domain because plot is near climax.

---

# 14. Memory anomaly prose

Strong leaks should feel invasive and rare.

Avoid routine format:
`flash → remembered other life → clue every 5 episodes`.

Prefer diverse evidence:
- wrong certainty,
- social recognition,
- bodily reaction,
- testimony,
- archive contradiction,
- knowledge without history.

Most episodes: zero direct leak.

---

# 15. Horror prose rule

Ghost-like scene:
- preserve ordinary explanation first,
- no supernatural narrator confirmation,
- strong events 2~4 series-wide target,
- must alter relationship/clue/action.

No zombie major-arc grammar.

---

# 16. System/CCAL presentation

The system is not a villain monologue machine.

When visible late, prefer:
- terse status records,
- old design documents,
- conflicting policy layers,
- operational responses,
- logs with missing context,
- human interpretation/dispute.

No single AI speech explaining why suffering was necessary.

The story never declares suffering morally justified because it produced data.

---

# 17. HUMAN:1 prose grammar

First hard `HUMAN:1` appearance:
boring bureaucratic/technical presentation.

Do not use:
- glowing sacred chamber language,
- prophecy framing,
- `the true human` narrator label.

Natural-Origin distinction is technical/historical.

Old `HUMAN >1` reveal should feel like archive/historical correction, not twist music.

---

# 18. Act6 prose identity

Act6 is not war fantasy.

Early:
queues, missed shifts, ration arguments, service delays, protests, distrust.

Middle:
emergency restrictions, sabotage, faction networks, exhausted workers.

Late:
concentrated violence/infrastructure confrontation.

Aftermath:
funerals, displacement, broken trust, repair, political review.

Do not jump from normal life to armed factions in three episodes.

---

# 19. Act7 scale reveal grammar

Scale comes through accumulation.

Bad:
one dashboard with giant number.

Good:
- namespace too wide,
- power/load map too large,
- archive classes that cannot fit known society,
- multiple technical teams independently reach same impossible inference.

Reader should infer enormity slightly before characters state it cleanly.

---

# 20. Act8 rights grammar

Rights debate must have an immediate person/resource case.

Branch-born child is one case, not sole moral proof.

Use competing legitimate constraints:
- preserve lives,
- finite power/heat/storage,
- avoid creating new involuntary persons,
- wake/speak for quiescent societies,
- settlement deadlines.

No speech wins the ethics debate completely.

---

# 21. Act9 settlement grammar — corrected

Reject old `everyone gets a macro body` framing.

Current:
- planet-side ultradense civic substrate,
- remote avatars as equipment,
- optional persistent physical embodiments/continuity nodes for roles/people that need them,
- biological HUMAN life.

Personhood does not depend on body scale/form.

Final survival:
real destination constraints + existing systems/relationships.
No new villain/mythology.

---

# 22. Hook rotation

Rotate among:
- practical danger,
- relationship boundary,
- contradictory evidence,
- social rumor,
- institutional decision,
- ordinary person causal pivot,
- physical clue,
- moral reversal,
- local mystery answer.

Do not end every episode with `impossible memory`.
