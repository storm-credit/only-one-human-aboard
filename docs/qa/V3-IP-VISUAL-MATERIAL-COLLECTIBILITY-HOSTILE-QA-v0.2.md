# 2. Second-order hostile attacks

## Attack A — `We fixed collectibility by making a hidden mecha franchise.`

Risk: HIGH.

Attack:
The machine/craft package is now detailed enough that concept art could drift toward armed humanoid frames, ace machines, signature protagonist vehicle, upgrade progression, or fleet spectacle.

Current defense:
- high-memory machine families are utility-first,
- no protagonist signature craft,
- no ace-pilot lane,
- no fighter-combat fleet,
- service frame is a tool platform, not power armor fantasy,
- public-safety equipment is boundary/containment oriented,
- Outer-Ark craft exist to inspect, move, repair, rescue, deploy, and maintain.

Hard art rule:
**If an illustration becomes cooler by adding weapons, humanoid heroic proportions, or fighter styling, reject the addition.**

Verdict:
**PASS.**

---

## Attack B — `Merchandising has started selecting story elements.`

Risk: HIGH.

Attack:
Once model-kit/figure/pin potential is explicitly considered, design can reverse causality: retain an element because it would sell rather than because the civilization needs it.

Governance:
1. world/story utility first,
2. recurring reader recognition second,
3. optional IP/merch potential third.

No canon fact may be introduced solely for merchandise differentiation.
No fake rarity/limited-edition logic becomes a diegetic hierarchy unless society independently needs it.

Verdict:
**PASS WITH GOVERNANCE.**

---

## Attack C — `Every character is now a silhouette plus prop gimmick.`

Risk: MED-HIGH.

Attack:
Strong visual anchors can flatten people into props.

Repair rule:
- action/private goal/relationship must identify the character before the prop carries emotional meaning,
- personal anchors need not transform,
- several possessions should simply persist, wear out, get replaced, or disappear without becoming clues/payoffs,
- no prop may substitute for a missing relationship scene.

Specific enforcement:
The authoring pass must keep **at least three recurring personal possessions persistence-only** across the series: recognized through familiarity/wear, not converted into mystery evidence or symbolic master payoff.
Exact items may be chosen during scene cards; no new lore is required.

Verdict:
**PASS / EXECUTION GUARDRAIL.**

---

## Attack D — `Act7 proves the first six Acts were fake and therefore emotionally worthless.`

Risk: CRITICAL.

Attack:
The reveal that Experienced Meridian is implemented in ultradense substrate could be read as `none of that city was physically real`.

Required framing:
- experienced bodies, injury, distance, institutions, grief, sex, childhood, work and death were real causal lived experiences for D3 persons,
- the reveal changes implementation scale, not moral status,
- no character or narrator may use `가짜`, `게임`, `NPC`, `그냥 시뮬레이션` as authoritative explanatory language,
- characters may say those things as frightened/hostile interpretations, but the story must answer through consequences/personhood rather than lecture,
- Old Works/Midring/Garden memories remain emotionally valid after Outer Ark reveal,
- visual reveal should be **scale disjunction**, not `behind the cardboard set` exposure.

Recommended visual contrast:
Experienced Meridian is a mature inhabited civic world; Outer Ark is an austere industrial mission body. One is not the cheap backstage of the other.

Verdict:
**PASS IF FRAMING RULE IS ENFORCED / NO ARCHITECTURAL REOPEN.**

---

## Attack E — `Act9 becomes a toy catalogue because the reader finally reaches physical space.`

Risk: HIGH.

Current defense:
- settlement field-rig lineage descends from known clamp/isolation/cargo/service standards,
- final technical survival depends on familiar principles and ordinary worker decisions,
- exact lander remains lower-memory/open until mission/aero details justify it.

Hard cap:
**Act9 may introduce at most two new HIGH-memory machine/craft families.**
Everything else should be:
- a descendant of a familiar family,
- MED/LOW background equipment,
- or named functionally once and not collected.

Recommended HIGH candidates:
1. settlement commissioning field rig / local-island hardware family,
2. one deployment/lander family only if repeated enough to deserve it.

Verdict:
**PASS WITH CAP.**

---

## Attack F — `The 15 chains make the world feel written by a spreadsheet.`

Risk: HIGH.

Attack:
If tracked items recur too cleanly, readers infer that every mundane object matters.

Current defense:
exact overlay already defines ordinary-noise neighbors and most tracked families are not clues.

Additional execution rule:
- never force a tracked return into a scene whose current action does not naturally contain it,
- do not stack more than two deliberate recognition payoffs into an ordinary scene unless the scene is explicitly a transformation/recovery sequence,
- at least several early-life details must vanish from narrative attention forever,
- `missing return` is not automatically a continuity error.

Verdict:
**PASS.**

## Attack I — `Old equipment is fetishized because heritage sells.`

Risk: HIGH.

Current defense:
old != powerful; provenance != usefulness; certified legacy gear has both compatibility strengths and disadvantages.

New hard execution requirement:
At least one recurring old machine/equipment generation must be **retired because the newer solution is plainly safer/better for its actual job**, with no tragic `lost soul of craftsmanship` framing required.
This prevents heritage bias from becoming covert technology morality.

This can be inserted at existing maintenance/resource scenes and does not need a new arc.

Verdict:
**PASS WITH MICRO-EXECUTION REQUIREMENT.**
