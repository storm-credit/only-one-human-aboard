# 2. Heat rejection / radiator prominence

Known spacecraft engineering:
spacecraft must reject waste heat radiatively; high-power concepts can require large radiator areas and thermal-control architecture can become a major mass/geometry driver.

NASA examples/support families:
- spacecraft thermal-control/radiator systems,
- large high-temperature radiator concepts for nuclear electric propulsion,
- deployable/variable heat-rejection work.

Implication for OA-C1:
- visible radiator/thermal-rejection fields are justified,
- radiator orientation/deployment/service can affect silhouette,
- radiator damage/maintenance can create real operational constraints,
- `space is cold so cooling is trivial` is forbidden.

Verdict:
**PASS STRONGLY.**

Do not infer exact radiator area from present-day systems for far-future ship.

# 3. Charging / particle environment

Known engineering:
spacecraft charging can disturb/damage electronics and requires mitigation; energetic particles can produce transient/permanent electronic faults.

Implication:
- Act9 charging/particle hazard family is grounded at the level of phenomenon,
- local control isolation / fault containment / independent power are reasonable resilience architecture ideas,
- `asynchrony blocks radiation` remains rejected.

Verdict:
**PASS WITH EXISTING HARD-SCIENCE WORDING.**

# 4. Radiation shielding / forward protection

Known boundary:
shielding reduces radiation exposure/risk but is mass- and material-dependent; it is not perfect.

Interstellar-velocity dust/debris protection for a centuries-long fast ark is beyond proven engineering and remains speculative.

Implication:
- OA-C1 forward protection mass is logically appropriate as a fiction-engineering requirement,
- exact layered materials, stand-off systems and charged-particle deflection must remain unfrozen,
- no `perfect shield` or one magical material.

Verdict:
**MODIFY-ACCEPT / TOPOLOGY ONLY.**
