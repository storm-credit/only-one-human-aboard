## RT-MC-01 — Does adding weapons turn the novel into action/military SF?

Risk: `HIGH`

Attack:
Once named weapons, tactical units and cool gear exist, future drafting agents may escalate conflict through combat because it is easier to dramatize than law/family/resource pressure.

Mitigation:
- weapons remain a narrow public-safety subdomain,
- no weapon progression system,
- no standard civilian gun culture by default,
- high-penetration/high-energy force requires narrow authorization,
- ordinary security scenes should prefer access control, isolation, barriers, evacuation, restraint and investigation,
- tactical equipment only receives a proper noun/model name when it recurs materially.

Verdict:
`PASS WITH HARD GENRE GUARDRAIL`

---

## RT-MC-02 — Is “habitat-safe weapon” just a convenient handwave?

Risk: `P1`

Attack:
Calling a weapon safe does not make force harmless. A closed habitat still contains humans, critical equipment, crowds and confined geometry.

Rule:
Never claim a weapon is categorically safe. The setting may prefer lower-penetration / controlled-force tools because they reduce some risks, but every use retains injury, misidentification, equipment damage and escalation risk.

Verdict:
`PASS IF LANGUAGE = RISK-REDUCING, NOT SAFE`

---

## RT-MC-03 — Do industrial tools become an easy loophole for weapons?

Risk: `MED-HIGH`

Attack:
A society can restrict firearms while leaving cutting, thermal, pressure, chemical or heavy robotic tools readily available.

Required design:
- authorization follows hazard class and work zone,
- dangerous tools have workplace custody/audit expectations,
- removing them from controlled work context can itself be an offense or safety breach,
- not every tool is biometric-locked sci-fi magic,
- ordinary theft/misuse remains possible.

Verdict:
`OPEN UNTIL PACKAGE A CRIMINAL JUSTICE + PACKAGE C OPERATIONS`

## RT-MC-04 — Do relics become another hidden-truth delivery system?

Risk: `HIGH`

Attack:
The project already has controlled MacGuffins. Adding founder artifacts, old terminals and Earth objects could accidentally create a new “lost recording explains everything” structure.

Hard stop:
No artifact may contain:
- the whole Seed truth,
- Amara's identity,
- a founder command key,
- a hidden destination ownership grant,
- a larger ontology twist.

Historical artifacts may reveal local context, personal memory or era texture, but the central truth architecture remains distributed.

Verdict:
`PASS WITH CANON HARD STOP`

## RT-MC-05 — If every old object matters, does the world feel like a museum set?

Risk: `HIGH`

Mitigation:
- most old objects are repaired utility objects or ordinary possessions,
- only a minority have tracked provenance,
- Earth-origin objects range from culturally precious to merely interesting,
- replicas and restored composites are common enough that “old-looking” does not equal priceless,
- many family heirlooms matter only to the family.

Verdict:
`PASS WITH ORDINARY-OBJECT MAJORITY`

## RT-MC-06 — Does rarity become an implausible wealth ladder?

Risk: `MED`

Attack:
If elites collect Earth originals and large private spaces, collectibles could quietly create a hereditary aristocracy inconsistent with Layered Commons + Market City.

Required later economy cross-check:
- heritage goods can be expensive/status-bearing,
- survival capacity and scarce civic systems cannot simply be purchased through wealth,
- cultural prestige ≠ governing authority,
- inheritance/property taxes or stewardship rules may limit extreme concentration depending Package A selection.

Verdict:
`OPEN DEPENDENCY ON ECONOMY DEEP LAYER`

## RT-MC-08 — Can a physical heirloom create strong Reconstruction conflicts without breaking ontology?

Yes, but guardrails are required.

Example class:
A singular physical object belonged to a citizen before injury/death/reconstruction while different relatives or legal successors hold current possession claims.

Useful questions:
- who owns the object now?
- does reconstructed continuity restore ownership automatically?
- what if inheritance already settled?
- what if the object has family/public heritage value?

Hard stop:
The object does not prove metaphysical soul continuity.

Verdict:
`STRONG STORY ENGINE COMPATIBILITY`

## RT-MC-09 — Does strategic heritage create a fake preservation-vs-survival binary?

Risk: `HIGH`

Attack:
An old machine could be framed as “save history or save colony,” creating an artificial two-button moral puzzle.

Mitigation:
Possible options may include:
- document before transfer,
- remove nonfunctional heritage components,
- replicate some parts,
- keep operating temporarily,
- transfer only modules,
- preserve one example and move another,
- accept irreversible loss where no cheap third way exists.

The final choice can still be costly, but not cartoonishly binary.

Verdict:
`PASS WITH ACT5 CONTINUOUS-CHOICE RULE`

## RT-MC-10 — Does a collectibles market imply unlimited private consumption in a closed ecology?

No, if material accounting is layered.

Required principle:
- ordinary durable goods and collectibles can trade in a market,
- material/energy/waste externalities are priced or regulated through the broader commons system,
- some materials are capped/recycled/restricted,
- private ownership does not nullify safety/ecology rules.

Verdict:
`CONDITIONAL PASS / PACKAGE A+B+C DEPENDENCY`

## RT-MC-12 — Does this actually create useful collection pleasure?

Yes, if it stays layered.

Strong collection candidates:
1. recurring district objects/equipment the reader learns to recognize,
2. a few emotionally meaningful family heirlooms,
3. selected Earth/voyage artifacts that expose culture/history rather than secrets,
4. industrial assets whose meaning changes from everyday redundancy to arrival capital,
5. affordable ordinary collectibles showing actual hobbies and trends.

Weak/bad collection candidates:
- numbered legendary items,
- hidden superweapons,
- founder keys,
- every old object as clue,
- power progression through gear.

Verdict:
`PASS / GOOD SECONDARY SERIAL ENGINE`
