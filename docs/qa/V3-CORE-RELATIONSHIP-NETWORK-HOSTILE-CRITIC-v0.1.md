---

# Attack 1 — Is protagonist still clearly the main character?

PASS.

The network deliberately expands secondary causality but does not give another character an equivalent series-spine mystery/identity arc.

Guardrail:
- protagonist owns central H-A→H-B subjective rupture,
- Arun wound,
- major interpretive chain,
- final personal worldview movement.

Risk remains if secondary POV allocation later exceeds the v0.2 protagonist-centered band.

---

# Attack 2 — Does every important relationship secretly still route through protagonist?

PASS.

Strong bypass edges exist:
- Mira↔Juno,
- Selene↔Rafi,
- Selene↔Sora,
- Rafi↔Leila,
- Sora↔Hana,
- Niko↔Gideon,
- Mira↔Selene,
- Juno↔Rafi,
- Sora↔Cassian,
- Hana↔Ivo.

At least one Act6 solution path bypasses protagonist entirely.

Remaining gate:
these edges must actually receive scenes/consequences; listing them in a bible is not enough.

---

# Attack 3 — Does H-B become too different too quickly?

PASS only with strict delta budget.

If Mira, Arun, family, job, home, mentor, and institutions all differ immediately, regression imitation collapses too early.

Required:
- initial H-B recognition remains ~80–90% familiar in texture,
- only 3~5 high-salience deltas across Act3,
- family remains mostly stabilizing early,
- Arun contradiction should not be fully paid before its intended window.

P1-1.

---

# Attack 4 — Does H-A romance make H-B Mira feel like a replacement?

Risk: HIGH.

Repair requirement:
- H-B Mira must get more cumulative page-time than H-A Mira after Act3,
- her present goals and relationships must matter independently,
- protagonist's attempt to restore intimacy must fail early enough to establish boundary,
- later alternate spouse/child material may recontextualize but cannot become the desired `true family`.

P1-2.

---

# Attack 5 — Does the protagonist family become cheap stakes?

PASS with guardrail.

Parents/sibling/child make the city personal, but repeated family-danger arcs would feel manipulative.

Required:
- at least half of family scenes across series are mundane/private, not crisis,
- no family member secretly knows mission truth,
- no repeated kidnapping/hostage mechanism,
- family H-B deltas stay low-frequency.

P1-3.

---

# Attack 6 — Are secondary relationships too schematic (`order vs compassion`, `truth vs secrecy`)?

Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH.

Current edges are structurally useful but can feel like debate diagrams if every interaction is ideological.

Repair:
Every durable pair also needs ordinary texture:
- shared work history,
- private joke or irritation,
- practical favor/debt,
- social context outside crisis,
- mundane disagreement.

Do not let every conversation announce thematic opposition.

P1-4.

---

# Attack 7 — Does Act6 bypass path make protagonist irrelevant at the climax?

PASS if separated into local vs Act climax.

A major population corridor/resource outcome may be solved without protagonist.
The Act's emotional/series-level climax should still involve a consequential protagonist choice or cost.

World can solve one problem without him; the novel cannot outsource his entire Act arc.

---

# Attack 8 — Does emergent actor cascade become deus ex machina?

PASS only if selected actor is seeded early and uses visible access/skill.

Do not choose exact actor until district/incident map exists.

P1-5.

---

# Attack 9 — Do too many relationship contradictions repeat the same gimmick?

Risk: HIGH.

Rotate contradiction families:
- romance/intimacy,
- authority/reputation,
- professional competence,
- guilt/debt,
- family role,
- rescue/abandonment,
- truth/publication,
- convergent trust despite divergent history.

Not every testimony should be correct.

P1-6.

---

# Attack 10 — Can the relationship graph support 400+ episodes?

PASS CANDIDATE.

Reasons:
- 10+ persistent secondary edges,
- 3 durable off-protagonist arcs,
- institutional triangles,
- family cluster,
- H-A/H-B selective deltas,
- emergent actor insertion points.

But scene ownership and voice are not yet frozen.
