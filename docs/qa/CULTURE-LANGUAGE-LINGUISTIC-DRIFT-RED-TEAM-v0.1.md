# RT-LNG-01 — Would 450 years really preserve one common language?

Yes plausibly under unusually strong stabilizing forces:
- 300k-scale connected population
- mass literacy
- universal schooling
- continuous recorded media
- law/technical standards
- cross-Habitat movement
- electronic communication

The model still requires noticeable pronunciation/slang/semantic drift.

Verdict: `PASS`.

---

# RT-LNG-02 — Why wouldn’t it become a totally new creole?

It may evolve enough that a launch-era speaker would hear strong differences, but authoring a full conlang brings little story value.

Canon can treat current Common as a descendant language while Korean prose translates it naturally.

Verdict: `PASS`.

# RT-LNG-03 — Why are multicultural surnames still around after centuries?

Naming traditions can persist through family/cultural continuity even when common spoken language converges.
Intermarriage does not require surname homogenization.

Verdict: `PASS STRONG`.

# RT-LNG-04 — Could heritage languages survive for 450 years without territorial states?

Unevenly, yes through:
- family practice
- religion
- arts/media
- schools/clubs
- archives

Hard realism:
Some shrink to partial/ceremonial fluency; not all remain equally vibrant.

Verdict: `PASS WITH UNEVEN SURVIVAL`.

# RT-LNG-05 — Does excellent translation make language learning pointless?

No.
People learn languages for:
- intimacy/family
- identity
- art/religion
- status/hobby
- historical research
- nuance

Translation handles utility, not every social meaning.

Verdict: `PASS`.

# RT-LNG-06 — Could translation software solve the old Human/Seed terminology mystery instantly?

No.
The issue is not lexical translation but institutional semantic mapping across eras and databases.
A translator can render a word while still missing legal/provenance category equivalence.

Verdict: `PASS STRONG`.

# RT-LNG-09 — Does Korean translation convention hide character voice?

Voice differences are rendered through natural Korean choices in:
- sentence length
- politeness
- slang density
- professional register
- age
- social relationship

# RT-LNG-16 — Does semantic drift look like author cheating?

It is fair only if:
- old/current usages appear before payoff
- experts disagree over mapping for concrete reasons
- terms evolved through ordinary institutional history
- no narrator falsely defines the term

Existing Foreshadow/Information Ladder satisfies the structural basis.

Verdict: `PASS WITH EP REGRESSION`.
