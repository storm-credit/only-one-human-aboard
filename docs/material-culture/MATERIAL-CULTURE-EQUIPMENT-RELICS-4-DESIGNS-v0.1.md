# Material Culture / Equipment / Relics — 4 Designs v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC DEEP DESIGN / PACKAGE C-M / DESIGN COMPARISON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-17
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## Purpose

The Meridian is a ~450-year-old closed civilization. A reference-grade setting bible must define not only institutions and technology, but also the material objects people live with, inherit, repair, collect, regulate, preserve and fight over.

This package covers:
- civilian everyday objects,
- personal interfaces,
- work / medical / survival equipment,
- public-safety equipment and weapon boundaries,
- restricted or dangerous technology,
- Earth-origin and voyage-era artifacts,
- family heirlooms,
- museums / preservation / provenance,
- antique and collectible markets,
- strategic industrial assets that can carry cultural as well as functional value.

It must NOT convert the novel into:
- loot SF,
- secret-superweapon fiction,
- dungeon archaeology,
- a collectible checklist visible to the reader.

The intended function is world depth + selective collection/completion pleasure + concrete story consequences.

---

# Design MC-A — Functional Minimalism

Concept:
Only define objects when an episode directly needs them.

Strengths:
- lowest lore burden,
- avoids gadget fetish,
- easy prose execution.

Weaknesses:
- a 450-year civilization feels strangely objectless,
- historical continuity remains abstract,
- weapons/security rules get invented ad hoc,
- museums, heirlooms, repair culture and collectibles lack texture,
- random-scene test likely fails.

Verdict:
`REJECT AS ENCYCLOPEDIC STANDARD`

---

# Design MC-B — Tactical / Gadget Catalogue

Concept:
Deep catalogue of weapons, suits, drones, interfaces and specialized hardware.

Strengths:
- strong visual identity,
- easy marketing/art reference,
- strong equipment-recognition pleasure.

Weaknesses:
- pushes the work toward military/action SF,
- encourages escalation through better gear,
- makes technology solve social conflict,
- risks an implausibly broad private weapons culture inside a closed habitat,
- distracts from the social-SF reader promise.

Verdict:
`REJECT AS PRIMARY / USE ONLY SELECT TECHNICAL DEPTH`

---

# Design MC-C — Museum Civilization

Concept:
Material culture is organized around inherited Earth objects, launch-era artifacts, family heirlooms, old district objects and preservation institutions.

Strengths:
- excellent fit for 450-year history,
- makes Earth memory physically concrete,
- creates ownership/provenance/museum disputes,
- supports emotional collection pleasure without RPG logic,
- gives Old Quarter and family history tactile depth.

Weaknesses:
- can become nostalgia-heavy,
- too many meaningful relics make ordinary life feel curated,
- every old object may start looking like a clue.

Verdict:
`STRONG CULTURAL LAYER / INSUFFICIENT ALONE`

---

# Design MC-D — Layered Material Ecology

Concept:
Treat objects as a complete ecology with five layers:

1. ordinary disposable/recyclable goods,
2. durable personal/family goods,
3. professional and safety equipment,
4. scarce strategic machinery / legacy components,
5. historical or provenance-rich artifacts.

Weapons are a narrow subset of safety/security regulation rather than a genre pillar.
Relics are valuable because of provenance, continuity and social meaning—not hidden power.

Strengths:
- strongest encyclopedic coverage,
- ordinary objects greatly outnumber plot objects,
- naturally supports repair/reuse culture,
- integrates economy, law, history and arrival logistics,
- supports subtle collection pleasure,
- avoids turning every old object into MacGuffin.

Weaknesses:
- requires provenance/ownership rules,
- requires careful boundary between collectible artifact and strategic asset,
- can become overdesigned if every object gets a named model.

Verdict:
`SELECTED BASE`

---

# Selected Hybrid — MC-H1

Name:
**MC-H1 — Layered Material Ecology + Selective Heritage Objects**

Base:
- MC-D material ecology.

Add:
- MC-C heritage/provenance depth.
- MC-B only for visually/operationally recurring equipment.
- MC-A as anti-overdesign discipline for unimportant objects.

Core principles:
1. Most objects are ordinary and unnamed.
2. Named equipment must recur, affect safety/choice, or carry strong visual/story identity.
3. Historical objects have provenance value, not supernatural/secret technical power.
4. Earth-origin objects are rare but not all priceless sacred relics.
5. Repair, remanufacture and parts cannibalization are normal material culture in a closed system.
6. High-energy/high-penetration weapons are not routine civilian lifestyle objects.
7. Public safety prioritizes habitat-safe restraint, isolation, access control and controlled force.
8. Industrial tools may be dangerous without being treated as weapons by default.
9. Strategic settlement assets cannot be reduced to private collectibles because their functional value belongs to commons/enterprise governance.
10. A collectible/relic can matter strongly to one family without becoming a series MacGuffin.
11. Ordinary material details must outnumber clue-bearing objects.
12. No hidden superweapon / founder key / command artifact may be introduced without Canon reopening.

# Required Bible Domains

## MC-01 — Civilian Everyday Objects
Lock reference-grade answers for:
- personal terminals/interfaces,
- bags/containers,
- household tools,
- kitchenware,
- school objects,
- toys,
- paper/physical media niche,
- reusable packaging,
- repairability expectations,
- personalization/status signals.

## MC-02 — Personal Computing / Wearables
Need:
- ordinary interface form,
- authentication,
- privacy boundary,
- offline/emergency function,
- child access,
- workplace devices,
- what people do when a device fails.

No magical universal AR exposition layer.

## MC-03 — Medical / Reconstruction Equipment
Need hierarchy between:
- ordinary clinic tools,
- emergency tools,
- imaging/monitoring,
- Neural Anchor/Recovery Map infrastructure,
- scarce Reconstruction equipment.

Never allow a handheld adult-origin scanner.

## MC-04 — Work / Utility / Industrial Equipment
Need recurring families for:
- water/reclamation,
- precision manufacturing,
- maintenance,
- structural inspection,
- thermal systems,
- cargo handling,
- agricultural/ecology work.

## MC-05 — EVA / Outer Works / Emergency Gear
Need:
- suits/protective layers,
- tether/access philosophy,
- portable seal/air tools,
- emergency beacons,
- rescue hardware,
- restricted-access equipment.

Reader-facing terminology should stay simple unless the device is story-relevant.

## MC-06 — Public Safety / Weapon Boundary
Need explicit world rules for:
- civilian weapon possession,
- public-safety force options,
- detention/restraint tools,
- dangerous tool regulation,
- high-penetration projectile restrictions,
- emergency exceptions,
- security storage/audit,
- weaponized drone/autonomous-force prohibition or limits.

Design direction:
Meridian should prefer habitat-safe containment/access-control/less-lethal methods over ordinary high-penetration gun culture.
Exact criminal-law implementation belongs to Package A and must cross-link here.

## MC-07 — Restricted / Dangerous Technology
Candidates requiring rule treatment:
- unauthorized medical modification,
- identity/provenance tampering devices,
- surveillance hardware,
- industrial cutting/energy tools in public areas,
- dangerous autonomous machinery,
- emergency override hardware.

Do not create cool banned tech solely for plot temptation.

## MC-08 — Earth-Origin Objects
Reference classes:
- authenticated Earth-manufactured objects,
- launch cargo objects,
- founder-generation personal possessions,
- biological/cultural archive objects.

Potential examples:
- mechanical watch,
- printed book,
- musical instrument,
- textile/garment,
- child's toy,
- hand tool,
- sports object,
- photographs/physical art.

Rule:
Their value comes from provenance and continuity, not superior function.

## MC-09 — Voyage-Era Artifacts
Era bands should eventually align with Package D history:
- launch/founding,
- early survival,
- Seed adoption,
- civic normalization,
- habitat renovation/expansion,
- modern pre-arrival.

## MC-10 — Family Heirlooms / Personal Keepsakes
Need:
- inheritance norms,
- reconstructed-person ownership disputes,
- duplicated records vs singular physical object,
- co-parent/family possession conflicts,
- memorial objects.

## MC-11 — Museums / Archives / Preservation
Need:
- who authenticates provenance,
- public vs private ownership,
- loan/donation rules,
- conservation resources,
- contested removal from working infrastructure,
- replicas vs originals.

## MC-12 — Collectibles / Antique Market
Need:
- legal private collecting,
- provenance certificates,
- fakes/restorations,
- market pricing under scarcity,
- cultural fashions,
- ordinary affordable collectibles,
- elite-status signaling limits.

This is where subtle reader-facing collection pleasure can emerge without loot mechanics.

## MC-13 — Strategic Industrial Heritage
Some old machinery can be both:
- historically important,
- still functional,
- needed for arrival bootstrap.

This creates real tension:
`preserve / keep operating / transfer / dismantle / replicate first`.

It must link to CK-01 and Act 5 but not every strategic asset needs heritage status.

## MC-14 — Salvage / Ownership / Found Objects
Need:
- abandoned object definition,
- ship/common infrastructure ownership,
- private salvage limits,
- found property,
- historical discovery reporting,
- derelict/closed-zone recovery permissions.

No frontier `finders keepers` assumption inside Meridian.

## MC-15 — Material Status Signals
Need to define what subtle status looks like in a society with a civic floor:
- better location/access,
- bespoke craftsmanship,
- rare materials,
- heritage goods,
- private space,
- time/service access,
- professional equipment.

Avoid costume-coded caste shorthand.

# Collection / Completion Pleasure Rule

The setting may intentionally support four material collection pleasures:

1. **Recognition** — readers recognize recurring equipment/place objects.
2. **Provenance** — an ordinary-looking object gains historical/family meaning.
3. **Return** — an early object/system matters later in a different context.
4. **Completion** — readers gradually understand how Meridian's material civilization fits together.

Forbidden primary loop:
`find rare item → gain power → seek rarer item`.

Preferred loop:
`see ordinary object → understand use → later learn history/ownership/cost → see it return in a consequential choice`.

# Completion Gate

Package C-M cannot pass until:
1. object taxonomy is complete,
2. weapon/public-safety boundary is reconciled with criminal justice,
3. everyday tech does not contradict AI/automation rules,
4. at least 5 artifact/provenance classes are grounded in 450-year history,
5. museum/private ownership/salvage rules exist,
6. collectible market has ordinary + high-end examples,
7. recurring cast receive only story-useful personal objects,
8. strategic heritage conflicts regress against Act 5/7,
9. no secret-superweapon/chosen-one artifact regression,
10. random-object scene test passes without inventing major new rules.
