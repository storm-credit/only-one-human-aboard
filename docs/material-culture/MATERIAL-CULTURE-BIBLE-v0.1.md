# 1. Core Material-Culture Principle

Meridian is not a minimalist spacecraft interior preserved unchanged for centuries.
It is a ~300,000-person city-civilization that has spent centuries:
- making,
- repairing,
- remanufacturing,
- inheriting,
- discarding,
- recycling,
- collecting,
- authenticating,
- adapting
physical objects.

Therefore the world should contain both:
- extremely advanced systems,
- boring durable household goods,
- repeatedly repaired old objects,
- fashionable new goods,
- cheap souvenirs,
- family keepsakes,
- museum artifacts,
- industrial machines older than their operators,
- replacements that are technically better but culturally less valued.

Hard rule:
**Old ≠ powerful. Rare ≠ useful. Historically important ≠ technically superior.**

# 2. Functional Object Classes

These are author-facing functional classes, not reader-facing rarity ranks.

## O1 — Ordinary Cycle Goods
Examples:
- food containers,
- hygiene consumables,
- simple school supplies,
- low-value household components,
- common textiles,
- replaceable filters/liners,
- routine packaging.

Characteristics:
- high recycling/remanufacture rate,
- low provenance interest,
- usually unnamed in prose.

## O2 — Durable Personal / Household Goods
Examples:
- furniture modules,
- cookware,
- personal terminals,
- musical instruments,
- hobby equipment,
- tools,
- children's objects,
- decorative goods.

Characteristics:
- repaired rather than constantly replaced,
- personalization common,
- can become family heirlooms without being objectively rare.

## O3 — Professional / Controlled Equipment
Examples:
- medical tools,
- structural inspection gear,
- water/reclamation equipment,
- cargo systems,
- Outer Works protective equipment,
- emergency-response equipment,
- hazardous industrial tools.

Characteristics:
- training/authorization may be required,
- custody and maintenance matter,
- misuse can be a safety/legal issue.

## O4 — Strategic Commons / Enterprise Assets
Examples:
- reserve pumps,
- radiator modules,
- precision fabrication systems,
- calibration/measurement systems,
- high-value manufacturing tools,
- redundant life-support machinery,
- arrival-bootstrap designated equipment.

Characteristics:
- cannot be treated like casual private collectibles,
- allocation is tied to institutional/cooperative/common-resource governance,
- may become Act 5/7 irreversible assets.

## O5 — Heritage / Provenance-Rich Objects
Examples:
- authenticated Earth-origin possessions,
- launch-generation objects,
- early-voyage artifacts,
- locally important Old Quarter objects,
- family heirlooms,
- objects tied to major historical reforms or disasters.

Characteristics:
- value depends on provenance, story and social meaning,
- may still be functional or completely obsolete,
- do not contain hidden superpowers or universal truth.

One object can belong to more than one functional class over time.
Example:
a working legacy machine may be O3/O4 today and O5 historically.

# 3. Provenance States

Do not use RPG-like rarity tiers.
Use provenance quality instead.

## P-A — Documented Provenance
Chain of custody / manufacture / ownership is strongly supported.

## P-B — Attributed Provenance
Strong contextual evidence, but incomplete chain.

## P-C — Restored / Composite Historical Object
Substantial original material remains, but later replacement parts are significant.

## P-D — Historical Replica / Reproduction
Made later to reproduce an earlier object/design.
Can still have cultural value.

## P-E — Uncertain / Disputed Provenance
Origin claim is unresolved or contested.

Important:
`P-A` is not automatically more emotionally valuable than `P-C` or `P-D`.
A repaired family object can matter more to its owners than an authenticated museum piece.

# 4. Repair / Replacement Culture

Closed-system life creates a strong repair culture, but not a romantic universal anti-consumption ideology.

Normal assumptions:
- many durable goods are designed for repair or module replacement,
- local fabrication makes common components,
- some high-precision or legacy components are difficult to reproduce,
- remanufactured parts are ordinary,
- visible repair is not automatically a poverty marker,
- replacement can still be desirable for fashion, safety, efficiency or convenience,
- old equipment may be kept because certification/compatibility makes replacement expensive.

Social variation:
- some people value immaculate new goods,
- some prefer repaired heritage objects,
- some do not care,
- some hobbies revolve around restoration.

# 5. Personal Interfaces / Everyday Devices

Design direction:
Most citizens use a personal networked interface ecosystem rather than one magical all-purpose hologram.

Reference requirements:
- identity/authentication is layered,
- household/work/public systems do not expose the same permissions,
- devices can fail or lose network access,
- emergency/public alerts have separate resilient paths,
- children have age/guardian-dependent permissions,
- privacy-sensitive records require more than casual device possession.

Hard stops:
- no omniscient personal assistant that knows all protected information,
- no device capable of casually scanning adult origin provenance,
- no universal interface that makes every scene visually identical.

Exact everyday AI assistance remains dependent on Package C AI/Automation Bible.

# 9. Public Safety / Weapon Boundary — PROVISIONAL

This section is deliberately high-level until Package A criminal justice is selected.

## Core direction
Meridian is a closed inhabited environment where uncontrolled force can injure people and damage critical systems.

Therefore:
- devices primarily designed for high-penetration/high-energy interpersonal violence are not assumed to be ordinary civilian lifestyle goods,
- public-safety doctrine prefers prevention, controlled access, barriers, isolation, evacuation, restraint and risk-reducing force options,
- specialized higher-force response may exist but requires narrow authorization, storage and review,
- dangerous industrial tools are regulated primarily by hazard/work context, not declared weapons by default,
- personal safety for ordinary citizens should rely more on alert/escape/access/security systems than an arms-race culture.

## Civilian self-protection candidates
Non-offensive baseline options may include:
- emergency alert/wearable distress function,
- location-share-by-consent/emergency escalation,
- secure doors/compartment access,
- public call points / rapid response,
- locally legal low-harm personal safety tools if later approved by criminal-law design.

## Public Safety response categories
Author-facing hierarchy:
1. communication / de-escalation,
2. access denial / compartment control,
3. physical barrier / separation,
4. manual restraint / controlled capture,
5. risk-reducing incapacitation tools,
6. specialized armed response for extraordinary threats.

Hard rule:
No category is described as perfectly safe.

## Autonomous force
No broad autonomous system may independently decide guilt and apply open-ended lethal force.
Exact automation boundary remains Package C dependency.

# 10. Dangerous Industrial Tools

Problem:
A closed civilization cannot pretend violence disappears because conventional weapons are restricted.
Many necessary tools are dangerous.

Design direction:
Hazardous professional equipment may use combinations of:
- training requirements,
- zone restrictions,
- issue/return records,
- tool-room or workplace custody,
- maintenance logs,
- supervisor/work-order authorization where appropriate.

Guardrail:
Do not make every dangerous tool impossible to steal or misuse through magical biometric locks.
Safety systems reduce risk; they do not remove crime or human error.

Exact offense/sentencing rules belong to Package A.

- mechanical watch,
- printed book,
- musical instrument,
- hand tool,
- textile/garment fragment or complete garment,
- child's toy,
- sports equipment,
- physical artwork,
- household object,
- analog camera/optical object.

Strong rule:
Earth objects should usually be technologically obsolete or ordinary compared with modern equivalents.
Their power is:
- direct material continuity,
- provenance,
- rarity,
- family/public memory,
- symbolic argument over what “home” means.

Do not make an Earth object necessary to save Meridian.

# 13. Family Heirlooms

A complete setting must distinguish public history from private inheritance.

Heirlooms may be:
- mundane household objects,
- repaired tools,
- jewelry/personal adornment,
- recipes/handwritten physical media,
- toys,
- instruments,
- occupation-linked objects,
- Earth/voyage artifacts,
- ship-born local items.

Reconstruction creates special legal/emotional questions:
- if inheritance settled during death/recovery, what returns with the reconstructed citizen?
- does restored legal continuity restore possession automatically?
- what if a singular object is already held by a descendant?
- what if public heritage claims conflict with family ownership?

Core direction:
A reconstructed citizen's personhood does not automatically erase intervening property rights.
Exact doctrine belongs to Package A property/inheritance.

# 14. Museum / Heritage Governance — PROVISIONAL

Meridian should support a distributed heritage ecosystem rather than one giant sacred museum.

Possible institutional functions:
- public museum/exhibition spaces,
- archive-linked provenance specialists,
- district/local heritage collections,
- school/educational collections,
- cooperative/private collections,

Needed later:
- authentication standards,
- donation/loan rules,
- stewardship of fragile objects,
- export/transfer restrictions during arrival,
- dispute process,

# 16. Salvage / Found Object Direction — PROVISIONAL

Inside an inhabited generation ship, `I found it, therefore it is mine` is not the default.

Likely distinctions:
- abandoned personal property,
- lost property,
- cooperative/company property,
- public/common infrastructure,
- decommissioned equipment,
- controlled-zone materials,
- historical artifacts.

Found historical material may create a reporting/provenance duty.
Decommissioned strategic machinery remains subject to ownership/safety rules.

Exact salvage law waits for Package A property/criminal law.

# 17. Strategic Heritage Objects

A few machines/places may carry both functional and historical value.

Examples:
- early-generation fabrication equipment still used for niche work,
- a long-running pump/utility assembly with replaced internals but continuous service history,
- an old district structural element or public mechanism,
- legacy precision equipment difficult to reproduce immediately.

Story value:
These create multi-axis decisions:
- keep working,
- preserve,
- document,
- dismantle,
- transfer,
- replicate first,
- accept partial loss.

Hard rule:
Do not make all Act 5 strategic assets heritage objects. That would sentimentalize an engineering/resource conflict.

# 19. Reader-Facing Collection Engine

The novel should not display a checklist.
The authoring system may track recurring material motifs privately.

## CE-M1 — Place Objects
Objects that make recurring locations recognizable.

## CE-M2 — Profession Objects
Recurring tools that teach the reader how a profession works.

## CE-M3 — Personal Objects
A small number of possessions that accrue character history.

## CE-M4 — Heritage Objects
Occasional objects that make 450 years of history tactile.

## CE-M5 — Transforming Assets
Objects/systems whose meaning changes across Acts.
Example pattern:
ordinary redundancy → budget dispute → arrival asset → physical loss/transfer → new settlement infrastructure.

Best practice:
The reader first encounters the object in its ordinary function before it acquires larger narrative meaning.

# 20. Candidate High-Salience Object Archetypes

These are archetypes, NOT locked exact props.

1. **Earth-made mechanical personal object** — cultural continuity; no technical advantage.
2. **Repaired family kitchen object / recipe medium** — intimate inheritance; useful for Ella-type domestic history if later selected.
3. **Youth-program or sport object** — Noah's non-theme life / growing autonomy.
4. **Old Quarter building object/sign/tool** — place continuity without turning Jun into nostalgia mascot.
5. **Water/reclamation work tool** — Amara's profession, explicitly non-unique.
6. **Reconstruction rehab possession returned late** — ownership/continuity case engine.
7. **Historical legal/administrative physical artifact** — local context only; never truth key.
8. **Precision calibration object/system** — explains why industrial capability is more than raw metal.
9. **Retired popular-culture collectible** — demonstrates hobbies/fashion independent of plot.
10. **Strategic machine with partial heritage status** — Act 5 preservation/function tradeoff candidate.

Do not lock these to characters until Character Encyclopedia v2 unless story function is clear.

# 21. Narrative Engineering Cross-Link Rules

Only high-salience objects enter Package G Device Atlas.
