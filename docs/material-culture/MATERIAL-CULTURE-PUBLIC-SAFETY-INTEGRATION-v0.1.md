# 1. Locked Provisional Direction

Selected compatibility direction:

**Material Culture MC-H1 + Criminal Justice CJ-H1 are mutually compatible.**

Core logic:
- Meridian is a civilian city in a closed engineered environment.
- violence exists, but high-penetration/high-energy weapons impose unusual third-party infrastructure risk.
- public-safety doctrine therefore emphasizes separation, barriers, restraint and controlled incapacitation before high-force options.
- dangerous industrial tools remain common enough that weapon restriction cannot equal elimination of violent capability.

This is not a pacifist utopia and not an armed spaceship society.

# 2. Author-Facing Equipment Classes

These are functional design classes, not reader-facing weapon tiers.

## PS-E1 — Civilian Safety / Escape
Functions:
- call for help
- alert nearby systems/people
- improve escape/access security
- emergency communication

May include regulated low-harm defensive tools later.

Default cultural emphasis:
`escape + rapid help + secure access`, not personal arms race.

## PS-E2 — Routine Public-Safety Control
Functions:
- protective barriers
- separation
- restraint
- scene safety
- controlled low-penetration incapacitation where lawful

Doctrine:
These tools are not described as harmless; injury review still applies.

## PS-E3 — Specialist High-Risk Response
Functions:
Respond to rare imminent grave violence or dangerous stolen equipment.

Rules:
- restricted storage
- restricted issue
- explicit authorization chain
- use logging
- mandatory review

Not routine patrol equipment by default.

## PS-E4 — Hazardous Professional Tools
Examples by function:
- cutting
- high-force fastening/removal
- pressure systems
- thermal work
- powered maintenance
- hazardous material handling

These are work tools, not automatically weapons.
Controls focus on:
- training
- work-zone access
- custody/issue where appropriate
- maintenance
- work authorization

Misuse can become criminal without criminalizing ordinary professional possession.

## PS-E5 — Emergency / Technical Incident Equipment
Functions:
- seal/isolate
- evacuate
- rescue
- protect breathing/eyes/body
- detect hazardous conditions
- control access to incident zones

Technical incident command and civic public safety share some emergency equipment but retain different authority.

# 3. Civilian Weapon Policy — Provisional Rule

Civilian ownership is **not defined as absolute zero**.

However, ordinary civilian culture does not normalize devices optimized for:
- uncontrolled penetration
- broad-area injury
- high-energy discharge
- environmental contamination

Reason:
The policy is based on habitat externality and public risk, not on the claim that future humans no longer commit violence.

Exact licensing/categories wait for Society Bible consolidation.

---

# 4. Public-Safety Weapon Policy

Public-safety staff are not a permanently militarized occupation.

Routine doctrine prioritizes:
1. communication and de-escalation
2. distance / exit / evacuation
3. barriers and compartment/access control
4. physical separation/restraint
5. controlled incapacitation
6. exceptional higher force

Higher-force capability exists because:
- industrial tools can be weaponized
- restricted equipment can be stolen
- severe interpersonal violence exists
- improvised threats exist

But it is not the visual default of public-safety presence.

---

# 5. Ship Operations ≠ Police Arsenal

Ship Operations Command may control hazardous technical equipment for navigation/structural/exterior work.

This does NOT mean:
- operations staff are police
- technical command can deploy equipment for political coercion
- engineering access authorizes criminal investigation

During incidents:
- technical command controls the hazard
- civic public safety handles people/evidence/order within its authority
- persistent rights restrictions require civic/legal basis

# 6. Autonomous Force Boundary

Until Package C AI/Automation Bible closes:
- no autonomous system independently selects citizens for open-ended coercive force
- automated doors/barriers may perform narrow pre-authorized safety isolation
- anomaly detection may alert staff
- guilt, arrest and punitive decisions require accountable human/legal process

This is a hard compatibility requirement with CJ-H1.

---

# 7. Object Evidence / Forensics

A tool/object may carry:
- issue history
- maintenance history
- work-order context
- access metadata
- physical traces

But no object log is omniscient.

Evidence principle:
`credential/object log = evidence of system interaction, not automatic proof of identity, motive or guilt`.

This mirrors PR-H1 access-metadata discipline.

---

# 8. Heritage Weapons / Historical Arms

Possible Earth/founding-era weapons may survive as heritage objects, but if selected later:
- they are historically valuable rather than combat superior
- operability may be restricted or disabled by stewardship rules
- possession/display/transfer follows heritage + safety law
- they do not become collectible power-ups

No hidden founding arsenal may become a late solution without Canon reopening.

---

# 9. Black / Gray Market Material Culture

Illicit object markets may plausibly involve:
- stolen professional tools
- unauthorized modifications
- restricted components
- forged provenance
- stolen credentials/devices
- diverted luxury/heritage goods

Avoid:
- giant underground weapons economy unless later world design proves demand/supply plausibility
- criminal control of oxygen/water as default setting shorthand

---

# 10. Collection-Engine Guardrail

Weapons must NOT become the easiest route to reader collection pleasure.

Preferred repeat-recognition order remains:
1. ordinary place object
2. profession tool
3. personal object
4. heritage object
5. transforming strategic asset

Public-safety objects may recur to make institutions legible, but the series should not read like equipment unlock progression.
