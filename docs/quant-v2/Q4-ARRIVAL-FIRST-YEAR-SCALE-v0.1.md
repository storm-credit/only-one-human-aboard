# 1. Temporal Rule

## EP216 / T=0
`System insertion` means the 450-year interstellar-voyage phase ends and Meridian is securely bound to the destination-system operational plan.

It does **not** require Meridian to instantaneously occupy its final long-term planetary staging orbit on the same day.

## T=0 → first weeks/months
- refine in-system trajectories,
- deploy probes/communications,
- move to/establish stable operational geometry,
- begin orbital/resource-site construction,
- keep surface deployment smaller/slower.

This preserves EP217 `Orbit First` without an implausible instant finished orbit-city.

# 2. Planetary Hill / Staging-Orbit Sanity

Working settlement planet:
~1.01 M⊕ around ~0.75 M☉ at ~0.52 AU.

Approximate Hill radius:
**~0.86 million km**.

A conservative prograde long-term operational region is well inside roughly half that scale.

Selected Meridian high-staging-orbit candidate:
**~100,000–250,000 km altitude class** above the planet after initial in-system stabilization.

Approx circular periods for Earthlike mass:
- 100k km altitude → ~4 days,
- 150k → ~7 days,
- 200k → ~11 days,
- 250k → ~15 days.

Exact orbit may be elliptical or selected for thermal/navigation/resource geometry and remains C2 within safe bound.

Why high:
- keeps a civilization-scale ship away from atmosphere/debris/surface traffic,
- preserves operational room,
- supports staged lower-orbit transfer nodes,
- makes surface settlement meaningfully separate rather than `city parked overhead`.

---

# 3. Lower Planetary Transfer Layer

Surface logistics need not operate directly from Meridian's high orbit every time.

Candidate architecture:
- Meridian high staging orbit,
- one or more lower transfer/communications/assembly nodes in **~1,000–10,000 km altitude class** or equivalent useful orbits,
- surface descent vehicles from lower nodes,
- separate resource-node trajectories where needed.

Exact low-orbit altitude is C2 because atmosphere, moons and traffic architecture remain mission-survey dependent.

This layered system helps distinguish:
`Meridian home ↔ orbital logistics ↔ surface`.

# 4. First-Year Off-Meridian Population Ceiling

Series-ending scale target:
**fewer than ~1,500 people are in persistent/long-rotation residence away from Meridian at any one time by ~+1 year.**

Preferred center:
**~600–1,000 people total external long-rotation/persistent population**.

This keeps >99.5% of ~300k citizens primarily Meridian-based.

The exact number is not reader-facing unless useful.

# 8. External Power Scale

The external sites are industrially power-rich per person but tiny relative to Meridian.

## Orbital/resource network by +1y
Installed reliable power:
**~100–350 MW class**.

## Surface foothold by +1y
Installed reliable power:
**~30–120 MW class**.

## Combined external installed/available industrial power
**~0.15–0.45 GW class**.

Sources may include Meridian-transferred compact fusion/power modules plus local storage/distribution.
Exact reactor models remain C2.

This is enough for:
- robotics,
- water/oxygen processing,
- initial mining/processing,
- sealed habitation/agriculture,
- construction,
without pretending the external economy already matches Meridian's multi-GW civilization.

# 9. First-Year Transferred Capital Mass

Important principle:
The value of transferred equipment is determined by function, not percentage of ship mass.

Recommended permanently deployed Meridian-origin hardware/capital by +1y:
**~20,000–80,000 tonnes class** across orbit/resource/surface systems.

Possible split:
- orbital/resource systems: ~15k–60k tonnes,
- surface systems: ~5k–20k tonnes,
with overlap/staging and wide mission variation.

This is a tiny fraction of Meridian's total mass but can include disproportionately valuable:
- power modules,
- thermal hardware,
- precision fabrication,
- robots,
- chemical/recycling systems,
- medical/biotech equipment,
- spares.

Hard:
Do not equate `only 50k tonnes` with low strategic cost.

# 10. Cargo Vehicle Throughput

Author-facing vehicle classes only:
- crew/small cargo transfer vehicles,
- medium/heavy orbital cargo tugs,
- surface cargo/landing vehicles,
- resource-body robotic haulers.

Heavy transfer payload candidate:
**~50–300 tonnes per sortie/vehicle movement class**.

Moving ~50,000 tonnes over a year at ~150 t average payload is only a few hundred heavy-equivalent trips, order **~1/day**, before reuse/multi-leg logistics.

This is plausible without a giant launch every minute.

Exact vehicle count/thrust/propellant remains later technical C2 unless scene-critical.

# 11. Local ISRU First-Year Output

First-year ISRU is not yet a mature planetary mining economy.

Combined useful local bulk-output target by +1y:
**~10,000–100,000 tonnes class** of:
- water/ice/process feedstock,
- shielding/aggregate,
- simple metals/silicates/basic structural material,
depending site success.

Early output may be dominated by one easy resource stream rather than balanced industrial self-sufficiency.

Hard:

- extraction tonnage ≠ finished construction tonnage,

# 12. Meridian ↔ Orbital Node Transfer Time

Within the planet-centered operational architecture:
- scheduled crew/cargo movement between Meridian and lower orbital nodes: **hours to ~1 day class**, strongly orbital-phase/vehicle dependent,
- not an instant elevator,
- not a weeks-long interplanetary trip.

Routine travel remains scheduled because energy, docking, safety and traffic matter.

---

# 13. Surface Transfer / Rescue Time

From a lower orbital transfer layer:
physical descent/ascent can be **hours class**,
but real end-to-end medical/logistics response includes:
- vehicle readiness,
- orbital phase,
- weather/site status,
- landing window,
- patient stabilization.

Author-facing emergency evacuation target:
- favorable planned urgent case: **~6–18 h class** from surface request to specialist handoff,
- difficult weather/vehicle/trajectory case: **~1–3 days**.

Therefore the surface foothold needs meaningful:
- emergency medicine,
- stabilization,
- oxygen/pressure-safe shelter,
- redundant comms,
not merely a first-aid kit.

Exact rescue SLA is not guaranteed.

---

# 14. Resource-Body Transfer Times

Volatile/metal resource bodies are a family, not one conveniently parked asteroid.

Operational bands may range:
- near/co-orbital favorable resources: **days class**,
- less favorable resource transfers: **weeks class**,
- some bodies not worth early use despite abundance.

This is why orbital/resource crews require more autonomy than a low planetary station.

Exact orbits/delta-v remain C2 and should be selected only for recurring locations.

- service/utility modules,
- medical/storm/emergency refuge,

# 17. Family Migration Constraint

First-year external populations are primarily role/skill/rotation-driven.

Families may split because:
- limited berths,
- school/medical depth,
- job timing,
- child preference,
- risk tolerance.

Mass family migration waits for:
- more housing,
- deeper healthcare,
- education/childcare,
- reliable utilities,
- transport redundancy.

This prevents `equal rights = everyone can move tomorrow`.

# 18. Hostile Checks

## Is <1,500 external people too small to feel like arrival payoff?
No. Hundreds of real residents/workers, first addresses, civilian food service and a permanent surface foothold are qualitatively historic while keeping the scale honest.

## Is 0.15–0.45 GW external power excessive for <1,500 people?
Per-capita it is high, intentionally: most load is industrial/mining/life-support bootstrap rather than household consumption.

## Is 20k–80k tonnes transferred too little?
It is a tiny mass fraction but can include the most valuable precision-capital systems; local bulk resources later dominate mass.

## Can surface rescue take hours without making settlement irresponsible?
Yes if the foothold has real stabilization capability and selection is conservative. It reinforces why surface scale starts small.

## Does high Meridian orbit make surface work impossible?
No because lower transfer nodes decouple everyday surface logistics from the giant ship's safe staging orbit.
