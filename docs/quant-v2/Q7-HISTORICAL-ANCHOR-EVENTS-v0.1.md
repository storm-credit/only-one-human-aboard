# HIST-A1 — 2환 수질 격리 사건 / The Second-Ring Water Isolation

Approx:
**VY 40–55**, preferred center ~VY47.

Era:
Early Voyage Consolidation.

What happened:
A contamination-control failure plus delayed isolation forced several 2환 water branches into multi-day restriction.
The event affected thousands of residents through:
- reduced domestic service,
- school/work disruption,
- temporary relocation,
- emergency distribution,
- public distrust over how technical operators communicated uncertainty.

What did NOT happen:
- no near-extinction event,
- no Habitat-wide dehydration,
- no sabotage plot required,
- no single heroic engineer saves the ship.

Institutional consequences:
- stronger distributed processed-water buffers,
- clearer branch isolation standards,
- mandatory public incident reporting,
- stronger separation between `technical immediate action` and `longer civic restriction`,
- local council oversight of recovery priorities.

Historical meaning:
One early precedent for modern commons principle:
**survival infrastructure can demand expert control without granting experts general political sovereignty.**

Material-culture hooks:
- emergency water containers,
- printed/physical ration notices,
- old service badges,
- neighborhood oral history.

Story use:
Optional background reference for Amara/utility culture.
Never make it a hidden clue to her origin.

# HIST-A2 — 연속성 기준 판례군 / The Anchor Doctrine Cases

Approx:
**VY 95–115**, preferred cluster ~VY103–108.

Era:
Continuity Medicine / First Seed Cohorts.

What happened:
A series of severe neural-injury cases exposed disagreement among clinicians, families and administrators about what stored longitudinal neural data could legitimately do.

Some proposals treated increasingly rich Recovery Maps as if better data could substitute for destroyed biological continuity.
Clinical outcomes and legal review showed that this assumption was unsafe and conceptually incoherent.

Institutional consequences:
Modern doctrine converged toward:
- Recovery Map = repair/reference constraint,
- surviving Neural Anchor = continuity prerequisite,
- no anchorless adult restoration,
- staged rehabilitation after Reconstruction,
- separate clinical, ethics and legal review where stakes are high.

Historical meaning:
Modern Meridian did **not** reject software-person metaphysics because AI was feared.
It developed a narrower medical continuity doctrine from hard cases involving living injured people.

Material-culture hooks:
- obsolete clinical mapping hardware,
- redacted teaching cases,
- early rehab manuals,
- preserved medical-institution exhibits.

Hard:
Do not turn these cases into proof that a soul is scientifically measurable.

# HIST-A3 — 기원평등 판결군 / Origin Equality Decisions

Approx:
**VY 165–185**, preferred center ~VY176.

Era:
Seed Normalization / Rights Reform.

What happened:
As Seeded citizens became a large demographic cohort, inherited law produced inconsistent results in:
- insurance/care access,
- family status,
- inheritance,
- professional eligibility,
- continuity recognition,
- citizenship wording.

Several high-profile appeals and legislative reforms rejected the idea that developmental origin could itself reduce civic personhood.

Institutional consequences:
- Seeded/Natural origin loses ordinary civic-ranking function,
- modern equal-personhood language enters the ancestry of 시민기본법,
- protected medical provenance becomes more separated from ordinary public civic records,
- origin terminology begins social/legal deprecation.

Historical meaning:
This is why modern citizens can know Seed exists without treating `Synthetic-Origin` as an everyday social caste.

Material-culture hooks:
- campaign posters,
- legal pamphlets,
- memorial exhibitions,
- restored courtroom/public-hearing artifacts.

Hard:
This was not `AIs demanded human rights`.
It was a civil-rights conflict among biologically embodied citizens with different prenatal developmental histories.

# HIST-A4 — 공용구조·주거권 조정기 / Commons–Tenure Settlement

Approx:
**VY 245–270**, preferred center ~VY257.

Era:
Civic Maturation / Urban Layering.

What happened:
A prolonged wave of disputes emerged as Meridian's neighborhoods matured:
- residents wanted durable housing wealth and inheritance,
- businesses wanted secure premises,
- civic authorities needed emergency access and structural renewal,
- some operators/owners tried to treat critical building interfaces as ordinary private assets.

No single case solved this.
A sequence of law, court decisions and municipal practice separated:
- structural/life-support commons,
- transferable residential tenure interests,
- private interior property,
- public acquisition/compensation duties.

Institutional consequences:
- modern PROP-H1 architecture,
- more stable housing market without privatizing the hull,
- clearer renovation/maintenance authority,
- heritage/Old Quarter preservation law becomes possible later.

Historical meaning:
This is the legal ancestry of `you can own a home interest aboard Meridian without owning the ship's pressure hull`.

Material-culture hooks:
- old cooperative share certificates,
- neighborhood renovation plaques,
- early tenure contracts,
- disputed heritage fixtures.

# HIST-A5 — 3생활환 공통망 장애 / Three-Habitat Common-Mode Mesh Incident

Approx:
**VY 325–345**, preferred center ~VY336.

Era:
Networked Civilization / Automation Governance.

What happened:
A widely deployed software/model-stack update produced a common-mode failure across several civic and operational services in all three Habitats.

Effects lasted from hours into roughly a day-class recovery window depending on service:
- transit scheduling degraded,
- routine administrative services stalled,
- some logistics required local manual/fallback operation,
- communications quality fragmented,
- critical life-support safety remained functional because lower-level controls were not one universal cloud system.

What did NOT happen:
- no AI rebellion,
- no ship mind takeover,
- no mass death,
- no proof that automation should be banned.

Institutional consequences:
- stronger network/domain segmentation,
- rollback certification,
- independent model/version review,
- local fallback modes,
- cold/offline critical archives,
- stricter purpose-limited gateways between civic, medical and engineering data.

Historical meaning:
Modern federation is a learned resilience pattern, not anti-AI ideology.

Material-culture hooks:
- manual emergency route cards,
- obsolete local terminals,
- public service posters,
- archived software-version exhibits.

# HIST-A6 — 지연응답 헌정정리 / The Long-Reply Settlement

Approx:
**VY 385–405**, preferred center ~VY397.

Era:
Mature Meridian / Earth as Ancestral Relation.

What happened:
A politically significant exchange with Earth demonstrated that even authoritative, thoughtful guidance could arrive after:
- the original local circumstances had changed,
- interim Meridian law had already evolved,
- a return exchange would take another decade-scale interval.

The issue itself need not be one dramatic crisis.
The constitutional significance came from accumulated recognition that Earth could remain culturally and historically important without functioning as Meridian's real-time executive government.

Institutional consequences:
- clearer formal distinction between Earth correspondence/guidance and binding local authority,
- archival respect for Earth-origin legal/cultural materials without automatic supremacy,
- Meridian's civilian institutions become explicitly self-governing for current operational law.

Historical meaning:
Autonomy emerged through latency and lived legitimacy, not rebellion or proof Earth was destroyed.

Material-culture hooks:
- delayed message bundles,
- public listening/viewing events,
- preserved response drafts,
- school civics material.

Hard:
Do not confirm Earth apocalypse merely to explain autonomy.

# HIST-A7 — 도착준비 예비자산 논쟁 / Early Arrival Reserve Hearings

Approx:
**VY 410–425**, preferred center ~VY416–420.

Era:
Transition into Final Approach / Arrival Preparation.

What happened:
Early aggressive settlement proposals identified many Meridian systems as apparently `underused` or transferable.
Residents, engineers, clinicians and civic institutions discovered that utilization percentage alone hid critical value in:
- redundancy,
- repair time,
- spares,
- thermal geometry,
- precision manufacturing,
- future failure recovery.

Public hearings and technical reviews rejected both extremes:
- `never transfer anything from Meridian`,
- `anything not used today is spare colony equipment`.

Institutional consequences:
- strategic asset criticality classes,
- redundancy-impact statements,
- transfer/replacement planning,
- social displacement review,
- legal ancestry of the later Final Approach certification architecture.

Historical meaning:
Act5's asset conflict is not invented when the story reaches it.
The society has spent decades building rules for a conflict that remains genuinely hard.

Material-culture hooks:
- decommission tags,
- preserved asset labels,
- public hearing recordings,
- old district survey marks.

Hard:
These hearings do not predetermine the four settlement paths or give Amara any special role.
