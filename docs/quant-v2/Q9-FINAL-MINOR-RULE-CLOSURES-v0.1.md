# A. FOUND PROPERTY / SALVAGE — PROP-SALV-H1

## Rule
**Finding an object does not by itself create ownership.**

Objects are classified by prior custody/status:
1. lost personal property,
2. apparently abandoned personal property,
3. private/co-op/business property,
4. public/common infrastructure,
5. controlled hazardous/professional equipment,
6. decommissioned public/enterprise equipment,
7. heritage/provenance-significant material.

## Lost property
Finder has a reasonable reporting/return duty when ownership can be identified.

## Apparently abandoned personal property
May become claimable only after:
- reasonable owner search/notice,
- required holding period where applicable,
- confirmation it is not controlled/public/heritage property.

Exact waiting period is procedural Elastic.

## Public/common/strategic infrastructure
Does not become private because:
- it is old,
- disconnected,
- stored in a service corridor,
- decommissioned.

Disposition requires authorized decommission/surplus process.

## Controlled/hazardous tools
Custody rules continue after loss/finding; finder should not treat possession as lawful use authorization.

## Heritage objects
Discovery can trigger provenance/reporting/public-interest review.
Private ownership may still exist, but undisclosed removal from public/common/controlled space is not automatically lawful.

## Arrival/resource salvage
`first to find` does not create celestial sovereignty or ownership of strategic public mission assets.

Verdict:
`PASS / P0=0`.

# B. HUMAN-SUBJECT RESEARCH ETHICS — RES-H1

## Ordinary research principle
Research involving identifiable living citizens requires a lawful basis and, where the research intervenes on or recruits the person, **informed voluntary consent** unless a narrow legally recognized waiver applies.

## Independent review
More-than-minimal-risk human research requires independent ethics/scientific review appropriate to:
- physical risk,
- privacy/data risk,
- coercion/dependency,
- vulnerable populations,
- capacity/consent.

## Consent
Consent must identify material:
- purpose,
- procedures,
- risks,
- data use,
- foreseeable future use,
- compensation where relevant,
- withdrawal limits after data are irreversibly aggregated/deidentified.

## Withdrawal
A participant may generally stop future participation.
Already lawfully collected data may be retained only as allowed by the consent/statute/ethics plan; withdrawal does not magically erase already published aggregate knowledge.

## Data minimization
Researchers receive the minimum data/access needed.
Research status does not grant unrestricted D4 provenance access.

## Vulnerability / coercion
Special safeguards apply when recruitment depends on:
- medical care,
- employer/school authority,
- detention,
- guardianship,
- scarce-capacity allocation.

Citizens may not be threatened with loss of Civic Floor services for refusing research.

## Amara / Natural-Origin lock
Amara has **no research duty** because she is unique.
She may:
- accept,
- refuse,
- limit,
- later withdraw where possible.

Her refusal cannot cost citizenship, healthcare or migration rights.

## Existing-record research
Deidentified/aggregate historical or operational research may sometimes proceed under statutory/ethics waiver without individual reconsent if:
- risk is low,
- purpose is legitimate,
- reidentification is controlled,
- protected provenance remains segregated.

This does not authorize a public identity hunt.

Verdict:
`PASS / P0=0`.

# C. MEDICAL AID-IN-DYING — 4 DESIGNS

This is intentionally low-salience but must have one answer because finite death/chosen finality already exist.

## DESIGN C-A — TOTAL PROHIBITION

A competent adult may refuse treatment and receive palliative sedation, but clinicians may never intentionally provide medication for the patient to end life.

Strengths:
- simple,
- avoids coercion concerns.

Weaknesses:
- less consistent with Meridian's strong bodily autonomy/supported decision-making,
- creates arbitrary distinction for rare irreversible suffering after advanced medicine fails.

Verdict:
`REJECT`.

---

## DESIGN C-B — TERMINAL-DISEASE-ONLY

Allowed only when a competent adult has an incurable condition expected to cause death within a relatively short prognosis window.

Strengths:
- conservative,
- clear eligibility.

Weaknesses:
- prognosis becomes artificially central,
- excludes irreversible intolerable suffering where death timing is uncertain,
- advanced medicine makes prognosis windows especially unstable.

Verdict:
`STRONG BUT TOO NARROW`.

---

## DESIGN C-C — COMPETENT-ADULT REFRACTORY-SUFFERING MODEL

Allowed for a competent adult with an irreversible serious condition causing enduring suffering judged intolerable by that adult, after reasonable treatments/support have failed or been informedly declined.

Strengths:
- coherent autonomy principle,
- does not make doctors predict an exact six-month clock.

Weaknesses:
- requires strong capacity/coercion safeguards,
- risks overbreadth if disability or social deprivation is treated as a reason to die rather than a reason to provide support.

Verdict:
`SELECT WITH NARROW SAFEGUARDS`.

---

## DESIGN C-D — ADVANCE-DIRECTIVE / PROXY AID-IN-DYING

A previous directive or proxy may authorize active aid-in-dying after the citizen loses decision capacity.

Strengths:
- respects older preferences.

Weaknesses:
- repeats the exact agency problem central to PR-H1,
- creates severe ambiguity between past self/current interests,
- too easy to turn Reconstruction/Continuity cases into proxy-death disputes.

Verdict:
`REJECT FOR ACTIVE AID-IN-DYING`.

A prior directive may still guide refusal/limits of burdensome treatment under ordinary end-of-life law; it cannot authorize another person to actively end the life of a currently nonconsenting/incapacitated citizen.

# SELECTED — MAID-H1

# `Contemporaneously Competent Adult + Irreversible Serious Condition + Refractory Suffering + Multi-Step Independent Review`

## Eligibility — LOCK CANDIDATE
All must be true:
- adult **18+**,
- current decision-making capacity for this decision,
- voluntary request free of coercion,
- serious irreversible/incurable medical condition,
- enduring suffering the patient finds intolerable,
- reasonable treatment/palliative/support options explained,
- requested pathway remains voluntary even if the patient declines some treatments.

Disability, poverty, unemployment, lack of housing or social isolation **alone** are never qualifying medical grounds.
The Civic Floor must not make death the cheaper substitute for support.

## Process — LOCK DIRECTION
Requires:
1. initial voluntary request,
2. treating clinician assessment,
3. independent second clinician assessment,
4. decision-specific capacity assessment where doubt exists,
5. palliative/support alternatives offered,
6. waiting/reconfirmation period appropriate to urgency/condition,
7. final contemporaneous confirmation immediately before provision where feasible,
8. mandatory documentation and retrospective oversight review.

Exact number of days is procedural Elastic because rapidly deteriorating and slowly progressive cases differ.

## Proxy / guardian prohibition — LOCK
No:
- spouse,
- guardian,
- family member,
- emergency proxy,
- AI,
- old Recovery Map,
- old advance directive
may actively request/authorize aid-in-dying **on behalf of a currently incapable person**.

Advance directives may still govern withholding/withdrawal of unwanted burdensome treatment under separate end-of-life law.

## Mental illness
Psychiatric diagnosis alone does not automatically qualify or disqualify.
However, if the condition materially impairs decision capacity or the request is driven by a treatable acute crisis, the pathway pauses and ordinary mental-health/support law applies.

The series does not need to explore sole-psychiatric-condition eligibility; if a future plot does, run a narrow micropass first.

## Reconstruction interaction
A patient is not required to undergo invasive/high-load Reconstruction merely to retain the right to refuse treatment or pursue MAID-H1 if otherwise eligible.

Conversely:
- a Recovery Map is not a substitute decision-maker,
- anticipated future Reconstruction scarcity is not a reason to pressure someone toward death,
- capacity allocation officials cannot recommend death as a resource-saving measure.

## Organ/material/resource incentive
No institution may gain allocation priority or direct financial benefit from inducing MAID-H1.
Post-death medical donation/material handling follows separate consent/finality rules.

`PASS / P0=0`.
