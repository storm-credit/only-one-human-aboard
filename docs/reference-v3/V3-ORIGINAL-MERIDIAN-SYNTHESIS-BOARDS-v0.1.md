# B. HIGH machine synthesis boards

## M1 — Structural Crawler Family

Core sentence:
**A low, surface-hugging inspection/service machine whose body is organized around staying attached and reaching damaged structure, not around looking like an animal.**

Silhouette:
- low central equipment slab,
- multiple short independent clamp/traction units placed wide,
- sensor/tool head is offset and replaceable,
- no obvious front `face`,
- transport mode folds protrusions inward.

Function geometry:
- clamp spacing changes by structure generation,
- older models use larger universal mechanical interfaces,
- newer models carry better local mapping but narrower certified interface assumptions.

Material language:
- replaceable edge bumpers,
- visible certified load points,
- local abrasion around clamp paths,
- no decorative armor.

Movement:
slow deliberate reposition → lock → measure/work → release.

Collector recognition:
family identity comes from clamp spacing + offset service head + standardized underside interface.

Generation tradeoff:
old = broader compatibility/easier field repair, heavier/slower;
new = better sensing/control, more specialized modules.

Act lineage:
Act1 ordinary → Act2 heat/contamination → Act5 legacy adapter → Act6 mixed modules → Act9 field-rig descendant.

## M2 — Damage Mapping Drone Family

Core sentence:
**A light sensor carrier designed to establish geometry and chronology from viewpoints a worker cannot safely hold.**

Silhouette:
- thin plate/tri-lobed sensor frame,
- no heavy manipulator,
- sensor apertures distributed rather than one robot eye,
- deployable reference emitters only when task requires.

Movement:
short stable observation moves rather than acrobatic flight.

Material language:
protected sensor edges, replaceable sacrificial outer ring, minimal cargo volume.

Collector recognition:
plate proportions and sensor geometry identify family; payload insert identifies job/generation.

Hard:
never becomes armed scout/drone companion.

---

## M3 — Utility Tug / Freight Tractor Family

Core sentence:
**The city’s workhorse vehicle: the thing being moved determines the machine’s shape more than the driver/control unit does.**

Silhouette:
- compact control/power unit,
- visually dominant coupling/load interface,
- easy-to-read approach clearances,
- no passenger-car nose.

Family interface:
standard load collars / alignment surfaces persist for decades.

Generations:
control/autonomy/sensors change while mechanical compatibility remains partly continuous.

Maintenance:
access panels and service path are readable from outside; common wear concentrates near couplings and wheel/guide modules.

Collector recognition:
route markings, coupling generation, rebuilt modules and regional modifications.

Hard:
newer model is not automatically better in every old terminal.

---

## M4 — Rescue Skiff Family

Core sentence:
**A mobile protected space built around reaching, stabilizing and removing injured people under damaged-infrastructure conditions.**

Silhouette:
- casualty access/cradle opening dominates one side/axis,
- protected central service/med zone,
- tool stowage flush and secondary,
- movement system tucked to preserve patient access.

Visual distinction from tug:
tug wraps around cargo coupling; rescue skiff opens toward people.

Wear:
hand-contact edges, stretcher/cradle tracks, quickly replaced protective panels.

Collector recognition:
changes in patient access, medical support modules and rescue-route mobility rather than speed styling.

Hard:
not an ambulance-shaped sci-fi van by default.

---

## M5 — External Service Frame Family

Core sentence:
**A nonhumanoid anchored work structure whose manipulators, fixtures and reaction-force paths are more visually important than any operator compartment.**

Silhouette:
- open frame,
- two or more unequal-purpose manipulators,
- large anchor/reaction interfaces,
- tool cradle/parts rack,
- little/no anthropomorphic torso-head structure.

Movement:
reposition slowly; work while anchored.

Use:
heavy alignment, panel/service replacement, bracing, access support.

Collector recognition:
manipulator family + anchor standard + parts rack arrangement.

Hard:
no fists, shoulders, legs, weapon hardpoints or hero cockpit.

---

## M6 — Public-Safety Barrier Drone Family

Core sentence:
**A moving piece of civic infrastructure that creates boundaries, signs and safe flow rather than pursuing people.**

Silhouette:
- folded panel mass obvious even when stowed,
- deployable physical/optical boundary elements,
- low stable mobility body,
- sensors oriented toward crowd/route safety.

Use:
closures, hazard marking, queue/channel shaping, temporary access separation.

Collector recognition:
panel hinge generation, authority/safety interface and wear at deployment edges.

Hard:
not police attack robot.

# E. Major civic-space synthesis boards

## E1 — Transfer Belt Layered Interchange

Core sentence:
**A place where passenger movement, service traffic and freight cross at different heights, while small pause spaces make it part of civic life rather than a station machine.**

Geometry:
- long directional channels,
- cross-bridges at different heights,
- glimpses of freight/service movement behind/under public circulation,
- one tall orientation void rather than endless corridor.

Light:
movement/task light + one broad vertical light/orientation source.

Slow-life insert:
benches, food/service edge, waiting overlook, limited planted/rest zone.

Acoustic identity:
vehicle/guide hum + announcements + crowd pulse.

Hard:

## E2 — Midring Shared Courtyard

Core sentence:
**A human-scale residential commons where home thresholds, school movement, food/service and children’s routines visibly overlap.**

Geometry:
multiple residential access levels face/meet a common irregular court; not one monumental atrium.

Material life:
resident-added storage, notices, seating, repairs, children’s objects and reused furniture coexist with standardized utility interfaces.

Light:
domestic/service mix, warmer only by use—not one palette code.

Acoustic identity:
conversation, school movement, cooking/service, maintenance, children.

Collector appeal:

Hard:
not minimalist future apartments.

## E4 — Old Works Multi-Generation Junction

Movement:

Material identity:
accessible controls, durable hand-contact surfaces, replaceable care fixtures.

Acoustic identity:

# I. Source-dominance audit method

Do NOT invent fake influence percentages.
Instead audit final design axes.

For each HIGH asset use six axes:
1. function/role,
2. structural/silhouette logic,
3. service/use behavior,
4. material/wear logic,
5. generation/history logic,
6. story/character meaning.

Rule:
- final story/world authority must own axis 1 and 6,
- real functional references should strongly inform 2~4 when applicable,
- one entertainment IP may influence at most ONE of axes 2~5 for a final HIGH asset,
- no entertainment source may own both silhouette and material language of the same final asset.
