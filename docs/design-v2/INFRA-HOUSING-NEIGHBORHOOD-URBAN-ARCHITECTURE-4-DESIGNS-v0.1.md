# DESIGN A — SUPERBLOCK RING CITY

## Core
Each rotating habitat is organized into large mixed-use superblocks separated by major structural/fire/pressure boundaries.

A block contains:
- housing towers/terraces or slab-like modules,
- school/childcare,
- clinic,
- food retail,
- small workplaces,
- public square/green,
- utility/service core.

### Advantages
- excellent emergency isolation
- efficient utilities
- easy service access
- intuitive infrastructure planning
- supports strong neighborhood identity

### Weaknesses
- can feel too planned and uniform after 450 years
- risks `arcology blocks` repetition
- neighborhoods may look like copies of one template

### Biggest Trap
`Everywhere looks like the same megastructure mall.`

---

# DESIGN B — ORGANIC LAYERED CITY

## Core
Centuries of renovation have produced irregular streets, mezzanines, reused structural bays, infill housing and locally evolved districts over an original planned frame.

### Advantages
- strongest 450-year historical texture
- perfect for 구시가지
- supports shops, alleys, retrofits, odd apartments, heritage conflict
- visually memorable

### Weaknesses
- difficult emergency/isolation logic
- risks impossible hidden volume
- utility servicing can become handwave
- if universal, ship seems dangerously unmaintainable

### Biggest Trap
`Fantasy old city pasted inside a spacecraft.`

---

# DESIGN C — MODULAR CORRIDOR HABITAT

## Core
Urban life clusters along major transit/service corridors, with plug-in residential modules and standardized vertical/radial utility stacks.

### Advantages
- highly plausible maintenance
- clear growth/reconfiguration
- easy accessibility
- clear distinction between public and service layers

### Weaknesses
- can feel like a station/hotel
- weak street life
- over-modular homes may feel disposable and emotionally thin

### Biggest Trap
`Everyone lives in interchangeable pods.`

---

# DESIGN D — GREEN COURTYARD NETWORK

## Core
Housing is arranged around numerous public courtyards, gardens, water/green corridors and indoor civic streets. Local environmental quality is a major amenity.

### Advantages
- humane and visually rich
- strong family/community scenes
- supports 2환 identity
- creates desirable-location economics without unsafe housing

### Weaknesses
- space/lighting/landscape cost
- if everywhere, 2환 loses distinction
- can make Meridian look utopian and spacious beyond its resource logic

### Biggest Trap
`Luxury biosphere for everyone with no density pressure.`

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — URB-H1

# `Compartmented Mixed-Use City + Layered Retrofit + Local Public Realms`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## Core Principle
Meridian's urbanism has two layers at once:

1. **hard engineering frame** — pressure/fire/structure/utilities/transit boundaries that remain legible and maintained,
2. **soft civic city** — 450 years of renovation, infill, shops, schools, homes, plazas, gardens, local style and historical layering inside that frame.

The ship is neither a sterile station nor an impossible free-form metropolis.

---

# 1. Urban Scale Hierarchy

Author-facing hierarchy:

### Habitat / 생활환
1환 / 2환 / 3환.
Population order per ring need not be equal; each is roughly large-city-district scale, not a village.

### District
Large municipal/service area.
Contains multiple neighborhoods and at least one major transit/service node.

### Neighborhood
Everyday lived unit:
- several thousand residents order,
- primary school/childcare access,
- groceries/food outlets,
- small parks/courtyards,
- local clinic/service point,
- neighborhood commerce,
- ordinary pedestrian routes.

### Block / pressure-service sector
Engineering/maintenance unit that may cut across social naming.
Citizens need not think of pressure-sector IDs as their neighborhood identity.

Hard rule:
**social neighborhood boundaries and engineering isolation boundaries do not have to be identical.**

---

# 2. Housing Stock Sanity

Existing total:
~120k–130k households.

Provisional central planning target for QA:
- occupied household units: ~122k–128k order,
- additional vacant/turnover/temporary/maintenance stock: ~4–8% order,
- total physically usable dwelling stock: roughly ~128k–136k order.

Reason:
A 300k city cannot operate with zero vacancy. Moves, renovations, family change, disability adaptations, emergencies and temporary displacement require slack.

Do not make vacancy a freely wasted luxury. It is partly operational resilience.

---

# 3. Dwelling Size Bands — AUTHOR-FACING / ELASTIC

Not reader-facing fixed standards.

### Compact single / couple
~25–45 m² order.

### Standard 1–2 bedroom equivalent
~40–65 m² order.

### Family dwelling
~60–90 m² order.

### Larger family / multigenerational / high-market unit
~90–120 m² order.

### Premium / unusual large units
~120–160+ m² possible but uncommon.

Important:
- rooms may use adaptable partitions,
- storage is highly engineered,
- built-in services reduce appliance clutter,
- private space still matters socially,
- housing is not pod-sized by default.

Rough sanity:
At ~50–60 m² average private dwelling area across ~125k occupied households, private residential floor area is ~6–7.5 million m² order before common/service space.
This is compatible with the enormous interior surface area available across three ~km-scale rotating habitats.

Exact floor-area model remains Package H/Quant QA.

# 4. Civic Floor Housing

The Civic Floor guarantees:
- safe pressure/environment,
- potable water/sanitation,
- basic kitchen/food access,
- sleeping/privacy minimum,
- heating/cooling/air quality,
- network/public-service access,
- accessibility adaptation when medically required,
- reasonable access to essential transit/services.

It does NOT guarantee:
- premium view,
- shortest commute,
- extra rooms,
- high acoustic isolation,
- private garden terrace,
- heritage location,
- prestige address.

This preserves meaningful housing economics without survival blackmail.

---

# 5. Tenure / Property Physical Expression

Compatible with PROP-H1:
Citizens may hold durable residential occupancy/property interests, but not absolute ownership of pressure hull/utility commons.

A home transaction may transfer rights to:
- the dwelling interior/use-right,
- defined fixtures/improvements,
- a share/claim in cooperative building structures where applicable,
- long-duration occupancy interest.

It does not transfer private sovereignty over:
- pressure structure,
- life-support trunk,
- emergency corridor,
- utility service core.

Housing forms may include:
- owner-occupied long-duration units,
- cooperative buildings,
- regulated rentals,
- public/basic housing,
- employer-linked temporary housing only in limited sectors,
- youth/student/residential program housing,
- elder/supportive housing,
- emergency/transition units.

No single tenure form dominates every district.

# 6. Class / Status Through Housing

Status differences may appear through:
- floor area,
- private room count,
- acoustic isolation,
- ceiling height/local volume,
- naturalistic/artificial daylight quality,
- park/water/civic-view access,
- proximity to desirable transit,
- heritage address,
- custom interior work,
- storage/workshop space,
- balcony/terrace-like protected private-public edge where geometry allows.

Forbidden:
- rich people get clean air, poor people get unsafe air,
- poor districts knowingly receive lethal structural maintenance,
- 1환 = elite / 2환 = farmers / 3환 = workers.

# 7. 1환 Urban Character

Weighted traits:
- older civic core,
- denser institutional presence,
- older street patterns,
- mixed-age housing stock,
- prestigious AND inconvenient old addresses,
- major schools, courts, medicine, archives, culture,
- **구시가지** as strongest layered-retrofit zone.

구시가지 characteristics:
- narrower/irregular internal streets in some sectors,
- legacy structural bays,
- converted older service spaces only where certified,
- small shops/workshops,
- heritage facades/interiors,
- recurring accessibility/repair dilemmas,
- not a slum by definition.

Some wealthy residents romanticize it; some residents hate the maintenance burden.

---

# 8. 2환 Urban Character

Weighted traits:
- more visible water/ecology systems,
- larger share of planted public spaces,
- greenhouse/watershed interfaces near but not inside every home,
- fully urban schools, offices, retail, apartments, clinics, restaurants,
- water/reclamation and biological infrastructure jobs more visible.

Potential housing premium:
quiet planted courts / water-adjacent public views / stable family neighborhoods.

Hard rule:
Amara's 2환 residence should feel like ordinary city housing near her work ecology, not `farm worker housing`.

---

# 9. 3환 Urban Character

Weighted traits:
- more logistics/technical/rehab/industrial interfaces,
- higher share of shift-oriented services,
- stronger freight separation,
- buildings designed around vibration/acoustic mitigation near industrial interfaces,
- lively 24-hour food/service pockets serving shift workers,
- mixed ordinary family districts away from heavy interfaces.

Hard rule:
3환 is not uniformly noisy, dirty or lower-class.

# 10. Public Realm

Citizens should have spaces analogous in function to:
- streets,
- small squares,
- courtyards,
- parks,
- school grounds,
- market streets,
- indoor sports spaces,
- community rooms,
- libraries/media spaces,
- worship/assembly rooms,
- cafés/restaurants,
- memorial/heritage spaces.

These need not imitate Earth exterior architecture literally.

The important social distinction:
`private home → building/shared edge → neighborhood public realm → district civic realm`.

Without this hierarchy every interaction collapses into corridor + room.

---

# 11. Lighting / Sky / Windows

Rotating habitat interiors may use combinations of:
- large civic voids with long sightlines,
- controlled daylight-spectrum artificial lighting,
- light wells,
- projected/architectural sky cues,
- limited protected real exterior/space views from specific locations.

Do not assume every apartment has a real window to space.

Housing value can differ by:
- interior long view,
- planted court,
- civic boulevard,
- quiet light well,
- rare protected external viewing geometry.

Package C later map/lighting work decides exact implementation.

---

# 12. Noise / Vibration / Smell

A real city has sensory geography.

Common issues:
- neighbor noise,
- transit hum,
- ventilation noise,
- industrial vibration in some interfaces,
- restaurant/cooking smell,
- school/sport noise,
- maintenance outages.

Advanced construction reduces but does not erase them.

Class difference can legitimately appear in acoustic isolation and environmental customization.

---

# 13. Accessibility / Aging / Disability

Baseline:
- step-free main routes are normal,
- lifts/ramps/assist interfaces where level changes exist,
- tactile/audio/visual redundant wayfinding,
- adaptable bathroom/home modules,
- mobility-device compatibility,
- service animals/robotic aids subject to later animal/robotics rules,
- old districts may require retrofits and occasional imperfect routes.

Housing adaptations are part of health/civic support, not luxury-only upgrades.

---

# 14. Children / Youth Housing Ecology

Normal children live in ordinary households.

Additional forms:
- residential youth programs,
- student/technical training residence,
- temporary family-support housing,
- protected housing where safety requires it.

No boarding-school caste.

Noah's Act 1 youth program fits this ecosystem as a short residential autonomy/education option, not removal from family society.

---

# 15. Temporary / Emergency Housing

Required stock includes:
- unit renovation displacement,
- fire/pressure incident displacement,
- domestic-violence safety placement,
- family transition,
- hospital/Reconstruction recovery-adjacent needs,
- arrival-era relocation.

Emergency units should be real homes at modest standard, not perpetual shelter bunks.

Long displacement can create serious social/economic conflict even when no one becomes homeless to vacuum.

---

# 16. Building / Utility Separation

Homes connect to shared:
- air circulation/conditioning,
- water/wastewater,
- power,
- data,
- thermal loops,
- fire detection/suppression,
- emergency communication.

But buildings/neighborhoods include local isolation capability so a fault need not threaten an entire Habitat.

Exact valve, pressure, fire and contamination topology belongs to later Package C utilities/emergency blocks.

---

# 17. Pressure / Fire Compartment Logic

Critical principle:
Urban continuity does not mean one open pressure volume with no isolation.

Likely structure:
- numerous rated structural/pressure sectors,
- normal doors/passages remain open during ordinary life,
- emergency barriers can isolate bounded zones,
- transit/service penetrations have controlled closures,
- evacuation is usually lateral/local first rather than `everyone leaves the Habitat`.

Old Quarter cannot be exempt from safety segmentation merely because it is historic.

---

# 18. Address / Wayfinding

Citizens use human-readable civic addresses based on:
- Habitat,
- district/neighborhood,
- building/street/local name,
- unit.

Engineering systems additionally retain sector/deck/structural coordinates.

Ordinary dialogue should not require coordinates such as `Ring-2 Deck 37 Sector K-19` unless context is technical.

---

# 19. Renovation Over 450 Years

Meridian's city has repeatedly:
- combined small units,
- subdivided large units,
- converted offices/housing,
- retired service bays,
- rebuilt public spaces,
- re-routed noncritical utilities,
- upgraded accessibility,
- preserved selected heritage.

Hard engineering trunks/pressure boundaries change much more slowly than interiors and social use.

This creates visible historical layering without losing ship plausibility.

---

# 20. Housing Capacity and Population Policy

Housing capacity is not only a count of bedrooms.
It includes:
- vacancy/turnover reserve,
- renovation downtime,
- accessibility stock,
- family-size match,
- emergency displacement reserve,
- district service capacity.

Thus `there is technically an empty room` does not automatically equal one additional sustainable birth slot.

This reinforces H6 without converting housing into a tradable birth license.

---

# 21. Arrival-Era Housing Pressure

Acts 5–7 may introduce:
- staging workers closer to departure/industrial nodes,
- temporary relocations around dismantling,
- housing conversion for new mission teams,
- future orbit/surface housing applications,
- conflict over whether old housing volume should remain city fabric or become material/industrial input.

Guardrail:
No mass instant emptying of Meridian.
Ending remains polycentric; the ship stays a living city.
