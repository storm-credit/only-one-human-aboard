# INFRA — EMERGENCY / FIRE / DECOMPRESSION / CONTAMINATION — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE C / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Existing dependencies:
- URB-H1 compartmented mixed-use city.
- UTIL-H1 hierarchical loops + district isolation.
- CJ-H1 narrow coercive escalation / due process.
- GOV-H1 narrow technical emergency command.

Goal:
Define realistic incident response without turning Meridian into either a permanently militarized vessel or a magically safe city.

---

# DESIGN A — CENTRAL SHIP EMERGENCY COMMAND

One shipwide emergency service directs all major incidents.

Pros:
- clear authority,
- strong standardization.

Cons:
- slow/local overreach,
- militarized tone,
- single command bottleneck.

---

# DESIGN B — FULLY LOCAL RESPONSE

Each district/Habitat manages its own fire/rescue/medical incidents.

Pros:
- fast local response,
- strong civic texture.

Cons:
- inconsistent standards,
- weak cross-Habitat coordination,
- large events exceed local capacity.

---

# DESIGN C — LAYERED INCIDENT COMMAND

Local first response handles ordinary events; Habitat and shipwide technical command scale up when boundaries are crossed.

Pros:
- realistic,
- matches governance,
- keeps most incidents ordinary while preserving major-event capability.

Cons:
- role boundaries must be explicit.

---

# DESIGN D — AUTOMATED SAFETY DOMINANT

Sensors/doors/robots handle incidents before humans arrive.

Pros:
- plausible advanced technology.

Cons:
- erases drama/accountability,
- risks AI emergency dictatorship.

Use only as subsystem, not governance model.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — EMG-H1

# `Layered Incident Command + Automatic Physical Safeties + Human Escalation`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. Incident Levels

Author-facing, exact public labels may differ.

### Level 0 — routine fault
Handled by building/site operators.
Examples: minor leak, local alarm, equipment trip.

### Level 1 — local emergency
Fire/rescue/medical/public-safety response within one building/neighborhood sector.

### Level 2 — district/Habitat emergency
Multiple buildings, major transit, contamination, pressure boundary or large casualty risk.

### Level 3 — shipwide strategic emergency
Cross-Habitat cascading risk, major structural/thermal/propulsion/atmospheric threat.

Hard rule:
Authority expands by **hazard scope**, not political convenience.

---

# 2. Fire

Closed habitats make smoke/toxic products at least as important as flame.

Layered response:
- detection by heat/smoke/gas/optical sensors,
- local alarms,
- automatic power/fuel/process isolation,
- ventilation/smoke control,
- local suppression where appropriate,
- compartment/fire-door closure,
- trained response crews,
- medical triage,
- post-fire air cleanup.

Not every sprinkler event seals a whole district.

---

# 3. Decompression / Pressure Loss

Causes may include:
- structural damage,
- failed seal/door,
- maintenance error,
- industrial incident,
- external impact in vulnerable interfaces.

Response:
- rapid pressure sensing,
- local automatic closures,
- leak localization,
- evacuation away from affected pressure sector,
- emergency breathing support in transition zones,
- structural inspection and controlled repressurization.

Ordinary Habitat streets are protected by multiple isolation layers.
A localized breach is dangerous but not automatically civilization-ending.

---

# 4. Contamination

Types:
- smoke/toxic chemical,
- biological pathogen,
- water contamination,
- radiological contamination,
- industrial dust/aerosol,
- unknown material.

Response differs by pathway.
Do not use one generic `quarantine` button.

May require:
- zone isolation,
- sample testing,
- alternate water/air route,
- decontamination,
- medical observation,
- contact tracing under health/privacy rules,
- waste segregation.

---

# 5. Medical Mass-Casualty Response

Hospitals/clinics maintain surge protocols:
- triage,
- local stabilization,
- distributed patient routing,
- temporary treatment spaces,
- blood/tissue/pharmacy reserves,
- mental-health follow-up.

Reconstruction is not the first-line answer to mass injury and has limited throughput.

---

# 6. Evacuation Philosophy

Default:
**local/lateral relocation first.**

Possible sequence:
room → building zone → neighborhood refuge → district relocation → other Habitat only for major prolonged disruption.

No routine `evacuate an entire ring` order.

---

# 7. Refuge / Safe Areas

Key facilities/large buildings include protected refuge functions:
- independent short-duration air/power,
- emergency communications,
- medical supplies,
- occupancy accounting at aggregate level.

These are not apocalypse bunkers for elites.

---

# 8. Drills

Citizens receive age-appropriate basic emergency education.

Examples:
- school evacuation drills,
- pressure-door etiquette,
- shelter/refuge location familiarity,
- fire/smoke response,
- transit incident instructions.

Specialist/EVA/industrial response training remains professional.

Do not make every citizen a trained astronaut/firefighter.

---

# 9. Emergency Alerts

Multi-channel resilient system:
- personal device alert,
- building/public address,
- visual signage,
- tactile/accessibility channels,
- local physical indicators where network fails.

Alerts specify:
- affected zone,
- action,
- route/refuge,
- expected update.

Avoid vague `all citizens remain calm` omnipresent announcements.

---

# 10. Automatic Safeties

Allowed narrow automation:
- close pressure/fire doors,
- isolate ventilation,
- trip dangerous machinery,
- reroute utility flows,
- switch emergency power,
- stop trains entering unsafe zones.

These actions must be:
- pre-authorized by safety rules,
- logged,
- reversible/overridable by authorized incident command when safe.

Automation does not become a political sovereign.

---

# 11. Human Incident Command

Local incident commander coordinates immediate scene.
Technical domain leads own safety decisions.
Higher command enters only when scope crosses thresholds.

Examples:
- fire/rescue lead does not become mayor,
- Ship Operations Command may isolate a damaged transport/structural zone but cannot suspend unrelated civil rights shipwide.

---

# 12. Police / Public Safety During Emergencies

Roles:
- perimeter/access control,
- crowd movement,
- missing-person coordination,
- evidence preservation where crime suspected,
- protection of vulnerable people.

Guardrail:
Emergency safety orders cannot become excuse for unrelated searches/arrests.

---

# 13. Emergency Duration / Rights

Immediate physical orders may precede judicial review when seconds matter.
Longer restrictions require:
- documented hazard basis,
- defined geographic/domain scope,
- periodic review,
- expiry/narrowing,
- later audit/appeal where rights were materially affected.

This integrates GOV-H1/CJ-H1.

---

# 14. Shelter-in-Place vs Evacuation

Decision depends on hazard:
- smoke/fire nearby → move to clean zone/refuge,
- external contamination → shelter/air isolation may be safer,
- pressure loss → move behind pressure boundary,
- pathogen → health isolation may be targeted.

No one universal instruction.

---

# 15. Household Emergency Kit

Ordinary home kit may include:
- compact emergency breathing/smoke protection appropriate to local standards,
- first aid,
- light/communications backup,
- water/food short-duration reserve,
- basic sealing/safety items where useful.

Not a full EVA suit in every closet.

---

# 16. Schools / Children

Schools have:
- local refuge plans,
- guardian reunification protocol,
- emergency contact redundancy,
- staff responsibilities,
- child-specific medical/access needs.

Children are not automatically sent home into unsafe transit during an incident.

---

# 17. Hospitals / Reconstruction Facilities

Critical facilities have stronger redundancy and isolation.
But they can still be overwhelmed or partially evacuated.

Recovery Map/Neural Anchor handling follows medical/continuity rules; emergency teams cannot bypass them casually.

---

# 18. Outer Works / Spine

Different emergency doctrine:
- low/zero-g restraint,
- pressure suit/rescue gear,
- radiation/thermal/industrial hazards,
- specialized rescue teams.

No assumption that ordinary civic responders are instantly EVA-qualified.

---

# 19. Aftermath

Every significant incident can produce:
- safety investigation,
- repair work,
- compensation/insurance/public support,
- temporary housing,
- mental-health effects,
- political accountability,
- criminal investigation if warranted.

This prevents incidents from resetting after one episode.

---

# 20. Arrival-Era Emergency Shift

As orbital/surface operations begin:
- new jurisdiction boundaries,
- unfamiliar environmental hazards,
- longer rescue times,
- distributed emergency caches,
- ship/orbit/surface mutual aid.

Do not assume mature planetary emergency services exist immediately.
