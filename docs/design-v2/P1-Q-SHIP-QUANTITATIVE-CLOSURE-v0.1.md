# 1. Habitat Radius / Rotation — 4 Bands

Assume outer inhabited urban surface near ~1g.

## QSH-A — Compact
- radius ~0.70–0.85 km
- required rotation >~1.03–1.12 rpm for ~1g

Strength:
less structural span/mass.

Weakness:
more noticeable Coriolis/orientation effects and more pressure to explain why a 300k mature city chose compact geometry.

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`.

## QSH-B — Balanced 1-km Class
- radius **0.90–1.10 km**
- outer inhabited gravity target **~0.90–1.00g ordinary band**
- nominal rotation **~0.90–1.00 rpm**
- rotation period roughly **60–67 s** at ~1g center assumptions.

At radial movement ~1.5 m/s, Coriolis acceleration is roughly **0.029–0.032g** order: noticeable in radial/low-g transition, not debilitating in normal tangential neighborhood life.

Strength:
- matches existing Canon,
- supports recognizable near-Earth daily movement,
- keeps low-g transition physically meaningful,
- avoids tiny-ring trope.

Verdict: **SELECTED WORKING BAND**.

## QSH-C — Large Comfort
- radius ~1.2–1.5 km
- lower rpm for same gravity.

Strength:
less Coriolis.

Weakness:
adds structure/mass/volume without current story need and drifts above established ~1km order.

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY / ALLOWED ONLY IF LATER ENGINEERING REOPENED`.

## QSH-D — Different Gravity Habitats
Each Habitat deliberately uses substantially different radius/rpm/gravity.

Strength:
visual/cultural difference.

Weakness:
risks turning Habitat identity into biological/social partition and adds constant physiology/exposition load.

Verdict: `REJECT`.

# 2. Selected Habitat Geometry Band

For each of the three major rotating living structures:
- outer effective radius: **0.9–1.1 km**
- inhabited longitudinal length: **5–7 km order**
- nominal outer-city gravity: **~0.9–1.0g**
- rotation: **~0.9–1.0 rpm**

Exact structural shell geometry remains C2.
`생활환` remains civic inherited terminology, not a claim of thin-torus geometry.

## Surface-area sanity
For a 1-km radius / 5–7 km-long cylindrical inner urban surface:
- ~31–44 km² per Habitat primary inner surface order,
- ~94–132 km² across three Habitats before multilevel built floor area.

At ~100k residents/Habitat order, gross inner-surface population density is roughly **2,300–3,200 people/km²** before multilevel space.

This is compatible with mixed urban/green/industrial city texture rather than packed corridor housing.

# 3. Gravity Bands Inside A Habitat

Use qualitative bands rather than centimeter-precise gravity maps.

## G1 — Main Urban Band
~0.9–1.0g ordinary target.
Most homes/schools/shops/normal offices.

## G2 — Radial Transition / Reduced-g Service Band
Below main urban gravity as citizens/workers move inward toward transfer interfaces.
Movement/orientation assistance becomes more important.

## G3 — Near-Axis / Spine Interface
Low-g to near-zero-g controlled interface environments.
Handholds/restraints/orientation protocols common.

Hard:
- ordinary residents do not sleep/work their whole lives in G3 by default,
- reduced-g sport can use dedicated spaces rather than defining a Habitat's population physiology,
- no permanent gravity caste.

# 4. Pressure / Fire / Engineering Sector Scale

Goal:
local failures remain local without claiming perfect compartmentalization.

## Selected hierarchy

### Building / Local Compartment
Typical affected population:
**tens to several hundreds** depending use.

### Major District Engineering / Pressure Sector
Working population-equivalent order:
**~1,000–3,000 people** plus nonresidential volume.

For ~100k residents/Habitat this implies roughly:
**~35–80 major pressure/isolation sectors per Habitat order**, with many smaller internal fire/building subzones beneath them.

### Habitat Strategic Layer
Multiple major sectors can be coordinated/islanded together for large incidents, but a Habitat is not one undivided pressure room.

Hard:
- these are engineering sectors, not neighborhoods,
- boundaries may cross civic districts,
- exact number/layout remains C2 inside the band,
- no single apartment breach depressurizes 100k people.

# 5. Emergency Refuge Scale

Refuges are distributed, not one stadium-sized bunker.

## Selected operating rule
A normal residential/work area must have **walking/mobility-accessible refuge capacity for its local evacuation population plus a neighboring overflow margin**.

Author-facing capacity band:
- local refuge cluster: **hundreds to low-thousands** depending district function,
- distributed system sized so one major pressure/fire sector can be evacuated without requiring whole-Habitat relocation.

Design margin:
**~15–25% local overflow above designated immediate catchment** is the planning target candidate.

Hard:
- not every refuge must support weeks of autonomous living,
- accessible evacuation/support is part of capacity planning,
- pet/service-animal procedure does not override human life-safety.

Exact refuge count = C2.

# 6. Spine Transfer Interfaces

Passenger movement is polycentric.

Selected working band per Habitat:
- **~8–12 major public transfer interfaces** connecting Habitat trunk/radial movement toward the Spine,
- additional service/freight/emergency interfaces exist but are not all public stations.

This is compatible with:
- ~100k residents/Habitat order,
- only a minority crossing Habitats daily,
- shipwide cross-Habitat commuters ~10k–30k/day Canon sanity range,
- 60–120m ordinary door-to-door trips.

Exact station count/layout remains C2.

Hard:
no single central elevator is the only inter-Habitat route.

# 7. Water Resilience

Important distinction:
**total ship water inventory is enormous compared with treated potable buffer.**
Water also exists in process/ecology/shielding/storage loops.

Selected operational bands:

## Building/local treated-water buffer
- **~6–24 hours** of critical domestic/clinical demand under isolation/degraded supply, depending building function.

## District/Habitat potable/process resilience
- **~2–5 days** of prioritized drinking/clinical/critical sanitation demand without normal bulk treatment flow, using distributed tanks/reserves/reconfiguration.

## Strategic total water inventory
Much larger than the above because water circulates through ecology/process/shielding/storage.
It is not described as only `five days of water on the ship`.

Hard:
- routine hot-water failure may last days while potable safety remains intact,
- closed loop is highly efficient but imperfect,
- major contamination can consume treatment time/media rather than instantly threaten total inventory.

# 8. Atmospheric / Gas Resilience

Do not define a simple `days of air` number for a massive closed Habitat.

Selected operating grammar:
- Habitat atmosphere itself is the main working inventory,
- bulk reserve/makeup gases and chemical processing support routine leakage/local refill,
- major sectors can be isolated,
- reserve strategy must support **repressurization of several local/major damaged sectors over time**, not full-Habitat replacement from tanks,
- catastrophic Habitat-wide atmospheric loss remains outside normal recoverable reserve design.

Author-facing quantitative target:
keep **makeup/compressed/cryogenic gas reserve in the low-single-digit percent order of actively managed atmospheric constituent mass**, distributed/segmented rather than one tank farm.

Exact percentage/chemistry = C2.

Hard:
no `air expires in 3 days` trope for an otherwise intact Habitat.

# 9. Electrical Resilience

Selected hierarchy:

## Local life-safety ride-through
- **~15–60 minutes** for immediate critical controls/lighting/doors/communications/safe shutdown depending site.

## Critical facility backup / island operation
Hospitals, control centers, refuges and some utility nodes maintain **hours-to-day class** backup/island capability through combinations of storage/local generation/reconfigured grid.

## Habitat strategic resilience
Primary recovery strategy is **grid reconfiguration + distributed generation + load shedding**, not batteries powering an entire Habitat for days.

Hard:
- discretionary loads go first,
- critical water/air/medical/control loads have priority,
- local backup is not equivalent to propulsion energy.

Exact storage technology = C2.

# 10. Thermal Headroom

Thermal reserve is **operating headroom**, not stored cold for days.

Selected author-facing bands:
- normal mature-city planning prefers roughly **10–20% thermal-rejection headroom** against expected sustained load,
- planned high-load/maintenance/arrival windows may temporarily operate around **5–10% headroom**,
- below roughly **~5% sustained headroom** is treated as an uncomfortable/restricted operating regime requiring load/schedule action rather than normal business.

These are story-engineering bands, not exact safety code.

Hard:
- heat cannot be `saved` like fuel indefinitely,
- a short thermal bottleneck can throttle industry/services without causing immediate blackout,
- arrival tension can consume margin without the interstellar engine stealing household power.

# 12. Strategic Spare / Redundancy Grammar

A universal `20% spare parts` number is misleading.
Use criticality classes.

## S-A — Non-fabricable / long-lead critical systems
Target:
- **N+2 or equivalent independent recovery paths** when practicable,
- at minimum N+1 before accepting major external transfer,
- multi-year component/consumable planning.

## S-B — Fabricable but critical systems
Target:
- installed redundancy where necessary,
- **at least one ready spare / swap path** for common single-point modules,
- certified local fabrication/repair route,
- raw/feedstock/tooling reserve.

## S-C — Common maintainable components
Target:
- weeks-to-months demand stock by failure/production cadence,
- routine remanufacture/reorder from local industry.

## Arrival burn-down
Act5~7 conflict can move selected systems from comfortable `N+2-like` resilience toward `N+1-like` resilience or extend replacement lead time.

Hard:
- `redundancy burn-down` means increased recovery cost/risk, not inevitable failure,
- no percentage of hull dismantled automatically translates to settlement speed.

# 13. Emergency Operating Margins

Use four states:

### GREEN — Normal
full ordinary services and planned maintenance flexibility.

### AMBER — Constrained
reduced redundancy/headroom; maintenance/work windows tightened; discretionary load/activities may be deferred.

### RED — Incident
local/regional emergency control, isolation, refuge/evacuation, prioritized life-safety loads.

### BLACK / LOSS
unrecoverable local asset/volume or catastrophic loss case; not automatically shipwide.

This vocabulary is author-facing unless later reader usage is justified.
