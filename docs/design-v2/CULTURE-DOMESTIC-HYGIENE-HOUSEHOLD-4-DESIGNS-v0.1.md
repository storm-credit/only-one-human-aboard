# CULTURE DOMESTIC / HYGIENE / HOUSEHOLD ROUTINE — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design ordinary home life in a closed-loop city: bathing, toilets, laundry, cleaning, cooking, air/noise/privacy and domestic automation.

---

# DESIGN A — EXTREME WATER-RATION DOMESTICITY
Core: sonic showers, almost no running water, strict quotas.
Strength: space trope/resource efficiency.
Weakness: overstates scarcity in a mature closed-loop city and makes daily life needlessly austere.
Verdict: `REJECT AS NORMAL BASE / RETAIN EMERGENCY RESTRICTIONS`.

# DESIGN B — EARTH-LIKE URBAN COMFORT
Core: normal showers, toilets, laundry, kitchens with hidden recycling infrastructure.
Strength: lived comfort/readability.
Weakness: can ignore physical/resource culture.
Verdict: `STRONG BASE WITH CONSERVATION MODIFIERS`.

# DESIGN C — HIGH-AUTOMATION HOUSEHOLD
Core: robots clean/cook/launder most tasks.
Strength: future feel.
Weakness: erases domestic labor and class/service texture; AI becomes magic servant.
Verdict: `USE TASK AUTOMATION SELECTIVELY`.

# DESIGN D — COMFORTABLE CLOSED-LOOP DOMESTICITY
Core: ordinary comfort is normal, but appliances/fixtures are efficient, maintenance-aware and visibly part of resource cycles.
Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — DOM-H1

Name: **Urban Comfort + High-Efficiency Closed Loops + Repairable Domestic Systems + Selective Automation**
Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. WATER USE
Citizens normally:
- drink freely
- wash dishes
- shower/bathe
- cook
- clean
without treating each liter as emergency ration.

Conservation is embedded in fixtures/pricing/norms rather than constant deprivation.

## 2. SHOWERS / BATHING
Efficient recirculating/heat-recovery showers are common.
Baths or higher-volume bathing may exist as less frequent/premium choices where housing permits.

Emergency shortages can impose temporary restrictions.

## 3. TOILETS
Toilets separate/route waste into treatment/recovery systems.
User experience is civilized and private; citizens do not manually handle waste as a daily ritual.

## 4. LAUNDRY
Efficient low-water/recycling laundry appliances or shared building facilities coexist.
Household wealth/space can affect private vs shared appliance convenience.

## 5. DISHWASHING
Efficient dishwashers/sinks are normal.
Reusable tableware dominates everyday life, with recyclable/compostable disposables for specific settings.

## 6. CLEANING
Households use a mix of:
- manual cleaning
- compact automated cleaners
- building maintenance services
- professional cleaning services

No universal humanoid robot maid is required.

## 7. DOMESTIC AUTOMATION
Automation can handle:
- floor/surface cleaning
- appliance monitoring
- basic scheduling
- inventory reminders
- leak/air-quality alerts

Hard rule:
Household systems do not have unrestricted access to medical/civic/private data by default.

## 8. COOKING
Homes support real cooking per FOOD-H1.
Ventilation/fire rules shape appliances toward electric/contained heating.

## 9. AIR
Indoor air is supplied/conditioned through building/habitat systems.
Homes can monitor local air quality, humidity and allergens.

Citizens do not buy better breathable air as a normal class commodity.
Premium housing may offer quieter/better personalized environmental control within safe civic standards.

## 10. WINDOWS / VIEWS
Real external windows may be limited by structure/radiation/location.
Housing can use:
- interior atrium views
- green-space views
- display surfaces
- protected real viewports in selected architecture

A `view` can be economically valuable without every apartment having a literal star window.

## 11. LIGHTING
Homes follow personal/circadian lighting controls within CAL-H1.
Lighting design is ordinary aesthetic choice, not only utilitarian white panels.

## 12. TEMPERATURE
Safe comfort band is civic baseline.
Households may have limited local adjustment; extreme individualized heating/cooling is constrained by energy/thermal efficiency.

## 13. NOISE
Noise is a major urban quality-of-life issue because structure can transmit vibration/sound.
Housing design includes insulation/isolation standards.
Quiet-hours/social rules exist.

Premium housing can buy better acoustic privacy.

## 14. PRIVACY
Homes are legally/socially private spaces.
No continuous state camera/microphone recording by default.
Building safety sensors detect system conditions, not intimate narrative detail.

## 15. DOORS / ACCESS
Secure digital/mechanical access exists with emergency override under defined safety/legal conditions.
No magic biometric lock makes theft/entry impossible.

## 16. HOUSEHOLD STORAGE
Space is finite, so storage matters.
Households keep:
- clothes
- hobby goods
- heirlooms
- tools
- food
- children's possessions

This supports material-culture accumulation without giant attics.

## 17. REPAIR
Minor repairs can be handled by residents or services; structural/utility work requires authorized professionals.
Repair culture is normal but not every citizen is a mechanic.

## 18. FURNITURE
Furniture is adapted to compact urban space and repairability, but not necessarily fold-away everything.
Larger/premium homes can support more conventional permanent furniture and private rooms.

## 19. HOUSEHOLD SIZE
Exact dwelling sizes wait Package C Housing Bible.
Domestic culture supports:
- single adults
- couples
- children
- sole parents
- shared households
- temporary caregiving arrangements
without one mandatory family unit.

## 20. CHILDCARE AT HOME
Homes can be childproofed/monitored with ordinary safety systems, but children are not under permanent algorithmic surveillance.

## 21. PETS / PLANTS
Houseplants are ordinary where light/space permit.
Pet ecology remains Package C/B ecology dependency; do not assume unrestricted Earth pet ownership until designed.

## 22. HOUSEHOLD SERVICE MARKET
Citizens can pay for:
- cleaning
- repair
- cooking/catering
- care work
- interior design

Automation does not erase service labor.

## 23. CLASS SIGNALS
Domestic class differences appear in:
- floor area
- number of private rooms
- view
- acoustic isolation
- storage
- appliance quality
- customized interiors
- location
- private vs shared facilities

Not in basic air/water safety.

## 24. DISABILITY ACCESS
Housing modification/support includes:
- accessible doors/bathrooms
- assistive controls
- mobility clearances
- sensory accommodations

PROP/MED social rights apply.

## 25. EMERGENCY DOMESTIC SYSTEMS
Homes/buildings include location-appropriate:
- fire alert/suppression
- pressure/air alerts
- emergency lighting
- communication
- compartment/safe-route information

Exact safety architecture belongs Package C.

## 26. ARRIVAL
Early surface/orbit housing may temporarily be more utilitarian and communal than mature Meridian homes.
This difference should make Meridian's ordinary domestic comfort feel valuable rather than obsolete.

# CURRENT VERDICT
`DOM-H1 = PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`
Key principle: **Closed-loop sustainability is built into the plumbing, not performed as daily misery.**
Promotion blockers: Package C housing/utilities/emergency/AI, exact water quant, pets, character homes.
