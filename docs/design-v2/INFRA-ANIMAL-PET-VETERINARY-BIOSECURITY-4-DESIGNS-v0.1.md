# INFRA — ANIMAL / PET / VETERINARY / BIOSECURITY — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE C / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Goal:
Determine whether and how nonhuman animals exist in a ~300k, 450-year closed generation-ship civilization without creating either an implausibly sterile world or an Earth zoo with no resource cost.

---

# DESIGN A — NO LIVE COMPANION ANIMALS

Only microbes/agricultural biology; all pets are robotic/virtual.

Pros:
- efficient,
- simple biosecurity.

Cons:
- culturally thin over 450 years,
- unnecessary given city scale,
- removes veterinary/animal welfare/ecology texture.

---

# DESIGN B — BROAD EARTH PET / LIVESTOCK ECOLOGY

Cats, dogs, birds, fish, livestock and many zoo species persist widely.

Pros:
- familiar,
- rich biodiversity.

Cons:
- high habitat/feed/veterinary/genetic-management cost,
- implausible large-animal abundance,
- cultured meat makes large live livestock economically unnecessary.

---

# DESIGN C — SELECTIVE MANAGED COMPANION ECOLOGY

A limited set of small/medium companion species persists, plus controlled educational/conservation populations and genetic archives.

Pros:
- strongest culture/resource balance,
- realistic city-scale pet ownership,
- creates veterinary/biosecurity institutions.

---

# DESIGN D — ROBOTIC PET DOMINANT + RARE BIOLOGICAL ANIMALS

Most households use synthetic companions; biological pets are prestige/rare.

Pros:
- resource efficient.
Cons:
- risks unnecessary artificiality and class-coded biological animals.

Use synthetic companions as an option, not default hierarchy.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — BIO-PET-H1

# `Selective Companion Species + Managed Breeding + Veterinary Commons + Genetic Conservation + Strong Biosecurity`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. Companion Animals Exist

Meridian can plausibly support companion animals because:
- population ~300k,
- mature food/medical infrastructure,
- centuries of cultural continuity,
- high recycling efficiency,
- animal populations can be limited and managed.

Hard rule:
Pets are not treated as impossible luxuries reserved for elites.

---

# 2. Likely Common Companion Categories

Strong candidates:
- cats,
- small/medium dogs,
- small mammals,
- selected fish/aquatic pets,
- selected small birds only under managed housing rules,
- invertebrate/terrarium hobbies.

Exact species list remains elastic until historical/ecology design.

Large animals such as horses/cattle are not ordinary companion/livestock populations.

---

# 3. Dogs / Cats

These are plausible because of:
- deep human cultural association,
- manageable population sizes,
- veterinary/genetic management,
- feed produced from byproducts/microbial/fermentation/cultured protein streams.

Not every household owns one.
Building/co-op rules may constrain:
- number,
- noise,
- allergies,
- shared-space behavior.

---

# 4. Livestock

Large live livestock is not required for ordinary meat production.

Cultured meat/fermentation and plant systems dominate.

Small live populations may exist for:
- education,
- research,
- genetic conservation,
- cultural heritage,
- limited specialty food traditions where welfare/resources allow.

No giant cattle decks.

---

# 5. Genetic Conservation

Meridian preserves far more Earth biodiversity genetically than it maintains alive.

Possible repositories:
- seed banks,
- gametes,
- embryos,
- tissue/cell lines,
- genomic archives,
- microbiome libraries.

Important:
Genetic archive ≠ instantly re-creatable ecosystem.
Species restoration requires gestation, surrogate/biotech methods, habitat, nutrition, veterinary expertise and ecological planning.

---

# 6. Breeding

Companion animal breeding is managed enough to avoid severe bottlenecks/overpopulation.

Mechanisms may include:
- registration,
- veterinary reproductive care,
- genetic diversity planning for small populations,
- spay/neuter or reversible reproductive control,
- licensed/regulated breeding for scarce lines.

Guardrail:
Do not turn ordinary pet ownership into oppressive biological bureaucracy. Rules scale with population/welfare risk.

---

# 7. Veterinary Medicine

Veterinary services include:
- preventive care,
- surgery,
- infectious-disease control,
- reproduction/genetics,
- dentistry,
- behavior/welfare,
- emergency care.

Advanced medicine helps animals too, but animal Reconstruction equivalent is not assumed to be routine unless separately designed. Do not casually extend human Continuity law to pets.

---

# 8. Animal Welfare Law

Baseline:
- cruelty prohibited,
- minimum care standards,
- abandonment regulated,
- dangerous-animal handling rules,
- breeding welfare standards.

Animals are not civic persons equivalent to humans by default.

---

# 9. Housing / Public Space

Possible pet infrastructure:
- designated relief/exercise areas,
- pet-permitted parks/courtyards,
- waste collection,
- building policies,
- veterinary clinics,
- transport carrier/restraint rules.

No free-roaming pets in critical utility/food-production zones.

---

# 10. Waste / Feed

Pet waste enters controlled household/organic waste treatment.

Animal feed can use:
- plant streams,
- fermentation protein,
- cultured animal protein,
- safe food-production byproducts,
- tailored veterinary nutrition.

Pets do not need to compete directly with humans for premium food.

---

# 11. Allergy / Zoonosis

Mature closed habitats take allergy and disease seriously.

Controls:
- ventilation/building policies,
- hygiene,
- vaccination/testing,
- quarantine for outbreaks,
- veterinary surveillance,
- food-production separation.

No claim that closed society eliminated zoonoses forever.

---

# 12. Service Animals

Biological service animals can exist alongside robotic/accessibility aids.

Possible roles:
- sensory/medical alert,
- mobility/psychiatric assistance,
- therapeutic support.

No assumption that robots automatically replace every human-animal bond.

---

# 13. Synthetic / Robotic Companions

AI-H1 allows robotic pets/companions as ordinary consumer products.

They are options for:
- people who prefer them,
- allergy constraints,
- housing restrictions,
- hobby/entertainment.

Do not frame biological pets as inherently superior or synthetic pets as secretly sentient.

---

# 14. Wildlife

There is no uncontrolled wild megafauna ecosystem inside Meridian.

Some intentionally maintained semi-wild populations may exist in ecological/educational systems:
- pollinating insects,
- beneficial invertebrates,
- selected small aquatic/avian organisms where safely enclosed.

Any such population requires ecological monitoring.

---

# 15. Agriculture Ecology

Food systems may use:
- managed pollinators where needed,
- microbial consortia,
- soil/substrate organisms in specific systems,
- biological pest control.

Most production does not rely on uncontrolled natural ecosystem complexity.

---

# 16. Escapes / Invasive Species

A pet/pest escape is a real operational issue because closed ecosystems amplify some risks.

Response can include:
- tracking,
- capture,
- quarantine,
- ecological monitoring.

Do not make every escaped cat a life-support emergency.

---

# 17. Arrival Biosecurity

This is crucial.

On arrival:
- Earth-origin organisms are not casually released onto the destination world,
- local biosignatures/ecology must be assessed,
- surface habitats initially remain controlled,
- quarantine separates ship biology from unknown local biology,
- genetic conservation plans do not imply immediate terraforming.

No pet dog runs freely into an alien ecosystem in early settlement.

---

# 18. Cultural Role

Animals can appear in:
- family life,
- childhood,
- grief,
- media,
- sport/training,
- art,
- neighborhood conflict,
- elderly companionship.

They should not become mandatory symbolic clues.

---

# 19. Character Use

Do not assign pets to core cast merely because this Bible exists.
Package F should decide based on character function/taste/time.

A pet must not become a shortcut for making a character likable.

---

# 20. Population Quant

Exact companion-animal numbers remain open.
Sanity rule:
The total animal biomass/feed/veterinary burden should be a small fraction of human civilization load, not an invisible second population approaching human scale.

Quantify later only if recurring plot requires it.
