# CULTURE SHIP-BORN IDENTITY / EARTH MEMORY / MISSION CULTURE — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design what `home`, `Earth`, `mission`, `Meridian`, `Habitat`, `founders` and `arrival` mean culturally after ~450 years.

---

# DESIGN A — MISSION-FIRST IDENTITY
Core: citizens primarily see themselves as descendants charged with completing a sacred interstellar mission.
Strength: strong purpose.
Weakness: makes society ideological/monolithic; ship-home politics become deviance.
Verdict: `REJECT AS UNIVERSAL CULTURE`.

# DESIGN B — EARTH-NOSTALGIC DIASPORA
Core: Earth remains imagined true homeland; ship is temporary vessel.
Strength: poignant.
Weakness: implausible after many generations; undermines Meridian-as-city/home.
Verdict: `RETAIN AS ONE SUBCULTURAL ATTITUDE`.

# DESIGN C — HABITAT LOCALISM
Core: 1/2/3환 identities dominate culture and stereotypes.
Strength: local texture.
Weakness: hidden faction/caste architecture.
Verdict: `ALLOW LIGHT LOCAL IDENTITY, REJECT PRIMARY POLITICAL ETHNICITY`.

# DESIGN D — LAYERED SHIP-BORN IDENTITY
Core: citizens combine Meridian civic identity, district/Habitat familiarity, family heritage, profession/community and different attitudes toward Earth/mission/arrival.
Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — ID-HOME-H1

Name: **Meridian-Home Majority + Layered Heritage + Contested Mission Memory + Plural Arrival Futures**
Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. MERIDIAN AS HOME
For most current citizens, Meridian is not experienced as a vehicle they happen to occupy.
It is:
- birthplace
- city
- family geography
- school/work network
- cultural archive
- grave/memorial space
- political community

`ship` and `home` are both true.

## 2. EARTH
No living current citizen remembers Earth firsthand under existing lifespan/history architecture.

Earth is known through:
- archives
- family stories
- religion
- history education
- art/media
- preserved objects

Earth can be emotionally important without being experienced memory.

## 3. EARTH ATTITUDE SPECTRUM
Citizens vary:
- deep romantic nostalgia for a world never seen
- historical fascination
- religious significance
- mild cultural affection
- indifference
- criticism of founder-era mythology

No single attitude is normal truth.

## 4. FOUNDER MEMORY
Departure generations are historically important but not sacred genetic aristocracy.

Possible cultural layers:
- monuments/archive exhibits
- family genealogical pride
- revisionist criticism
- myths/jokes
- anniversary ceremonies

Hard rule:
Founder descent grants no civic authority.

## 5. GENEALOGY
Some families track launch-era ancestry; others do not care or have mixed/fragmented records.

Because 450 years creates massive pedigree overlap, `founder descendant` is not a rare noble bloodline.

## 6. MISSION CULTURE
The original mission remains a constitutional/historical fact, not one mandatory personal purpose.

Citizens may regard it as:
- inherited responsibility
- practical reality
- extraordinary historical project
- burden imposed by ancestors
- opportunity
- something they want completed but not worshipped

## 7. ARRIVAL ATTITUDE
Spectrum includes:
- surface enthusiasm
- orbital-industrial preference
- ship-home attachment
- cautious balanced approach
- new-habitat ambition
- personal indifference compared with career/family

This maps to existing four strategic paths without turning them into four personality types.

## 8. HABITAT IDENTITY
Residents can have local pride/stereotypes/jokes tied to:
- architecture
- commute
- district history
- local food/venues
- work clusters

Hard rule:
Habitat residence is not ethnicity, political party or caste.
People move/intermarry/work across them.

## 9. DISTRICT IDENTITY
District/neighborhood identity can be stronger in daily life than whole-Habitat identity.

Old Quarter is a strong example:
- historical attachment
- business/community networks
- repair politics
without representing all 1환.

## 10. PROFESSIONAL IDENTITY
Long careers and high-skill professions create meaningful cultures:
- utility shift workers
- legal/civic workers
- medical/rehab
- logistics
- maintenance
- hospitality
- arts/media

No profession owns moral legitimacy.

## 11. FAMILY HERITAGE
Families can retain:
- names
- recipes
- languages
- religious practices
- objects
- stories

Mixed heritage is normal after centuries.
A person's surname is not a purity certificate.

## 12. `EARTH AUTHENTICITY` STATUS
Some cultural elites/collectors may value:
- real Earth-origin objects
- old recordings
- heritage cultivars
- historically faithful performances

Countercultures may mock this as expensive ancestor cosplay.

This creates taste/class debate without magical relics.

## 13. SHIP-BORN CULTURAL PRIDE
People can value uniquely Meridian things:
- ship-created music/food
- zero-g sport
- architecture
- repair culture
- local festivals
- voyage history

This prevents Earth archive from defining culture entirely.

## 14. OPEN SKY / PLANET IMAGINATION
A real planetary horizon, weather and open sky are culturally powerful because nobody has ordinary lived memory of them.

But citizens have:
- simulations
- archival media
- large interior vistas/green spaces
so they are not conceptually ignorant.

Emotional reactions can include awe, fear, disappointment, motion sickness, indifference.

## 15. NATURE
`Nature` aboard Meridian is not universally considered fake.
2환 ecosystems/parks/plants are living engineered environments and can be emotionally authentic.

Surface nature does not automatically invalidate ship ecology.

## 16. HOME LANGUAGE
By Acts 5–7, `home` can refer to:
- a dwelling
- neighborhood
- Meridian
- future orbital place
- future surface place

The title `두 고향` works because this word becomes plural through lived choices.

## 17. EVERYDAY JOKES
Humor can target:
- commute
- shift schedules
- old mission slogans
- habitat stereotypes
- fake Earth expertise
- maintenance annoyances
- arrival bureaucracy

Do not invent a joke dictionary; Package F voices will select recurring examples.

## 18. TABOOS
Potential cultural sensitivities:
- treating essential repair/safety as someone else's invisible labor
- using real disasters casually
- invasive questions about Reconstruction/medical history
- post-Count origin slurs/essentialism
- desecrating heritage/memorial objects

Exact taboo strength varies by community/generation.

## 19. CONSERVATION ETIQUETTE
Citizens generally know not to:
- damage shared infrastructure
- waste unusually large resources casually
- obstruct emergency access

But they do not perform constant ritual guilt over normal showers/meals.

## 20. SOCIAL POLITENESS / PERSONAL SPACE
Urban norms value:
- queue/order in shared transit/services
- home privacy
- appointment/schedule reliability
- quiet rules in housing

Exact etiquette remains flexible by district/generation.

## 21. CLASS TASTE
Class/status can express through cultural choices:
- premium quiet/private venues
- heritage authenticity
- rare collections
- elite coaching/arts training
- fashionable districts
- travel/time flexibility

No legal aristocracy.

## 22. RECONSTRUCTION CULTURE
Reconstruction is socially known and serious but not everyone's defining obsession.

Etiquette generally discourages treating reconstructed people as public philosophical experiments.
Actual prejudice/awkwardness can persist.

## 23. ORIGIN AFTER COUNT
The Reveal creates new labels/memes/prejudices but cannot instantly erase centuries of current civic identity.

Most Seeded citizens do not wake up believing they were secretly machines once Meaning is explained.

Social conflict centers on classification, history, privacy and institutional use.

## 24. AMARA
Amara is culturally disruptive because the category suddenly attaches to an ordinary known family.

Hard rules:
- not sacred relic
- not founder reincarnation
- not true Earth representative
- not rightful settler ruler

## 25. ARRIVAL GENERATION LABELS
As story progresses, people may informally distinguish cohorts such as:
- those who grew up before Final Approach
- arrival trainees
- first surface/orbit workers

Do not Canonize catchy generation names until Package D/F shows need.

## 26. POST-ARRIVAL IDENTITY
Meridian identity persists after physical branching.

Over time, new identities may form:
- ship resident
- orbital resident
- surface resident
but ending occurs too early for fully separate nations/cultures.

## 27. RED FLAGS
P1-ID-01: Meridian-home majority must not erase citizens who resent mission life.
P1-ID-02: Earth nostalgia can become overly sentimental.
P1-ID-03: Habitat jokes can become caste stereotypes.
P1-ID-04: founder heritage can accidentally become elite lineage.
P1-ID-05: arrival paths can become clean political tribes.
P1-ID-06: exact historical myths/jokes/rituals need Package D.
