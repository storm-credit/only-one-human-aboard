# CULTURE CALENDAR / CIVIL TIME / HOLIDAYS — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design ordinary timekeeping for a multi-century generation ship with no natural Earth sunrise/year cycle.

Must support:
- M1 age/time architecture
- school/work schedules
- birthdays
- shifts
- holidays
- religious calendars
- cross-Habitat transit
- artificial day/night
- arrival countdown
without excessive new calendar jargon.

---

# DESIGN A — FULL EARTH/GREGORIAN RETENTION

Core:
24h days, 7-day weeks and Gregorian months/year continue unchanged by convention.

Strengths:
- maximum reader legibility
- biological circadian fit
- easy age/history records

Weaknesses:
- 450 years aboard ship feels culturally static
- Earth seasonal month meanings become detached

Verdict: `STRONG INFRASTRUCTURE BASE / NEED SHIP-BORN CULTURAL LAYER`.

---

# DESIGN B — NEW MISSION CALENDAR

Core:
Departure creates Year 0, new months/weeks/holidays optimized for ship operations.

Strengths:
- distinctive ship identity
- easy mission chronology

Weaknesses:
- large reader burden
- birthdays/ages become conversion problem
- risks unnecessary sci-fi naming

Verdict: `USE VOYAGE YEAR AS SECONDARY ARCHIVE FRAME, NOT DAILY CALENDAR REPLACEMENT`.

---

# DESIGN C — HABITAT-SPECIFIC LOCAL TIME

Core:
Each rotating habitat runs distinct day cycles/time zones suited to local work/ecology.

Strengths:
- texture
- plausible local autonomy

Weaknesses:
- transit/coordination nightmare in one city
- artificial complexity
- can reinforce Habitat separation/caste

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY / ALLOW SHIFT-LOCAL CULTURE ONLY`.

---

# DESIGN D — EARTH-DESCENDED CIVIL TIME + VOYAGE EPOCH + SHIP HOLIDAYS

Core:
Human-friendly Earth-derived civil time remains standard, while long history is also indexed by voyage-year/mission epoch and annual civic holidays become ship-born.

Strengths:
- readable
- historically plausible institutional continuity
- ship identity develops without calendar puzzle
- supports heritage/religious calendars

Weaknesses:
- must explain why month/week structure persists

Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — CAL-H1

Name:
**Synchronized Earth-Descended Civil Time + Voyage-Epoch Archival Time + Ship-Born Observance Calendar**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

---

# 1. CIVIL DAY

Meridian uses a shipwide synchronized **24-hour civil day**.

Reasons:
- human circadian biology
- medical/sleep continuity
- launch-era institutional inheritance
- transit/emergency coordination
- archive compatibility

Artificial lighting supplies local day/night cycles rather than astronomical sunrise.

---

# 2. SHIPWIDE TIME

All three Habitats, Spine and main civic systems share one official civil clock.

This avoids internal time zones in a 300k connected city.

Individuals/industries still live different shift schedules.

Reader-facing scenes can simply use ordinary clock time unless a specialized shift context matters.

---

# 3. DAY/NIGHT LIGHTING

Residential/public environments generally follow synchronized circadian lighting bands:
- brighter active period
- evening transition
- darker sleep period

24/7 zones such as hospitals/transport/utilities remain operational.

Not every corridor literally turns dark at night; safety lighting remains.

---

# 4. WEEK

A **7-day week** persists as inherited social coordination convention.

Reasons:
- historical/religious continuity
- labor law
- school scheduling
- culture
- no strong benefit to replacing it

Exact names are rendered in ordinary Korean translation convention.

---

# 5. WORK / REST RHYTHM

Common office/school life uses a five-work/two-rest style weekly center, but not universally.

Variation:
- 24/7 shift sectors
- hospitality
- medical
- utility
- transport
- Outer Works
- creative/freelance

Package A LAB-H1 remains authority for hours/overtime/safety.

Hard rule:
`weekend` is socially real even though the ship never stops operating.

---

# 6. CIVIL YEAR

Meridian retains an Earth-descended ~365-day civil year for:
- age
- birthdays
- law
- school cycles
- financial accounting
- archives

Leap adjustment can remain inherited civil convention; exact calendar arithmetic need not appear in prose.

The year is conventional, not tied to Meridian orbiting a star during cruise.

---

# 7. MONTHS

Earth-descended month structure remains in common civil use for readability and long institutional continuity.

Seasonal names/associations have weakened or changed because ship climate is artificial.

Do not invent twelve new month names unless later culture/history produces strong value.

---

# 8. VOYAGE EPOCH

Historical/mission records also track a departure-relative **Voyage Year / mission epoch**.

Uses:
- engineering history
- institutional chronology
- museum/archive labeling
- public anniversaries

Ordinary citizens do not need to say Voyage Year in every conversation.

Exact departure epoch numbering will be set in Package D.

---

# 9. BIRTHDAYS

Birthdays remain ordinary annual personal/family celebrations using civil calendar dates.

Advanced longevity does not remove age identity.

Exact character birthdays remain ELASTIC unless later Character Bible needs them.

---

# 10. SCHOOL YEAR

Schools use annual terms/blocks with major breaks, but not necessarily a long agricultural/summer vacation.

Likely pattern:
- multiple teaching terms
- shorter distributed breaks
- annual transition/graduation window

Exact term lengths wait Education + Culture integration.

---

# 11. ARTIFICIAL SEASONALITY

Meridian need not imitate Earth seasons physically everywhere.

However cultural/green-space lighting/planting cycles may create mild seasonal aesthetics in some districts.

These are programmed ecological/cultural cycles, not real planetary weather seasons.

Different Habitats need not run opposed climates.

---

# 12. CIVIC OBSERVANCE TYPES

Exact names/dates wait Package D history, but reference-grade holiday functions include:

## Departure / Founding observance
Marks voyage beginning / founding generation.
Can contain both pride and critique.

## Collective remembrance observance
For major voyage losses/crises, potentially separate or combined historically.

## Civic / rights observance
May commemorate major constitutional/personhood reform if later History supports it.

## Local district festivals
Old Quarter and other districts maintain local events not equal to national holidays.

## Arrival-era observances
During story, countdown/certification/insertion eventually generate new commemorative dates rather than instantly becoming ancient traditions.

---

# 13. NO SINGLE MISSION RELIGION HOLIDAY

Civic holidays do not require shared religious belief.

Religious communities maintain their own calendars/observances in addition to civic calendar.

---

# 14. RELIGIOUS CALENDARS

Earth-derived lunar/solar/liturgical calendars can be computed conventionally even without local Earth astronomy.

Communities decide how to adapt:
- civil-date mapping
- symbolic astronomical calculations
- local lighting/ritual timing

No need for one state solution.

---

# 15. SHIFT TIME CULTURE

24/7 workers may identify strongly with:
- first/second/night shift
- handover windows
- off-cycle meals
- quiet-hours exceptions

Shift slang/ritual belongs later character/culture design.

Amara's water/reclamation work can use this naturally.

---

# 16. QUIET HOURS

Residential districts commonly enforce/socially expect quieter night periods even if work continues elsewhere.

Noise policy can vary by district/building.

This later integrates Housing/Domestic Infrastructure.

---

# 17. TIME ACROSS ROTATION / ZERO-G

Official clock does not change when moving between rotating Habitats and Spine.

Physiological transition may matter; time-zone jet lag does not.

This keeps cross-Habitat commute manageable.

---

# 18. ARRIVAL COUNTDOWN

Technical institutions track precise time-to-burn/insertion.

Public culture may increasingly refer to:
- years to insertion
- Final Approach milestones
- school/career cohorts framed around arrival

Hard rule:
No permanent giant countdown dominates every early public screen.
TK-01 remains background→primary escalation.

---

# 19. POST-ARRIVAL TIME

After insertion, ship civil time initially remains authoritative for continuity.

Surface settlement later faces local:
- day length
- seasons
- year length

Package E must decide whether/when local calendars supplement civil Meridian time.

No instant abandonment of 450-year civil calendar.

---

# 20. AGE OF MAJORITY

Civil/legal age thresholds use civil years.

Exact general majority age remains provisional until Family/Education/Culture final integration.

No biological/Seed-origin-specific age rules.

---

# 21. MEDIA / BUSINESS

Markets, bills, contracts and news use standardized civil timestamps.

Special technical systems may additionally use monotonic mission time to prevent ambiguity.

Reader need not see engineering timestamps unless plot-relevant.

---

# 22. RED FLAGS

P1-CAL-01: Gregorian-like persistence can feel overly conservative.
P1-CAL-02: exact holiday names need 450-year history first.
P1-CAL-03: school-year structure remains incomplete.
P1-CAL-04: religious calendar adaptation needs actual traditions.
P1-CAL-05: post-arrival local time needs Package E astronomy.
P1-CAL-06: exact majority age remains open.
