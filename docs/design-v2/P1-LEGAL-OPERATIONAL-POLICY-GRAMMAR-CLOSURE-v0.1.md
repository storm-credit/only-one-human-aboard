# 1. Civil Majority / Youth Autonomy — AGE-H1

## Alternatives
A. full adult rights at 16
B. full adult rights at 18 with child status until then
C. no fixed age; competence only
D. **18-year civil majority + staged independent rights before 18**

Selected: **D / AGE-H1**.

## General civil majority
**18 Meridian civil years**.

At 18, default full adult capacity includes:
- contracts,
- independent residence,
- ordinary medical consent,
- voting where otherwise eligible,
- employment/education decisions,
- privacy/accounts without guardian default.

Exact election registration timing around birthdays remains ordinary administrative detail.

## Staged youth rights

### ~14+ — mandatory direct voice band
For high-impact family/residence/guardianship decisions:
- the young person must be heard directly unless impossible/unsafe,
- views receive increasing weight with maturity,
- adults may not reduce youth to evidence object.

This does NOT mean a 14-year-old automatically wins every residence dispute.

### ~16+ — independent routine-care / privacy band
A competent 16+ youth normally may independently consent to:
- routine/low-risk medical care,
- mental-health counseling,
- contraception/sexual-health care where applicable,
- ordinary personal-record privacy choices,
subject to emergency/safety and capacity law.

For high-risk/irreversible major treatment before 18:
- enhanced capacity review,
- guardian/support involvement where appropriate,
- independent clinical/ethics/court route if conflict exists.

### Paid apprenticeship
~16+ is a normal baseline for substantial paid apprenticeship/work integration,
with stricter hazardous-work limits/credentials before full adulthood.

Hard:
- no single birthday erases competence context,
- no guardian owns a teenager's body/identity,
- no child philosopher-jurisprudence burden in narrative.

# 2. Data Retention / Correction — DATA-H1

## Alternatives
A. one universal retention period
B. permanent storage of everything
C. deletion-first privacy with minimal archives
D. **purpose-class retention + deep protected provenance + correction-by-version**

Selected: **D / DATA-H1**.

## Core principle
**Retention follows function, risk and lawful purpose; not every datum lives forever and not every deletion request can erase legal/medical provenance.**

## Category bands

### D1 — ordinary personal/service data
Examples:
- convenience history,
- recommendation logs,
- routine commercial usage.

Default:
- user/service configurable,
- short-to-moderate retention,
- deletion/portability generally available subject to active disputes/contracts.

Author-facing order:
**days to low-years**, not decades by default.

### D2 — routine civic/financial/contract records
Needed for:
- taxes,
- benefits,
- contracts,
- ordinary litigation/audit.

Author-facing retention:
**~5–10 years after closure/order**, longer if dispute/asset relationship continues.

### D3 — safety/incident/critical-system logs
Raw high-rate telemetry:
- short rolling windows where no incident exists.

Incident-relevant extracts/audit trail:
- retained much longer for engineering/legal learning.

Author-facing:
- ordinary raw detail **weeks-to-months**,
- incident/critical evidence **~5–20 years** depending severity/system.

### D4 — medical longitudinal / identity / Continuity records
Core treatment/identity/Continuity evidence persists through life.

After legal finality:
- major medical/Continuity/provenance records may remain protected for **decades** and/or designated archival retention where rights/history/integrity require.

Recovery Maps/Neural Anchor records are not casual consumer data.

### D5 — deep historical/archive provenance
Schemas, migration maps, institutional snapshots, founding/history records may be **permanent archival records**.

Permanent does not mean public or in the live current registry.

## Correction grammar
- current-facing record can be corrected,
- legally significant prior version is not silently overwritten,
- provenance/audit trail records correction source/time,
- public correction does not require exposing protected source data,
- wrongful public data should be corrected/distribution limited even when protected archive evidence remains.

Hard:
- no one master forever-database,
- no total right to erase evidence of major legal/medical events,
- no automatic government access because data exists.

# 3. Weapon / Dangerous Tool Categories — FORCE-H1

## Alternatives
A. near-total civilian weapon ban with vague exceptions
B. Earthlike firearm permit culture
C. universal nonlethal technology solves violence
D. **closed-habitat risk tiers based on penetration/energy/control context**

Selected: **D / FORCE-H1**.

## T0 — ordinary tools / household implements
Examples:
kitchen knives, hand tools, sport equipment.

Normal possession/use.
Criminal misuse remains criminal.

## T1 — controlled professional dangerous tools
Examples:
industrial cutters, high-energy maintenance tools, demolition-adjacent equipment, hazardous chemical/process access.

Control:
- job/site authorization,
- training where needed,
- custody/access logs for high-risk systems.

Not classified as `weapons` merely because misuse is possible.

## T2 — public-safety protective/less-lethal force tools
Authorized public-safety/security roles may use graduated restraint/less-lethal tools under policy/training/review.

Hard:
`less lethal` ≠ harmless.

## T3 — high-penetration / high-energy interpersonal weapons
Private ordinary civilian possession is strongly restricted/prohibited because missed shots/penetration can endanger multiple people and critical infrastructure.

Specialized secure authorization only for narrow public-safety/security/testing needs.

## T4 — explosive / hull-critical / strategic destructive systems
Stored/used only under specialized engineering/security controls.
Industrial demolition tools can exist but are not privately carried as arms.

Hard:
- no ubiquitous gun culture assumed,
- no magical stun setting,
- restricting weapons does not eliminate assault,
- AI may assist threat detection but does not independently assign guilt/open-ended lethal force.

Exact product catalog/models remain C2.

# 4. Essential-Service Strike — STRIKE-H1

## Alternatives
A. critical workers cannot strike
B. unrestricted strike including immediate total life-support shutdown
C. state may compel any worker indefinitely during dispute
D. **strike right + pre-defined minimum life-safety service + rapid neutral review**

Selected: **D / STRIKE-H1**.

## Rule
Workers in critical life-safety sectors retain organization/bargaining/strike rights.

A strike may reduce service substantially but cannot intentionally remove the minimum staffing/functions required to prevent imminent serious harm.

## Minimum-service design
- defined by safety-critical functions, not employer revenue/business continuity,
- preferably pre-negotiated/certified before disputes,
- disputed minimum reviewed rapidly by independent labor/safety adjudication,
- technical managers cannot relabel normal production as `life safety` merely to ban a strike.

## Emergency exception
If an objective unplanned emergency arises:
- narrow temporary staffing/order may be required,
- duration/scope logged and reviewable,
- cannot become indefinite forced normal labor.

Cross-trained qualified relief/volunteer pools may be used where safe.

Hard:
no permanent forced occupation.

# 5. Early Off-Ship Tax / Business / Conflict-of-Law — ARRLAW-H1

## Alternatives
A. every node instantly becomes independent legal jurisdiction
B. all off-ship activity remains legally identical to a Meridian district forever
C. overlapping full taxes/laws accumulate without hierarchy
D. **shared civic-law baseline + location/service nexus + staged municipal competence**

Selected: **D / ARRLAW-H1**.

## Fundamental hierarchy
Early arrival phase:
1. 시민기본법 / core civic rights remain shipwide/personwide,
2. 도착전환규정 governs transition interfaces,
3. location/site safety and operational rules govern physical conduct locally,
4. local municipal rules grow only with real resident/institutional maturity.

No external node begins as sovereign state.

## Criminal/public safety
Default:
conduct is handled under law applicable where conduct occurred, with common civic rights/appeal protections.

Cross-node investigation/cooperation is ordinary because shared citizenship/institutions remain.

## Family / personal status
Marriage/parenthood/guardianship/personhood status recognized across Meridian/orbit/surface.
Moving sites does not reset family law.

## Property / land-use
Physical property/use rules follow the node/location of the asset.
Surface `land` initially means licensed/allocated use/stewardship under capacity/environment rules, not first-come sovereign title.

## Contracts
Parties may choose a recognized governing framework for ordinary commercial contracts where lawful.
Default early-arrival commercial law remains shipwide civic/commercial law with site-specific safety/property rules.

## Tax/service nexus
Early phase uses **anti-double-tax nexus**:
- shipwide common levy/fund continues to support shared institutions,
- local service/use charges or later municipal tax can arise where off-ship services/residence/business actually occur,
- credits/offsets prevent the same base being fully taxed twice merely because a worker crosses nodes,
- business obligations track meaningful operations/assets/workforce rather than registration-shell games.

Exact rates remain C2.

Hard:
- no tax haven asteroid by default,
- no citizenship loss by moving,
- no first lander sovereignty,
- no Amara exception.

# 6. Surface Biosecurity / Organism Import — BIO-H1

## Alternatives
A. rapid Earth ecology release
B. total permanent ban on all living organisms outside Meridian
C. species-by-species whitelist only
D. **risk-tier quarantine + contained living systems + outdoor release moratorium**

Selected: **D / BIO-H1**.

## B0 — sterile / nonliving processed cargo
Standard decontamination and inspection.

## B1 — low-risk nonliving biological materials / prepared food
Controlled packaging/decon; no living release.

## B2 — contained living systems
Examples:
- greenhouse crops,
- research cultures,
- microbiome/bioprocess organisms,
- selected companion/service animals if later approved.

Requirements:
- permit,
- contained habitat,
- quarantine/health screening,
- waste/escape control,
- traceable custody.

## B3 — deliberate outdoor environmental release
During the series ending window:
**presumptively prohibited / moratorium.**

Requires future:
- indigenous-life assessment,
- ecological model,
- public/scientific/legal review,
- long-term monitoring.

No individual settler may release seeds/pets/microbes because `the planet looks empty`.

## B4 — local planetary biological/geochemical samples
Treated as potentially unknown hazard/contamination source.

Requirements scale by evidence:
- sealed sample chains,
- controlled labs,
- quarantine/containment,
- no casual souvenir biological material brought into homes/Meridian ecology.

## Human movement
Humans are not sterile cargo.
Protocol focuses on:
- health screening where needed,
- decon of suits/tools/vehicles,
- environmental sampling,
- preventing cross-contamination both directions.

Hard:
- no instant terraforming by gardening,
- no alien plague twist required,
- biosecurity remains precautionary even if no life is ultimately found.
