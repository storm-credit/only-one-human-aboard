# INFRA — TRANSIT / COMMUTE / FREIGHT — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE C / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Existing constraints:
- three rotating Habitats + non-rotating Spine.
- ordinary cross-Habitat door-to-door ~60–120 minutes.
- cross-Habitat commuters ~10k–30k/day order.
- schools/jobs/services exist in every Habitat; cross-Habitat commute is normal but not universal.
- no Habitat caste.
- Spine is lived infrastructure and may experience temporary faults.
- cargo/logistics and emergency transport must coexist with civilian transit.

Goal:
Build a transit topology that supports ordinary life, freight, maintenance and emergency response without requiring magic elevators or instant teleportation.

---

# DESIGN A — CENTRAL SPINE HUB-AND-SPOKE

Each Habitat has several radial transfer trunks leading to rotating/non-rotating transfer hubs; the Spine carries express passenger and freight movement between Habitats.

Advantages:
- mechanically legible,
- excellent long-distance/freight separation,
- supports 60–120m cross-Habitat travel,
- easy to stage disruptions.

Weaknesses:
- transfer hubs can become chokepoints,
- overcentralization makes every trip feel the same.

---

# DESIGN B — HABITAT INTERNAL METRO + SPINE EXPRESS

Each Habitat has a local high-capacity rail/guideway network; only cross-Habitat trips enter Spine express links.

Advantages:
- strongest city feel,
- distributes local access,
- realistic commute layering.

Weaknesses:
- more infrastructure,
- can feel too Earth-metro if copied literally.

---

# DESIGN C — AUTONOMOUS POD NETWORK

Small on-demand vehicles dominate both local and cross-Habitat trips.

Advantages:
- flexible,
- accessible,
- low transfer burden.

Weaknesses:
- huge traffic/control complexity,
- risks generic futurism,
- bad for mass evacuation/freight,
- overreliance on automation.

Verdict candidate: reject as primary.

---

# DESIGN D — WALKABLE DISTRICTS + LIMITED HIGH-CAPACITY TRUNKS

Most daily trips are on foot/cycle/mobility-assist within neighborhoods; public transit is concentrated on district and inter-Habitat trunks.

Advantages:
- humane,
- low transport energy,
- strong local life,
- infrastructure efficient.

Weaknesses:
- insufficient alone for 300k city and industrial freight.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — TRANS-H1

# `Walkable Polycentric Habitats + Local Trunks + Spine Express + Separated Freight`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. Trip hierarchy

### Tier 0 — Walking / mobility aid / small personal cycle-equivalent
Default for many neighborhood trips.

### Tier 1 — Local district transit
Frequent medium-capacity electric guideway/shuttle/tram-like systems within a Habitat.
Exact vehicle technology may vary by district and era.

### Tier 2 — Habitat trunk
High-capacity lines connecting major district nodes and rotation-transfer stations.

### Tier 3 — Spine transfer / inter-Habitat express
Passengers transition between rotating Habitat infrastructure and non-rotating Spine links through engineered transfer hubs.

### Tier 4 — Freight/service network
Cargo uses partially separated time/track/tunnel/service corridors, with direct connections to logistics/industrial nodes.

---

# 2. Why cross-Habitat travel is 60–120 minutes

Door-to-door includes:
- walk/local ride to trunk,
- wait/transfer,
- travel to rotation-transfer node,
- safe rotating/non-rotating transition,
- Spine segment,
- destination transfer,
- local final leg.

The Spine segment itself need not take an hour.

This makes 60–120m plausible while avoiding absurdly slow trains.

---

# 3. Rotation transfer

Hard principle:
Passengers do not casually jump from a 1g rotating city directly into zero-g.

Transfer architecture gradually changes effective gravity through:
- radial movement toward axis,
- controlled rotating interfaces,
- vestibules/platforms,
- restraints/handholds and accessibility assistance where gravity becomes low.

Most ordinary citizens can make the trip with routine familiarity.
Motion sickness remains possible.

Exact mechanics/geometry remain Package H quantitative/map work.

---

# 4. Internal Habitat movement

Each Habitat should support:
- walkable local neighborhoods,
- at least several major transit corridors,
- cross-town trips without entering the Spine,
- local elevators/radial lifts where terrain/deck geometry changes,
- service access separate from public areas when practical.

No single `one train around the ring` solves the whole city.

---

# 5. Freight

Freight categories:
- household/commercial deliveries,
- food and consumables,
- medical,
- industrial components,
- waste/recycling streams,
- strategic cargo,
- hazardous material.

Principles:
- bulk freight avoids peak passenger flows,
- industrial freight can use dedicated service corridors/tracks,
- hazardous cargo has route/time restrictions,
- last-mile neighborhood delivery uses smaller vehicles/lockers/service access,
- not every package is delivered by drone.

---

# 6. Logistics and Kai

Kai's Spine logistics/traffic apprenticeship fits:
- routing,
- cargo flow,
- transfer timing,
- safety separation,
- exception handling,
- emergency rerouting.

Automation supports scheduling but humans remain accountable for exceptional/high-consequence decisions.

---

# 7. Accessibility

Public transit baseline:
- step-free access on primary routes,
- mobility-device securing,
- redundant visual/audio/tactile information,
- assistance at low-g transfer interfaces,
- medical priority routing when necessary.

No disabled citizen should require a private vehicle simply to participate in city life.

---

# 8. Fare / payment

Candidate:
Basic civic transit is either zero-fare at point of use or heavily public-funded with trivial/pass-based pricing.
Reason:
Transit is civic infrastructure and labor-access equalizer in a closed city.

Premium/private transport may exist for:
- charter,
- cargo,
- special comfort,
- specialized scheduling.

Exact fiscal model remains Society/Budget integration.

---

# 9. Traffic peak culture

Peaks exist because:
- schools,
- civic office hours,
- shift changes,
- events.

But staggered industrial shifts reduce one giant rush hour.

3환 has stronger around-the-clock service due to technical/shift work.

---

# 10. Outages

Normal faults may cause:
- a line closure,
- delayed transfer,
- rerouting,
- reduced capacity,
- missed appointments.

A single local transit fault does not paralyze the whole civilization.
Critical cross-Habitat links have redundant routes/modes where physically feasible.

EP014-style Spine delay remains plausible.

---

# 11. Emergency transport

Priority mechanisms may include:
- reserved routing windows,
- local emergency vehicles,
- medical transfer cars,
- rapid access through service corridors,
- temporary passenger-line priority.

Major emergency plans can reverse/hold normal passenger flow.

---

# 12. Personal vehicles

Full-size private cars are not a default urban object.
Small mobility devices/cycles and limited neighborhood service vehicles are plausible.

Reason:
Dense controlled interior city + excellent public transit + limited corridor width makes mass private-car ownership inefficient.

This is not an ideological ban; it is urban form.

---

# 13. Transit privacy / surveillance

Payment/access systems need not create a permanent police-readable movement history.
Operational logs and personal identity data must be separated under CJ-H1 / privacy law.

Safety telemetry can track vehicle occupancy/load without making every passenger's full life queryable by default.

---

# 14. Freight / passenger class divide guardrail

3환's freight density cannot make residents second-class.
Freight separation, acoustic barriers and local routing keep ordinary neighborhoods livable.

---

# 15. Arrival transition

Late Acts may add:
- industrial staging flows,
- personnel movements to orbital craft,
- cargo priority conflicts,
- temporary transit bottlenecks,
- new transfer nodes for landing/orbit operations.

Meridian's civilian network remains in use; it is not converted wholesale into military mission logistics.

---

# 16. Reader-facing transit vocabulary

Keep simple recurring terms:
- local line / local shuttle,
- trunk line,
- Spine transfer,
- transfer hub,
- freight/service corridor.

Avoid dozens of vehicle model names unless a recurring location needs one.
