# DESTINATION — PLANET ENVIRONMENT — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE E / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Real-system anchor:
- Target star: Epsilon Indi A.
- Current real astronomy describes it as a nearby K-type dwarf; exact stellar parameters vary by solution.
- The fictional rocky settlement world is a **future mission-era discovery**, not a claim about a currently confirmed real exoplanet.
- Current real-system giant-planet knowledge does not define the fictional inner rocky world.

Existing Canon:
- one stable rocky habitable candidate exists in future mission surveys.
- it is not a perfect Earth clone.
- no confirmed intelligent/complex alien-life main conflict.
- water/volatile and metal/silicate resource bodies also exist elsewhere in system.
- surface settlement begins small; Meridian remains a living city.

Goal:
Choose a planet that is genuinely rewarding to reach, physically credible around a K dwarf, and difficult enough that `surface = automatic correct future` never becomes true.

---

# DESIGN A — NEAR-EARTH BREATHABLE WORLD

## Core
- ~1g
- ~1 bar N2/O2 atmosphere
- open-air breathing immediately possible
- abundant surface liquid water
- mild temperate climate

### Strength
- strongest emotional arrival payoff
- easiest reader comprehension
- surface civilization can grow quickly

### Weakness
- makes Meridian retention much harder to justify
- very easy to become `Earth 2.0`
- surface-first path becomes objectively dominant

### Biggest Trap
**Arrival solves too much.**

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`

---

# DESIGN B — PRESSURE-COMPATIBLE, OXYGEN-POOR TEMPERATE WORLD

## Core
- roughly Earthlike gravity
- atmosphere near human-compatible total pressure
- abundant liquid water
- temperate climate
- oxygen fraction too low for safe normal long-duration breathing
- CO2/trace composition requires filtering/managed breathing support
- early outdoor work needs respirator/air support, not necessarily a full pressure suit

### Strength
- planet visibly rewards centuries of travel
- surface can be experienced directly rather than only through pressure domes
- still requires infrastructure and medical/environmental adaptation
- prevents `surface is simply nicer Earth`
- allows real outdoor labor, storms, landscapes and open terrain

### Weakness
- atmospheric numbers must be chosen carefully
- long-term atmospheric modification could become a distracting terraforming subplot if overemphasized

### Biggest Trap
`Why not just wear oxygen masks and move everyone down immediately?`
Answer must remain industrial/logistical/housing/ecological capacity, not only breathing.

Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`

---

# DESIGN C — DENSE CO2 / HIGH-PRESSURE GREENHOUSE WORLD

## Core
- liquid water in selected climates
- ~1g gravity
- several-bar atmosphere
- strong CO2/greenhouse burden
- pressure-safe architecture but extensive atmosphere processing required

### Strength
- distinctive
- strong environmental engineering

### Weakness
- pushes novel toward planetary engineering
- first surface scenes require too much technical explanation
- weakens emotional sense of `we reached somewhere habitable`

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`

---

# DESIGN D — COLD / THIN-ATMOSPHERE OCEAN-ICE WORLD

## Core
- lower atmospheric pressure
- cold mean climate
- major ice/water resources
- temperate seasonal/volcanic/coastal zones only

### Strength
- strong visual identity
- keeps orbital/ship habitats highly valuable

### Weakness
- feels like another sealed-base world
- less payoff after 450 years
- can make mission target look poorly chosen

Verdict: `SUPPORTING EDGE CASE ONLY`

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — PLAN-H1

# `Temperate Water-Rich Rocky World + Pressure-Compatible Oxygen-Poor Atmosphere + Moderate Seasons`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

This is a mission-era planet model, not current real astronomical fact.

---

# 1. Orbital Position — WORKING MODEL

Use a future-discovered rocky planet around Epsilon Indi A at approximately:
- semimajor axis: **~0.50–0.54 AU candidate**
- low eccentricity: **~0.02–0.06 candidate**

Reason:
For a K dwarf around roughly quarter-Solar luminosity, this produces near-Earth-order stellar flux.

Working center candidate:
**~0.52 AU.**

At current approximate stellar-mass estimates, orbital year is on the order of **~150–160 Earth days**.

Exact mission-era measured value stays ELASTIC until final quantitative pass.

---

# 2. Planetary Size / Gravity

Working candidate:
- radius: ~0.95–1.03 Earth
- mass: ~0.90–1.05 Earth
- surface gravity: **~0.94–1.01g**

Preferred story center:
**~0.97g.**

Reason:
- different enough to mention occasionally,
- not enough to demand radical human adaptation,
- surface children can plausibly develop normally with monitoring.

Do not use gravity to create superhuman/weak human effects.

---

# 3. Day Length

Candidate:
**~28–31 Earth hours.**

Preferred working value:
**~29.5 h.**

Why:
- clearly not Earth,
- still compatible with human circadian adaptation using lighting/schedule management,
- gives surface settlements a distinct daily rhythm,
- avoids tidally locked-world complexity.

Early settlers may initially keep Meridian 24h civic schedules in some institutions and gradually adopt local-day scheduling.

This becomes Package E/B post-arrival culture work.

---

# 4. Axial Tilt / Seasons

Working candidate:
- obliquity ~10–18°
- preferred center ~14°
- eccentricity low

Result:
- real but moderate seasons,
- shorter seasonal cycle because year is ~150–160 Earth days,
- multiple local seasons per Earth-descended civil year possible.

This creates an actual difference between ship/artificial calendar and surface environmental time.

No extreme global monsoon/ice-age cycle required.

---

# 5. Atmosphere

Preferred physical direction:
**near-Earth total pressure, oxygen-poor, not acutely corrosive/toxic.**

Working candidate at lowland settlement elevations:
- total pressure: ~0.95–1.15 bar
- N2-dominant
- O2: roughly **10–14% candidate**
- CO2: elevated above Earth, but low enough that filtered breathing systems are practical rather than full chemical suits
- argon/trace gases ordinary planetary background

Do NOT freeze exact fractions until atmospheric physiology QA.

Operational consequence:
- unassisted long-duration breathing is unsafe/inefficient,
- short accidental exposure is not instant death,
- outdoor workers use lightweight breathing support / masks / local air systems,
- buildings can operate near ambient pressure, dramatically reducing structural burden compared with vacuum/Mars-like environments.

This is one of the planet's greatest settlement advantages.

---

# 6. Why The Atmosphere Is Not Immediately `Breathable`

Breathability is not a binary astronomy label.

Even if pressure and temperature are friendly, humans need:
- adequate oxygen partial pressure,
- acceptable CO2,
- safe trace gases/aerosols,
- biological hazard verification.

Thus the planet can genuinely be `habitable` while early settlement still requires controlled breathing/indoor air processing.

---

# 7. Water

Working direction:
- stable surface liquid water is abundant,
- ocean/lake/river systems exist,
- permanent ice exists at high latitude/high elevation,
- groundwater accessible in many regions.

Candidate surface distribution:
roughly **40–65% water coverage** rather than an Earth clone.

Preferred visual identity:
several major seas + continental/highland landmasses, not one global ocean.

Water availability lowers long-term bulk-resource pressure but does not eliminate purification/ecology/industrial problems.

---

# 8. Mean Climate

Preferred direction:
**cool-temperate overall, with broad habitable low/mid-latitude zones.**

Working global mean:
~5–12°C candidate.

Preferred first settlement region:
- temperate basin/coastal-inland-sea interface,
- reliable water,
- nearby mineral-rich highlands,
- low flood/tectonic baseline by planetary standards,
- enough flat/stable terrain for landing and industry.

No `perfect California everywhere`.

---

# 9. Weather

Expected ordinary hazards:
- strong frontal storms,
- rain/snow depending latitude/elevation,
- dust/pollen/mineral aerosol where dry,
- coastal storms,
- river flooding,
- lightning.

K-dwarf stellar environment is quieter than many M-dwarf settlement stories, but weather itself remains a serious local operational variable.

No constant lethal radiation storms needed.

---

# 10. Geology

Desired settlement value:
- differentiated crust,
- accessible silicates/metals,
- tectonic/volcanic activity not zero but not globally catastrophic,
- geothermal resources possible in selected zones.

First city should not sit directly atop the richest mine merely for convenience.
Resource transport remains an economic system.

---

# 11. Local Biology / Biosignature — HARD SCOPE LIMIT

Current series must not pivot into alien ecology.

Preferred status:
**no confirmed indigenous complex/macroscopic life.**

Mission surveys may find:
- ambiguous atmospheric disequilibrium,
- simple organic chemistry,
- possible microbial biosignature candidates,
or even ultimately sterile chemistry.

Keep exact answer ELASTIC unless needed.

Early settlement policy assumes uncertainty and applies biosecurity anyway.

Hard rule:
No sentient natives, alien ruins, parasite twist or secret biosphere takeover.

---

# 12. Outdoor Human Experience

PLAN-H1 should allow scenes where settlers can:
- stand under open sky,
- feel wind/rain/cold,
- walk long distances,
- use ordinary rugged clothing plus breathing support,
without full pressure/EVA suits.

This is a major emotional reward after ship life.

But they still need:
- breathing support,
- weather protection,
- communication/navigation,
- environmental monitoring,
- shelter/rescue planning.

---

# 13. Agriculture

Do not assume local soil is immediately farmable.

Early food remains in controlled systems because of:
- unknown/toxic mineral chemistry,
- local biology uncertainty,
- low oxygen/CO2 balance,
- nutrient mismatch,
- biosecurity.

Long-term agriculture may use:
- processed local substrates,
- controlled greenhouses,
- imported/engineered microbiomes,
- gradually tested open/semi-open cultivation.

No instant wheat fields in year one.

---

# 14. Surface Construction

Major advantage over vacuum/thin atmosphere:
- near-ambient-pressure buildings need less pressure differential,
- bulk local materials can provide structure/shielding,
- outdoor robots/people can work with lighter environmental systems.

Still needed:
- clean breathable indoor atmosphere,
- insulation,
- weatherproofing,
- contamination control,
- utilities,
- medical/rescue systems.

---

# 15. Radiation

K-type host and atmosphere/magnetosphere candidate reduce surface radiation burden enough for long-term settlement planning.

Do not require perfect Earth-strength magnetosphere.

Mission-era survey should establish:
- acceptable background exposure in selected sites,
- shelter requirements for rare stellar/space-weather events if any.

Exact magnetic field remains quantitative/elastic.

---

# 16. Surface Site Selection

First permanent foothold should be chosen by multi-factor score:
- water access,
- stable terrain,
- low flood/storm exposure,
- nearby construction feedstocks,
- reasonable mineral/logistics access,
- orbital communication visibility,
- biosecurity confidence,
- landing approach,
- future expansion room.

No single magical `best valley`.

---

# 17. Why Meridian Still Matters

Even on a physically promising world, the surface initially lacks:
- mature housing stock,
- hospitals,
- schools,
- full precision industry,
- redundancy,
- stable local power/thermal networks,
- trained rescue coverage,
- social institutions at 300k scale.

Therefore the planet is a **resource and living-space opportunity**, not an instant replacement civilization.

---

# 18. Planet Naming

Do not lock a flashy exoplanet letter now.
Reader-facing terms can remain:
- 목적행성
- 인디 정착행성
- later a mission/ship-born proper name after Package D/F naming pass.

A local proper name should arise from 450-year culture/arrival planning, not from arbitrary sci-fi syllables.

---

# 19. Post-Arrival Calendar Pressure

A ~29.5h day and ~150–160d orbital year create practical questions:
- work shifts,
- school schedules,
- local dates,
- birthdays/legal age,
- seasonal planning.

Recommended direction:
- Meridian civil time remains legal/interoperable baseline initially,
- surface operations also track local sol/day and season,
- later local civic time evolves.

Do not rewrite every existing age/legal chronology at landing.

---

# 20. Hard Stops

Do not turn PLAN-H1 into:
- breathable Earth clone,
- death-world,
- tidally locked M-dwarf engineering novel,
- alien ecosystem mystery,
- terraforming main plot,
- instant open-air mass migration.
