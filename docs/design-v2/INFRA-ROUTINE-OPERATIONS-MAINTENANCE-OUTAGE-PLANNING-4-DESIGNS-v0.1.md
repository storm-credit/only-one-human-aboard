# INFRA — ROUTINE OPERATIONS / MAINTENANCE / OUTAGE PLANNING — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE C / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Goal:
Define how Meridian remains operational every ordinary day for centuries: maintenance, inspections, spare parts, shutdowns, certification, work permits, incident learning, and handover between shifts.

---

# DESIGN A — HEROIC ENGINEERING CORPS

A highly elite centralized technical corps maintains the whole ship.

Pros: clear responsibility.
Cons: technocratic caste, weak ordinary contractor/co-op economy, story overfocus on one corps.

---

# DESIGN B — FULL MARKET CONTRACTING

Most maintenance is privately/cooperatively contracted under service agreements.

Pros: economic texture.
Cons: critical safety fragmentation and perverse incentives.

---

# DESIGN C — PUBLIC COMMONS OWNERSHIP + MIXED DELIVERY

Critical systems remain public/commons governed; work is performed by public crews, cooperatives and regulated contractors under common certification/work-control rules.

Pros: fits existing economy and Jun.
Cons: coordination complexity.

---

# DESIGN D — PREDICTIVE AUTONOMOUS MAINTENANCE

Sensors/robots/models schedule nearly all work.

Pros: advanced, efficient.
Cons: erases human craft, overtrusts automation, weak legacy adaptation.

Use as strong support layer, not sole model.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — OPS-H1

# `Commons Asset Ownership + Mixed Skilled Workforce + Predictive Maintenance + Formal Work Control`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. Asset Ownership vs Work Delivery

Critical systems such as pressure structure, life-support trunks, major thermal/power/transit infrastructure remain civic/commons assets.

Maintenance may be performed by:
- public utility/operations staff,
- long-term cooperatives,
- specialist contractors,
- certified vendors/fabrication shops.

No contractor gains sovereignty over the system it maintains.

---

# 2. Maintenance Layers

### Routine inspection
- sensors,
- visual checks,
- sampling,
- calibration,
- housekeeping.

### Preventive maintenance
- scheduled replacement,
- lubrication/seals/filters,
- software updates,
- test cycles.

### Predictive maintenance
- anomaly trends,
- vibration/thermal/flow models,
- remaining-life estimates.

### Corrective maintenance
- repair after failure/degradation.

### Major overhaul
- planned long-duration shutdown or staged replacement.

### Life-extension / modernization
- retrofit old systems to newer standards.

---

# 3. Work Orders / Permits

High-risk work may require:
- defined job scope,
- hazard analysis,
- lockout/isolation,
- responsible supervisor,
- tool/material control,
- restoration test,
- handback to operations.

Not every household repair needs bureaucracy.
The rigor scales with hazard.

---

# 4. Shift Handover

Continuous systems use formal handover:
- current state,
- open alarms,
- temporary bypasses,
- active work permits,
- pending tests,
- unusual operating conditions.

Human-readable summaries supplement machine logs.

---

# 5. Temporary Configurations

Real maintenance creates temporary states:
- bypass valve open,
- redundant pump unavailable,
- one transit route closed,
- fire barrier under test,
- backup system carrying load.

These states are tracked explicitly because forgotten temporary configurations are major accident risks.

---

# 6. Spare Parts

Spare strategy tiers:
- common locally fabricated parts,
- repairable modules,
- strategic high-precision spares,
- long-lead specialist materials,
- cannibalizable retired equipment where lawful/safe.

Not every part can be printed on demand.

This is central to arrival-era redundancy conflict.

---

# 7. Fabrication

Meridian can manufacture a great deal:
- structural parts,
- piping,
- electronics to substantial sophistication,
- tools,
- replacement housings,
- many sensors/actuators,
- medical/consumer goods.

But certain high-end components/material processes remain costly, slow, yield-limited or dependent on specialized facilities.

No universal replicator.

---

# 8. Calibration / Metrology

Long-term civilization requires traceable measurement standards.

Critical instruments use:
- calibration schedules,
- reference standards,
- inter-lab comparison,
- error budgets,
- archived calibration history.

This supports Material Culture's strategic precision equipment.

---

# 9. Inspection Independence

The same team that owns schedule/budget pressure should not always be sole safety certifier.

Major systems use some independent inspection/audit function for:
- pressure integrity,
- life safety,
- major electrical/thermal work,
- medical/critical equipment.

Avoid dozens of named agencies; function can exist without reader-facing proper noun.

---

# 10. Deferred Maintenance

Deferred work exists because:
- labor shortage,
- spare scarcity,
- competing shutdown windows,
- budget priority,
- arrival preparation,
- heritage/access complications.

It is tracked as risk, not magically hidden until plot needs disaster.

Jun/Old Quarter conflicts fit this logic.

---

# 11. Outage Planning

Planned outages aim to:
- isolate smallest practical zone,
- provide temporary alternate service,
- schedule around schools/hospitals/industry,
- notify residents,
- define contingency if work overruns.

High-impact work may be staged across multiple short windows instead of one giant shutdown.

---

# 12. Incident Learning

Meaningful failures produce:
- root-cause analysis,
- corrective actions,
- updated inspection intervals,
- training changes,
- design modifications where justified.

No assumption that every incident leads to perfect prevention; organizational learning can be incomplete or contested.

---

# 13. Certification / Skills

Hazardous technical work requires competency/certification appropriate to the task.

Credentials can expire or require continuing practice.
AI tutoring/simulation supports training but does not replace hands-on competence.

---

# 14. Contractors / Co-ops

Jun's contractor/co-op lead role fits a mature ecosystem where:
- public/common infrastructure owners commission work,
- specialist firms/co-ops maintain defined assets,
- contracts include safety/quality requirements,
- public inspectors/owners retain acceptance authority.

This avoids `all engineers work for the state`.

---

# 15. Resident Interface

Residents may see:
- maintenance notices,
- access requests,
- noise windows,
- temporary utility change,
- scaffolding/barriers,
- complaint/compensation channels.

They do not need to understand the full work-control system.

---

# 16. Robotics

Robots assist:
- inspection,
- cleaning,
- repetitive replacement,
- hazardous access,
- inventory handling.

Humans remain important for:
- unusual legacy conditions,
- residents/stakeholder coordination,
- tradeoff decisions,
- troubleshooting uncertain failures.

---

# 17. Inventory / Strategic Reserve

Critical assets maintain reserve policies based on:
- failure consequence,
- fabrication time,
- common-mode risk,
- shelf life,
- arrival usefulness.

Reserve is not simply `one spare for everything`.

Act 5 can turn reserve policy into political conflict when transferring an asset improves settlement bootstrap but reduces Meridian resilience.

---

# 18. Documentation

Every critical asset has:
- current configuration,
- maintenance history,
- known defects,
- temporary modifications,
- manuals/procedures,
- responsible organization.

Legacy documentation can be incomplete in detail but not so bad that civilization operates by folklore.

---

# 19. Staffing Resilience

Critical functions avoid single-expert dependency through:
- cross-training,
- succession,
- documentation,
- apprenticeships,
- simulation/drills.

Rare specialist bottlenecks can still exist, especially during arrival.

---

# 20. Arrival Transition

Arrival increases workload through:
- new fabrication lines,
- asset dismantling/transfer,
- orbital/surface equipment commissioning,
- simultaneous maintenance of aging ship systems,
- training new site crews.

This makes specialist labor a real scarce resource without making everyone conscripted.

---

# PROVISIONAL JUDGMENT

Best model:
**OPS-H1 — Commons Asset Ownership + Mixed Skilled Workforce + Predictive Maintenance + Formal Work Control**

Dependencies:
- exact governance/inspection placement,
- strategic reserve quant,
- map/service access,
- arrival labor and asset transfer.
