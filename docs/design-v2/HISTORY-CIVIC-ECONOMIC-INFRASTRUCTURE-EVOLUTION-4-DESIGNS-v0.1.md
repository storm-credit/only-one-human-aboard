# DESIGN A — FOUNDERS DESIGNED THE MODERN SYSTEM

Modern government/economy largely prewritten before launch.

Strength:
continuity.

Weakness:
450 years of history becomes fake; current institutions feel frozen.

Reject as primary.

---

# DESIGN B — REVOLUTIONARY BREAK

A major revolt overthrows mission command and creates modern civic order.

Strength:
dramatic and memorable.

Weakness:
puts too much on one event and risks `captain dictatorship` history.

Use only if a limited constitutional crisis is useful later.

---

# DESIGN C — GRADUAL CIVILIANIZATION

Mission provisioning/command gradually yields to civilian institutions as ship-born generations become majority.

Strength:
plausible.

Weakness:
can feel bloodless unless punctuated by disputes/reforms.

---

# DESIGN D — GRADUAL CIVILIANIZATION + PUNCTUATED REFORMS

Long gradual transition shaped by recurring practical disputes:
- ration/allocation fairness,
- worker autonomy,
- family/property permanence,
- technical command boundaries,
- maintenance failures,
- Seed/Continuity rights.

Strength:
best fit with A/C present-day systems and HIST-H1.

# ERA 0 — FOUNDING MISSION ECONOMY

## Governance
Launch-era authority combines:
- mission technical command,
- civilian representation,
- medical/safety administration,
- Earth-origin charter rules.

Technical command has broader practical influence because survival systems are new and procedures unsettled.

Hard rule:
Even here, do not require a hereditary or absolute captain-state.

## Economy
High proportion of strategic goods allocated administratively:
- food reserves,
- habitat volume,
- critical tools,
- medical capacity,
- maintenance labor.

Personal property and exchange exist, but much production remains mission-scheduled.

## Labor
Some launch roles carry strong duty commitments tied to mission staffing.
This is closer to expedition/service obligation than the modern free labor market.

## Housing
Initial housing is allocation-heavy and standardized.

---

# ERA 1 — CIVILIANIZATION

As first ship-born generations become adults:
- `temporary mission` assumptions lose social force,
- families expect durable homes and careers,
- local councils/school/community institutions gain power,
- professional civil service separates from mission command.

## Key disputes
- who controls non-emergency resource allocation?
- can technical command override civilian choices outside immediate safety?
- do assigned mission professions pass obligations to descendants?

Long-term answer trends toward:
- no inherited occupational duty,
- elected civic authority over ordinary policy,
- technical command narrowed to safety/operations.

---

# ERA 1–2 — FROM RATION FLOOR TO CIVIC FLOOR

As food/water/power systems stabilize:
- emergency rationing becomes less central to daily life,
- baseline survival provision is institutionalized as a civic guarantee,
- above-baseline consumption/services move increasingly through wages, prices, co-ops and enterprise.

This is the ancestor of:
`Layered Commons + Market City`.

Critical shift:
**survival provision stops being the whole economy without becoming fully privatized.**

---

# ERA 2–3 — MARKET / COOPERATIVE EXPANSION

Growth of:
- shops,
- hospitality,
- professional services,
- small manufacturing,
- arts/media markets,
- cooperatives,
- private firms,
- regulated rentals/property interests.

Strategic commons remain outside ordinary sale:
- air/water capacity,
- pressure structure,
- core utility trunks,
- major strategic reserves.

Cooperatives become especially strong where:
- shared capital is large,
- local labor/community identity matters,
- public infrastructure work is contracted.

This later supports Ella/Jun-type institutions.

---

# ERA 2–4 — LABOR LIBERALIZATION / PROFESSIONALIZATION

Launch duty-roster culture evolves toward:
- paid employment,
- credentials,
- unions/professional associations,
- apprenticeships,
- career changes,
- public incentives for shortage occupations.

Critical-services staffing becomes a planning problem solved by:
- training capacity,
- wages,
- benefits,
- prestige,
- work conditions,
- scholarships,
- limited emergency duties only during actual crises.

No permanent hereditary profession assignment.

---

# ERA 2–4 — HOUSING TENURE EVOLUTION

Initial allocation rights gradually become more durable because families need:
- long-term stability,
- inheritance clarity,
- investment in interiors,
- ability to exchange/move.

Modern result:
- durable occupancy/property interests,
- rentals,
- co-op housing,
- public/basic housing,
while pressure structure/utilities remain commons.

Historical conflict likely includes disputes over whether early assigned quarters were `property` or revocable mission resources.

---

# ERA 3–4 — CIVIC LAW DEEPENING

Seed/Continuity rights conflicts accelerate broader legal maturation.

Institutions develop clearer distinctions among:
- administrative decisions,
- civil/family disputes,
- criminal matters,
- rights/charter appeals.

Modern 시민항소원 can emerge from this longer review tradition rather than appearing fully formed at launch.

---

# ERA 3–5 — PUBLIC SAFETY EVOLUTION

Early mission security functions transition toward modern civic public safety.

Key shift:
`protect mission systems` broad security mandate narrows into:
- ordinary policing/public safety,
- due process,
- specialized infrastructure security,
- emergency technical authority.

Closed-habitat weapon limits increasingly become civilian safety law rather than command discipline.

---

# ERA 1–4 — HABITAT URBANIZATION

The three major rotating structures are not newly built in the middle of voyage; their hard structures are mission-scale infrastructure from early voyage.

What changes is urban occupation and interior use:
- neighborhoods expand/reconfigure,
- schools/markets/public realms deepen,
- service bays are converted where safe,
- original housing is merged/subdivided,
- new local identities emerge.

1환's earliest civic concentration becomes the strongest heritage core.

---

# ERA 4 — OLD QUARTER EMERGENCE

`구시가지` becomes an explicit civic/historical category only after enough time has passed for:
- original street patterns,
- old public buildings,
- early market/community spaces,
- layered repairs
 to be regarded as heritage.

Important:
Much material fabric is replaced over centuries.
Continuity lies in district form/use/history, not 100% original atoms.

---

# ERA 4–5 — MAINTENANCE PROFESSIONALIZATION

As systems age:
- predictive maintenance expands,
- independent inspection becomes more important,
- strategic spares policies formalize,
- calibration/metrology becomes an explicit long-term infrastructure discipline,
- contractor/co-op work-control standards mature.

Possible punctuation:
a serious but contained common-mode maintenance/inspection failure can drive stronger independence/temporary-configuration controls.

Do not make one disaster explain the whole operations system.

---

# ERA 4–6 — WELFARE / SOCIAL INSURANCE MATURATION

A mature long-lived population requires stable systems for:
- disability,
- unemployment transition,
- elder support,
- family support,
- health care,
- temporary housing,
- retirement.

These develop over generations from mission mutual-aid / service guarantees into modern civic social policy.

---

# ERA 5–6 — CLASS STRUCTURE WITHOUT CASTE

As markets mature, inequality becomes visible through:
- housing location/space,
- business ownership,
- savings/assets,
- education/network advantages,
- leisure/custom goods.

But constitutional/common infrastructure prevents legal conversion into:
- private oxygen sovereignty,
- hereditary Habitat caste,
- citizenship purchase.

Political history includes recurring disputes over how much inequality is acceptable above the civic floor.

---

# ERA 6 — AGING INFRASTRUCTURE / HERITAGE CONFLICT

Late mature Meridian increasingly faces:
- old district retrofit cost,
- replacement-versus-preservation disputes,
- strategic spare scarcity,
- whether historical working equipment remains worth maintaining.

This supplies the historical base for Jun/Old Quarter and Material Culture heritage conflicts.

---

# ERA 7 — ARRIVAL PREPARATION ECONOMY

Long-range arrival planning exists earlier, but final-approach years intensify:
- specialist training,
- industrial retooling,
- strategic asset classification,
- orbital/surface logistics,
- relocation pressure,
- tension between ship redundancy and bootstrap equipment.

Modern civic institutions remain in force.
No return to permanent mission dictatorship.
