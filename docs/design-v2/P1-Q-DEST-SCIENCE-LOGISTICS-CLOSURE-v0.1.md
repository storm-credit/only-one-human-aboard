Current real-system sanity anchor checked against NASA Exoplanet Archive / NASA Science:
- Epsilon Indi A is about 3.64 pc / 11.9 ly from Earth,
- K-type main-sequence star,
- representative modern solutions put stellar mass near ~0.75 M☉,
- representative luminosity near ~0.24 L☉,

Hard rule:
**The story's inner temperate rocky settlement world is a future mission-era fictional discovery.**
Do not assign it an existing real exoplanet letter as though current astronomy has confirmed it.

# 4. Atmosphere — Four Models

Package E requires:
- near-Earth-order pressure,
- oxygen-poor,
- outdoor work possible without full pressure EVA suit,
- world not instantly Earth-breathable.

## ATM-A — Altitude-Like Oxygen, ~10–15% O2
Humans could sometimes function without support after acclimatization.

Problem:
- high free oxygen becomes a major biosignature/photochemical question,
- risks making the world too Earthlike,
- makes breathing-support rule inconsistent scene to scene.

Verdict: `REJECT`.

## ATM-B — Near-Earth Total Pressure, Effectively Non-Breathable O2-Poor Air
Selected.

Author-facing band:
- total surface pressure: **~85–110 kPa**
- working common lowland band: **~90–105 kPa**
- O2: **trace-to-low-single-digit fraction; pO2 far below safe human breathing**
- N2/Ar-like inert background dominates,
- CO2 and trace chemistry sufficient for greenhouse/geochemistry but below levels that require pressure protection; exact composition remains C2.

Human consequence:
- **breathing support is mandatory outdoors**,
- no pressure suit required in ordinary weather because ambient total pressure is human-compatible,
- normal gear can be weather/thermal/protective clothing + sealed/positive-flow mask or lightweight hood/rebreather,
- losing breathing support is an acute emergency,
- people can feel wind/rain/snow through non-pressure clothing.

Scientific advantage:
free atmospheric oxygen does not itself force a strong complex-life conclusion.

Verdict: **SELECTED**.

## ATM-C — High-Pressure Nitrogen / Low-O2 Fraction
Could provide adequate pO2 with odd fraction but increases decompression/nitrogen/engineering complications.

Verdict: `REJECT`.

## ATM-D — CO2-Dominant ~1 bar
Pressure compatible but chemically/physiologically harsher and pushes the planet toward a different visual/operational genre.

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`.

# 5. Outdoor Breathing System

Selected baseline:
**Ambient-Pressure Breathing Support, Not Pressure EVA.**

Normal surface worker equipment:
- weather/thermal protective clothing appropriate to local conditions,
- eye/face protection as job/weather requires,
- lightweight sealed or positive-flow breathing mask/hood,
- oxygen supply from tank/rebreather/local certified line depending job,
- CO2/moisture control in closed/semi-closed systems,
- personal atmospheric monitor,
- emergency reserve.

Because local water is abundant, mature settlement can generate oxygen locally by electrolysis/processing; early systems still use imported/seed power/control hardware.

Hard:
- no casual unmasked long outdoor conversations,
- no bulky full pressure suit for every normal surface walk,
- indoor habitats maintain normal human-breathable atmosphere rather than slowly oxygenating the whole planet.

# 9. Hill Sphere / Meridian Parking Orbit

For ~Earth-mass planet at ~0.52 AU around ~0.75 M☉ star, Hill sphere is roughly **~0.8–0.9 million km order**.

Meridian should not sit in a low atmospheric-adjacent orbit.

## Parking options

### PARK-A — low planetary orbit
Advantages:
short transfer.

Problems:
- unnecessary debris/traffic/planet operations risk for a ~10^12 kg-class city,
- makes a giant interstellar city casually skim the planet.

Reject.

### PARK-B — high stable planetocentric staging orbit
Selected.

Author-facing center-distance family:
**~30,000–80,000 km from planet center**, exact radius/inclination chosen after final navigation survey.

Typical orbital period order:
**~0.6–2.6 days** for Earth-mass-like gravity across that band.

Advantages:
- safely far from atmosphere/normal low-orbit traffic,
- well inside Hill sphere,
- practical enough for repeated tug/shuttle transfers,
- supports `Meridian remains city` rather than `planet dock` imagery.

### PARK-C — distant Hill/Lagrange-like staging
Safe but adds transport time/delta-v without story benefit.

Not primary.

Hard:
- exact insertion orbit is selected by late navigation, not predetermined mystical coordinate,
- Meridian never lands.

# 10. Planetary Orbital Transfer Grammar

Because Meridian is in high planetocentric orbit:
- high-orbit ↔ low-orbit transfers cost **few-km/s class** depending exact staging orbit,
- idealized Hohmann-like high-to-low transfer across selected band is roughly **~3–4 km/s one-way total burn order** before operational margins,
- cargo can use slower efficient tugs where schedule permits,
- crew/urgent cargo use higher-thrust vehicles,
- atmospheric entry/aerobraking can reduce descent propellant for surface-bound cargo,
- ascent from near-Earth-gravity surface remains expensive.

Story consequence:
**orbit-first industry is materially easier than mass surface migration.**

Hard:
no cheap elevator/teleportation at arrival unless Canon reopened.

# 11. Resource Families / Heliocentric Access

Do not create one magic asteroid.
Use several surveyed candidates in two broad resource families.

## M — Metal/Silicate Near-System Family
Preferred:
- low inclination,
- relatively low eccentricity,
- neighboring heliocentric orbital-energy regime around the settlement world.

Author-facing planned rendezvous:
- mission delta-v **~1.5–4 km/s one-way class** for selected favorable bodies/windows after leaving local planetary gravity well,
- typical transfer **~20–90 days** depending body/window/vehicle.

## V — Hydrated / Volatile-Rich Family
Because exposed water ice is less stable near ~0.5 AU, early targets can be:
- hydrated minerals,
- volatile-bearing carbonaceous material,
- protected/shadowed/interior volatiles,
- somewhat more distant/eccentric favorable small bodies where necessary.

Author-facing planned rendezvous:
- **~2.5–6 km/s one-way class** for selected favorable targets/windows,
- **~40–150 days** typical transfer order.

Hard:
- not every body is equally accessible every month,
- survey uncertainty/rotation/regolith/composition matter,
- `resource-rich` does not mean `industrial feedstock already delivered`.

Exact asteroid count/names/orbits remain C2.

- accessible water,
- landing corridor,
