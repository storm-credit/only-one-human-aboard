# DESIGN A — CENTRAL CIVIC BROADCAST MODEL
Core: public broadcaster/news service dominates.
Strength: trusted alerts, coherent city information.
Weakness: state-media monotony; weak rumor/influencer culture.
Verdict: `RETAIN PUBLIC-SERVICE LAYER ONLY`.

# DESIGN B — PRIVATE PLATFORM OLIGOPOLY
Core: a few large social/media platforms dominate feeds and advertising.
Strength: familiar virality.
Weakness: copy-paste present internet; corporate sovereignty risk.
Verdict: `ALLOW LARGE SERVICES, NOT TOTAL ECOSYSTEM`.

# DESIGN C — FEDERATED / INTEROPERABLE MEDIA ECOLOGY
Core: private publishers, community channels, creators and social services interoperate around shared identity/messaging standards.
Strength: diverse, resilient, plausible for civic infrastructure.
Weakness: can sound overly technical/idealized.
Verdict: `STRONG BASE IF READER-FACING TERMS STAY SIMPLE`.

# DESIGN D — AI PERSONALIZED INFORMATION LAYER
Core: personal agents summarize/filter nearly all media.
Strength: future-feeling.
Weakness: agents become epistemic sovereigns; common public conversation disappears; manipulation becomes opaque.
Verdict: `USE ASSISTIVE SUMMARIZATION, NOT TOTAL MEDIATION`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — MEDIA-H1

Name: **Public-Service Information + Plural Journalism/Creators + Interoperable Social Layer + Bounded Recommenders**
Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. INFORMATION ECOLOGY
Citizens encounter information through a mix of:
- public-service news/alerts
- private/commercial journalism
- local/district publications
- professional/trade channels
- independent creators/commentators
- social feeds/forums/groups
- private/group messaging
- school/work/institution channels

No single feed equals public reality.

## 2. PUBLIC-SERVICE INFORMATION
A civic public-service layer provides:
- emergency alerts
- election/public-hearing information
- official notices/data access
- basic news/public-interest coverage

Editorial safeguards matter; it is not simply government press office.

Official agencies separately publish primary documents/statements.

## 3. PRIVATE JOURNALISM
Private/co-op/nonprofit newsrooms can investigate:
- government
- business
- institutions
- science
- culture
- neighborhoods

Revenue may mix:
- subscriptions
- advertising/sponsorship
- memberships
- grants

No need to lock exact models yet.

## 4. LOCAL MEDIA
Habitats/districts have local news/community channels, but citizens commonly consume shipwide sources too.

Local media does not equal Habitat nationalist press.

## 5. SOCIAL NETWORKS
Citizens use networked social services for:
- posts
- images/video
- interest communities
- local groups
- event coordination
- creator following
- professional networks

Services can be large and influential without one monopoly owning civic identity.

## 6. INTEROPERABILITY / IDENTITY
Civic legal identity and social-profile identity are separable.

A person may use:
- real-name verified public/professional account
- ordinary named social account
- pseudonymous account where lawful

Platforms can authenticate uniqueness/account ownership without exposing protected civic data publicly.

## 7. PRIVATE MESSAGING
Strong private/group messaging is normal.

Lawful investigative access requires process under CJ-H1.
No general government backdoor as cultural default.

## 8. RECOMMENDATION SYSTEMS
Algorithms/AI recommend:
- news
- entertainment
- friends/communities
- local events

But:
- citizens can access chronological/direct/source feeds
- recommenders are not official truth engines
- no unified social-credit score
- political/public regulation may require transparency/user control for major systems

## 9. PERSONAL AI SUMMARIES
Personal tools may summarize long documents/news, but:
- source links/provenance remain available
- summaries can be wrong/biasing
- protected data is not magically accessible

Do not let personal AI answer the main mystery from hidden databases.

## 10. JOURNALISM ETHICS
Norms/law distinguish:
- public-interest reporting
- privacy
- protected medical/reproductive/Continuity data
- minors
- doxxing/harassment
- corrections
- source protection

Ethics do not eliminate sensationalism.

## 11. OFFICIAL DOCUMENTS
Public laws, budgets, reports and hearings are searchable.

Not all archives are public:
- medical
- Seed provenance
- private communications
- protected family records
remain restricted.

## 14. RE-IDENTIFICATION
Public fragments can include:
- old consent/refusal advocacy trace
- anonymized ethics records with residual metadata
- age/residence/family context

Journalists with strong ethics may refuse to publish names while decentralized users still speculate.

This preserves ID-H1R.

## 15. FALSE CANDIDATES
Several plausible candidate families can be targeted incorrectly.

Consequences:
- harassment
- reputational harm
- school/work pressure
- correction efforts

This motivates Amara's later limited confirmation without making her responsible for public misconduct.

## 16. DOXXING / HARASSMENT
Illegal or sanctionable conduct can include:
- publishing protected addresses/data
- threats/stalking
- credential hacking
- unauthorized medical/provenance disclosure

Publicly inferable facts are more legally/ethically complicated than stolen protected data.

## 17. MISINFORMATION
False claims spread through:
- motivated interpretation
- clipped context
- fake screenshots/media
- outdated documents
- satire mistaken as fact
- deliberate fraud

No `perfect fact-check AI` eliminates this.

## 18. AUTHENTICITY / SYNTHETIC MEDIA
Advanced media generation makes provenance tools common:
- source signatures
- capture/edition history for professional media
- institutional signing

But authenticity metadata proves media provenance, not the truth of every claim.

## 19. CORRECTIONS / REPUTATION
Professional outlets maintain correction histories.
Creators/social users vary widely.

Deletion does not guarantee disappearance because repost/archive copies exist, but privacy law can still govern major services/search indexing.

## 20. SEARCH / ARCHIVES
Public web/social history is searchable to varying degrees.
Old platform migrations create gaps, broken context and metadata loss.

This supports semantic drift and re-identification without requiring conspiracy.

## 21. INFLUENCERS / CELEBRITY
Celebrities/creators exist in:
- sport
- music
- entertainment
- commentary
- education/science communication
- lifestyle

300k population produces recognizable shipwide figures while still supporting subcultures.

## 22. POLITICS
Candidates/advocates use media/social systems.
Political ads/campaign finance rules remain GOV-H1 open detail.

No official feed may silently suppress lawful opposition as default architecture.

## 23. MINORS
Children/youth have stronger privacy and age-sensitive account controls.
School data is not automatically public.

Noah can use media/social life without becoming a public child pundit.

## 24. WORKPLACE MEDIA
Internal collaboration/work channels exist but employers do not automatically own all workers' private social accounts/messages.

## 25. EMERGENCY ALERTS
Emergency alerts use resilient channels that can override ordinary notification priority for genuine hazards.
They are signed/authenticated and distinct from news/recommender systems.

## 26. ARRIVAL MEDIA
As communities split geographically:
- local orbital/surface channels emerge
- communication latency within system is small but nonzero operationally
- shared shipwide media culture persists initially

No immediate separate national internets.

## 27. READER-FACING TERMINOLOGY
Use ordinary terms:
- 뉴스
- 피드
- 메시지

- 생방송/스트림
unless a recurring platform actually earns a proper name.
