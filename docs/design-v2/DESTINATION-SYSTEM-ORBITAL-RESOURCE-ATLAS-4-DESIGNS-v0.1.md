# DESTINATION — SYSTEM / ORBITAL / RESOURCE ATLAS — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE E / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Existing Canon:
- Epsilon Indi A system contains, in future mission-era survey knowledge:
  - one stable rocky habitable candidate,
  - volatile/water-rich small bodies,
  - metal/silicate-rich small bodies.
- local ISRU eventually supplies bulk water/oxygen/feedstocks/metals/silicates/shielding mass.
- Meridian supplies early power/thermal/precision/robotic/medical capital.
- ending begins orbital/asteroid industrial footholds + small surface foothold.

Goal:
Create an operational system geography without exploding scope into dozens of planets/states.

---

# DESIGN A — PLANET-CENTRIC SYSTEM

Almost all useful resources are on/near the settlement planet.

Pros:
- simple,
- keeps focus on surface.

Cons:
- weakens orbital-habitat path,
- deep gravity well makes every industrial choice surface-biased,
- underuses existing Canon resource bodies.

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`

---

# DESIGN B — THREE-NODE RESOURCE TRIANGLE

Three strategic resource environments:
1. settlement planet — long-term gravity/living space/atmosphere/water,
2. volatile-rich small-body cluster — water/chemical feedstocks/reaction mass,
3. metal/silicate-rich accessible bodies — construction/industrial bulk.

A Meridian-connected orbital depot coordinates early flows.

Pros:
- strongest Canon fit,
- explains polycentric ending,
- makes orbital industry independently useful,
- keeps named geography manageable.

Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`

---

# DESIGN C — GIANT-PLANET MOON ECONOMY

Confirmed/future outer giant and moons become major resources.

Pros:
- visually dramatic,
- rich system architecture.

Cons:
- long travel times,
- scope explosion,
- current real giant orbit remains uncertain and distant,
- unnecessary for first decades.

Verdict: `BACKGROUND ONLY`

---

# DESIGN D — DENSE ASTEROID INDUSTRIAL BELT

A large convenient asteroid belt near the planet supplies everything.

Pros:
- easy logistics.

Cons:
- can feel designed for plot convenience,
- `asteroid belt = tightly packed minefield` misconception risk.

Verdict: `USE SELECTIVE SMALL-BODY FAMILIES, NOT DENSE BELT TROPE`

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — SYS-H1

# `Settlement World + Accessible Volatile Cluster + Metal/Silicate Cluster + Meridian-Centered Orbital Bootstrap Network`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

---

# 1. System Story Map

Keep reader-facing system geography small.

Primary strategic nodes:

## P — Settlement World
PLAN-H1 rocky temperate world.
Functions:
- long-term large population living space,
- atmosphere/water,
- surface agriculture potential,
- bulk construction resources,
- eventual cities.

## V — Volatile Resource Family
One or several surveyed small bodies with useful:
- water ice/hydrated minerals,
- carbon/nitrogen-bearing compounds,
- other chemical feedstocks.

They need not be one famous moon.

## M — Metal / Silicate Resource Family
Accessible bodies with useful concentrations of:
- iron/nickel,
- aluminum/magnesium-bearing minerals,
- silicates,
- construction/shielding bulk.

## O — Orbital Bootstrap Node
Initially a depot/worksite near the settlement world or in a dynamically useful nearby orbit.
Functions:
- cargo aggregation,
- propellant/feedstock handling,
- repair,
- staging,
- early fabrication,
- surface transfer.

## Meridian
Remains the largest mature population/industrial/service node at arrival.

---

# 2. Why Multiple Resource Nodes Matter

A surface planet provides huge bulk mass but has a gravity well.

Small bodies provide:
- low escape cost,
- easy excavation for robotics,
- useful feedstocks for orbit-based construction,
- shielding mass without lifting it from the planet.

Therefore:
- surface settlement is not the only rational destination investment,
- orbital industry can grow while Meridian remains inhabited,
- new orbital habitat path has an independent economic basis.

---

# 3. Orbital Distances — PROVISIONAL / AUTHOR-FACING

Do not freeze an over-detailed planetary system before Package H quant.

Useful working architecture:
- settlement world: inner temperate orbit ~0.5 AU order,
- some metal/silicate targets: inner/nearby eccentric small bodies reachable on mission-relevant transfer windows,
- volatile-rich targets: somewhat farther/cooler orbits, potentially ~1 AU-plus order,
- known/future outer giant system remains remote enough to be irrelevant to first-stage bootstrap.

The useful resource bodies are selected partly because their transfer energy/windows are favorable, not merely because they contain material.

---

# 4. Travel-Time Categories

Reader/author should think in broad classes, not arbitrary hours.

### Local orbital
Meridian ↔ nearby depot / planet orbit:
- hours to days depending orbital geometry/vehicle.

### Planet surface transfer
Orbit ↔ surface:
- mission measured in hours to a day including rendezvous/weather/landing logistics, not `elevator ride`.

### Nearby small-body missions
- days to weeks for exceptionally favorable near-space targets,
- weeks to months for many industrial targets.

### Outer-system missions
- many months to years and not important to early series ending.

Exact delta-v/travel matrices remain Package H/quant work.

---

# 5. No Space Elevator At Start

Early settlement does not have:
- mature space elevator,
- planetary mass driver network,
- giant orbital ring.

Initial surface/orbit movement uses reusable powered vehicles appropriate to advanced fusion/electric/chemical hybrid technology as later propulsion subdesign allows.

This preserves gravity-well cost.

---

# 6. Meridian Arrival Position

Meridian cannot simply park beside the planet like a ship at dock.

Arrival sequence should place it into a stable, operationally useful system trajectory/orbit after deceleration and insertion planning.

Preferred strategic direction:
- Meridian remains in space as city/industrial capital,
- smaller transfer craft connect it to planetary orbit/resource nodes,
- final exact orbit waits for system dynamics quant.

Do not place the enormous generation ship in low planetary orbit without thermal/gravity/traffic reasoning.

---

# 7. Orbital Bootstrap Node O

First O-node starts small:
- docking/transfer structures,
- fuel/feedstock tanks,
- maintenance/fabrication modules,
- crew shelter,
- communication/navigation,
- medical emergency capability.

It is not instantly a second city.

Growth path:
`depot → industrial worksite → persistent settlement → possible future habitat nucleus`.

---

# 8. Volatile ISRU

Early products may include:
- water,
- oxygen,
- hydrogen-bearing feedstocks,
- carbon/nitrogen chemical stocks where available,
- radiation shielding mass,
- propellant/reaction-mass precursor depending vehicle technology.

Important:
Mining ice is not enough.
Need:
- prospecting,
- excavation,
- heating/separation,
- purification,
- storage,
- transport,
- power/thermal systems.

---

# 9. Metal / Silicate ISRU

Early use prioritizes low-complexity bulk products:
- shielding,
- aggregate/ceramic/sintered structure,
- simple metals,
- tanks/beams/plates,
- landing pads/roads/surface foundations.

High-purity alloys/electronics remain dependent on Meridian's industrial capital much longer.

No `asteroid rock directly becomes a CPU` shortcut.

---

# 10. Surface ISRU

Planet provides:
- water,
- atmosphere-derived gases,
- rock/soil,
- construction aggregate,
- selected ores.

Early extraction is intentionally near first settlement only where environmental/biosecurity review allows.

Mining and city siting are separate decisions.

---

# 11. Power

Initial new nodes depend on imported/transferred power capital.

Candidate mix over time:
- compact fusion systems,
- local solar as secondary depending site/orbit,
- energy storage,
- eventually locally fabricated expansion.

No assumption that the planet's lower stellar luminosity makes solar useless; it is simply one part of the system.

Large industrial growth remains power + heat-rejection constrained.

---

# 12. Heat Rejection

Orbit:
- radiators remain major industrial assets.

Surface:
- atmosphere/ground/water can help reject/use heat, but local environmental temperature and infrastructure still matter.

Thus some radiator modules that are priceless in orbit may be less valuable on the surface than precision plant or power systems.

Asset transfer debate must distinguish destination.

---

# 13. Precision Capital Ladder

## Tier 1 — import from Meridian
- reactors/control systems,
- semiconductor/sensor equipment,
- precision machine tools,
- medical/biotech systems,
- high-end robotics,
- metrology.

## Tier 2 — local assembly from imported precision components + local bulk

## Tier 3 — local production of increasing precision classes

## Tier 4 — mature independent industrial ecology

Transition takes years to decades, not weeks.

---

# 14. Resource Ownership Principle

Before mature off-ship jurisdictions exist, surveyed resource bodies are not `first miner owns asteroid forever`.

Initial extraction rights should be statutory/common-license based under Arrival Transition law.

Later property/jurisdiction package decides:
- leases,
- cooperative/public operating rights,
- private enterprise rights,
- royalties/taxes,
- environmental constraints.

Hard rule:
No Amara-linked ownership of the system.

---

# 15. Strategic Transfer Logic

A Meridian asset should be evaluated on at least four axes:
1. how much it accelerates a new node,
2. how much ship redundancy is lost,
3. whether local replacement can soon be built,
4. whether the transfer is reversible.

Examples:
- spare precision machine line: high bootstrap / moderate-high redundancy cost,
- raw structural plate: low unique value if local silicate/metal is accessible,
- entire occupied Habitat system: enormous civic cost, bad early transfer,
- specialized radiator: high orbital bootstrap value, potentially serious ship thermal redundancy cost.

---

# 16. New Orbital Habitat Path

A future habitat is not made by copying Meridian overnight.

Required:
- local structural/shielding mass,
- power/thermal systems,
- pressure systems,
- ecology/food loops,
- precision control equipment,
- civic services,
- population willing to move.

Therefore series ending can begin a habitat program without completing it.

---

# 17. Surface vs Orbit Comparative Advantage

## Surface
Advantages:
- gravity,
- vast bulk materials,
- atmosphere/water,
- long-term open expansion.

Costs:
- gravity well,
- weather,
- breathing environment,
- biosafety,
- greenfield infrastructure.

## Orbit/small bodies
Advantages:
- low gravity for bulk materials,
- efficient space construction,
- direct connection to Meridian.

Costs:
- radiation/pressure dependence,
- radiator requirement,
- fully engineered life support.

Neither dominates all tasks.

# 18. Existing Outer Giant

The real Epsilon Indi system's known distant giant planet may exist as astronomical background in the fictional future system.

Current Package E recommendation:
**do not make it an early strategic node.**

Reason:
- far from inner settlement/resource network,
- adds scope without strengthening the core story.

It may appear in maps/astronomy culture later without becoming a plot destination.
