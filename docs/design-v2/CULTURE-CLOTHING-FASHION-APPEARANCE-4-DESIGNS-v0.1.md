# CULTURE CLOTHING / FASHION / APPEARANCE — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design what ordinary citizens wear and how appearance signals identity/status without `everyone in jumpsuits` or Habitat costume-castes.

---

# DESIGN A — FUNCTIONAL SHIP UNIFORM CULTURE
Core: durable standardized garments dominate.
Strength: efficient.
Weakness: implausible for 450-year civilian city; visually sterile.
Verdict: `REJECT AS CIVILIAN DEFAULT / RETAIN WORK UNIFORMS`.

# DESIGN B — EARTH FASHION CONTINUITY
Core: recognizable Earth-derived clothing silhouettes/fashion cycles continue.
Strength: readable, human.
Weakness: can feel 21st-century frozen.
Verdict: `STRONG ANCESTRAL BASE / NEED MATERIAL AND CULTURAL EVOLUTION`.

# DESIGN C — SMART-TEXTILE FUTURISM
Core: adaptive fabrics, wearable interfaces and modular garments dominate.
Strength: future texture.
Weakness: gadget costume; every shirt becomes device.
Verdict: `USE SELECTIVELY`.

# DESIGN D — PLURAL REPAIRABLE FASHION ECOLOGY
Core: Earth-descended silhouettes + ship-manufactured/recycled textiles + real fashion cycles + specialized workwear + optional smart features.
Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — CLO-H1

Name: **Recognizable Civilian Fashion + Closed-Loop Textiles + Specialized Workwear + Selective Wearables**
Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. CIVILIAN BASELINE
Ordinary citizens wear recognizable categories:
- shirts/tops
- trousers/skirts/dresses where preferred
- jackets/layers
- shoes/soft indoor footwear
- athletic wear
- formal/ceremonial clothing

No mandatory civilian jumpsuit aesthetic.

## 2. TEXTILE MATERIALS
Likely common sources:
- regenerated cellulose fibers
- microbial/biopolymer fibers
- recycled synthetic polymers
- locally produced composite textiles
- limited heritage/natural-fiber lines where ecology supports them

Exact fiber catalog waits Package C-M/ecology.

## 3. REPAIR / CIRCULARITY
Garments are:
- repairable
- recyclable
- resellable
- alterable

Visible repair is not automatically poverty-coded, though high-end invisible restoration/custom work can signal wealth.

## 4. FASHION CYCLES
Fashion changes through:
- media
- youth culture
- historic revivals
- material innovations
- arrival-era aesthetics

450 years does not produce one timeless utilitarian style.

## 5. STATUS SIGNALS
Status can show through:
- custom fit
- craftsmanship
- rare heritage fabrics/materials
- designer/artisan work
- subtle wearable tech
- condition/maintenance

Not through formal class uniforms.

## 6. HABITAT DIFFERENCE
Local trends can exist but are porous.
Forbidden:
- 1환 formal suits / 2환 green robes / 3환 industrial clothes as caste shorthand.

## 7. WORKWEAR
Specialized clothing exists for:
- utilities
- clinics
- food service
- manufacturing
- public safety
- transport
- Outer Works

Work garments emphasize practical hazards/identification, not occupational nobility.

## 8. OUTER WORKS
EVA/environmental suits are specialist equipment, not everyday fashion.
Citizens do not casually wear spacesuits through shopping districts.

## 9. FOOTWEAR / ROTATION
Ordinary rotating Habitat gravity permits familiar footwear.
Spine/low-g work may use secure-grip/attachment solutions in appropriate zones.

## 10. POCKETS / CARRY
Personal interfaces and compact daily objects encourage practical integrated carry, but not every garment is a tactical vest.

## 11. WEARABLE INTERFACES
Optional functions may include:
- identity/communication
- health/safety alerts
- access
- display/haptics

No garment is an adult-origin scanner or universal surveillance device.

## 12. FORMAL CLOTHING
Partnership ceremonies, funerals, civic events and professional occasions support formal dress traditions.
These may draw on both heritage and ship-born aesthetics.

## 13. RELIGIOUS/CULTURAL CLOTHING
Protected where safe.
Industrial/EVA zones can require compatible safety adaptations.

## 14. HAIR
Hair length/styles/dye are diverse.
Practical restraints apply in machinery/medical/Outer Works contexts.
No shipwide short-hair norm.

## 15. COSMETICS / BODY ART
Cosmetics, tattoos, piercings and body art can be ordinary.
Material/medical safety standards apply.

## 16. PROSTHETICS / ASSISTIVE DEVICES
Advanced prosthetics/assistive devices may be visually customized rather than hidden by default.
No assumption that disability tech must imitate an unmodified body.

## 17. BODY MODIFICATION
Elective nonmedical modifications may exist within health/safety law, but do not become a cyberpunk enhancement arms race.
Exact scope later MED/Culture integration.

## 18. SCHOOL/YOUTH
Youth fashion and school dress vary. No universal school uniform is required.
Specific schools/programs may use uniforms/workwear where functional.

## 19. SECONDHAND / VINTAGE
Resale, repair, vintage and historical-revival markets are normal.
True Earth-origin garments are rare heritage objects, not everyday vintage.

## 20. ARRIVAL FASHION
Arrival era can popularize:
- practical field-influenced silhouettes
- heritage Meridian motifs
- surface/orbit identity fashion
without instant faction uniforms.

# RED FLAGS
P1-CLO-01: too recognizable = contemporary cosplay.
P1-CLO-02: too smart = gadget overload.
P1-CLO-03: heritage fashion can become ethnic stereotype.
P1-CLO-04: local styles can become Habitat caste coding.
P1-CLO-05: exact textiles need ecology/material dependency.

# CURRENT VERDICT
`CLO-H1 = PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`
Key principle: **Meridian citizens dress like civilians with centuries of fashion history, not crew members trapped in mission uniforms.**
