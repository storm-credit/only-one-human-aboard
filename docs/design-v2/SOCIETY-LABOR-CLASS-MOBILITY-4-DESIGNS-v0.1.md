# DESIGN A — OPEN LABOR MARKET + UNION RIGHTS

Core:
Citizens choose careers/employers; firms/agencies hire through ordinary labor markets. Unions bargain wages/conditions.

Strengths:
- intuitive
- preserves autonomy
- supports career plots
- real bargaining/conflict

Weaknesses:
- critical-service strikes can endanger ship
- scarce professions may be under-supplied
- market wages alone may create extreme premiums

Verdict:
`STRONG BASE / NEED CLOSED-SYSTEM SAFETY RULES`.

---

# DESIGN B — PROFESSIONAL ORDERS / GUILD SYSTEM

Core:
Critical professions are governed by strong professional orders controlling training, certification and discipline.

Strengths:
- plausible for medicine/Outer Works/engineering
- maintains safety competence
- supports apprenticeship

Weaknesses:
- risks hereditary prestige caste
- credential cartels
- guilds could become political sovereigns

Verdict:
`USE FOR SAFETY-CREDENTIAL FUNCTIONS ONLY`.

---

# DESIGN C — WORKPLACE DEMOCRACY / COOPERATIVE LABOR

Core:
Many workplaces use worker councils/cooperative governance.

Strengths:
- fits Ella/Jun
- worker agency
- long-term closed-community logic

Weaknesses:
- ideological monoculture
- slows management decisions
- not every sector/worker wants governance participation

Verdict:
`USE AS PLURAL FORM, NOT UNIVERSAL`.

---

# DESIGN D — CIVIC LABOR ASSIGNMENT

Core:
Government forecasts needed labor and assigns citizens to essential roles/training.

Strengths:
- efficient scarce-skill planning
- robust survival staffing

Weaknesses:
- authoritarian
- kills career autonomy
- AI/planning bureaucracy becomes life allocator
- family/mobility stories become assignment compliance

Verdict:
`REJECT AS NORMAL SYSTEM / RETAIN NARROW EMERGENCY OBLIGATION ONLY`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — LAB-H1

Name:
**Open Occupational Mobility + Plural Worker Power + Safety-Credential Boundaries**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## Principle
Citizens normally choose careers.
The civilization steers scarce skills through:
- education capacity
- scholarships
- apprenticeships
- wage differentials
- relocation support
- public recruitment
rather than forced lifetime assignment.

Critical systems use competence licensing and minimum emergency staffing, not hereditary castes.

---

# 1. EMPLOYMENT ECOLOGY

Citizens may work for:
- public agencies
- private firms
- cooperatives
- nonprofits/associations
- self-employment
- contract/freelance work

Long-term career attachment is common but not mandatory.

---

# 2. HIRING

Hiring can consider:
- skill
- credential
- experience
- schedule
- location
- compensation

Illegal discrimination protections later integrate with rights law.

Origin class cannot lawfully be an employment hierarchy after Reveal.

---

# 3. PROFESSIONAL CREDENTIALS

Required where incompetence creates major harm:
- medicine
- some Reconstruction roles
- structural/Outer Works
- high-risk utility operations
- transport control
- certain legal/public-safety roles

Credentials are:
- earned
- reviewable
- renewable where appropriate
- disciplinable

They are not inherited.
Professional bodies cannot create sovereign citizenship classes.

---

# 4. APPRENTICESHIP / TRAINING

Apprenticeship is important for:
- logistics
- maintenance
- manufacturing
- utilities
- skilled service

Can combine:
- classroom education
- supervised work
- progressive credential
- paid training

Kai fits naturally.

---

# 5. UNIONS / WORKER ASSOCIATIONS

Workers may organize by:
- occupation
- employer
- sector
- cooperative

Rights include:
- collective bargaining
- representation in discipline/grievance
- safety advocacy
- lawful collective action

No single labor federation represents all citizens.

---

# 6. WORKPLACE COUNCILS / CO-OPS

Some workplaces use:
- elected worker committees
- co-op boards
- profit/surplus sharing
- participatory scheduling

Others remain conventional managerial firms/agencies.

Pluralism is deliberate.

---

# 7. STRIKES / CRITICAL SERVICES

Normal sectors:
ordinary strike/bargaining law.

Critical life-safety sectors:
strike right exists but may require:
- notice
- minimum emergency staffing
- safety handover
- rapid mediation/arbitration option

Government cannot classify every inconvenient job `critical` to ban strikes.

Emergency compulsory duty, if ever used:
- narrow
- time-limited
- hazard-specific
- compensated
- reviewable

---

# 8. WORK HOURS

Exact week/calendar waits for Package B.

Direction:
- ordinary full-time work has bounded standard hours
- shift systems common in 24/7 services
- overtime/extra shifts compensated
- hazardous work may use shorter exposure cycles
- remote/hybrid work exists where physically possible

No universal 24/7 duty culture just because it is a spaceship.

---

# 9. SHIFT CULTURE

Important sectors:
- utilities
- healthcare
- transport
- public safety
- manufacturing
- Outer Works
- hospitality

Shift premiums and schedule status can be meaningful class/lifestyle variables.

Amara's worker/shift-lead life fits here.

---

# 10. HAZARDOUS WORK

Compensation may include:
- pay premium
- shorter rotations
- additional leave
- enhanced insurance/disability cover
- earlier pension eligibility

Risk cannot be justified by `mission necessity` without safety investment.

---

# 11. JOB LOSS

Possible causes:
- firm closure
- automation
- misconduct
- health
- relocation
- funding cuts
- arrival transition

Job loss does not erase:
- housing floor
- healthcare
- citizenship

But it can cause:
- lower disposable income
- loss of premium housing affordability
- career/status shock
- retraining
- family move

---

# 12. RETRAINING

Public/employer/co-op systems support retraining where:
- technology changes
- sectors shrink
- arrival skills are needed
- disability changes work capacity

Not every retraining succeeds.
Adults can resist moving into high-demand sectors.

---

# 13. AUTOMATION TRANSITION

When automation removes roles:
- employers/public bodies have notice obligations in major restructures
- unions/workers bargain implementation
- retraining/placement may be funded
- productive gains do not automatically belong only to machine owners without tax/labor consequences

No AI assigns replacement careers.

Exact automation ontology waits for Package C.

---

# 14. CLASS AXES

Class is multidimensional.

Possible axes:
- income
- accumulated wealth
- housing size/location
- job security
- schedule control
- professional prestige
- business ownership
- social networks
- heritage ownership/cultural capital
- access to leisure

No single formal caste label.

---

# 15. LOWER-INCOME LIFE

Likely features:
- civic housing or modest rental/occupancy
- less savings
- more schedule constraint
- longer commute possibility
- fewer premium services
- debt sensitivity

But still:
- safe housing
- basic healthcare
- education
- civic standing

---

# 16. MIDDLE STRATA

Large share of stable skilled/public/service households likely sit here:
- regular wages
- chosen tenure
- hobbies/restaurants
- savings
- career mobility

Amara/Leo household can plausibly be skilled-middle rather than poverty symbol.

---

# 17. PROFESSIONAL / MANAGERIAL ELITE

May have:
- higher income
- desirable housing
- strong networks
- schedule flexibility
- cultural capital

But professional authority is bounded.
A senior engineer/doctor/judge does not own civic sovereignty.

Maren/Tomas may have prestige without extreme wealth.

# 18. BUSINESS / CAPITAL WEALTH

Entrepreneurs/investors can become richer than salaried professionals.

Limits come through:
- taxation
- anti-monopoly
- finite housing
- commons ownership rules
- labor law

No private billionaire can own the air supply.

- wealth

- professional license
