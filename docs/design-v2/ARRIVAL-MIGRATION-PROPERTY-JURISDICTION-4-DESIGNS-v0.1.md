# ARRIVAL — MIGRATION / PROPERTY / JURISDICTION — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE E / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Existing Canon:
- current ~300k citizenship is not erased by destination law.
- Human Settler legacy class creates a translation problem, not sole legitimacy.
- Amara has no sole vote/ownership/governor authority.
- four strategic settlement paths remain real.
- Arrival Transition bodies are temporary/statutory.
- ending is polycentric, not one-state replacement.

Goal:
Define early migration, residency, property, resource rights, representation and family continuity without creating frontier land-grab or Amara sovereignty.

---

# DESIGN A — FIRST-COME FRONTIER PROPERTY

First settlers/miners can claim land/resource bodies by occupation.

Pros:
- simple frontier story.

Fatal weaknesses:
- rewards insider timing,
- privatizes commons built by 300k society,
- makes early technicians accidental aristocracy,
- conflicts with existing economy/property logic.

Verdict: `REJECT`

---

# DESIGN B — TOTAL PUBLIC OWNERSHIP / ASSIGNED SETTLEMENT

All off-ship land, housing and businesses remain state-owned; residents are assigned.

Pros:
- strong coordination.

Weaknesses:
- revives mission allocation culture,
- weakens market/co-op system,
- makes settlement authoritarian.

Verdict: `REJECT AS TOTAL MODEL`

---

# DESIGN C — IMMEDIATE INDEPENDENT COLONIES

Surface/orbital nodes become politically independent as soon as founded.

Pros:
- dramatic politics.

Fatal weaknesses:
- tiny work camps cannot realistically become equal sovereign states immediately,
- scope explosion,
- weakens current 시민기본법 continuity.

Verdict: `REJECT FOR SERIES END ERA`

---

# DESIGN D — TRANSITIONAL COMMONS / MUNICIPALIZATION

Initial off-ship sites operate under shared Arrival Transition law and resource licenses.
As population/institutions mature, they gain local municipal representation, property/tenure markets and eventually more autonomous constitutional arrangements.

Pros:
- strongest fit,
- avoids land grab,
- preserves citizenship continuity,
- leaves future plural politics open.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — JUR-H1

# `Shared Civic Citizenship + Transitional Commons Stewardship + Capacity-Based Migration + Gradual Local Municipal Rights`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

---

# 1. Citizenship Continuity

A citizen does not stop being a Meridian civic person merely by:
- moving to orbit,
- working at a resource node,
- residing on the surface.

Early arrival uses **shared civic citizenship continuity** under the modern legal order while local residence categories develop.

This prevents:
`ship people vs colony people` from becoming instant separate species/states.

---

# 2. Legacy Human Settler Conflict

ALX-H1 remains exactly a schema/jurisdiction translation problem.

Possible practical legacy effects:
- early settlement registration label,
- mission-trust eligibility,
- historical berth/residency class,
- old representation or service wording.

Modern rule:
No literal legacy definition can erase established civic rights.

Amara's classification creates legal urgency and symbolic controversy, not sovereignty.

---

# 3. Migration Eligibility

Early migration cannot be pure money auction or political loyalty test.

Capacity-based system may consider:
- available housing,
- job/role needs,
- medical/environmental suitability,
- family unity/dependency,
- disability accommodation,
- rotation/return options,
- volunteer preference,
- community/service balance.

Hard rule:
No origin category priority.
Natural-Origin status gives Amara no migration preference.

---

# 4. Volunteer Principle

Ordinary civilian permanent relocation is voluntary.

Employers/public services may recruit/offers incentives for off-ship roles.

Narrow emergency deployment obligations can exist for already-certified critical staff under LAB/GOV emergency rules, but cannot become settlement conscription.

---

# 5. Early Worker Rotation vs Permanent Residence

Distinguish:

### Deployment / rotation
Temporary work assignment; primary home remains Meridian.

### Transitional residence
Months/years at off-ship node with some local services.

### Permanent residence
Household intends to make node home and receives durable local tenure/civic rights.

This prevents a 3-month mining rotation from being treated as founding a new nation.

---

# 6. Family Unity

Migration policy must recognize:
- spouses/partners,
- dependent children,
- guardianship,
- elder/dependent relatives,
- disability/care networks.

It cannot guarantee every family moves together immediately if capacity/services differ.

Therefore real conflicts include:
- one partner gets early opportunity,
- child's schooling/health delays move,
- adult children choose different nodes,
- elder parent remains aboard.

Family separation is a real ending cost.

---

# 7. Housing Allocation / Tenure

Early stage:
- scarce off-ship housing is capacity/licensing based,
- tied to actual residency/use,
- not freely hoardable speculative property.

As settlements mature:
- durable residential tenure develops,
- rental/co-op/private/public models can emerge under PROP-H1 principles,
- common pressure/utilities remain nonprivate infrastructure.

No citizen may buy 100 empty surface apartments before they exist.

---

# 8. Surface Land

Early default:
Land is held in **public/common stewardship** during survey and infrastructure buildout.

Permitted private/co-op interests may later include:
- building/use rights,
- long leases,
- commercial sites,
- farms/industrial operating rights.

Underlying planetary sovereignty does not belong to whoever planted the first marker.

This is analogous to Meridian housing distinction between dwelling rights and shared structural commons.

---

# 9. Resource Bodies / Mining Rights

Early extraction uses:
- public/common operating licenses,
- concession/lease terms,
- safety/environmental requirements,
- production reporting,
- fees/royalties/tax where appropriate.

Operators can be:
- public,
- cooperative,
- private firms.

No `discover asteroid = own asteroid forever` rule.

---

# 10. Business

Off-ship private/co-op businesses can exist early in:
- food/service,
- maintenance,
- transport,
- construction,
- hospitality,
- fabrication,
- communications.

But scarce life-support/housing/resource licenses prevent monopolization by simple first arrival.

Market life follows people outward rather than waiting for state-owned colony completion.

---

# 11. Taxes / Public Finance

Early nodes remain dependent on shared civilization capital.

Likely funding mix:
- shipwide taxes/budget,
- user/business fees,
- resource royalties,
- local service charges as population grows.

No expectation that a 200-person surface base is fiscally independent in year one.

Longer term, local budgeting/municipal taxation grows with permanent population.

---

# 12. Representation

Early stage:
Small operational sites receive:
- worker/resident councils,
- formal representation/consultation in transition governance,
- rights/appeal access,
without instantly gaining sovereign veto equivalent to 300k Meridian.

As population becomes permanent:
- local councils/municipal representation formalize,
- representation formula changes according to stable law,
- no permanent rule that founding crews own political privilege.

---

# 13. Courts / Law Enforcement

Initially:
- shared civic law travels with citizens,
- local authorized officials/responders handle immediate matters,
- serious appeals/investigations may return to Meridian institutions,
- remote hearings/network access common.

As sites grow:
- local courts/public-safety functions emerge.

No lawless frontier zone.

---

# 14. Criminal Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction follows combinations of:
- location,
- citizenship,
- operator/license authority,
- victim/offense type,
- common infrastructure rules.

Early statutes should avoid loopholes such as:
`crime on asteroid = no law because nobody owns it`.

Exact conflict rules wait final legal consolidation.

---

# 15. Medical / Reconstruction Jurisdiction

Personhood/medical rights remain consistent across nodes.

However capability differs:
- local clinic may stabilize only,
- advanced care/Reconstruction may require transfer,
- risk consent for remote work matters.

No lower legal personhood because a citizen moved to a frontier node.

---

# 16. Birth / Family Law Off-Ship

Existing family/parenthood law continues.

Off-ship birth approval/capacity considers the node's actual:
- maternity/pediatrics,
- housing,
- food,
- education,
- emergency evacuation,
not `empty land` alone.

Birth at surface does not make child a different species/citizenship class.

---

# 17. Education

Children of permanent residents must have real educational access.

Early small populations may use:
- mixed-age local teaching,
- remote/synchronous support,
- periodic Meridian programs,
- visiting specialists.

A site that cannot support child welfare cannot claim to be family-ready merely to raise population numbers.

---

# 18. Property After Death / Reconstruction

PROP-H1 applies off-ship too.

Death/estate processes remain final enough for stability.
Reconstruction continuity does not automatically unwind settled land/housing/business transfers.

This prevents frontier assets from becoming legally frozen around Reconstruction uncertainty.

---

# 19. Heritage / Export

Earth-origin and major Meridian heritage objects cannot simply be exported/transferred because a wealthy settler wants prestige.

Rules may require:
- provenance review,
- cultural/public-interest assessment,
- conservation conditions,
- temporary loan rather than permanent transfer.

Ordinary personal heirlooms remain personal property absent special heritage designation.

This closes Package C-M arrival dependency.

---

# 20. Genetic Diversity Archive Governance

GA-H1 remains shared strategic/medical heritage.

No one settlement acquires exclusive ownership merely by hosting a duplicate repository.

Use is governed by:
- reproductive medicine,
- diversity planning,
- consent/provenance law,
- shared civic/arrival institutions.

Amara has no key/veto/ownership.

---

# 21. Environmental / Biosecurity Law

Surface/resource licenses can impose:
- quarantine,
- waste standards,
- extraction zones,
- biological release restrictions,
- monitoring.

This is not `planet worship`; it is precaution under unknown ecology and long-term habitability value.

---

# 22. Freedom To Return

Early migration should preserve practical return routes where possible.

A citizen taking a 2-year surface posting should not automatically lose:
- ship citizenship,
- all housing rights,
- family connection,
- career portability.

Permanent housing transfers can still have ordinary property consequences.

This reduces settlement into a choice, not exile.

---

# 23. No Founder Aristocracy

First landing/mining crews receive:
- professional credit,
- perhaps compensation/prestige,
not hereditary political/land sovereignty.

Their children have no special constitutional rank.

---

# 24. Jurisdiction Evolution

### Phase 1 — Transitional Sites
Shared civic law + delegated local operations.

### Phase 2 — Permanent Municipal Nodes
Local elected councils/services under broader shared constitutional framework.

### Phase 3 — Mature Polycentric Civilization
Future constitutional negotiations may create stronger orbital/surface autonomy/federal/polycentric arrangements.

The main series ends before this is fully settled.

---

# 25. Ending Compatibility

At EP230-era ending:
- legal framework is deliberately **incomplete but functional**,
- people can live/work off-ship without losing rights,
- resource operators have licenses,
- early residents have representation/appeal,
- future constitutional settlement remains an open civic project.

This matches `arrival is not a final verdict`.

---

# PROVISIONAL JUDGMENT

Best model:
**JUR-H1 — Shared Civic Citizenship + Transitional Commons Stewardship + Capacity-Based Migration + Gradual Local Municipal Rights**

Hard stops:
- no first-come planetary sovereignty,
- no Amara land/settler key,
- no origin-priority migration,
- no instant independent colonies,
- no settlement conscription,
- no citizenship erasure.
