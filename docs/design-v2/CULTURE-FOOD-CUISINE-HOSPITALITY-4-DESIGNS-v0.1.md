# DESIGN A — UTILITARIAN CLOSED-SYSTEM FOOD

Core:
Most citizens eat optimized nutrient staples; restaurants/luxury are rare.

Strengths:
- resource efficient

Weaknesses:
- implausible for 300k civilization over 450 years
- kills hospitality/culture
- conflicts Ella's role

Verdict: `REJECT AS DAILY DEFAULT / RETAIN EMERGENCY RATIONS`.

---

# DESIGN B — RECONSTRUCTED EARTH CUISINES

Core:
Earth recipes remain primary culinary identity, adapted to available ingredients.

Strengths:
- cultural continuity
- easy emotional hooks

Weaknesses:
- 450 years of ship-born culinary innovation disappears
- authenticity can become frozen ethnicity coding

Verdict: `ESSENTIAL HERITAGE LAYER, NOT WHOLE CUISINE`.

---

# DESIGN C — SHIP-BORN FUSION CUISINE

Core:
Generations of ingredient substitution/fermentation produce distinctive Meridian food culture.

Strengths:
- strong setting identity
- makes ecology tactile

Weaknesses:
- generic `future fusion food` risk
- can erase inherited cuisines

Verdict: `STRONG EVOLUTION LAYER`.

---

# DESIGN D — PLURAL CULINARY ECOLOGY

Core:
Everyday staples are shaped by ship production, while heritage recipes, district restaurants, family foods and new ship-born dishes coexist.

Verdict: `CURRENT BEST BASE`.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — FOOD-H1

Name:
**Closed-Loop Staples + Heritage Recipe Lineages + Ship-Born Adaptation + Real Hospitality Market**

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. DAILY FOOD BASE

Ordinary diets draw from already locked production systems:
- grains/starchy crops
- legumes
- vegetables/greens
- fungi
- microbial/algal protein ingredients
- precision-fermented proteins/fats/flavor compounds
- cultured meat
- limited richer ecological products where sustainable

Citizens do not experience these as `space food`; they are normal ingredients after centuries.

---

# 2. CIVIC FOOD FLOOR

Everyone can obtain nutritionally adequate basic food.

This may include:
- household staples
- school/work meals
- subsidized basic dining options

The floor does not guarantee luxury ingredients, restaurant dining or cultural specialty foods.

---

# 3. HOUSEHOLD COOKING

Homes generally support real cooking at an appropriate compact urban scale.

Typical methods can include:
- induction/electric heating
- pressure/steam cooking
- baking in efficient appliances
- fermentation
- countertop preparation

No open-flame norm in ordinary housing unless later safety design permits narrow cases.

---

# 4. RESTAURANTS / CAFES / FOOD STALLS

A normal market exists for:
- restaurants
- cafés
- bakeries
- bars if culture/law permits
- food stalls/markets
- cooperative dining
- catering

Food businesses compete on:
- flavor
- atmosphere
- location
- service
- cultural identity
- rarity/quality of ingredients

Ella's hospitality/food cooperative fits naturally.

---

# 5. HERITAGE CUISINES

Earth culinary traditions survive through:
- recipes
- family practice
- religious/cultural communities
- restaurants
- archived media

But recipes adapt substantially when original crops/animals/techniques are unavailable or expensive.

`Authentic` becomes a contested cultural claim rather than objective unchanged continuity.

---

# 6. SHIP-BORN DISHES

New dishes emerge from:
- production constraints
- available textures/proteins
- fermentation
- mixed communities
- institutional meals
- district histories

Do not invent dozens now.
Later Character/History design should create only recurring dishes with actual scene value.

---

# 7. PROTEIN CULTURE

Citizens do not necessarily rank microbial/cultured protein as fake.
After centuries it is ordinary food technology.

Cultural distinction can instead center on:
- preparation
- freshness
- texture
- provenance
- artisanal vs mass production
- rare heritage ingredient strains

---

# 8. FRESHNESS / LUXURY

Likely premium categories:
- very fresh specialty produce
- rare heritage cultivars
- labor-intensive artisanal food
- limited resource-intensive ingredients
- authentic historical recipe components
- private dining/service

No need for starvation scarcity to make luxury meaningful.

---

# 9. CULTURED MEAT

Cultured meat can range from common processed/minced forms to more resource/tech-intensive structured cuts.

It is not automatically luxury or novelty.

Exact frequency remains quantitative/ecology dependency.

---

# 10. COFFEE / TEA / CACAO-LIKE HERITAGE CROPS

Some Earth beverage crops or close preserved cultivars may exist in limited production, while many flavor compounds/substitutes are precision-fermented.

This creates plausible tiers:
- everyday brewed analog/blends
- real heritage-crop premium products

Do not lock a specific crop inventory until ecology/culture pass.

---

# 11. ALCOHOL / STIMULANTS

Provisional direction:
Moderate legal adult alcohol/stimulant culture can exist under ordinary health/public-safety regulation.

Fermentation makes alcoholic beverages technically feasible.

No need for prohibition or universal sobriety.
Exact age and social norms wait intimacy/calendar/culture integration.

---

# 12. CHILDREN'S FOOD

Children eat broadly the same cuisine ecology with age-appropriate school/home foods.

School meals can be part of civic education/food floor.

Do not make children consume nutrient paste by default.

---

# 13. FOOD SAFETY

Strong regulation/monitoring due closed ecology:
- contamination tracking
- allergen information
- microbial control
- traceability for production lots

Traceability does not imply every meal is state-surveilled.

---

# 14. ALLERGIES / DIETARY NEEDS

Allergies/intolerances still exist.
Schools/restaurants generally accommodate medically necessary diets.

Religion/ethical diets also exist where feasible.

---

# 15. VEGETARIAN / ETHICAL FOOD

Cultured/fermented proteins alter but do not abolish ethical food identities.
Some citizens may choose diets based on:
- ecology
- tradition
- religion
- taste
- health

No one diet is canonical moral truth.

---

# 16. WATER CULTURE

Water is recycled infrastructure but ordinary citizens are not expected to experience each drink as sewage reclamation.

Conservation etiquette exists without constant disgust/jokes.

Premium beverages differ by ingredients/service, not by buying `cleaner survival water`.

---

# 17. FOOD WASTE

Waste sorting/recovery is culturally normal.

Restaurants/households face incentives/norms against large waste, but food enjoyment is not criminalized.

Food waste becomes input to material/ecological cycles after safe processing.

---

# 18. HABITAT FOOD DIFFERENCE

Habitats can have local specialties because of:
- nearby growers/producers
- immigrant/community history
- restaurant clusters
- district fashion

Forbidden:
- 1환 = refined cuisine
- 2환 = farm food
- 3환 = worker protein

---

# 19. SHIFT FOOD

24/7 workers create:
- late-night meal services
- portable shift foods
- handover cafés/canteens
- breakfast-at-night culture

Amara/medical/transport workers can experience this differently from office/school schedules.

---

# 20. FESTIVAL / CELEBRATION FOOD

Birthdays, partnership ceremonies, religious holidays, civic festivals and memorial gatherings develop special foods.

Exact signature dishes wait Calendar/Religion/History integration.

---

# 21. FOOD + CLASS

Class signals can include:
- frequency of dining out
- premium ingredients
- private dining space
- rare heritage foods
- artisanal objects/service

Basic nutrition remains protected.

---

# 22. FOOD + MEMORY / RECONSTRUCTION

Taste and food routine are emotionally powerful but not metaphysical proof of identity.

Ella can remember an old meal while Noah's tastes changed.
Existing EP008 logic is strengthened.

---

# 23. ARRIVAL

Arrival may eventually introduce:
- new growing capacity
- surface agriculture experiments
- different water/mineral inputs
- biosafety constraints

No immediate abundance or wild alien cuisine.
Early settlers still depend heavily on Meridian food/biotech systems.
