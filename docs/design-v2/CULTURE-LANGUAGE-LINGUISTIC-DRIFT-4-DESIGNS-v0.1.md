# CULTURE LANGUAGE / LINGUISTIC DRIFT — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design how ~300k people communicate after ~450 years of ship-born civilization while preserving:
- multicultural naming,
- institutional legibility,
- historical semantic drift,
- ordinary slang/generation difference,
- reader accessibility.

Must resolve:
- lingua franca
- heritage languages
- translation technology
- literacy
- 450-year drift
- institutional language
- slang/profanity
- names
- Habitat variation without caste dialects
- reader-facing Korean convention.

---

# DESIGN A — PRESERVED MISSION ENGLISH

Core:
Launch-era international English remains the overwhelmingly dominant language, actively standardized through education/media/archives.

Strengths:
- easy
- supports mixed surnames/names
- historical documents legible

Weaknesses:
- 450 years of almost no drift feels artificial
- erases multilingual heritage
- makes semantic-drift plot less textured

Verdict:
`USE AS ANCESTRAL BASE, NOT FROZEN LANGUAGE`.

---

# DESIGN B — FULL MULTILINGUALISM + REAL-TIME TRANSLATION

Core:
Many Earth languages remain socially strong; near-perfect translation makes common speech unnecessary.

Strengths:
- cultural diversity
- future technology

Weaknesses:
- weak reason for a shared ship culture
- translation failures become arbitrary plot knobs
- schools/workplaces become cumbersome
- names/language communities risk enclave caste

Verdict:
`USE TRANSLATION AS SUPPORT, NOT REPLACEMENT FOR COMMON LANGUAGE`.

---

# DESIGN C — SHIP-BORN CREOLE / NEW LANGUAGE

Core:
Centuries of mixed population produce a distinct ship language descended from mission lingua franca plus many substrate languages.

Strengths:
- realistic drift
- strong identity
- ship-born culture feels distinct

Weaknesses:
- author would need to invent/maintain a conlang
- every dialogue risks jargon performance
- current names/institution terms may feel disconnected

Verdict:
`USE SOCIOLINGUISTIC EFFECTS, NOT FULL CONLANG`.

---

# DESIGN D — STANDARD COMMON + HERITAGE ECOLOGY

Core:
A shipwide common language evolves but remains standardized by mass literacy, education, law and archived media. Heritage languages survive at different strengths in families, arts, religion and study. Translation aids reduce barriers.

Strengths:
- plausible 450-year continuity + drift
- multicultural names survive
- common institutions remain legible
- allows generational slang and historical semantic shifts

Weaknesses:
- requires discipline not to make every character multilingual ornamentally
- heritage-language survival rates need historical support

Verdict:
`CURRENT BEST BASE`.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — LNG-H1

Name:
**Evolved Ship Common + Heritage Language Ecology + Translation Assist**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. SHIP COMMON

Meridian has one overwhelmingly practical common spoken/written language used for:
- government
- law
- mixed workplaces
- schools' common curriculum
- shipwide news
- technical cross-Habitat communication

Historical ancestry:
Launch-era international mission English is the strongest lexical/administrative ancestor, but after ~450 years the current Ship Common is not identical to 21st-century English.

Do not invent a full conlang.

---

# 2. WHY IT HAS NOT BECOME UNINTELLIGIBLE

For ~450 years, drift is moderated by:
- universal literacy
- recorded media
- formal education
- archived legal/technical standards
- cross-Habitat mobility
- small total population
- electronic communication
- translation/reference tools

Therefore drift is substantial in:
- pronunciation
- slang
- idiom
- semantic range
- shortened technical terms
- social registers
but grammar/core vocabulary remain historically traceable.

This is analogous to strong standardization pressure, not linguistic stasis.

---

# 3. HERITAGE LANGUAGES

Earth languages survive unevenly through:
- family use
- religious communities
- music/art/literature
- clubs/education
- archival scholarship
- naming traditions

No requirement that every ancestral language remains equally fluent after 450 years.

Common pattern:
- Ship Common native fluency is near-universal.
- Some citizens also speak one/more heritage languages at fluent/conversational/ritual/literary levels.
- Others only retain names, songs, sayings or cultural terms.

---

# 4. TRANSLATION TECHNOLOGY

Translation assist is excellent for surviving languages and archived texts.

Uses:
- subtitles/audio mediation
- historical documents
- specialist research
- heritage-language learning
- cross-community events

Limits:
- nuance/register/jokes remain imperfect
- archaic technical terms require human/contextual expertise
- translation cannot solve semantic category drift automatically

Hard plot compatibility:
A translator can render words but cannot know that an old legal `Human` category maps cleanly to a current civic ontology when institutions themselves changed meaning.

---

# 5. READER-FACING KOREAN

The Korean novel uses a **translation convention**.

Reader-facing Korean does NOT mean characters canonically speak modern Korean in every scene.

Rule:
- ordinary Ship Common dialogue is rendered as natural contemporary Korean prose.
- heritage-language moments are marked only when socially relevant.
- do not create pseudo-foreign syntax to signal futurity.

This supports the locked prose rule against translated-English cadence.

---

# 6. HISTORICAL DOCUMENT REGISTER

Documents from different eras can feel distinct through:
- obsolete category names
- older formal syntax
- changed institutional terminology
- translator/archive notes when necessary

Do not overuse fake archaic prose.

The important drift is conceptual/administrative, not a decoding puzzle.

---

# 7. SEMANTIC DRIFT

High-value world principle:
Words can remain recognizable while their institutional meaning changes.

Examples:
- Human
- Seeded / synthetic-origin cognition
- settler
- continuity
- household/property terms

This directly supports the main reveal architecture without conspiracy.

---

# 8. TECHNICAL REGISTER

Professionals develop compact jargon.

Rules:
- jargon is domain-specific
- specialists do not use it constantly outside work
- one term can have a formal archive form and shorter modern spoken form
- prose explains only when choice/consequence requires it

No universal techno-babble dialect.

---

# 9. LEGAL / ADMINISTRATIVE REGISTER

Formal law preserves older terminology longer than casual speech.

This creates plausible lag:
- current citizens use modern personhood vocabulary
- statutes/forms may retain legacy mappings
- specialists know fragments

But ordinary citizens are not expected to memorize deprecated database schema labels.

---

# 10. GENERATIONAL SPEECH

Age cohorts differ through:
- slang
- shortening
- media references
- politeness/register preferences
- arrival-era vocabulary

Noah at 12 should sound different from Tomas at 62 without becoming caricature.

Over 14 story years, Noah/Kai/Mina's speech matures.

---

# 11. HABITAT VARIATION

1/2/3환 may develop:
- local slang
- accent tendencies
- district references
- occupational loanwords

Hard rule:
No mutually unintelligible Habitat dialects and no language = caste coding.
Cross-commute/mixed families continuously blur differences.

---

# 12. SPINE / OUTER WORKS REGISTER

Spine/logistics/Outer Works professions can share concise safety shorthand.

This is occupational register, not a separate ethnic language.

Kai may naturally acquire more of it as his career advances.

---

# 13. NAMES

Multicultural personal names remain normal because naming traditions persist longer than full everyday language use.

Existing names are compatible:
- Maren Vale
- Ella Vale
- Raul Serra
- Ines Rahman
- Jun Imai
- Amara Okoro
- Leo Ortega
- Kai / Mina Okoro

A surname/name does not prove language fluency, ethnicity purity or residential enclave.

---

# 14. MARRIAGE / CHILD NAMING

Law does not force surname change.
Families may:
- retain names
- choose one parent surname
- combine names
- choose culturally meaningful given names

Exact popularity/conventions remain Package B/F detail.

---

# 15. WRITING SYSTEMS

Ship Common uses a standardized writing system descended from its historical base.

Heritage scripts survive digitally/educationally where languages survive.

Technical signage prioritizes high-legibility standardized symbols/text, often multilingual/icon-supported in safety contexts.

---

# 16. LITERACY

Functional literacy is near universal through public education.

Advanced archive/legal/technical literacy is specialist skill.

This helps explain why ordinary citizens can read public reports but not independently reconstruct legacy provenance architecture.

---

# 17. SLANG

Author-facing slang design should emerge from lived conditions, not generic cyberpunk vocabulary.

Sources:
- rotation/gravity
- transit
- shift work
- habitat geography
- repair culture
- Reconstruction
- arrival countdown
- school/media

Rule:
Create only recurring slang that earns use.
Do not build a dictionary before characters/culture supply need.

---

# 18. PROFANITY / TABOOS

Profanity can preserve old forms and develop new euphemisms/taboos.

Do not make every swear word ship-themed.

Package B religion/culture will later decide taboo clusters.

---

# 19. MEDIA LANGUAGE

Shipwide news tends toward standardized Common.
Local/social media permits more slang, mixed heritage terms and rapid new expressions.

Count Reveal can spread through:
- technical/formal wording
- journalistic simplification
- meme/slang reinterpretation

This helps explain how `Human=1` framing becomes socially explosive without the official report literally saying `real humans = 1`.

---

# 20. EDUCATION

Children learn:
- Ship Common literacy
- exposure to heritage languages/cultures
- optional deeper language study

No child is assigned a language based on ancestry.

Historical document reading may require specialist/archive support even for language descendants.

---

# 21. CLASS

Prestige may attach to:
- polished formal register
- certain heritage-literary fluencies
- elite professional jargon

But no official language caste exists.
Public services must remain accessible in Common and disability-accessible formats.

---

# 22. RECONSTRUCTION / LANGUAGE

A reconstructed citizen may retain older personal speech habits while society has changed during absence, but ordinary few-year gaps do not create separate languages.

Long historical records do not become person-memory backups.

---

# 23. ARRIVAL TRANSITION

New vocabulary develops around:
- orbit/surface work
- new geography
- settlement statuses
- multi-home identity

Different communities may name new places differently before standards settle.

This can create cultural texture without jurisdiction confusion.

---

# 24. RED FLAGS

P1-LNG-01: calling it English too explicitly may make future drift feel shallow.
P1-LNG-02: inventing a named conlang may create unnecessary burden.
P1-LNG-03: translation tech could accidentally solve semantic mystery.
P1-LNG-04: heritage languages could become decorative ethnic tags.
P1-LNG-05: local slang could harden Habitat stereotypes.
P1-LNG-06: exact history of language change depends on Package D.
P1-LNG-07: exact slang/naming inventory depends on Package F/B later blocks.
