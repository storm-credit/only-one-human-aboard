# SOCIETY DEATH / FINALITY / FUNERAL / MOURNING — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Parents:
- Reconstruction Canon v1
- MED-H1
- PROP-H1 estate/finality candidate
- FAM-H1 family/next-of-kin candidate
- MC-H1 material culture/heirlooms

Purpose:
Design how Meridian handles:
- medical death
- Reconstruction possibility
- legal finality
- end-of-life choice
- body disposition in a closed material cycle
- funerals/memorials
- mourning
- digital traces/memorial representations
- inheritance timing
without making death either meaningless or grotesquely utilitarian.

---

# DESIGN A — RECONSTRUCTION-FIRST SUSPENDED FINALITY

Core:
When recovery might be possible, death/funeral/estate finality is delayed until Reconstruction assessment is complete.

Strengths:
- logical with technology
- protects recoverable persons
- clean property status

Weaknesses:
- can freeze grief and estates too long
- makes Reconstruction culturally compulsory
- implies every death is an engineering queue

Verdict:
`USE ONLY FOR DEFINED RECOVERABLE CASES`.

---

# DESIGN B — BIOLOGICAL DEATH + SEPARATE LEGAL CONTINUITY STATUS

Core:
Medicine can declare biological/clinical death, while law separately records whether a viable Reconstruction/continuity pathway remains active.

Strengths:
- precise
- allows families to name loss without prematurely ending legal continuity
- fits existing Neural Anchor/Recovery Map rules
- supports staged estate management

Weaknesses:
- dual status can confuse readers
- bureaucracy risk

Verdict:
`STRONG BASE`.

---

# DESIGN C — RAPID FINALITY / SOCIAL CLOSURE

Core:
Society prioritizes prompt legal and emotional finality; Reconstruction decisions must happen within short windows.

Strengths:
- avoids endless limbo
- keeps death culturally strong

Weaknesses:
- medically arbitrary
- could sacrifice plausible recovery
- harsh in uncertain cases

Verdict:
`USE AS PRESSURE FOR TIMELY REVIEW, NOT HARD SHORT CLOCK`.

---

# DESIGN D — CONTINUITY-AS-LEGACY CULTURE

Core:
Even after final death, sophisticated memorial avatars/recorded personas continue social presence.

Strengths:
- futuristic mourning texture
- rich memory culture

Weaknesses:
- blurs personhood premise
- risks executable-soul-file confusion
- can make death emotionally reversible

Verdict:
`REJECT PERSON-LIKE MEMORIAL AVATARS AS DEFAULT / RETAIN NONPERSONAL ARCHIVES`.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — DEATH-H1

Name:
**Clinical Death + Continuity Review + Bounded Legal Finality + Material Return + Plural Mourning**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## Principle
Meridian distinguishes four questions:
1. Is the body/brain clinically alive?
2. Is there a viable lawful Reconstruction pathway under existing Canon?
3. Is legal continuity still pending or finalized?
4. How do family/community mourn and handle remains/property?

These questions are related but not identical.

Death remains real because no record can restore a person without the required surviving biological/continuity substrate.

---

# 1. CLINICAL DEATH

Medical declaration follows established neurological/circulatory criteria appropriate to advanced medicine.

Some catastrophic cases can be clinically dead while a narrow Reconstruction pathway is still being assessed/attempted under existing technology.

Hard rule:
`clinical death` does not imply an executable copy exists.

---

# 2. CONTINUITY / RECONSTRUCTION REVIEW STATUS

Where relevant, a separate status records whether:
- viable Neural Anchor remains
- appropriate Recovery Map exists
- Reconstruction is medically/legal feasible
- consent/preference requirements permit proceeding

This status applies only to cases that actually meet threshold conditions.
Most ordinary deaths do not become long Reconstruction investigations.

---

# 3. LEGAL FINALITY

Legal finality is reached when:
- no viable continuation pathway exists, or
- lawful review concludes Reconstruction will not proceed, or
- a competent/valid choice refuses it where applicable, or
- a defined recovery process fails/is discontinued under lawful standards.

Exact time periods remain ELASTIC pending medical/procedural quantification.

Hard rule:
The mere existence of old scans cannot keep a person legally alive forever.

---

# 4. PENDING PERIOD

Before finality, law can protect:
- dependents
- housing
- business continuity
- urgent bills
- medical decision process

through temporary administrators/proxies under PROP-H1.

This prevents both:
- instant estate liquidation,
- indefinite frozen property.

---

# 5. CHOICE NOT TO PURSUE RECONSTRUCTION

A competent citizen may make legally meaningful end-of-life/Reconstruction preferences subject to existing document/formality rules.

Possible valid choices can include:
- decline aggressive Reconstruction in specified circumstances
- designate proxies
- define treatment goals

Hard rule:
Not every draft/note has binding force.
PR-H1 remains explicitly nonbinding/unsubmitted.

---

# 6. ASSISTED DYING / MEDICAL AID IN DYING — PROVISIONAL

Recommended world direction:
A tightly regulated medical aid-in-dying pathway may exist for competent adults with severe irreversible suffering/terminal or comparably grave medical circumstances after safeguards.

Why include provisionally:
Advanced medicine and long healthspan do not remove bodily autonomy/end-of-life ethics.

Safeguards require later exact law:
- decisional capacity
- voluntary repeated request
- independent clinical review
- alternatives/palliative care offered
- coercion screening
- waiting/review appropriate to urgency

Hard rule:
Disability alone is not sufficient justification and social/resource pressure cannot substitute for voluntary choice.

Status:
`PROVISIONAL / REQUIRES FINAL SOCIETY RED TEAM`.

---

# 7. PALLIATIVE / HOSPICE CARE

Hospice-style care exists in:
- home
- hospital
- dedicated care settings

Focus:
- comfort
- family/community presence
- ritual preference
- privacy
- practical planning

Choosing palliative care is not treated as technological failure.

---

# 8. BODY DISPOSITION IN A CLOSED SHIP

Permanent Earth-style land burial at scale is physically impractical.

Meridian therefore supports several culturally acceptable paths after required medical/legal release:

## A. Thermal reduction / cremation-like process
Remains can be returned to material cycle under respectful controlled practice.

## B. Alkaline/biological reduction or equivalent controlled return
A lower-energy/material-recovery method may exist.

## C. Temporary ceremonial preservation / viewing
Possible before final disposition.

## D. Small retained memorial material
Families may retain a bounded amount of ash/mineralized material/object memorial where safe.

Hard rule:
Resource recovery does not mean bodies are casually processed as anonymous industrial feedstock.
Ritual/legal separation and consent matter.

---

# 9. MATERIAL RETURN

After respectful disposition, recovered simple materials may re-enter civic material cycles.

Cultural meaning varies:
- some families value literal material return to Meridian
- some find the idea uncomfortable and choose processes minimizing visible association

No single ecological doctrine is mandatory beyond physical safety.

---

# 10. FUNERALS / MEMORIAL CEREMONIES

No single universal funeral form.

Possible layers:
- family/private gathering
- religious ceremony
- secular civic memorial
- occupational/community remembrance
- later anniversary/remembrance event

Package B religion/ritual later defines cultural variants.

---

# 11. PUBLIC / CIVIC MEMORIALS

Reserved for genuinely public significance; not every citizen becomes a civic monument.

May commemorate:
- major accidents
- founders/early generations
- public servants/artists
- collective crises

Guardrail:
Do not turn founder memory into sacred chosen-lineage mythology.

---

# 12. DIGITAL / ARCHIVAL REMAINS

After death, a citizen may leave:
- messages
- recordings
- photos/video
- writings
- creative work
- legally preserved personal archive

These are records of a person, not the person.

Hard rule:
No memorial chatbot/avatar is legally or narratively treated as continuation of the dead person's consciousness by default.

If simulation exists as a media product, it must be clearly nonperson/derivative and cannot override estate/identity law.

---

# 13. POSTHUMOUS PRIVACY

Privacy does not instantly vanish at death.

Rules may protect:
- medical records
- private communications
- reproductive/Seed provenance
- sensitive family archives

Access can vary by:
- will/consent
- family interest
- historical/public interest
- court order
- time elapsed

This is essential to Count/legacy-record fairness.

---

# 14. ESTATE / INHERITANCE TRIGGER

Full estate distribution normally follows legal finality rather than mere first clinical declaration when a viable continuation pathway remains pending.

PROP-H1 governs assets.

No automatic rollback after lawful final distribution except specific wrongful/fraud relief.

---

# 15. DEATH OF A RECONSTRUCTED PERSON

A reconstructed citizen later dies under the same ordinary personhood rules as anyone else.

No special `second death is more real` category.

Their prior Reconstruction history may affect available medical data but not civic worth.

---

# 16. GRIEF AFTER RECONSTRUCTION

Families can experience ambiguous grief even when Reconstruction succeeds because:
- time passed
- memories/functions changed
- relationships continued without them

Society may recognize counseling/support needs without declaring the returned person a replacement/fake.

This supports Ella-family dynamics.

---

# 17. FAILED RECONSTRUCTION

If Reconstruction is attempted but fails to produce a viable continuing citizen under medical/legal criteria:
- legal finality follows defined review
- family receives ordinary bereavement/estate support
- no hidden partial copy is treated as person unless Canon separately permits, which it currently does not.

---

# 18. MISSING PERSON / NO BODY

Closed ship still has rare cases where remains are unavailable/inaccessible.

Law can declare death/finality after evidence and process.

No body is required for legal death if evidence is overwhelming.

Outer Works incidents are a plausible context, but not common spectacle.

---

# 19. MASS-CASUALTY INCIDENTS

Public systems need:
- identification
- family notification
- continuity/Reconstruction triage where medically relevant
- remains handling
- public memorial response
- estate/benefit support

Hard rule:
No giant disaster is required by main plot merely because this infrastructure exists.

---

# 20. CHILD DEATH

Rare but possible; advanced medicine does not make it impossible.

Family consent/decision standards differ appropriately from competent adults.

Do not use child death casually as worldbuilding proof of seriousness.

---

# 21. SUICIDE / SELF-HARM — WORLD DESIGN BOUNDARY

The society has prevention, crisis mental healthcare and bereavement support.

Suicide is not romanticized as a culturally normal solution to resource scarcity or identity conflict.

A death by suicide does not invalidate civic personhood or ordinary estate/funeral rights.

Exact clinical prevention detail need not be plot-centered.

---

# 22. OCCUPATIONAL DEATH / COMPENSATION

Work-related deaths can trigger:
- survivor/dependent benefits
- investigation
- safety review
- employer/public liability
- occupational memorial where culturally appropriate

Hazardous-work compensation does not purchase consent to negligent safety.

---

# 23. DEATH CERTIFICATES / RECORDS

Author-facing status should distinguish at minimum:
- clinical death
- continuation review pending where relevant
- legal finality

Reader-facing prose need not recite bureaucratic codes unless plot-relevant.

---

# 24. RELIGIOUS / PHILOSOPHICAL VARIATION

Beliefs may differ over:
- body treatment
- Reconstruction
- personhood continuity
- material return
- memorial practices

Law protects reasonable ritual choices within safety/material constraints.

Package B will design actual communities/rituals.

---

# 25. EARTH / SHIP-BORN MEMORY

A generation ship may develop powerful memorial traditions around:
- departure generations
- voyage deaths
- major ship crises
- people who never saw a planet

Arrival may change mourning language/place:
some citizens may choose future surface/orbital memorial forms.

Do not make planetary burial automatically more authentic than ship memorials.

---

# 26. MATERIAL CULTURE / HEIRLOOMS

Objects may become memorial anchors because permanent graves are limited.

Examples:
- repaired household object
- instrument
- tool
- book
- clothing item
- small physical memorial

This supports MC-H1 without turning every heirloom into clue/relic.

---

# 27. ECONOMIC SUPPORT AFTER DEATH

Dependents may receive:
- estate assets
- survivor/pension benefits
- temporary income/housing support
- workplace compensation where applicable

Death should not instantly turn dependents destitute because the civic floor persists.

---

# 28. ARRIVAL TRANSITION

New questions later Package E:
- where remains are processed when family members live across ship/orbit/surface
- whether surface burial/biological memorial becomes possible
- cross-jurisdiction death registration
- estate/inheritance across locations
- transport of remains/material memorials

No automatic shift to Earth-style cemetery culture.

---

# 29. CHARACTER FIT

Ella:
Her Reconstruction does not imply family completed an ordinary estate/funeral and then magically reversed it unless separately designed. Managed continuity status remains safer fit.

Maren:
PR-H1 concerns treatment choice/ethics, not secret murder.

Tomas/Amara's parents:
Older age creates realistic future planning/retirement/advance-directive texture without requiring deaths in main story.

No core character death is newly mandated.

---

# 30. RED FLAGS

P1-DEATH-01: dual clinical/legal status can become jargon-heavy.
P1-DEATH-02: exact Reconstruction finality threshold needs medical/Continuity integration.
P1-DEATH-03: assisted-dying law could create a second controversial theme if overemphasized.
P1-DEATH-04: body material return could feel cold if ritual separation is weak.
P1-DEATH-05: memorial simulation could blur ontology if not tightly bounded.
P1-DEATH-06: actual religious/ritual diversity belongs Package B.
P1-DEATH-07: post-arrival burial/property rules open.

---

# CURRENT VERDICT

`DEATH-H1 = PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

Key principle:
**Clinical death, Reconstruction possibility, legal finality, mourning and property finality are connected but distinct.**

Promotion blockers:
- hostile Red Team
- exact Continuity/finality procedure
- assisted-dying scope decision during integrated Society review
- Package B religion/ritual
- Package E arrival death/property law
