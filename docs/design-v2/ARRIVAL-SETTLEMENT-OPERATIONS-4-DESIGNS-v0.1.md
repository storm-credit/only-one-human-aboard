# ARRIVAL — SETTLEMENT OPERATIONS — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE E / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Existing Canon:
- insertion occurs around EP216.
- ending around ~+1 year after insertion.
- Meridian remains a living city.
- orbital/asteroid industrial footholds begin.
- small surface foothold begins.
- future new-habitat program begins but is not mature.
- no instant colony.
- no free third way.

Goal:
Define what physically happens before and after system insertion, and what population/industry scale is credible at +1y, +5y, +10y, +30y.

---

# DESIGN A — SURFACE-FIRST LANDING

Most available bootstrap capital and early settlers go directly to PLAN-H1 surface.

Pros:
- emotionally direct,
- simple narrative.

Cons:
- wastes orbital/small-body comparative advantage,
- makes surface path look preselected,
- increases dependence on gravity-well transport.

Verdict: `REJECT AS SOLE STRATEGY`

---

# DESIGN B — ORBIT-FIRST INDUSTRIAL BASE

First years focus almost entirely on orbit/resource nodes; surface settlement waits.

Pros:
- efficient industrial bootstrap,
- protects Meridian.

Cons:
- delays emotional payoff of reaching the world,
- risks turning arrival into engineering-only ending.

Verdict: `STRONG COMPONENT, NOT SOLE STRATEGY`

---

# DESIGN C — MASS EVACUATION / DISTRIBUTED COLONIZATION

Immediately split large population among ship/orbit/surface.

Pros:
- dramatic transformation.

Fatal weaknesses:
- no housing/services/industry exist for tens of thousands immediately,
- violates ending scale,
- trivializes migration logistics.

Verdict: `REJECT`

---

# DESIGN D — STAGED POLYCENTRIC BOOTSTRAP

Meridian remains principal city while:
- orbital depot/industry comes online,
- robotic resource extraction starts,
- small crewed surface foothold begins,
- settlement capacity expands according to actual power/housing/service growth.

Pros:
- strongest Canon/ending fit,
- keeps multiple strategic paths real,
- gives visible arrival reward without false instant civilization.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — ARR-H1

# `Meridian Retained + Orbit/ISRU First-Mover + Small Surface Foothold + Capacity-Gated Migration`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

---

# 1. T-14 to T-10 — Story Opening Arrival Context

Already in long final approach/deceleration era.

Work ongoing:
- destination observations refined,
- resource target catalog improved,
- settlement equipment design,
- training cohorts,
- industrial reserve classification,
- legal schema reconciliation planning.

Ordinary citizens still live normal lives; arrival is future pressure, not daily emergency.

---

# 2. Around T-10±1 — Final Approach Certification Threshold

Matches ATL-H1 Canon.

Technical/legal readiness crosses threshold where:
- intended insertion trajectory is sufficiently committed,
- destination survey confidence high enough,
- major asset/industrial choices become path-dependent,
- arrival jurisdiction mapping can no longer remain academic.

This makes transition law binding without Amara activating anything.

---

# 3. T-8 to T-3 — Bootstrap Manufacturing / Training

Major activities:
- preassemble detachable depot/industrial modules,
- fabricate surface habitat/utility kits,
- train landing/resource/field medical crews,
- qualify new orbital craft and robotics,
- stage genetic/agricultural libraries,
- expand simulation/mission rehearsals,
- select candidate surface sites,
- debate strategic equipment transfer.

Key tension:
Every duplicate shipped outward is one less reserve or one more manufacturing burden aboard Meridian.

---

# 4. T-3 to Insertion — Final Staging

No mass migration yet.

Focus:
- final remote sensing,
- probe/robot deployment where trajectory permits,
- crew rosters,
- cargo sequence,
- emergency fallback plans,
- target-orbit navigation,
- surface weather/landing confirmation.

Some autonomous scouts may precede crewed arrival by limited time depending trajectory, but do not build a hidden mature colony before Meridian arrives.

---

# 5. Insertion / Arrival Week 0

The accomplishment is not `everyone landed`.

Immediate priorities:
1. confirm Meridian stable post-deceleration/insertion configuration,
2. verify communication/navigation network,
3. update object ephemerides/resource target certainty,
4. deploy/commission nearby orbital staging assets,
5. reassess surface atmosphere/weather/biosecurity with in-system data,
6. check ship maintenance debt accumulated during final approach.

Arrival itself creates maintenance work.

---

# 6. Month 0–3 — Robotic / Orbital First Operations

Likely achievements:
- first stable orbital depot/worksite modules,
- robotic prospecting/mining trial,
- surface robotic weather/geology/biosecurity stations,
- uncrewed cargo demonstrations,
- first high-resolution landing-zone validation,
- limited transferred power/precision assets commissioned.

Population off Meridian remains tiny relative to 300k.

---

# 7. Month 3–6 — First Persistent Crewed Nodes

Possible scale candidates:

## Orbital/resource nodes
- dozens to low hundreds permanently rotating through early work sites.

## Surface
- first persistent crew: dozens to perhaps low hundreds depending safety confidence.

They need:
- medical evacuation plan,
- breathing support,
- clean habitat air,
- power,
- water treatment,
- controlled food systems,
- communication,
- refuge/rescue.

No families required in the first crew if social policy chooses occupational pioneers first, but family access cannot remain permanently prohibited.

---

# 8. Month 6–12 — Series Ending Scale

Target ending state should remain modest but irreversible.

### Meridian
- still home to overwhelming majority of ~300k,
- normal schools, homes, shops and government continue,
- some redundancy/industrial margin has been surrendered,
- some old-city/asset losses are now physically irreversible.

### Orbital / small-body network
- persistent depot/industrial foothold,
- first regular resource deliveries or validated production streams,
- crew population perhaps hundreds order across sites, not a city.

### Surface
- small permanent foothold,
- perhaps hundreds-order total capacity/resident/rotating staff at most as working candidate,
- first ordinary civic functions appear alongside technical camp functions,
- no large city.

### New habitat program
- site/concept/resources/industrial plan committed,
- perhaps first structural demonstrators,
- no mature habitat population.

Hard rule:
Do not make the final chapter secretly take place in a completed second civilization.

---

# 9. +1 to +5 Years — Early Expansion Reference

Not necessarily shown in main series, but useful for setting closure.

Potential range:
- orbital/resource workforce grows to low thousands if industry proves reliable,
- surface settlement grows from hundreds to several thousand,
- controlled agriculture and local-material construction expand,
- local clinics/schools/family housing begin becoming permanent,
- first off-ship-born children possible only after medical/legal capacity is deliberately established,
- Meridian remains dominant population center.

Migration remains application/capacity-driven.

---

# 10. +5 to +10 Years — Local Bulk Industry Takes Hold

Potential:
- local water/oxygen/construction mass significant,
- surface population perhaps several-thousand to low-tens-thousands depending chosen path,
- orbital industry can build larger structures from local mass,
- more precision assembly moves outward,
- Meridian dependence remains strongest for specialized medicine/high-end capital/knowledge networks.

Exact population curve depends strategic path and must not be locked before migration/economy quant.

---

# 11. +10 to +30 Years — True Polycentric Civilization

Possible mature direction:
- Meridian remains a major city,
- surface has one or more true towns/cities,
- orbital/resource settlements are permanent communities,
- new habitat may become habitable,
- citizenship/jurisdiction becomes genuinely multi-polity or multi-municipal within a shared constitutional framework.

The main series does not need to depict this fully.

---

# 12. Surface Foothold Functional Order

Recommended sequence:

1. weather/geology/biosecurity instruments
2. landing/transport pad and navigation
3. power + backup
4. breathable shelter/refuge
5. water capture/treatment/storage
6. communication
7. medical stabilization
8. waste treatment
9. controlled food reserve/agriculture pilot
10. workshop/repair
11. expanded housing
12. civic/community spaces
13. permanent school/family services

A ceremonial flag does not precede breathable shelter in operational priority, though ceremony may occur after safe arrival.

---

# 13. Surface Housing

First habitat design may exploit PLAN-H1 near-ambient pressure:
- lighter pressure differential than vacuum habitats,
- local shielding/structure,
- sealed clean breathable interior,
- airlocks focused on contamination/air chemistry more than huge pressure difference.

Later buildings can become more architectural/city-like while retaining controlled air.

No instant suburban sprawl.

---

# 14. Surface Food

Early:
- Meridian/imported preserved food,
- controlled hydro/aeroponics,
- compact fermentation/cultured protein systems.

Then:
- processed local water/substrate,
- scaled controlled agriculture,
- gradually validated local environmental interfaces.

No assumption that the first settlers eat local wild plants/animals.

---

# 15. Surface Medicine

Initial site needs:
- stabilization,
- surgery/trauma capability appropriate to crew,
- pharmacy,
- environmental medicine,
- evacuation to Meridian/orbital advanced care.

Full Reconstruction capacity does not need to be duplicated immediately.

This creates real consequences for early off-ship risk.

---

# 16. Rescue / Failure

Early nodes have longer rescue chains and lower redundancy than Meridian.

Possible policies:
- weather launch windows,
- standby transport,
- emergency caches,
- refuge modules,
- buddy/site rules,
- medical evacuation thresholds.

Do not make pioneering risk zero.

---

# 17. Family Migration

Do not treat first settlers as disposable single workers forever.

Longer-term settlement must provide:
- family housing,
- schools,
- maternity/pediatric care,
- disability access,
- social spaces,
- work diversity.

A site is not a true civilian settlement merely because technicians can survive there.

---

# 18. Birth Capacity Off-Ship

New off-ship births do not instantly add unlimited capacity.

Each node needs sustainable:
- housing,
- food,
- medicine,
- education,
- thermal/power,
- caregiver/support capacity.

As local capacity grows, total civilization birth capacity can grow too.

This is a long-term payoff of arrival, not an immediate post-landing bonus.

---

# 19. Genetic Diversity Archive Use

GA-H1 is a long-term diversity tool.

Early arrival use:
- secure replicated storage,
- contingency copies,
- reproductive medicine support where appropriate.

It is not:
- opened to replace current citizens,
- used to mass-produce Natural-Origin infants,
- tied to Amara authorization.

Any future child from archived germline can be Seeded or Natural-Origin depending actual prenatal Seed outcome/refusal.

---

# 20. Asset Transfer Classes

### Low civic-cost early candidates
- purpose-built arrival kits,
- robots made for transfer,
- duplicated equipment,
- selected empty industrial modules.

### Contested candidates
- reserve power modules,
- precision machine lines,
- radiators,
- pumps,
- medical/biotech systems,
- specialist teams.

### High civic-cost candidates
- occupied housing systems,
- critical redundancy,
- irreplaceable shipwide life-support nodes,
- heritage urban fabric.

Ending costs should come from some contested/high-cost decisions actually being made.

---

# 21. No Hidden Third Way

Local ISRU means Meridian does not have to be scrapped.
It does **not** mean everyone gets everything immediately.

Balanced path costs include:
- slower surface population growth,
- years of continued scarcity in off-ship nodes,
- reduced ship redundancy where assets are transferred,
- delayed new habitat,
- family geographic separation,
- expensive duplicate institutions.

---

# 22. Series-Ending Visual Rewards

The ending can show concrete but modest achievements:
- first water/ore shipment arriving at orbital depot,
- a surface door opening onto real wind while settlers wear breathing support,
- first permanent street-like connector between surface modules,
- children/school planning board still mostly empty,
- a new-habitat structural test article,
- Meridian lights still full and inhabited in orbit/space.

Avoid authorial thesis narration; let physical coexistence show the ending.

---

# PROVISIONAL JUDGMENT

Best model:
**ARR-H1 — Meridian Retained + Orbit/ISRU First-Mover + Small Surface Foothold + Capacity-Gated Migration**

At series end (~+1y):
- Meridian remains overwhelming population center,
- orbital/resource footholds = persistent but small,
- surface = small permanent foothold,
- new habitat = committed program, not city.

Exact populations remain quantitative/strategic-path dependent.
