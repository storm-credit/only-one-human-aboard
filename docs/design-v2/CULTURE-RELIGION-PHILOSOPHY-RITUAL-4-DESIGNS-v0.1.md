# CULTURE RELIGION / PHILOSOPHY / RITUAL — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE B / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Purpose:
Design belief/ritual life after ~450 years aboard Meridian without:
- making religion the hidden main conflict,
- declaring future people uniformly secular,
- inventing one ship cult that explains everything,
- mapping beliefs neatly onto Seeded/Natural origin or settlement factions.

Must cover:
- inherited Earth religions
- secular/nonreligious life
- ship-born philosophies/movements
- birth/adulthood/marriage/death rites
- Reconstruction attitudes
- Seed attitudes
- voyage/arrival commemorations
- sacred/secular spaces
- interbelief law/norms.

---

# DESIGN A — POST-RELIGIOUS SECULAR SOCIETY

Core:
Most organized religion faded; civic ethics/science dominate.

Strengths:
- simple
- avoids theology clutter

Weaknesses:
- implausibly uniform
- leaves death/Reconstruction rituals thin
- repeats old SF trope that technology erases religion

Verdict:
`REJECT AS TOTAL MODEL / SECULAR MAJORITY OR LARGE SECULAR POPULATION MAY EXIST`.

---

# DESIGN B — PRESERVED EARTH RELIGIONS

Core:
Major launch-era religions survive institutionally with modified practices.

Strengths:
- historical continuity
- natural ritual depth
- multicultural names/culture fit

Weaknesses:
- 450-year change can feel ignored
- denominational detail can explode
- risk of copy-pasting 21st-century conflicts

Verdict:
`ESSENTIAL HERITAGE LAYER / MUST EVOLVE`.

---

# DESIGN C — SHIP-BORN SYNCHRETIC CIVIC FAITH

Core:
Voyage produces a new dominant spiritual tradition around continuity, ancestors, closed ecology and destination.

Strengths:
- distinctive
- powerful cultural identity

Weaknesses:
- could become second premise
- risks cultic mission ideology
- too neat/monolithic
- may accidentally moralize Seed/Reconstruction ontology

Verdict:
`ALLOW SMALL/VARIED NEW MOVEMENTS, REJECT DOMINANT SINGLE SHIP FAITH`.

---

# DESIGN D — PLURAL INHERITED + SHIP-BORN CIVIC RITUAL ECOLOGY

Core:
Earth religions, secular worldviews, new philosophical communities and shared nonreligious civic rituals coexist.

Strengths:
- realistic diversity
- lets society share commemorations without shared theology
- flexible around Reconstruction/death
- no one belief faction owns plot truth

Weaknesses:
- needs disciplined examples rather than exhaustive theology catalog

Verdict:
`CURRENT BEST BASE`.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — REL-H1

Name:
**Plural Inherited Traditions + Secular Life + Ship-Born Civic Rituals + Small New Movements**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## 1. BELIEF ECOLOGY

Meridian contains:
- descendants/transformations of several Earth religious traditions,
- nonreligious/secular citizens,
- philosophical/ethical associations,
- small ship-born spiritual movements,
- people with loose/private mixed practice.

No official state religion.
No mandatory secularism.

---

# 2. LEGAL PRINCIPLE

Freedom of belief/nonbelief is protected.

Belief cannot override:
- immediate habitat safety,
- another person's consent,
- equal civic personhood,
- child protection.

Within those limits, law accommodates ritual/practice where feasible.

---

# 3. EARTH RELIGIONS AFTER 450 YEARS

Inherited traditions change through:
- reduced geographic institutions,
- mixed families,
- shared Ship Common,
- altered calendars/resources,
- no access to Earth holy geography,
- Reconstruction/bioethics debates,
- centuries of local leadership.

They remain recognizable lineages without needing exact 21st-century denominational continuity.

Package D later supplies historical evolution.

---

# 4. SACRED GEOGRAPHY WITHOUT EARTH

Some traditions maintain symbolic orientation toward Earth/history; others reinterpret sacred geography through:
- community,
- text,
- ritual direction/time,
- remembered places,
- local designated spaces.

No doctrine is universally solved by technology.

---

# 5. RELIGIOUS SPACES

Meridian can support:
- dedicated worship/meditation rooms,
- multi-use community ritual spaces,
- home practice,
- occasional district-scale ceremonial venues.

Space scarcity discourages giant empty monuments but does not erase sacred architecture/decor.

Old Quarter may contain historically valued religious/community spaces without becoming `religion district`.

---

# 6. SECULAR CIVIC RITUALS

Shared civic ceremonies need not be religious.

Possible functions:
- departure/founding remembrance
- major disaster remembrance
- completion of education/civil majority
- public service/retirement recognition
- voyage milestones
- arrival/insertion commemorations

Exact holiday/calendar placement waits for next Package B block.

---

# 7. BIRTH / NAMING RITES

Families may combine:
- civil registration
- religious blessing/dedication
- naming gathering
- secular family celebration

Seed completion is medical/legal provenance, not universal sacrament.

A family can religiously interpret prenatal choices without law declaring one interpretation true.

---

# 8. SEED ATTITUDES

Belief communities vary.

Possible historical/current positions:
- ordinary acceptance as medicine
- acceptance with ethical caution
- preference for parental discretion
- rare principled refusal

Hard rules:
- no major faith is automatically `the Natural-Origin religion`
- Amara's parents' refusal does not prove cult membership
- Seeded citizens are not religiously coded as fake/artificial by Canon.

---

# 9. RECONSTRUCTION ATTITUDES

Belief communities may disagree over:
- whether Reconstruction should be pursued
- continuity meaning
- acceptable intervention limits
- funeral timing

But modern civil law treats reconstructed citizens as persons/citizens.

No church gets legal power to declare a reconstructed citizen soulless/nonperson.

---

# 10. DEATH / FUNERAL RITUAL

REL-H1 plugs into DEATH-H1.

Different families may prefer:
- religious funeral/prayer
- secular remembrance
- community/occupational memorial
- quiet family disposition

Material-return methods can be interpreted differently, but safe legal options provide plural practice.

---

# 11. MEMORIAL OBJECTS

Material culture may carry belief:
- texts
- jewelry
- prayer/ritual objects
- inherited objects
- memorial tokens

These can become heirlooms without supernatural power.

Earth-origin religious objects may have heritage + devotional value simultaneously.

---

# 12. MARRIAGE / PARTNERSHIP CEREMONIES

Civil marriage/partnership and religious/cultural ceremony are separable.

A couple may have:
- civil-only ceremony
- religious rite
- family/cultural rite
- mixed form

Law remains gender/orientation neutral even if particular private religious communities hold their own doctrines within rights boundaries.

---

# 13. COMING-OF-AGE

Civil majority is legal; communities may have distinct coming-of-age rites at different ages.

Do not confuse religious adulthood with full civil adulthood.

---

# 14. VOYAGE PHILOSOPHIES

Centuries aboard can generate nonreligious philosophies around:
- stewardship
- continuity
- future generations
- ship-home identity
- duty vs autonomy
- repair/reuse

Hard rule:
No single `Meridian philosophy` is universally internalized.
Characters do not speak like philosophers because the society has ethical traditions.

---

# 15. SMALL SHIP-BORN SPIRITUAL MOVEMENTS

Small movements can arise around:
- voyage ancestry
- ecological cycles
- stars/destination
- memory/continuity

But they remain plural/minority unless later History design justifies otherwise.

Do not create a secret founder cult tied to Amara/Count.

---

# 16. SCIENCE / RELIGION

Advanced science does not produce one official metaphysics.

Medical/technical institutions state operational facts:
- Neural Anchor requirements
- Seed procedure
- physiology

They do not pronounce theological truth about souls.

---

# 17. PUBLIC ETHICS

Civic law uses public/reviewable reasons rather than one theology.
Religious citizens can participate politically like anyone else.

No formal clergy veto/seat in government by default.

---

# 18. CLERGY / COMMUNITY LEADERS

Some traditions maintain trained clergy/teachers; others use lay/community leadership.

Roles may include:
- worship
- counseling
- education
- ritual
- bereavement support

They do not replace licensed clinicians/lawyers in their professional domains.

---

# 19. RELIGION + MENTAL HEALTH

Spiritual/community care can coexist with mental-health treatment.

Hard rule:
Belief practice cannot justify abuse, coercive confinement or denial of another competent person's medical choice.

---

# 20. CHILDREN

Parents may raise children in traditions, but:
- public education remains common
- child safety/right protections apply
- older youth gain increasing belief/nonbelief autonomy

Exact age conventions later.

---

# 21. FASTING / FOOD / BODY PRACTICES

Religious dietary/fasting practices can be accommodated where medically/safety feasible.

Food-production constraints may cause recipes/substitutions to evolve over centuries.

Package B cuisine will integrate.

---

# 22. CALENDAR ADAPTATION

Religious calendars can be computed independently of ship civil calendar.

Communities may preserve Earth-origin lunar/solar calculations symbolically even where local astronomical sky differs.

Exact practice waits calendar block.

---

# 23. ARRIVAL

Arrival can produce varied interpretations:
- fulfillment of ancestral mission
- ordinary relocation
- religious thanksgiving
- anxiety about leaving ship-home
- ecological moral caution

Hard rule:
No prophecy declares Amara/founder lineage rightful planetary ruler.

---

# 24. SHIP HOME VS PLANET

Religious identity does not map one-to-one onto settlement choice.
Members of the same tradition may choose ship/orbit/surface differently.

---

# 25. READER-FACING RULE

Do not introduce a religion encyclopedia in prose.

Use recurring practice only when tied to:
- character
- family
- holiday
- funeral/wedding
- community place
- ethical decision.

Names of actual sects/communities should be created sparingly after Package D/F.

---

# 26. RED FLAGS

P1-REL-01: inherited religions could look frozen from 21st century.
P1-REL-02: new ship spirituality could become second premise.
P1-REL-03: Seed refusal could accidentally be coded as religious extremism.
P1-REL-04: Reconstruction theology could overwhelm civil personhood plot.
P1-REL-05: exact denominations/practices need History/Character support.
P1-REL-06: funeral practice must match material constraints.
P1-REL-07: religion should add life texture, not faction checklist.
