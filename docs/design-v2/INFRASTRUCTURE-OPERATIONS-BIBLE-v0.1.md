# 1. Infrastructure Philosophy

Meridian is simultaneously:
- a spacecraft,
- a city,
- a closed-loop industrial civilization,
- a 450-year historical built environment.

Therefore its systems must support both:
- hard engineering constraints,
- ordinary civilian life.

Core Package C principle:
**Citizens should experience a mature city; operators should know it is an engineered closed habitat.**

The setting should not collapse into either:
- sterile station corridors,
- magical Earth-like metropolis with no pressure/heat/maintenance logic.

---

# 2. Urban Architecture

Meridian's three rotating Habitats are organized as mixed-use cities inside a persistent engineering frame.

## Hard layer
- pressure boundaries,
- structural frames,
- utility trunks,
- fire/smoke zones,
- transit/service corridors.

## Soft layer
- homes,
- schools,
- shops,
- clinics,
- cafés/restaurants,
- offices,
- workshops,
- public squares/courtyards,
- parks/green spaces,
- cultural/religious/community spaces.

The soft layer changes far more rapidly over centuries than the hard layer.

---

# 3. Housing

Existing total household order:
~120k–130k.

Provisional resilience direction:
The city maintains some vacant/turnover/temporary stock; 100% physical occupancy would be operationally brittle.

Housing varies by:
- size,
- room count,
- privacy,
- acoustics,
- light/view,
- location,
- commute,
- heritage value,
- custom interior quality.

Civic Floor guarantees safe minimum housing, not premium amenities.

Dwelling property/occupancy rights do not privatize:
- pressure hull,
- life-support trunks,
- emergency corridors,
- critical structural commons.

# 5. Transit

Daily movement uses a hierarchy:

1. walking / mobility aids / small personal mobility,
2. local district transit,
3. Habitat trunk transit,
4. controlled rotation-to-Spine transfer,
5. Spine inter-Habitat express,
6. destination Habitat local leg.

Existing cross-Habitat 60–120m range is interpreted as door-to-door, not pure Spine travel time.

Most life remains local enough that only a minority of citizens cross Habitats daily.

Large-scale private automobile culture is not required.

---

# 6. Freight / Logistics

Freight is partially separated from passenger movement.

Key streams:
- commercial deliveries,
- food,
- medical,
- waste/recycling,
- industrial components,
- hazardous material,
- strategic arrival cargo.

Bulk/hazardous flows use controlled corridors/times where practical.
Last-mile service remains visible enough to make neighborhoods feel economically real.

Kai's Spine logistics apprenticeship fits exception handling, routing and arrival staging around automated systems.

---

# 7. Rotation / Gravity Transition

Ordinary citizens do not jump directly from rotating ~1g urban surface into zero-g.

Cross-Habitat movement uses controlled radial transition toward lower gravity and engineered interfaces to the non-rotating Spine.

Low-g movement remains physically noticeable:
- handholds/restraints,
- orientation changes,
- accessibility assistance,
- occasional motion sickness.

Exact geometry remains quantitative/map work.

---

# 8. Air

Atmospheric management is hierarchical:
- strategic gas inventory / bulk processing,
- Habitat/district circulation,
- building local conditioning,
- room comfort controls.

Automatic systems monitor pressure, CO2/O2 balance, humidity and contaminants.

Citizens adjust comfort, not shipwide atmospheric chemistry.

---

# 9. Water / Wastewater

Water uses differentiated streams and high recovery.

Normal citizens can:
- shower,
- cook,
- clean,
- do laundry.

High-efficiency treatment/recovery occurs in infrastructure rather than permanent personal austerity.

Wastewater becomes:
- purified water,
- nutrient/feedstock streams,
- controlled residue.

Closed loop is high-efficiency, not perfect.

---

# 10. Power

Civil fusion supports ordinary civilization at multi-GW to ~10GW order, consistent with Core Canon.

Grid hierarchy:
- strategic generation/distribution,
- Habitat/district networks,
- building distribution,
- emergency critical-load backup.

Critical loads are protected before discretionary loads.

Local storage bridges faults/safe shutdown; it does not power whole Habitats indefinitely.

---

# 11. Heat

All civil power becomes heat eventually.

Thermal architecture includes:
- building/district loops,
- heat reuse where practical,
- Habitat thermal trunks,
- radiator/rejection infrastructure.

Thermal margin can constrain:
- industry,
- maintenance schedules,
- high-load services,
- arrival operations.

Hard rule:
Arrival pressure remains spares/thermal geometry/labor/redundancy/logistics, not `household lights versus propulsion reactor`.

---

# 12. Solid Waste / Material Cycling

Default hierarchy:
repair → reuse → component recovery → material recycling → feedstock conversion → secure residue handling.

Waste streams are separated by hazard/material type.

The civilization still needs:
- make-up materials,
- strategic stocks,
- contamination control,
- eventual ISRU.

No universal replicator.

---

# 13. Normal Utility Failure

Most failures are local and legible:
- hot-water outage,
- one building on backup power,
- reduced ventilation,
- local water restriction,
- delayed waste pickup,
- transit line closure.

District isolation prevents every fault from becoming shipwide catastrophe.

---

# 14. Emergency Architecture

EMG-H1 scales by hazard scope:
- routine/site,
- local emergency,
- district/Habitat emergency,
- shipwide strategic emergency.

Authority grows only when physical scope requires it.

Emergency response integrates:
- automatic physical safeties,
- trained local responders,
- medical/public-safety response,
- technical incident command,
- civic/legal review for prolonged rights restrictions.

---

# 15. Fire

Priority hazards include smoke/toxic gases as well as flame.

Response uses:
- detection,
- local suppression,
- power/process isolation,
- smoke/ventilation control,
- fire barriers,
- evacuation/refuge,
- cleanup/air verification.

---

# 16. Decompression

Localized pressure loss is dangerous but normally bounded by multiple sectors.

Response:
- rapid sensing,
- automatic closure,
- movement behind safe pressure boundary,
- breathing support where needed,
- leak localization,
- controlled repair/repressurization.

No routine Habitat-wide evacuation.

---

# 17. Contamination

Chemical, smoke, biological, water, radiological and industrial contamination require different responses.

No generic `quarantine all` button.

Health isolation must remain medically justified, scoped and reviewable.

---

# 18. Emergency Culture

Citizens know simple emergency actions and practice age-appropriate drills.

Specialist response remains professional.

Ordinary home kits are short-duration civic emergency kits, not personal EVA suits.

---

# 19. AI / Automation

No single ship AI.

Meridian uses federated classes of systems:
- control automation,
- predictive/diagnostic models,
- administrative/search agents,
- personal assistants,
- embodied robots/drones.

AI can be highly capable while remaining non-sovereign.

# 20. AI Authority Boundary

AI may:
- optimize,
- recommend,
- summarize,
- diagnose with uncertainty,
- search within permissions,
- execute predefined physical safeties,
- operate tools/robots.

AI may not be final autonomous authority for:
- citizenship/personhood,
- sentencing,
- indefinite detention,
- parental-right termination,
- political value allocation,
- Amara/settler legitimacy.

No universal citizen score.

# 36. Ordinary Citizen Day — Infrastructure Walkthrough

A normal citizen can:
1. wake in an ordinary safe apartment,
2. shower/use kitchen without thinking about recycling chemistry,
3. check local news/messages,
4. walk to school/work/local transit,
5. travel across district/Habitat if needed,
6. buy food/services digitally,
7. use AI assistance without granting it universal authority,
8. experience scheduled maintenance/outage notices,
9. visit a park/café/sport/clinic,
10. return home while infrastructure operates mostly invisibly.

No new major infrastructure rule is required.

---

# 37. Abnormal Day — Local Emergency Walkthrough

Example:
A battery/utility fire causes smoke in one mixed-use block.

System can:
1. detect fire/smoke,
2. isolate power/process,
3. control ventilation,
4. close local fire/pressure boundaries,
5. stop transit entering unsafe zone,
6. alert residents through multiple channels,
7. move people to adjacent refuge/sector,
8. provide medical/public-safety response,
9. reroute utilities/transit,
10. house displaced residents temporarily,
11. investigate and repair,
12. preserve legal/technical evidence.

No Habitat-wide collapse required.

# 38. Network Partition Day

If one district loses backbone connectivity:
- critical local automation continues,
- emergency communications use local/hardwired fallback,
- essential cached records remain,
- payments/messages may delay,
- work/transport operate in degraded mode,
- later state synchronization reconciles data.

No `internet off = oxygen off` problem.
