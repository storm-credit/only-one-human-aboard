# INFRA — UTILITIES / DOMESTIC INTERFACES — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `ENCYCLOPEDIC v2 DESIGN COMPARISON / PACKAGE C / NOT CANON`
Date: 2026-08-17

Scope:
- air circulation / atmospheric processing
- potable/service water
- wastewater / nutrient recovery
- electrical distribution
- local energy storage / emergency power
- thermal loops / heat rejection interfaces
- solid waste / recycling / hazardous waste
- citizen-facing meters / alerts / outage behavior

Existing Canon:
- advanced distributed fusion civil power, multi-GW to ~10GW order.
- heat rejection is a real civilization limit.
- closed loops highly efficient but imperfect.
- air/water/basic housing are Civic Floor.
- ordinary citizens can shower, cook, clean and live urban lives.
- hard capacities cannot simply be purchased with money.

---

# DESIGN A — CENTRALIZED PLANT DOMINANT

Large shipwide plants feed all Habitats through huge trunks.

Pros:
- efficient,
- easy accounting,
- simple quality control.

Cons:
- catastrophic common-mode failure,
- implausible after 450 years,
- weak neighborhood resilience.

---

# DESIGN B — MICROGRID / MICRO-LOOP DOMINANT

Every district nearly self-contained for power, water and air.

Pros:
- resilience,
- local maintenance.

Cons:
- duplication overhead,
- uneven quality,
- risks Habitat/local autarky and caste politics.

---

# DESIGN C — CASCADING HIERARCHICAL LOOPS

Shipwide strategic plants + Habitat-scale trunks + district/building secondary loops.
Local isolation/storage handles ordinary faults.

Pros:
- strongest resilience/efficiency balance,
- fits compartmented city,
- realistic maintenance hierarchy.

Cons:
- operational complexity.

---

# DESIGN D — MARKETIZED UTILITY SERVICES

Multiple providers sell water/power/thermal service.

Pros:
- economic texture.

Cons:
- incompatible with Civic Floor if core life-support is fragmented,
- safety/accountability problems.

Reject as primary; private/co-op contractors may operate noncore services under commons standards.

---

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — UTIL-H1

# `Hierarchical Commons Loops + District Isolation + Domestic Abstraction`

Status: `PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

Core idea:
Citizens experience utilities as reliable urban services. Behind the wall, they are heavily instrumented closed-loop commons with multiple scales of isolation and redundancy.

---

# 1. Atmospheric Architecture

Three layers:

### Ship/Habitat strategic processing
- bulk gas inventory management,
- CO2/O2 balancing,
- trace contaminant control,
- humidity balance,
- reserve gas storage,
- emergency atmospheric recovery.

### District/building air loops
- circulation,
- filtration,
- local humidity/temperature,
- smoke/contamination isolation,
- pressure-zone interface.

### Room/home controls
- comfort setpoints within allowed bands,
- local filtration/ventilation status,
- no citizen control over shipwide oxygen chemistry.

Air quality is measured continuously at engineering level but not turned into total personal surveillance.

---

# 2. Water Architecture

Water streams are not one magical pipe.

Likely categories:
- potable water,
- hot domestic water/service heat,
- reclaimed service water where appropriate,
- wastewater,
- industrial/process water,
- high-purity medical/technical water.

Closed-loop sequence includes:
- collection,
- biological/chemical/physical treatment,
- purification,
- mineral balancing,
- quality verification,
- redistribution.

Normal showers and laundry are possible because water is recovered, not because citizens are forced to live on tiny daily rations.

Short conservation measures may occur during faults or maintenance.

---

# 3. Wastewater / Nutrient Recovery

Human/food/organic waste enters separated treatment chains.
Outputs may return to:
- clean water,
- nutrient streams,
- feedstocks,
- microbial/biological systems,
- controlled waste residue.

Important cultural rule:
Citizens do not talk as though dinner is directly made from yesterday's sewage. Industrial treatment creates socially and chemically meaningful separation.

---

# 4. Power Distribution

Civil fusion generation feeds a layered grid:
- high-capacity ship/Habitat distribution,
- district substations/microgrids,
- building distribution,
- critical-load emergency systems.

Critical loads:
- life-support controls,
- emergency lighting/communications,
- medical functions,
- pressure/fire systems,
- essential pumps/valves,
- transit safety.

Noncritical loads may be shed before life-support during shortages.

No magical infinite battery.

---

# 5. Local Energy Storage

Possible forms:
- electrochemical batteries,
- flywheel/supercapacitor systems for short response,
- thermal storage,
- mechanical/other advanced storage where appropriate.

Purpose:
- bridge switching,
- stabilize local grids,
- support safe shutdown,
- keep emergency loads alive.

Not intended to run whole Habitats indefinitely after generation loss.

---

# 6. Heat / Thermal Network

Every watt eventually becomes heat.

Architecture:
- building/district heat collection,
- reusable low-grade heat where economically useful,
- Habitat thermal trunks,
- transfer to radiator/heat-rejection systems,
- temperature hierarchy between industrial/medical/domestic loads.

Citizens may experience thermal constraint through:
- temporary limits on high-energy amenities,
- industrial scheduling,
- hotter/less comfortable public spaces during abnormal load,
- delayed noncritical processes.

They do not experience propulsion as simply `the engine stole our electricity`.

---

# 7. Solid Material Waste

Default assumption:
`waste = material stream`, not garbage disappears.

Categories:
- clean recyclables,
- mixed household waste,
- organics,
- textiles,
- electronics,
- construction/demolition material,
- hazardous chemical/medical waste,
- contaminated material,
- irrecoverable residue.

Systems prioritize:
repair → reuse → component recovery → material recycling → feedstock conversion → secure disposal/storage.

Closed loop is high but not perfect; trace losses/contamination accumulate and require strategic make-up stocks/ISRU later.

---

# 8. Household Interface

Ordinary residents see:
- utility status,
- service notices,
- consumption trends,
- maintenance requests,
- emergency alerts,
- optional comfort controls.

They do NOT routinely need to manage oxygen percentages or wastewater chemistry.

Domestic systems should fail legibly:
- `hot water unavailable until 18:00`,
- `local ventilation in reduced mode`,
- `building power on backup`,
not endless engineering dashboards.

---

# 9. Metering and Billing

Civic Floor means survival quantity is not shut off for inability to pay.

Candidate economic model:
- baseline/basic utility service folded into civic housing/service framework,
- high discretionary consumption may incur ordinary pricing/fees,
- businesses/industrial users face metered cost structures,
- emergency conservation rules override price signals when physical capacity is threatened.

Do not create `rich citizen buys unlimited oxygen`.

Exact tariff model remains Society integration.

---

# 10. District Isolation

A district/building fault should usually be isolate-able without cutting the whole Habitat.

Isolation capabilities:
- water valves,
- electrical switching,
- ventilation/smoke dampers,
- thermal loop bypass,
- data network reroute,
- wastewater containment.

This supports URB-H1 pressure/service sectors.

---

# 11. Maintenance Windows

Planned maintenance is culturally normal.

Typical experience:
- advance notice,
- short utility interruption,
- temporary service route,
- building access request,
- quiet-hours exceptions for urgent repair.

Major deferred maintenance becomes political because it increases outage risk and consumes strategic spares.

Jun's Old Quarter work fits here.

---

# 12. Reliability Philosophy

Not `never fails`.

Normal hierarchy:
- faults happen,
- sensors detect,
- isolation limits scope,
- redundant path takes load,
- repair restores,
- incident review follows meaningful failures.

A serious cascading failure remains possible, but should require multiple defeated layers or extraordinary damage.

---

# 13. Utility Cybersecurity / AI Boundary

Automation may:
- detect abnormal pressure/flow,
- close a predefined safety valve,
- shift loads,
- start backup equipment,
- recommend maintenance.

Automation may not:
- impose indefinite civic punishment,
- search personal records unrelated to safety,
- decide guilt,
- silently override hard legal rights because an optimization score says so.

Exact AI ontology later in Package C.

---

# 14. Utility Data Privacy

Engineering data can reveal occupancy patterns indirectly.
Therefore:
- operational telemetry should minimize personal identity linkage,
- detailed household consumption access has privacy rules,
- emergency safety use is narrower than open police/media access.

No `smart meter instantly identifies Amara's whole family history` shortcut.

---

# 15. Hazardous / Contaminated Streams

Special handling needed for:
- medical biohazard,
- industrial chemicals,
- radiological material,
- contaminated filters/media,
- unknown historical materials.

These streams do not casually re-enter consumer recycling.

---

# 16. Arrival / ISRU Transition

Late story:
- strategic reserves are audited,
- some utility machinery may be duplicated/transferred,
- ISRU bulk water/metals gradually reduce dependence on stored make-up mass,
- Meridian must retain enough redundancy to stay inhabited.

This directly supports `Costly Polycentric Arrival`.

---

# 17. Reader-Facing Sensory Texture

Utilities can become visible through:
- maintenance doors,
- water/reclamation workwear,
- service interruptions,
- warm utility corridors,
- fan/pump vibration,
- public conservation notices,
- repaired fixtures.

Do not exposition-dump closed-loop diagrams during ordinary scenes.
