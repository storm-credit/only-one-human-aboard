# 1. Selected Geometry — QSH-B Balanced 1-km Class

Per major rotating Habitat:
- effective outer radius: **0.9–1.1 km**
- inhabited longitudinal extent: **5–7 km order**
- main urban gravity: **~0.9–1.0g**
- rotation: **~0.9–1.0 rpm**
- period: roughly **60–67 seconds** around the ~1g center assumptions.

Radial motion around ~1.5 m/s produces Coriolis acceleration around **~0.03g order** at the selected radius/rpm.

Interpretation:
- ordinary tangential neighborhood life feels broadly terrestrial,
- radial/low-g transitions remain noticeable,
- dedicated reduced/zero-g spaces are physically distinctive.

Rejected as primary:
- compact ~0.7–0.85 km high-rpm architecture,
- deliberately different-gravity Habitats,
- substantially larger >~1.2–1.5 km structures without story need.

# 2. Crucial Pressure-Volume Clarification

**The rotating Habitat is not one full-radius, end-to-end open atmospheric cylinder.**

Its 1-km-class rotating macrostructure contains:
- layered inhabited deck/terrace/atrium zones,
- structural/service volumes,
- pressure/fire boundaries,
- transit/service corridors,
- local public spaces of varying openness.

Some districts can contain large atria/long sightlines/green spaces, but the engineering design does not expose ~100k citizens to one shared pressure volume.

This is compatible with the existing civic language `생활환` and cylindrical/large-rotating-structure Canon while preserving local emergency isolation.

Hard:
- do not portray an O'Neill-style single open atmosphere spanning the whole 5–7 km structure unless Canon is deliberately reopened,
- civic streets/districts can feel open and urban without one pressure volume.

# 3. Urban Surface / Density Sanity

A 1-km-radius / 5–7 km-long cylindrical reference surface corresponds to roughly:
- **31–44 km²** primary circumferential inner-surface order per Habitat,
- **94–132 km²** across three before multilevel built floor area.

At ~100k residents/Habitat order:
- gross reference-surface density ~**2.3k–3.2k residents/km²**,
- effective usable built floor area is greater because the city is multilevel/terraced/compartmented.

Result:
a mixed city with homes, parks/green spaces, schools, shops, workshops and industry is spatially plausible without corridor-only life.

These are sanity numbers, not reader-facing cadastral figures.

# 5. Pressure / Fire / Isolation Hierarchy

## Local building compartment
Typical affected population:
**tens to several hundreds** depending use.

## Major district engineering / pressure sector
Population-equivalent planning band:
**~1,000–3,000 people** plus nonresidential volume.

For ~100k residents/Habitat, author-facing order:
**~35–80 major pressure/isolation sectors per Habitat**, with smaller building/fire subzones beneath them.

Because the Habitat is internally subdivided, these sectors are actual bounded pressure volumes rather than slices of one giant open cylinder.

Hard:
- engineering sector ≠ civic neighborhood,
- exact layout/count remains C2 inside band,
- one apartment breach does not depressurize a Habitat,

# 6. Emergency Refuge

Distributed refuge strategy.

Planning rule:
- each normal residential/work catchment has mobility-accessible refuge capacity,
- local refuge cluster handles **hundreds to low-thousands** depending district function,
- planning includes roughly **15–25% overflow margin** above immediate designated catchment,
- a major pressure/fire sector can evacuate without whole-Habitat relocation.

Refuges support incident survival/stabilization, not weeks-long bunker living by default.

Exact number/location remains C2.

# 8. Water Resilience

Distinguish total inventory from clean treated buffer.

## Local/building critical treated-water buffer
**~6–24 hours** depending building function.

## District/Habitat prioritized drinking/clinical/critical-sanitation resilience
**~2–5 days** under degraded bulk treatment, using distributed tanks/reserves/reconfiguration.

This is prioritized emergency demand, not normal full shower/laundry consumption.

## Strategic total water
Much larger through:
- process loops,
- ecology,
- storage,
- shielding inventory.

Hard:
`2–5 days` never means the ship only has 2–5 days of total water.

# 9. Atmospheric / Gas Resilience — REPAIRED

Do **not** use a whole-Habitat `days of air` or `% of total atmosphere in tanks` rule.

Selected resilience grammar:
- normal atmosphere is distributed across bounded Habitat pressure volumes,
- local/major sectors isolate after breach/contamination,
- makeup gas storage/process systems cover ordinary leakage, maintenance drawdown and **recovery/repressurization of one or several local engineering sectors**, depending sector size and damage,
- multiple distributed stores/process paths prevent one tank farm from being sole recovery path,
- catastrophic loss of a large fraction of an entire Habitat atmosphere is beyond routine reserve design and becomes major-civilization damage.

Quantitative author-facing lock:
**gas reserve is sized by recoverable sector volume and constituent balance, not by a fixed percentage of the whole Habitat atmosphere.**

Exact gas mass/chemistry remains C2.

# 10. Electrical Resilience

## Local life-safety ride-through
**~15–60 minutes** for doors/controls/alarms/comms/lighting/safe shutdown depending site.

## Critical facility island/backup
Hospitals, controls, refuges and key utility nodes support **hours-to-day-class** prioritized operation through storage/local generation/grid reconfiguration.

## Habitat strategy
reconfigure distributed generation + shed discretionary load + isolate faults.

Hard:
- no battery powers an entire Habitat normally for days,
- critical air/water/medical/control loads receive priority,
- propulsion energy remains separate in scale.

# 11. Thermal Headroom

Selected author-facing planning bands:
- normal sustained operation: **~10–20% rejection headroom** preferred,
- planned high-load/maintenance/final-approach windows: **~5–10%** can be accepted temporarily,
- sustained **<~5%** is a restricted/uncomfortable regime requiring load/schedule action.

These are story-engineering bands, not literal safety-code percentages.

Hard:
- thermal headroom is not stored energy,
- thermal pressure can throttle industry/maintenance without instant blackout,
- arrival pressure does not require household-power-vs-engine trope.

# 13. Strategic Spares / Redundancy

No universal spare percentage.

## S-A — Long-lead / hard-to-fabricate critical
- N+2 or equivalent independent recovery paths when practical,
- N+1 is the uncomfortable minimum before accepting major transfer,
- multi-year consumable/component planning.

## S-B — Fabricable but critical
- installed redundancy where safety requires,
- at least one ready swap/spare path for common single-point modules,
- certified local repair/fabrication route,
- feedstock/tooling/calibration reserve.

## S-C — Common maintainable
- weeks-to-months demand stock by failure/production cadence,
- routine remanufacture.

Act5~7 `redundancy burn-down` can mean:
- N+2-like → N+1-like recovery,
- longer replacement lead time,
- fewer maintenance scheduling options.

It does not imply inevitable disaster.
