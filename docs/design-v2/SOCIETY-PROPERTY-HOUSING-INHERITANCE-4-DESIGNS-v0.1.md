# DESIGN A — TRANSFERABLE LONG-TERM OCCUPANCY RIGHTS

Core:
Residential units are structurally public/common, but households can own/transfer long-term occupancy interests similar to very long leaseholds/condominium-use rights.

Can buy/sell:
- occupancy interest
- interior improvements
- eligible storage/amenity rights

Cannot own:
- hull
- life-support trunk systems
- common structural shell
- public corridor

Strengths:
- market legibility
- finite-space realism
- allows inheritance and wealth differences
- preserves common infrastructure sovereignty

Weaknesses:
- `ownership` semantics can confuse readers
- requires maintenance-charge/association rules
- speculative market possible

Verdict:
`STRONG BASE`.

---

# DESIGN B — COOPERATIVE HOUSING DOMINANT

Core:
Most citizens hold shares/membership in housing cooperatives that grant occupancy. Moving means transferring/exchanging shares or joining another co-op.

Strengths:
- strong community logic
- fits closed habitat
- limits speculative vacancy
- easy shared maintenance

Weaknesses:
- too ideologically uniform
- private wealth/property variety reduced
- every housing dispute becomes co-op governance

Verdict:
`USE AS ONE MAJOR TENURE FORM, NOT UNIVERSAL`.

---

# DESIGN C — FULL PRIVATE UNIT FREEHOLD

Core:
Apartments/units are privately owned like modern condominiums; common structure jointly managed.

Strengths:
- familiar
- easy mortgage/inheritance market
- strong wealth accumulation

Weaknesses:
- risks implying private sovereignty over critical structure
- hard to reconcile compulsory relocation/repair
- speculative vacancy problem in finite pressurized volume

Verdict:
`TOO EARTH-LIKE AS SOLE MODEL / RETAIN LIMITED ELEMENTS`.

---

# DESIGN D — CIVIC HOUSING LICENSE / ASSIGNMENT

Core:
Housing is fundamentally a public entitlement; households receive units through need/work/family assignment and cannot meaningfully trade them.

Strengths:
- clean scarcity control
- prevents homelessness/speculation

Weaknesses:
- kills market-city texture
- reduces moving choice
- creates bureaucratic assignment society
- weakens inheritance/property stories

Verdict:
`REJECT AS GENERAL MODEL / RETAIN MINIMUM-HOUSING FLOOR`.

# RECOMMENDED HYBRID — PROP-H1

Name:
**Layered Residential Tenure + Common Structure + Transferable Household Interests**

Status:
`PROVISIONAL PRIORITY / NOT CANON`

## Principle
Meridian separates:
1. survival structure,
2. residential occupancy interest,
3. private interior property.

No household owns a section of the pressure hull as sovereign land.
But households can still accumulate housing wealth and make meaningful location/space choices.

---

# 1. THREE PROPERTY LAYERS

## Layer S — Structural Commons
Never ordinary private property:
- hull/pressure structure
- major life-support trunks
- public utility backbone
- common fire/decompression systems
- structural rotation systems
- public corridors/transit interfaces

Managed by civic/common institutions.

## Layer R — Residential Tenure Interest
Can take several legal forms:
- long-term transferable occupancy right
- private residential-use interest
- cooperative share with occupancy
- ordinary rental lease
- employer/institutional temporary housing

## Layer P — Private Interior Property
Normally private:
- furnishings
- appliances
- personal objects
- interior nonstructural customization
- heirlooms
- household storage contents

Subject to safety code.

---

# 2. TENURE MIX

Recommended social ecology rather than one universal form:
- ~market transferable long-term occupancy interests
- housing cooperatives
- private/regulated rentals
- civic minimum-housing stock
- temporary/protected housing
- institutional/student/apprentice housing

Exact percentages remain ELASTIC until Package C Housing quantitative pass.

Reason:
Different family/job/life stages need different tenure.

---

# 3. CIVIC MINIMUM HOUSING

Every citizen has access to a safe minimum housing path.

This does NOT mean:
- preferred district
- large unit
- private view
- permanent unit choice
- same quality/amenity as market housing

Temporary minimum housing can be used after:
- disaster displacement
- domestic violence
- Reconstruction discharge
- eviction after lawful process where no other home exists
- sudden family separation

No one is legally left without breathable shelter as debt punishment.

---

# 4. MARKET HOUSING

Households can pay more for:
- size
- privacy
- location
- shorter commute
- view/green access
- historic character
- premium building services

Prices respond to demand but are constrained by finite habitat space and housing policy.

Speculative controls may include:
- vacancy charge
- secondary-unit tax
- use requirement
- limits on converting residential units to nonresidential use

No blanket prohibition on multiple occupancy interests because later ship/orbit/surface households may need multi-location arrangements.

---

# 5. RENTAL

Ordinary rental contracts exist.

Tenants have:
- habitability rights
- privacy
- notice/eviction due process
- deposit/accounting protections
- repair remedies

Landlords/owners have:
- rent rights
- damage remedies
- lawful termination routes

Emergency structural work can temporarily displace residents, with review/compensation/temporary housing obligations.

---

# 6. MORTGAGE / HOUSING CREDIT

Transferable residential interests can secure loans.

Default may cause loss/forced transfer of premium housing interest after due process, but debtor retains access to civic minimum housing.

No bank can foreclose on:
- citizenship
- survival air/water entitlement
- public life-support structure

---

# 7. COOPERATIVE HOUSING

Co-ops may own/manage:
- building interior common areas
- maintenance contracts
- shared amenities
- unit-allocation/share rules

Co-op rules cannot override shipwide rights/safety law.

They can have distinct culture/fees without becoming micro-states.

---

# 8. REPAIR / CONDEMNATION / RELOCATION

When a building/block becomes unsafe:
1. technical authority can impose narrow immediate safety closure,
2. longer relocation requires civic/housing process,
3. residents receive temporary housing path,
4. property/tenure compensation depends on legal interest and repair outcome,
5. heritage status does not trump immediate life safety,
6. residents can contest scope/timing/valuation, not demand unsafe operation indefinitely.

This supports Jun/Old Quarter arcs.

---

# 9. EMINENT-DOMAIN-LIKE STRATEGIC ACQUISITION

For major arrival/infrastructure projects, government/common bodies may compel acquisition/transfer of private/co-op interests under law.

Normally requires:
- public purpose
- published basis
- compensation/replacement
- appeal/valuation challenge
- relocation support

Emergency exceptions are narrower and temporary.

This enriches Act 5 asset/space conflicts.

---

# 10. BASIC INHERITANCE MODEL

Normally inheritable:
- money/savings
- business shares/interests
- personal objects
- heirlooms
- intellectual-property interests
- eligible residential tenure interests
- cooperative shares subject to co-op/housing rules

Not inheritable as private property:
- public office
- professional credential
- security clearance
- civic hard-capacity queue priority
- strategic commons asset
- ship structure

---

# 11. ESTATE PROCESS

After legal death/finality:
- debts/obligations are settled under protected-family rules,
- dependents receive priority protections where law provides,
- remaining estate transfers by will/default succession,
- disputed heritage/provenance objects follow ordinary property/evidence rules.

No assumption that all physical possessions are recycled immediately.

---

# 12. RECONSTRUCTION STATUS PROBLEM

Meridian cannot use a simple binary `dead = estate instantly final` because some severe cases enter prolonged Reconstruction/recovery pathways.

Recommended staged legal model:

## Stage 1 — Acute / Recovery Pending
If a viable Reconstruction/recovery process is actively underway:
- estate is not fully finalized,
- household/business affairs may be managed by proxy/temporary administrator,
- dependents receive support,
- urgent contracts can continue,
- irreversible transfers are limited where feasible.

## Stage 2 — Long Recovery / Functional Absence
If the person remains legally continuous but unable to exercise rights:
- guardian/proxy/administrator may manage assets,
- housing/business arrangements can change for practical reasons,
- third parties can acquire protected reliance interests.

## Stage 3 — Legal Finality
If legal process declares final death/no continuing viable legal recovery status:
- estate may be distributed.

Hard rule:
Exact medical possibility alone does not freeze an estate forever.
Law needs a finality threshold.

---

# 13. RETURN AFTER INTERVENING PROPERTY CHANGE

If a reconstructed/returned citizen remains legally continuous through Stage 1/2:
- they resume control of assets still legally theirs,
- but do not automatically displace lawful third-party occupancy/business rights created during protected administration.

If a genuinely finalized estate was lawfully distributed before an extraordinary later return/recognition scenario:
- return does not automatically unwind all good-faith transactions,
- person may receive statutory claims/compensation/restoration where feasible,
- fraud or wrongful finalization can be challenged separately.

This preserves third-party reliance and keeps Reconstruction from becoming a property-reset machine.

---

# 14. HEIRLOOM CONFLICT

A singular family object may already have transferred to an heir/relative during long absence.

Recommended rule:
Personhood continuity does **not automatically create physical repossession of every emotionally meaningful object**.

Possible remedies:
- negotiated return
- priority purchase/compensation in selected cases
- shared custody/display
- mediation
- court resolution based on actual legal transfer

This is a high-value material-culture story engine.

---

# 15. WILL / ADVANCE PROPERTY INSTRUCTIONS

Citizens can make:
- wills
- beneficiary designations
- business succession instructions
- heritage-object bequests
- temporary management directives for incapacity/Reconstruction

These are distinct from medical Reconstruction preferences.

No single document should control both medical personhood and every property outcome.

---

# 16. FAMILY STATUS / MARRIAGE EFFECT

Marriage/partnership may affect:
- default succession
- shared household property
- proxy rights
- dependent support

Exact marital-property model waits for Family/Marrriage block.

Guardrail:
No assumption that marriage automatically merges all property.

---

# 17. CHILDREN / DEPENDENTS

Minor children can inherit, but assets may be managed through trustee/guardian structures until legal thresholds.

A guardian does not become beneficial owner merely by managing the child's property.

Noah-type family disputes therefore separate:
- care authority
- property management
- child's own property/benefits.

---

# 18. BUSINESS SUCCESSION

If an owner/founder becomes incapacitated/dies:
- management succession can occur without transferring all beneficial ownership immediately,
- employee/co-op rights persist,
- critical-service continuity rules may apply,
- heirs do not automatically inherit professional licenses/operational credentials.

---

# 19. HERITAGE / PUBLIC-INTEREST OBJECTS

Private ownership of a historically important object can coexist with public heritage interest.

Possible obligations:
- provenance disclosure when sold as authenticated heritage
- conservation/export restrictions for highest-significance objects
- public acquisition/loan rights under defined law

No automatic state confiscation merely because an object is old.

---

# 20. FOUND / SALVAGED PROPERTY

`Found = mine` is not default.

Categories:
- lost personal property
- abandoned private property
- decommissioned corporate/co-op property
- strategic/common equipment
- controlled-zone materials
- heritage artifact

Rules differ by status.

Unauthorized removal of strategic equipment can be theft/safety offense even if physically discarded nearby.

---

# 21. CLASS EFFECTS

Housing is a major but not sole wealth marker.

Visible differences:
- location
- space
- privacy
- building age/status
- amenity
- ability to keep a second eligible occupancy interest
- quality of interior customization

Anti-caste rule:
No Habitat is uniformly rich/poor.
Each Habitat contains multiple housing classes/tenure forms.

---

# 22. ARRIVAL TRANSITION

Pre-arrival residential interests do not automatically convert into land title on the destination world.

Need later Package E rules for:
- ship property retained when household relocates
- dual occupancy during migration
- surface/orbit tenure creation
- compensation for requisitioned ship space
- inheritance across locations
- taxation/jurisdiction

Hard rule:
No first-landing homestead sovereignty.
