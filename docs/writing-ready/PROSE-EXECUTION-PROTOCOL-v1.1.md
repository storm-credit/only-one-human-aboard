# 1. POV — HARD DEFAULT

- one POV character per episode
- third-person limited
- no mid-episode head hopping

## Selection Order
1. irreversible-choice owner
2. immediate cost bearer
3. information-fair observer
4. if genuinely tied, prefer Maren as series anchor

`Episode Blueprint Primary Actor` is strong evidence but not automatic POV.

Do not choose a POV merely because that character can explain the information.

# 2. Maren-Led Plurality Anchor — POV-H1R

This is **not a majority-POV novel**.
It is a true ensemble with one primary series anchor.

## Full-Series Anchor Conditions

### A. Largest Single POV Owner
Maren must remain the largest single POV owner across the main series.

Current locked map:
- Maren 45
- Ella 27

### B. Majority of Acts
Maren should be plurality POV owner in a majority of Acts unless a specific Act has a stronger rightful emotional owner.

Current:

- Amara plurality: Act 3 — `1` becomes her life
- Ella plurality: Act 4 — PR-H1 missing-choice injury belongs to her

### C. Series Opening / Ending
Maren owns:
- EP001
- EP229
- EP230

### D. Major Checkpoints
Maren owns/receives selected series-defining turns without owning the whole system:
- EP056 Count=1
- EP064 / EP081 Reveal-professional reframes
- EP120 PR-H1 professional accountability
- EP145 Final Approach threshold experience
- EP216 system insertion

### E. No Percentage-Driven Theft
Never reassign an episode to Maren solely to increase her numerical share.

### F. Act Regression
At each Act end ask:
- Does reader still know what Maren wants now?
- Did her flaw/arc materially change?
- Does her next return feel like series home-base rather than a maintenance cameo?

### G. POV Share Is Not Presence Share
Maren may be emotionally/causally central in another character's POV episode.
PR-H1 is the clearest example: Ella owns discovery/confrontation POV while Maren remains the causal subject.

Do not use non-POV presence as excuse to make Maren control the scene.

# 5. Narrative Distance

Default:
**close-medium third person**.

Move closer for:
- embarrassment
- family conflict
- physical/medical pressure
- irreversible choices

Move slightly wider for:
- place reorientation
- time-skip re-anchoring
- public procedural context

Forbidden:
- omniscient explanation of hidden truth before POV knows it
- narrator declaring final philosophical meaning of `human`
- constant explicit internal monologue

Thought is usually rendered through:
perception → attention → decision pressure,
not essay-like self-analysis.

---

# 6. Episode Length

Soft production band:
**roughly 5,500~8,000 Korean characters including spaces**.

Longer major-payoff episodes allowed if earned.
Shorter episodes allowed if paid-installment satisfaction is complete.

Platform requirements may override mechanically.
Exact count is not Canon.

---

# 7. Scene Rhythm

Typical:
2~4 substantial scenes.
Not a hard formula.

Every scene should change at least one:
- plan
- access/status
- relationship temperature
- physical constraint
- public/private knowledge

A scene that only explains should be merged into active pressure.

Across ~5 episodes, avoid all scenes being:
- hearings
- archive searches
- committee meetings
- screen/report reading

Use family/work/physical/social/downtime variation.

---

# 8. Openings

Open on present pressure rather than background explanation.

Preferred:
- active job problem
- interrupted routine
- deadline
- interpersonal mismatch
- physical inconvenience
- consequence arriving from prior choice

Avoid repetitive:
- notification arrives
- summons arrives
- report arrives
- screen staring

First paragraph unfamiliar-jargon load:
prefer 0~1.

---

# 9. Episode End / Hook

Rotate:
- choice
- cost
- relationship turn
- changed physical state
- access gain/loss
- public consequence
- expectation reversal
- quiet irreversible emotional decision

Preferred principle:
**hook is caused by prior action**.

Not every episode needs a shock cliffhanger.

---

# 10. Dialogue

Dialogue amount is a soft diagnostic, not quota.

Rules:
- no complete essay speeches by default
- no perfect thesis/counterthesis/synthesis exchange
- interruptions/avoidance/adjacent answers allowed
- people can be tired, petty, incomplete, wrong
- do not narrate the same conclusion again after dialogue already carried it

Dense legal/technical dialogue must include a practical non-rhetorical constraint:
- deadline
- missing info/person
- capacity/queue
- physical inconvenience
- authority mismatch
- incompatible practical goal

# 13. Ordinary Imperfection

Do NOT inject `human texture` tokens into every episode.

Every 5-episode batch ask:
> Have characters been too efficient, articulate, self-aware, and plot-relevant for too long?

If yes, restore organic ordinary friction where available:
- tiredness
- minor selfishness
- awkwardness
- bad timing
- boredom
- irrelevant preference
- small error
- humor

No mandatory quirk quota.

---

# 14. Description

Priority:
1. used object
2. movement/path/queue/maintenance
3. body state
4. social use of space
5. sensory detail affecting action

Avoid:
- generic cinematic awe
- inventory-list SF
- repeating `vast ship` description

Habitats differ through routines/infrastructure use, not adjective themes.

---

# 15. Science / Law

Preferred technical-explanation order:
`problem → mistaken assumption → practical correction → consequence`.

Preferred law presentation:
- who may act
- who must wait
- whose access/status changes
- what can be appealed

Avoid statute recital unless literal wording is the active object of conflict.

# 19. Specialist POV Guardrail

Technical/legal specialists must not become explanation cameras.

Hard test:
> If half the explanation is removed, does the episode still contain a meaningful choice, loss, access change, or irreversible consequence?

If no:
rewrite or reassign POV.

Reuse established execution specialists before inventing new ones.

---

# 20. After-Draft Episode QA

## Blueprint
- core problem preserved?
- primary choice preserved?
- irreversible consequence preserved?
- next carry preserved?

## Canon
- no silent new rule?
- no forbidden tech/authority?

## POV
- one POV?
- knowledge boundary respected?
- POV chosen for action/cost rather than explanation convenience?

## Voice
- could another character's name be swapped in with no prose change?
If yes: repair.

## Exposition
- any paragraph exists only to explain?

## Hook
- consequence-driven or random drop?

## Anti-AI
- dialogue too symmetric/complete?
- everybody too articulate?
- every detail suspiciously meaningful?
- narrator over-explains theme?

Canon-impact failure:
**STOP. Do not patch around it.**
Run change control.
