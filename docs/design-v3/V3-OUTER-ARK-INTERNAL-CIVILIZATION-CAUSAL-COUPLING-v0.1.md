Goal:
Make internal survival events causally real without reducing every incident to `the system staged it`, and preserve the outer ark as a finite physical machine whose damage matters.

---

# 1. Two meanings of physical reality

The setting needs a precise author-side distinction.

## Outer-ark physical reality
Macroscopic Meridian moving through interstellar space.

Examples:
- reactor output,
- radiators,
- shielding,
- substrate banks,
- external radiation,
- impacts,
- fabrication hardware,
- HUMAN:1 biological vault.

## Lived-civilization causal reality
A D3 civilization is physically implemented by the substrate field, but its citizens experience a human-scale civic world.

Events inside that world are causally real to its persons:
- fire burns them,
- pressure loss kills,
- disease spreads,
- a gunshot wounds,
- food shortage changes politics,
- structural collapse destroys homes.

The event does **not** need to correspond one-for-one to a macroscopic fire or broken bulkhead in the outer ark.

Hard:
`not macroscopic outer-ark matter` ≠ `fake`.

# 2. Four source classes for internal events

Every major incident must have an author-side source class.

## C1 — Endogenous Civic Event
Generated by the ordinary causal dynamics of the lived civilization.

Examples:
- maintenance failure,
- fire,
- murder,
- corruption,
- industrial accident,
- epidemic,
- political crisis,
- relationship cascade,
- sabotage by residents.

The hidden system did not create the event as a test.

This should be the most common source class.

## C2 — Deliberate Boundary Perturbation
The civilization-analysis system intentionally changes a bounded condition to test resilience.

Examples:
- reduced resource reserve,
- communication delay,
- leadership absence,
- altered distribution constraint,
- controlled infrastructure fault,
- synthetic external-signal input.

After injection, outcomes are not scripted.
People can respond in ways the system did not predict.

Hard:
Do not directly puppet individual moral choices.

## C3 — Outer-Ark Constraint Translation
A real macroscopic ship problem forces a change in the substrate field.

Examples:
- power deficit,
- radiator damage,
- radiation storm,
- bank failure,
- interconnect isolation,
- fabrication shortage.

The normal scheduler attempts to preserve internal causal consistency.
Most external problems first appear as:
- reduced execution rate,
- delayed processing,
- lower redundancy,
- maintenance scarcity,
- altered resource ceiling.

They do NOT automatically appear as an arbitrary internal explosion.

## C4 — Integrity Fault / Leakage
A real substrate fault breaks the normal abstraction boundary.

Examples:
- cross-lineage memory contamination,
- wrong infrastructure version metadata,
- impossible repair history,
- identity-routing error,
- duplicated/missing records,
- protagonist Act2 failover misbinding.

This is the rarest source class and the main route by which hidden ontology leaks into the story.

# 3. Why C3 does not equal `god sends disaster`

Outer resource loss should normally affect **capacity**, not author arbitrary plot events.

Example:
Outer radiator damage reduces the substrate's safe thermal budget.

Normal response order:
1. slow cheap forecasts,
2. reduce D1/D2 depth,
3. slow subjective time in selected high-depth worlds where continuity allows,
4. postpone nonessential deepening,
5. quiesce protected D3 only if necessary,
6. accept risk only after lower-harm options are exhausted.

It would be bad design if:
`radiator -10% → protagonist's apartment suddenly catches fire`.

However reduced internal maintenance/resource capacity can **causally raise** fire risk over time.
That is acceptable because the chain is legible.

# 4. Internal resource accounting

A lived Meridian contains internally meaningful resources:
- electricity,
- water,
- food,
- air-processing capacity,
- replacement material,
- medical capacity,
- housing,
- labor/time.

These are not mere decorative counters.
They constrain internal causal behavior.

The substrate system maintains the experienced world's conservation/engineering grammar.
It does not casually spawn unlimited internal resources just because outer compute exists.

Why:
The purpose of lived histories is to observe decision-making under coherent conditions.
If the system cheats whenever convenient, the results become useless.

Therefore:
- internal engineers can genuinely run out of spare parts,
- food can genuinely fail within the lineage,
- a destroyed district cannot be instantly restored without an in-world causal process,
- deaths remain deaths within that lineage.

---

# 5. Outer resource vs internal resource

Do not make a naive one-to-one conversion.

Outer ark might spend more electrical power while an internal civilization experiences a low-energy society, because outer power is compute/implementation cost.

Conversely an internally powerful reactor is part of the civilization's causal state, not necessarily a literal corresponding reactor outside.

Author-side mapping:

`outer resources determine how faithfully/quickly/safely the lived world can be implemented`

while

`internal resources determine what citizens can causally do inside that world`.

This distinction is critical.

# 6. Subjective time under outer stress

Preferred response to ordinary outer scarcity:
**time scaling before reality corruption.**

If outer compute/thermal capacity falls:
- a D3 world can run more slowly relative to outer time,
- citizens do not necessarily feel slow motion,
- their local clocks remain coherent.

Hard limits:
- no arbitrary infinite acceleration,
- synchronization with external mission events can matter,
- real-time contact windows may require costly D4 allocation,
- emergency failover can still fail.

This helps explain how many histories can be explored over a centuries-long trip without giving infinite free compute.

---

# 7. Cross-scale catastrophic failure

Some outer failures can permanently kill internal civilizations.

Examples:
- substrate bank physically destroyed,
- unrecoverable anchor/interconnect loss,
- radiation damage beyond correction,
- fire/impact destroys redundant copies before failover completes.

Reader-facing people may experience the final failure as:
- sudden infrastructure collapse,
- escalating impossible faults,
- discontinuity,
- or no understandable warning at all.

Do not always translate catastrophic outer failure into a neat in-world apocalypse.
Sometimes a lineage simply becomes inaccessible/lost from the outer perspective.

This is morally equivalent to loss, not `closing a file`.

# 8. Act2 recommended source stack

To avoid `system did everything`, the first giant survival arc should be **compound-source**.

Leading stack:

1. `C1` — ordinary internal maintenance/industrial defect begins the crisis.
2. human decisions and possible sabotage amplify it.
3. a contemporaneous `C3` outer thermal/power stress quietly reduces redundancy.
4. emergency repairs push a substrate routing boundary.
5. at the climax, a `C4` integrity fault causes the protagonist's exceptional failover misbinding.

Therefore:
- the disaster was not staged just to move him,
- human choices remain consequential,
- the hidden outer layer matters,
- the apparent regression is still extraordinarily rare.

This is the current strongest Act2 mechanism.

# 9. Deliberate stress tests

C2 must exist because it is part of the premise, but not dominate the series.

Rule candidate for major arcs:
Across 8~10 major arcs, source causes should rotate.

Recommended distribution tendency, not quota:
- many C1-dominant,
- several C2-dominant,
- several C3-influenced,
- very few C4-dominant.

A C2 event may later be mistaken for C1 or vice versa.

The system itself may also misclassify an actual novel event as a known test family.

# 10. Novelty channel

The system's purpose requires it to encounter outcomes it did not generate.

Novelty can arise from:
- new social coalitions,
- unexpected forgiveness,
- panic cascades,
- spontaneous altruism,
- black-market adaptation,
- an unknown person becoming pivotal,
- technical improvisation,
- children/families creating new obligations,
- institutional interpretation not present in training histories.

The system can model probabilities without knowing which path will actually become causally decisive.

This prevents omniscience.

# 11. Protagonist profession interaction

The protagonist's damage-analysis job concerns **internal civic infrastructure** at first.

He can establish:
- a crack does not match the documented load,
- a fire pattern predates the reported incident,
- a repair alloy/version should not exist yet,
- a bulkhead was rebuilt under an unknown work order.

He cannot directly infer:
- D3 execution depth,
- substrate banks,
- number of lineages,
- HUMAN:1.

Late in the story, clues connect ordinary structural anomalies to C4 integrity failures and lineage metadata.

His profession reveals contradictions, not ontology answers.

# 12. Secrecy preservation

Why normal science does not discover the outer layer:
- local physics is internally consistent,
- substrate scheduler hides implementation details,
- C1 events dominate everyday life,
- C3 stress is usually translated through lawful capacity changes,
- C4 leakage is extremely rare,
- cross-history evidence is fragmented and socially explainable,
- no ordinary civic instrument can address the outer substrate directly.

A civilization can become scientifically sophisticated while remaining epistemically enclosed about its implementation layer.

---

# 13. Mystery clue hierarchy

Low intensity:
- odd maintenance timestamp,
- unmatched work order,
- one remembered layout discrepancy.

Medium:
- repeated material/version mismatch,
- infrastructure capacity inconsistent with local records,
- two people giving incompatible but internally coherent incident histories.

High:
- lineage identifiers,
- substrate integrity terminology,
- impossible civilization index counts,
- evidence that the protagonist's continuity crossed a lineage boundary.

Do not jump from low to high in Act1.

---

# 14. Harness / fail conditions

FAIL if:
1. every internal disaster maps to an outer hardware fault.
2. every important disaster was deliberately staged.
3. outer power can arbitrarily rewrite internal causality.
4. citizens' engineering efforts are meaningless because the substrate can simply cheat.
5. protagonist can diagnose the whole ontology from one crack.
6. external physical damage never causes permanent stakes.
7. internal resource shortages are treated as fake once the reveal occurs.
8. C4 anomalies become common enough that science should already know the truth.

PASS if:
- internal causality stands on its own,
- outer constraints matter without puppeteering events,
- deliberate experiments are only one source family,
- rare integrity failures generate the core mystery.
