# 3. Phase B — The small route choice
EP237~244
Week 2~3

## Transit Dispatcher — major emergent setup/payoff begins
A central route is repeatedly overloaded/partially unavailable.
Dispatcher uses ordinary accumulated knowledge to create a temporary unofficial-looking but lawful-enough routing pattern.

Immediate effect:
- a medical/supply group reaches a Midring corridor earlier,
- another group is delayed and angry.

Secondary effect:
- Juno's network receives people/resources it would otherwise not have,
- a relationship forms between two previously distrustful local groups,
- later this becomes the social basis of a neutral corridor.

Protagonist is unaware of the original routing choice.

## School/community
ordinary caregivers improvise child/family transport under reduced schedules.

## Mira
initially dislikes dispatcher deviation because it complicates network fairness/audit.
Later understands its local rationale without simply declaring it heroic.

### Purpose
Show path dependence before the reader knows how large the consequence will become.

# 5. Phase D — Factions form around real needs
EP253~261
Week 4~6

Do NOT create simple government vs rebels binary.

Possible overlapping groups:
- district mutual-aid coalition,
- labor/technical safety bloc,
- security-hardline civic faction,
- anti-secrecy protest network,
- opportunistic black-market/violent cells,
- families demanding restoration priority.

People can belong to more than one network.

## Mira ↔ Juno

Juno's network is now powerful enough to exclude outsiders if it chooses.

## Rafi ↔ Leila
Medical scarcity inverts expectations:
- Rafi may support stopping one rescue because field capacity is collapsing,
- Leila may authorize a risky individualized treatment because system-level rule is failing one person unfairly.

## Niko ↔ Gideon

- Gideon fears improvisation turning utilities into weapons.

## Protagonist
tries to keep technical network operational but cannot decide which district politically deserves power first.

### Relationship output
At least 3 known relationships change permanently in this phase.

# 6. Phase E — Infrastructure becomes leverage
EP262~270
Week 6~8

## Escalation
Some groups begin using access to infrastructure/logistics as bargaining power.
Not everyone using leverage is an extremist.

Examples:
- local workers refuse unsafe restart,
- a district blocks freight until medical allocation changes,
- violent cell attempts to seize a control point,
- emergency authority threatens compulsory staffing.

## Protagonist's key Act6 decision
Selected direction:
A damaged high-capacity transfer/utility path can be restored quickly only by accepting a failure mode that could cascade catastrophically under current unstable loads.

Political pressure:
restoring it would immediately relieve one large district/resource crisis.

Protagonist chooses/recommends **physical disablement or slower safe isolation**, making rapid restoration impossible.

Consequence:
- prevents a larger technical disaster,
- worsens scarcity for thousands in short term,
- some people see him as responsible for their suffering,
- this is NOT another `seal trapped people behind a door` copy of Arun/Act2.

Selene/civic authority own access/order consequences; protagonist owns technical irreversibility.

## Apparent hero causes harm
A known protector/organizer uses a virtue (order, rescue, solidarity, truth) in a way that amplifies conflict.

## Distrusted person shows compassion
Could be Cassian or another actor, but not automatically `rich man redeems himself`.
One genuine compassionate action does not erase prior exploitation.

# 7. Phase F — Neutral corridor payoff
EP268~274 overlaps Phase E/F
Week 7~9

The earlier Transit Dispatcher choice has now produced unexpected social connectivity.

Causal chain:
Dispatcher reroute
→ medical/community contact
→ Juno network trust edge
→ Mira identifies viable transport pattern
→ Rafi/Leila support medical corridor
→ Selene accepts bounded neutral-access guarantee
→ local groups cooperate enough to move civilians/supplies.

Protagonist is NOT intermediary.

Outcome:
- saves a meaningful population group,
- prevents one confrontation from escalating,
- does not `save civilization` alone.

Delayed recognition:
Only later do characters realize the original route decision mattered.

This is the main series proof of emergent importance.

# 12. Violence density

Avoid 50 episodes of constant gunfire.

Suggested rhythm:
- first 15 episodes: mostly scarcity/politics/logistics,
- middle: intermittent violence/sabotage,
- late: one or two concentrated armed/infrastructure confrontations,
- substantial aftermath/negotiation/repair.

This keeps Act6 distinct from pure war fiction.

---

# 13. Protagonist-centered POV rule

Act6 is the lowest protagonist-share Act:
working diagnostic **~50~65% protagonist-centered scenes**.

Still:
- no more than ~2~3 consecutive full episodes without protagonist unless one independent causal chain genuinely needs it,
- return to protagonist before reader loses emotional spine,
- other POVs should often be recurring characters, not new one-offs.
