# V3 MERIDIAN BACKGROUND / ART-DIRECTION BIBLE v0.1

Status: `IP/VISUAL DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / NOT CANON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-20
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Make Meridian visually and sensorially recognizable as a centuries-old city-civilization, not a generic spaceship hallway set.

This file controls reader/illustration-facing background grammar. It does NOT define the microscopic substrate's literal external geometry.

## Global art law

1. Meridian daily life must look like **a city built inside engineering constraints**, not a naval ship occupied for 450 years without cultural change.
2. Public-facing areas hide more infrastructure; work/service zones expose more of it.
3. Age appears as **layering, retrofit and compatibility**, not universal rust/grime.
4. Every major zone receives 3~5 stable visual anchors only.
5. Lighting changes by function/time/social use, not one neon cyberpunk palette.
6. Safety signage and wayfinding are abundant but readable; no wall of sci-fi glyphs.
7. Emergency mode visibly transforms familiar places.
8. Act6 damage/occupation states should alter known backgrounds rather than constantly invent new ones.
9. Act9 settlement should inherit visual DNA from Meridian, producing completion/continuity.
10. No district is a game biome/caste costume zone.

# B00 — Meridian exterior / macro silhouette

Reader-facing exposure:
rare until later Acts; mostly through external-work scenes, records, observation, arrival.

## Selected silhouette principle
**Long axial service/propulsion spine + distributed protected civic/habitat masses + large thermal rejection structures + forward protection geometry + external work/staging structures.**

Do NOT lock a single decorative sleek starship hull that hides all function.

Visual anchors:
1. **axial spine** — gives direction/orientation,
2. **layered forward protection mass** — visually denser/armored leading end,
3. **thermal rejection arrays/radiator structures** — large fragile-looking surfaces deployed away from protected mass where mission state permits,
4. **inhabited/industrial modules integrated as generations of additions**, not identical pods,
5. **outer works lattice / service access structures** visible around maintenance zones.

Surface language:
- repaired/changed panels,
- micrometeoroid/radiation shielding layers,
- thermal blankets/coatings only where functional,
- identification and maintenance marks,
- few large decorative logos.

Color logic:
material/thermal/safety driven; do not make white NASA aesthetic universal.

Act9 transformation:
external silhouette changes because staged settlement hardware has been removed/deployed and some radiator/control infrastructure is reconfigured. The ship should look visibly **used to create a future**, not parked unchanged as monument.

# B01 — Central / Spine

Core feeling:
**civic convergence + old structure under public modernization.**

Geometry:
- broad interchanges and public halls,
- long axial sightlines at selected points,
- compressed vertical service layers hidden behind cleaner public shells,
- not all institutions in one megahall.

Materials:
- durable composite/stone-like civic surfaces,
- metal edge/protection strips,
- repaired but intentionally finished public panels,
- occasional older structural ribs visible as design/history rather than ruin.

Lighting:
- broad diffuse public lighting,
- warmer social/civic zones,
- sharper neutral transit interfaces.

Sound:
- layered crowd/announcement/transit hum,
- public events echo differently from residential districts.

Smell/air:
mostly neutral conditioned air; food/crowd/public greenery create local pockets.

Wayfinding:
- large directional bands,
- floor/edge route logic,
- multilingual/text+symbol redundancy,
- accessibility tactile/haptic paths where appropriate.

Landmarks:
1. major transfer concourse with a recognizable old structural rib or seam retained through renovations,
2. public archive/civic hall frontage with temporary protest/exhibition space.

Emergency transformation:
public screens become route/closure information, barriers split flow, open halls become controlled staging—not instant military bunker.

Act6:
queue barriers, temporary resource desks, damaged information trust; physical hall may stay intact while social meaning changes.

Act9 descendant motif:
settlement governance spaces reuse the same readable public-wayfinding principles but on rougher unfinished infrastructure.

# B02 — Transfer Belt

Core feeling:
**movement never fully stops.**

Geometry:
- repeated transfer halls,
- freight interfaces separated but visible through barriers/windows/service cuts,
- ramps/platforms/short vertical transitions,
- back-of-house service routes close to public flow.

Materials:
- impact-resistant floor/wall surfaces,
- replaceable corner panels,
- route-color/material bands used sparingly,
- more visible patch replacement than Central.

Lighting:
- bright functional at hubs,
- darker/warmer food/service pockets,
- 24-hour shift continuity means less `night mode` than residential zones.

Sound:
- arrival tones,
- wheel/rail/drive hum,
- loading impacts,
- multilingual conversation,
- periodic quiet when a line closes—absence of normal sound becomes ominous.

Smell:
quick meals, warm machinery, cleaned freight areas, occasional industrial odors at controlled interfaces.

Crowd rhythm:
peaks around shift changes; people know shortcuts.

Wayfinding:
route strips, destination blocks, service icons; locals abbreviate names.

Landmarks:
1. major interchange where passenger and freight timing visibly intersect,
2. dispatch balcony/control frontage or visible routing wall, not omniscient command center.

Emergency transformation:
route lines flip to closure/isolation; stranded crowds occupy normally transient spaces; cargo areas become evacuation/support staging.

Act6:
temporary community/service tables appear beside formal logistics; route patches and hand-written/printed fallback signs proliferate.

Act9 descendant:
settlement cargo nodes inherit the standardized attachment/signage grammar of Transfer Belt.

# B03 — Midring

Core feeling:
**home, routine, repeated faces.**

Geometry:
- mixed mid-rise housing/community blocks,
- courtyards/local corridors,
- small commercial fronts,
- schools/clinics embedded rather than isolated monumental campuses,
- ceiling/space varies by renovation era.

Materials:
- more personal finishes,
- household door surfaces/customized panels,
- repaired flooring and local art,
- community notice areas both digital and physical.

Lighting:
- warmer domestic/commercial spectrum,
- simulated day-cycle more noticeable,
- homes differ genuinely.

Sound:
children, cooking/service, local transit, neighboring life, ventilation as background rather than dominant machine noise.

Smell:
food, laundry/cleaning, planted areas, local businesses.

Landmarks:
1. Juno's venue/community-service node candidate,
2. protagonist family/local residential route,
3. neighborhood school/courtyard/park element recurring without clue function.

Wayfinding:
less formal; locals navigate by stores, corners, schools and route stops.

Emergency transformation:
residential doors sealed/opened, school/community spaces become refuges, personal belongings spill into public zones, corridor lighting shifts to emergency guidance.

Act6:
mutual aid posters, ration/service queues, blocked/private access, improvised charging/cooking/community points; the same warm neighborhood can become socially divided without turning into slum warfare set.

Act9 descendant:
first settlement residential commons should borrow Midring's courtyard/community logic rather than start as sterile barracks.

# B04 — Garden Commons

Core feeling:
**intentional spaciousness in a closed civilization.**

Geometry:
- taller open-feeling chambers or connected atria,
- layered paths, planted terraces, sports/recreation clearings,
- production/research interfaces partially visible but not romanticized as total life support.

Materials:
- moisture-resistant structural surfaces,
- wood-like/biopolymer/stone-like civic finishes used where practical,
- many surfaces still visibly engineered.

Lighting:
- strongest daylight simulation in city,
- dynamic spectrum for plants/humans by zone,
- shadows and visual depth important.

Sound:
water features/plant systems/people/sport, but mechanical airflow remains subtly present.

Smell:
plant/soil/substrate/food production aromas—one of few zones where citizens consciously notice `fresh` smell variation.

Landmarks:
1. broad recreation terrace/field,
2. public greenhouse interface or garden walk tied to protagonist family life,
3. one elevated view revealing city enclosure subtly, preventing fake Earth park illusion.

Emergency transformation:
rare. When used, contrast is powerful: public leisure space becomes temporary care/rest/assembly, not routine disaster stage.

Act6:
arguments over allocation/use may change access, but preserve some ordinary life to prevent total grimness.

Act9 descendant:
surface/settlement green spaces visually echo Garden Commons but have genuinely uncontrolled sky/light/weather—important sensory payoff.

# B05 — Old Works

Core feeling:
**layers of labor and repair.**

Geometry:
- large and small scales mixed: fabrication bays, narrow service corridors, deep access cavities, structural junctions,
- irregular retrofit creates awkward routes,
- not one endless dark maze.

Materials:
- exposed metal/composite structural members,
- thermal discoloration,
- replaced panels with visible generation differences,
- stencils/inspection marks,
- sealant/patch geometry,
- floor surfaces scarred by real work.

Lighting:
- task lights dominate,
- broad bay lighting in active workspaces,
- service corridors use practical strip/point lighting,
- darkness is maintenance state, not aesthetic default.

Sound:
fabrication, ventilation, distant impacts, pumps, tugs, alarms during shifts; hearing protection in some zones.

Smell:
warm machinery, coolant/cleaners/material processing in bounded areas.

Wayfinding:
work codes, hazard boundaries, old/new identifiers layered; perfect place for physical history mismatch because markings survive retrofits.

Landmarks:
1. protagonist diagnostics base / failure board area,
2. recognizable structural junction/access route reused across Acts,
3. old fabrication/maintenance bay where machine generations coexist.

Emergency transformation:
isolation shutters, red/amber hazard lines, smoke/condensation, disabled work machines becoming obstacles, temporary markings everywhere.

Act5:
legacy contradiction appears through **normal known space becoming physically inconsistent**, not secret dungeon reveal.

Act6:
parts stripped/cannibalized; some bays dormant; others overcrowded emergency work hubs.

Act9 descendant:
settlement field workshops visibly inherit Old Works' modular repair culture but with new environmental wear.

# B06 — Med-University Cluster

Core feeling:
**care, learning, public traffic—not secret lab.**

Geometry:
- hospital/clinic wings,
- rehab areas,
- teaching/meeting spaces,
- public waiting circulation,
- service/logistics corridors clearly separate but intersecting.

Materials:
- easy-clean surfaces but not blank white,
- acoustic softening,
- replaceable privacy partitions,
- wayfinding highly legible.

Lighting:
- neutral clinical task lighting,
- warmer patient/recovery zones,
- night shift lighting subdued but operational.

Sound:
quiet alert tones, wheeled equipment, voices, ventilation; crisis creates queue/noise rather than sirens everywhere.

Smell:
cleaning/medical materials, food/coffee/public life nearby.

Landmarks:
1. trauma handoff zone,
2. rehab/public consultation corridor,
3. teaching commons where students make institution feel alive.

Emergency transformation:
triage zones physically redraw space; transfer cradles accumulate; elective areas become overflow; privacy boundaries become harder to maintain.

Act8:
HUMAN/D3 medical scenes must remain within this familiar care grammar; no sudden secret chrome womb cathedral.

Act9 descendant:
settlement clinic is smaller/rougher but visually inherits cradle interfaces, privacy partitions and care workflow.

# B07 — Rim / Expansion Belt

Core feeling:
**newer, planned, slightly unfinished social future.**

Geometry:
- broader modular residential corridors in parts,
- newer service cores,
- construction interfaces/empty future capacity in some segments,
- transit dependence visibly stronger.

Materials:
- more consistent panel generation,
- fewer layered repairs at opening,
- customizable interior modules.

Lighting:
cleaner integrated fixtures, slightly more current design language.

Sound:
quieter housing, construction/expansion pockets, longer transit intervals.

Landmarks:
1. newer family plaza/school-service node,
2. expansion/construction edge where citizens can see unfinished city growth.

Emergency transformation:
centralized dependencies make outages/transport cuts more disruptive despite newer infrastructure.

Act6:
visual irony—new clean surfaces with taped-off systems, stalled development, improvised local services.

Act9 descendant:
some Rim residents/skills naturally connect to settlement/new-build culture without becoming a faction uniform.

# B08 — Legacy Service Layer

Core feeling:
**obsolete/retired technical strata embedded discontinuously in the city.**

Not:
ancient alien ruins, secret undercity, endless dungeon.

Geometry:
- short disconnected old routes,
- sealed utility rooms,
- awkward bypasses,
- abandoned interfaces inside active structures,
- spaces reachable only for real maintenance reasons.

Materials:
- older standard modules,
- obsolete labels,
- repeated replacement scars,
- occasional preserved historical object by accident rather than museum curation.

Lighting:
usually restored portable/task lighting during entry; not permanently spooky green darkness.

Sound:
quiet primarily because systems are inactive; active nearby structure transmits vibration/noise through walls.

Smell:
stale sealants, dust-like particulates/cleaned surfaces, dormant machine spaces—within physically plausible closed-environment handling.

Act5 use:
contradiction comes from repair/material/traffic history, not spooky murals explaining truth.

Collectibility:
retired signs/interface shells may have ordinary heritage interest, but unauthorized salvage is regulated.

# Signage / graphic language

Principles:
- text remains common,
- icons supplement rather than replace language,
- hazard signage prioritizes shape/position/redundancy,
- service markings can show generations,
- personal/local stickers/patches exist where permitted,
- no undecipherable sci-fi rune aesthetic.

Historical layering clue:
old identifiers can survive beneath new labels, but most are boring migration history. Only a few become mystery evidence.

# Normal → emergency visual transformation grammar

Use familiar background change as tension:

Normal:
open routes / social clutter / ordinary light / mixed sound / flexible access.

Emergency Level 1~2:
route arrows / hazard boundaries / responders / localized closures / altered lighting.

Major Act2:
segmentation shutters / smoke-heat-condensation / dead screens / local backup lights / abandoned personal objects / stranded vehicles.

Act6 prolonged crisis:
repair patches / ration/service desks / layered temporary signage / missing panels/parts / improvised local power / contested access / worn staff/citizens.

Recovery:
do not instantly reset. Repairs remain visible, businesses/routes change, memorial/social traces persist.
