# OA-C — Layered Industrial Spine Ark

Form:
**forward protection mass → protected substrate/archive vault region → distributed fabrication/cargo/service blocks along a strong axial spine → large separated thermal-rejection structures → aft propulsion/deceleration complex**, with robotic Outer Works running along the structure.

This is not a sleek hull enclosing everything.
It is a **deep-space industrial carrier built around protected cores, service access and thermal geometry**.

## Silhouette anchors
1. **Forward shield head**
   - thick, blunt, layered protective mass;
   - visually broader/denser than most of spine;
   - travel direction instantly readable.

2. **Protected vault core**
   - compact nested/armored volumes behind shield;
   - houses critical substrate/biological/mission continuity systems;
   - no windows/city facade.

3. **Axial service spine**
   - carries structural load, power/coolant/data trunks, docking/service routes;
   - irregular blocks reflect centuries of replacements and additions.

4. **Fabrication / feedstock / settlement blocks**
   - physically chunky, replaceable modules;
   - some shrink/disappear during Act9 deployment.

5. **Thermal rejection fields**
   - large panels/booms/arrays separated from hot/protected masses;
   - configuration can change by mission phase;
   - visually fragile compared with shield/core.

6. **Aft propulsion complex**
   - clear exclusion geometry;
   - dedicated thermal/service infrastructure;
   - not a decorative glowing engine cone.

7. **Outer robotic service lanes / docking points**
   - external craft scale is tiny against Ark structure;
   - helps visual model/diorama appeal.

## Pros
- best match to current v3 ontology,
- supports real maintenance/craft ecology,
- permits visually obvious heat/shield/fabrication tradeoffs,
- settlement physically changes ship silhouette,
- avoids fake 300k macroscopic habitat.

## Cons
- less instantly `city ship` from outside,
- must avoid generic kitbash space-industrial look,
- exact size/mass cannot be honestly frozen yet.

# Scale policy

Do NOT freeze exact dimensions from aesthetics.

Current safe description:
- **macroscopically enormous industrial interstellar craft**, but much smaller/denser than a ship built to physically house 300k biological humans in kilometer-scale cities;
- main structural length may plausibly be `hundreds of meters to multi-kilometer class` depending final propulsion/shield/feedstock assumptions;
- deployed radiator span can visually rival or exceed core-body dimensions;
- settlement cargo/fabrication mass may dominate external bulk more than biological living quarters.

Hard:
No exact `3 km / 8 km / 10^12 kg` in current v3 Canon until a later quantitative freeze justifies it.

# Thermal visual law

Current spacecraft engineering supports the general principle that high heat loads require substantial radiator/heat-rejection area; large radiator systems can be a major spacecraft architecture element.

Fictional implication:
- radiator fields should be visually prominent,
- they need clear line-of-sight/thermal geometry,
- damage/orientation/service can constrain operations,
- not every radiator is permanently fully deployed in every mission phase.

The exact radiator technology is far-future and stays open.

---

# Shield visual law

Forward protection is layered and mission-critical.

Do not claim one material stops all interstellar dust/radiation.

Visual logic:
- replaceable/sacrificial outer layers,
- standoff sensing/charged-particle management may exist,
- critical vaults sit behind protection geometry,
- service/fabrication system must be able to inspect/replace accessible protection components over centuries.

Exact high-velocity dust mitigation remains speculative engineering; avoid fake certainty.
