## Global character visual law

A core character should be recognizable through **2~4 stable anchors**, not a costume code.

Each character gets:
1. silhouette,
2. face/hair impression,
3. ordinary clothing grammar,
4. work layer,
5. material anchor(s),
6. movement/gesture,
7. crisis transformation,
8. H-A/H-B delta only where narratively useful.

Hard:
- no beauty-score hierarchy,
- no one-color-per-character superhero coding,
- no mandatory cultural costume based on surname,
- no secret lineage clues hidden in appearance,
- no everyone-is-fashion-model cast,
- visible wear/repair should follow life/work, not aesthetic grime everywhere.

# C01 — Protagonist / NAME TBD

Age:
36~38.

## Silhouette
- medium-to-tall adult male range,
- lean practical build rather than gym-hero bulk,
- shoulders/neck show years of confined-space inspection posture,
- not visibly military.

Distance recognition:
**compact field jacket + narrow hard equipment case + habit of crouching/leaning close to surfaces rather than standing heroically in center.**

## Face / hair
- ordinary mature face with one slightly tired/alert quality,
- hair practical but not buzz-cut; short-to-medium, often flattened/untidy after protective head layer,
- faint under-eye fatigue can increase in crisis Acts,
- no signature scar that screams protagonist.

## Civilian clothes
- durable dark/neutral trousers,
- simple layered tops,
- repaired but well-kept jacket/overshirt,
- prefers clothes that survive kneeling/crawling rather than fashionable formal pieces,
- at family/community scenes he should look clearly civilian, not on-call astronaut.

## Work layer
- close-fitting inspection underlayer,
- abrasion-resistant overshell with removable hazard modules,
- low-profile protective head/eye unit when needed,
- gloves chosen by task rather than always worn,
- compact tether/anchor hardware in service/low-g spaces.

Workwear visual rule:
**asymmetry comes from tool placement/use, not decorative tactical straps.**

## Material anchors
1. **damage-marking stylus / physical marker kit** — leaves visible temporary marks, numbers, arrows and fracture-sequence notes on authorized surfaces.
2. **narrow field case** — contains sampling tabs, contact sensors, calibration blocks and gloves; visibly repaired shell across Acts.
3. **old repaired household item at home** — candidate ceramic/composite cup or hand tool; not heritage clue, simply shows repair habit.

## Movement anchor
- touches/looks along edges, seams, heat discoloration and fasteners before looking at a screen,
- in conversation under stress he turns toward the actual damaged object rather than holding eye contact,
- when afraid he physically narrows options: closes a latch, moves someone behind a line, takes control of tools.

## Act transformations
Act1:
clean but used field kit.

Act2:
outer shell scorched/cut; one tool lost; replacement is borrowed/mismatched.

Act3 H-B:
kit/body are nearly familiar but one or two banal life-history details differ—e.g. a case repair patch, certification sticker, callus/scar pattern. These remain subtle evidence, not immediate proof.

Act6:
more visible field wear; he starts carrying fewer tools and more isolation tags/lockout hardware, reflecting shift from `fix` to `prevent cascade`.

Act9:
old field case still exists but is no longer complete; one early tool is used in settlement commissioning context as familiarity return, not sacred relic.

## Figure/cover hook
Recognizable by **field-case silhouette + inspection posture + layered civilian/technical clothing**, not a weapon.

# H-A / H-B visual delta budget

Use only **life-history-derived** differences:
- haircut,
- scar/callus,
- clothing preference,
- work badge/certification,
- personal object,
- home decoration,
- relationship-owned item.

Avoid:
- random eye-color/body changes merely to signal timelines,
- everyone receiving a redesign,
- color-coded alternate versions.

Early Act3 target:
3~5 high-salience visual/material deltas across the whole cast are enough.
