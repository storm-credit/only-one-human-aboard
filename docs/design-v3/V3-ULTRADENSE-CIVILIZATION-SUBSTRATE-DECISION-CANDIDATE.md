# V3 — ULTRADENSE PHYSICAL CIVILIZATION SUBSTRATE — DECISION CANDIDATE

Status: `P0 LEADING CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》
Supersedes as working direction: the earlier provisional assumption that only `2~3 full civilization branches` can exist at once.

---

# 0. Decision Summary

The leading v3 ontology is NOT:
- parallel universes,
- pocket universes,
- multiple physical generation ships,
- three or four duplicated cities,
- a simple game simulation.

The leading model is:

> **One physical interstellar ship contains an astronomically large field of ultradense physical civilization substrates. Each substrate can carry and execute a civilization-state lineage. The ship continuously generates, evaluates, deepens, branches, recombines, and learns from an enormous possibility space while preserving exactly one Natural-Origin HUMAN.**

Author-side short form:
**Physical Ark + Ultradense Civilization Field (UCF).**

Reader-facing terminology should be delayed.

---

# 1. Physical Meridian

There is exactly one macroscopic interstellar vessel: Meridian.

It physically contains:
- propulsion,
- power generation,
- thermal control,
- external sensors,
- communications,
- repair/manufacturing systems,
- biological/genetic archives,
- settlement equipment,
- civilization substrate banks,
- and the independent preservation system for **HUMAN:1**.

The ship itself moves forward through external physical time. It does not literally time-travel backward.

---

# 2. Civilization Substrate Scale

User-intent target:
- civilization carriers may have external physical footprints on the order of molecular/atomic or even more speculative subatomic-scale engineered matter.
- this is NOT an atom containing a naturally occurring galaxy-sized space.
- it is an advanced physical information/experience substrate whose external scale is extremely small.

Hard SF warning:
Current known physics does not justify storing a fully lived 300,000-person civilization in one ordinary atom/electron.
Therefore the novel must treat this as **far-future speculative matter/computation engineering**, not as ordinary atomic physics.

Do not over-explain the mechanism early.

---

# 3. Astronomical Multiplicity

If civilization substrate units are ultradense, Meridian can physically contain an enormous number of them.

Therefore the working model is NOT:
`A/B/C = only three civilization worlds.`

Instead:
- possibility states may be effectively uncountable at human scale,
- civilization lineages may number in the millions, billions, trillions, or far beyond,
- exact total count is intentionally not frozen,
- the number is finite because Meridian is finite,
- but it can be so large that individual citizens cannot meaningfully conceptualize it.

Narrative rule:
**Never require the reader to track the global count.**
The vastness is background ontology; the story remains centered on a small human-scale cast.

---

# 4. Multi-Resolution Civilization Search

Not every possible civilization receives equal physical/compute depth.

The system explores at multiple levels.

## R0 — Possibility Generation
Inputs include:
- random perturbations,
- probabilistic variables,
- historical data,
- institutional changes,
- resource changes,
- accidents,
- social network perturbations,
- external physical events,
- deliberately adversarial scenarios.

Potential count can be astronomical.

## R1 — Low-Resolution Civilization Model
Agent/statistical society models.
No claim that each R1 agent is a full moral person.
Used for broad pruning.

## R2 — Deep Social Model
Higher-resolution identity, relationship, institutional and behavioral dynamics.
Can reveal unexpected social cascades.

## R3 — Lived Civilization State
A civilization is executed at sufficient fidelity that its persons possess real subjective continuity, relationships, suffering, choice and responsibility.

This is where the novel's morally significant histories occur.

Important correction:
There is **no fixed 2~3 hard cap** on R3 states in current design.
The upper bound remains open pending science/energy regression.
It may be extremely large, but still constrained by power, heat, substrate integrity, synchronization and error rates.

---

# 5. Why Lived Histories Are Needed

If Meridian could perfectly predict people from low-resolution simulation, the story engine would collapse.

It cannot.

Human-person behavior contains nonlinear cascades:

`individual choice → relationship shift → group reaction → institutional response → new actor becomes important → civilization-scale consequence`

Examples of drivers:
- family,
- friendship,
- love,
- grief,
- loyalty,
- resentment,
- altruism,
- fear,
- group identity,
- chance meetings,
- unmodeled physical accidents,
- novel problem-solving.

The key informational events are often **Novel Choices** that did not appear in the prior prediction set.

Therefore lived history is not merely a prettier simulation. It is how Meridian discovers behavior its own models could not derive.

---

# 6. Character Importance Is Emergent

There is no permanent system list called `MAIN CHARACTERS`.

Core recurring characters may become civilization attractors because their roles, relationships and decision patterns repeatedly matter.

But:
- Rowan can be irrelevant or die early in some histories.
- Selene can be hero, authoritarian, minor officer, or absent.
- Mira may never meet Rowan.
- an otherwise anonymous cook, child, courier, technician, teacher or medic may become civilization-critical in one specific lineage.

Rule:
> **Importance is a result, not a starting privilege.**

This is central to the series philosophy.

---

# 7. Same Person, Different Moral Outcome

Repeated identity lineages can preserve recognizable tendencies without preserving fixed choices.

Possible outcomes:
- same person makes the same choice across many histories,
- same person makes opposite choices under changed conditions,
- same person invents a third option not present in prior histories,
- a group relationship changes the person's decision,
- one stranger changes the whole cascade.

This allows good/evil/no-fixed-nature arguments to emerge through action rather than exposition.

---

# 8. Reader-Facing Multi-History Grammar

Do NOT explain the ontology by saying:
`In another civilization instance he was evil.`

Instead show it socially.

Examples:
- one survivor flinches when a currently kind official enters the room,
- another says: `저 사람을 왜 믿어요?`
- Rowan answers: `무슨 소리야. 저 사람은 우리를 살렸어.`
- the survivor replies: `당신한테는 그랬겠죠.`

Or:
- a child trusts a feared security officer because in another lived history that officer raised the child after the parents died.
- a woman refuses help from a doctor who is currently compassionate because she remembers that doctor choosing mass triage in another history.

Rule:
**Contradictory testimony should precede ontology explanation.**

---

# 9. Cross-History Memory Is NOT a Feature

Critical correction:
Cross-history memory is not a normal system capability available to citizens.

It is a **rare critical fault class**.

Possible causes:
- identity-address collision,
- lineage reconciliation error,
- corrupted pointer/reference,
- damaged isolation boundary,
- substrate interference,
- partial recovery from a failed merge,
- extremely rare physical defect.

Most citizens never experience explicit cross-history memory.

Even among anomalies, most get only:
- dream fragments,
- emotional familiarity,
- face recognition,
- sensory flashes,
- contradictory autobiographical fragments.

A person with useful, detailed memory should be extraordinarily rare.

This preserves:
- mystery,
- institutional ignorance,
- the rarity of prophets,
- the plausibility of divination folklore,
- and the stakes of each lived state.

---

# 10. Divination / Ghost / Prophecy Regression

Under this ontology:

## Divination
Mostly ordinary culture/psychology.
Rare apparent accuracy can arise when symbolic prompts trigger leaked memory fragments.
No deterministic birth-date API.

## Ghosts
Mostly cross-history perception residue, archive collision, environmental playback, or trauma projection.
No need for supernatural ontology.

## Prophets
Extremely rare people with unusually coherent leakage from another lived lineage.
Their information is not reliable future sight.

Because the source history differs, prophecy decays as the current civilization diverges.

---

# 11. HUMAN:1

There can be:
- astronomically many civilization states,
- immense numbers of person-lineage executions,
- countless lives and deaths,

while the physical ship still contains exactly:

**Natural-Origin HUMAN = 1.**

Working contrast:
- `PERSON` is a moral/social category.
- `HUMAN` is an old physical-origin classification.

HUMAN:1 is not the only person.
HUMAN:1 is the only preserved natural-origin human organism/continuity in the relevant system field.

Critical emotional contrast:
> countless persons may have many histories; the one HUMAN has none yet.

---

# 12. Physical Limits Must Remain

Ultradense does not mean unlimited.

Required hard constraints:
- finite ship mass,
- finite energy,
- finite heat rejection,
- substrate defect rates,
- radiation damage,
- synchronization cost,
- write endurance / state degradation,
- communication latency across substrate banks,
- active-depth allocation,
- catastrophic cascading errors.

If these limits disappear, survival tension disappears.

Therefore a physical attack on Meridian can destroy civilization substrates permanently.
A destroyed substrate lineage cannot simply be regenerated from nothing unless a valid backup exists.

---

# 13. Why the Ship Still Needs Centuries

The travel time is useful rather than wasted.

During the interstellar journey Meridian:
- preserves civilization continuity,
- explores massive possibility spaces,
- experiences selected histories,
- accumulates technical/social knowledge,
- identifies failure modes,
- discovers novel behavior,
- tests resilience,
- prepares settlement institutions.

The ship is both:
- transportation infrastructure,
- and a centuries-long civilization development environment.

---

# 14. Major Ethical P0 Still OPEN

This model solves scale better but does NOT automatically solve ethics.

If R3 inhabitants are real persons, then intentionally exposing them to war, terror, death or extinction remains morally serious.

Do not write:
`They were simulations, so nobody really died.`

Open ethical questions:
- did original designers believe lineage continuation made branch death acceptable?
- did the system drift beyond the designers' intended limits?
- can a civilization refuse reconciliation/termination?
- does creating a lived branch create a new person with rights?
- is deletion murder?
- can knowledge gained through suffering justify the process?

These remain central late-series conflicts, not engineering footnotes.

---

# 15. Science / Logic Red-Team Gates

Before v3 promotion, still test:

1. Information-density plausibility level: how speculative is acceptable?
2. Energy/heat budget: what limits active lived histories?
3. Subjective time ratio: can civilizations run faster/slower than ship time?
4. External-event injection: how does real ship damage affect internal civilizations?
5. Persistence: what physically constitutes a person's continuity?
6. Failure mode: what precisely causes rare memory leakage?
7. HUMAN:1 biology: embryo / fetus / infant / suspended organism?
8. Why exactly one Natural-Origin HUMAN?
9. Destination transition: how do person-lineages become settlement citizens?
10. Ethical authority: who gave Meridian the right to create/terminate lived histories?
