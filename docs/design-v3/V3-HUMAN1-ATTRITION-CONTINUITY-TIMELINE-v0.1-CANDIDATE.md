Status: `P0/P1 CLOSURE CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`

Inputs:

---

# 1. Four attrition architectures

## A — Exactly one from launch

The mission deliberately launches one living Natural-Origin embryo.

Pros:
- simple,
- count always one.

Fatal risk:
- makes `1` look sacred/designed,
- chosen-one interpretation becomes too strong,
- redundancy design is implausibly poor for a centuries-long mission.

Verdict: **REJECT**.

## B — One catastrophic accident kills all but one

Many continuity specimens launch; one event destroys all but a lucky survivor.

Pros:
- clear tragedy,
- `1` is contingent rather than sacred.

Weakness:
- proper ark design should physically separate redundant biological continuity assets,
- one common-mode event wiping almost everything feels engineered for the title.

Verdict: **WEAK ALONE**.

## C — Slow centuries-long attrition only

Multiple independent specimens are gradually lost through unrelated failures.

Pros:
- realistic redundancy erosion,
- physical voyage matters.

Weakness:
- can feel suspiciously neat that attrition stops exactly at one,
- requires explaining why the system never replenishes the cohort.

Verdict: **GOOD COMPONENT**.

## D — Mixed common-mode loss + long attrition + final preservation priority [LEADING]

Departure cohort is redundant and physically distributed.
An early/mid-voyage genuine external event causes a major but incomplete cohort loss.
Over later centuries, unrelated preservation failures reduce the remaining cohort further.
Once the count becomes critically low, mission policy raises preservation priority, making further loss much less likely but not impossible.
Eventually one living Natural-Origin continuity remains.

Pros:
- no sacred original one,
- no single convenient catastrophe explains everything,
- redundancy engineering remains sensible,
- outer-ark history matters,
- `HUMAN:1` becomes the residue of contingency + accumulated damage + policy response.

Verdict: **SELECT**.

# 2. What `HUMAN` counts

`HUMAN` is an old mission-schema field.

It counts a narrow class:
**viable living Natural-Origin Homo sapiens developmental continuities preserved from the departure cohort.**

It does NOT count:
- moral persons in general,
- civic substrate citizens,
- digitized genomes,
- DNA sequence archives,
- stem-cell diversity banks,
- unfertilized reproductive material,
- reconstructed future organisms not belonging to the original living departure-continuity cohort.

This is why:
`PERSONS` can be enormous while `HUMAN` is 1.

Hard:
The schema is archaic/technical. It is not the story's moral definition of human worth.

---

# 3. Why the genetic archive does not make HUMAN > 1

The biodiversity/reproductive archive is designed to preserve **genetic possibility**, not multiple continuously living humans.

Preferred archive composition:
- genomic sequence diversity,
- cell lines / stem-cell libraries,
- reproductive precursor material where useful,
- microbiome/ecological reference banks,
- reconstruction/assisted-reproduction protocols.

Avoid making the archive a huge rack of already-fertilized viable embryos, because that would make `HUMAN:1` classification confusing and title-fairness weak.

Future children reconstructed/created from archive material can be biologically human after settlement, but they are not members of the original departure continuity cohort counted by the legacy `HUMAN` field.

The legacy field can become obsolete after settlement.

---

# 4. Departure cohort size

Do NOT freeze an exact number merely for lore aesthetics.

Working author band:
**low dozens** of independent Natural-Origin developmental continuities.

Reason:
- enough redundancy to be rational,
- small enough for deep preservation hardware,
- genetic diversity is not their job,
- they are continuity/reference lives, not the colony's entire breeding stock.

Possible final exact value can be chosen later only if a document/log scene requires it.

---

# 5. Physical separation

The original cohort is not stored in one refrigerator.

Candidate architecture:
- multiple isolated preservation cassettes,
- distributed across several protected biological vault sectors,
- independent power/thermal control,
- physical separation from the main civilization substrate,
- no ordinary civilization branch access,
- periodic remote viability checks,
- very limited maintenance intervention.

This prevents one ordinary fault from explaining the entire attrition.

# 6. Attrition chronology — author-side phase model

Exact voyage years remain dependent on later chronology freeze.
Use phases rather than fake dates for now.

## Phase 0 — Departure

- low-dozens Natural-Origin continuities viable,
- all in very early developmental arrest,
- genetic archive separate,
- no individual is privileged,
- no `last human` concept exists.

## Phase 1 — Early voyage stabilization

Normal losses may occur at very low rate:
- failed arrest,
- cell-line damage,
- seal/preservation defects,
- quality-control rejection.

These are sad mission losses, not plot conspiracy.

## Phase 2 — Major genuine outer-ark event

One real physical event damages several but not all independent preservation sectors.

Candidate source families to compare later:
A. high-energy radiation/debris event,
B. thermal-control cascade during major power emergency,
C. structural shock + emergency isolation,
D. compound event combining A/B without total ark catastrophe.

Recommended current direction:
**compound radiation/thermal preservation emergency**, because it also leaves traceable outer-ark maintenance history without requiring a war.

Important:
This event predates the protagonist's story by a long margin.
It is not caused by CCAL to create HUMAN:1.

## Phase 3 — Long middle-voyage attrition

Remaining continuities are gradually lost for unrelated reasons:
- accumulated radiation damage,
- preservation-medium failure,
- hardware aging,
- repair material scarcity,
- one cassette judged nonviable after conservative diagnostics.

Losses occur over long intervals, not a countdown thriller.

## Phase 4 — Critical-count protocol

Once count enters a very low threshold, preservation priority escalates.

System behavior:
- greater redundancy allocation,
- wider physical isolation,
- stricter maintenance approval,
- fewer interventions,
- increased shielding/thermal reserve,
- settlement mission schedules begin treating the remaining continuity as an irreplaceable historical mission asset.

This is not personal worship.
It is normal risk management after redundancy erosion.

## Phase 5 — HUMAN:1

Eventually only one departure-continuity organism remains viable.

From this point onward accessible mission-era schemas repeatedly report:
`HUMAN: 1`

Most civic institutions do not understand what that legacy field counts.

Older deep archives may later reveal historical values >1.
This becomes a payoff:
**there was never a prophecy that there must be one. One is what survived.**

## Phase 6 — Final Approach

Under leading biological-form Design B:
- developmental arrest is ended during the final-approach window,
- artificial gestation begins,
- no exact symbolic birth date is preordained,
- the goal is to join the first settlement generation, not to be born at a ceremonial second.

HUMAN remains 1 during gestation.

## Phase 7 — Settlement

After birth and later archive-derived biological reproduction, the old `HUMAN` continuity field becomes historically obsolete or is replaced by more sensible biological/personhood records.

The title mystery has done its job; the new civilization should not preserve a caste around `HUMAN` status.

# 7. Why not manufacture a replacement during voyage?

Important distinction:
The mission **can preserve genetic possibility** but cannot recreate membership in the original living departure cohort once continuity is lost.

A newly reconstructed embryo would be a new biological person, not restoration of the dead continuity specimen.

Additionally:
- the mission has no demographic need for more biological bodies during transit,
- creating lives solely to refill a legacy count would itself be ethically dubious,
- final settlement reproduction is a separate mission stage.

Thus:
`HUMAN:1` is not maintained by killing/rejecting replacement humans.
The system simply does not treat new biological creation as repairing the historical continuity count.

---

# 8. Why preserve even one?

The Natural-Origin cohort originally served several modest mission purposes:
- preserve direct biological continuity from departure-era humanity,
- retain unmodified developmental/epigenetic reference data,
- provide one pathway for ordinary biological human life to enter the settlement generation,
- hedge against errors in reconstructed biology,
- cultural/historical continuity.

None is sufficient to justify sacrificing the entire civilization field.

Mission priorities are plural:
- ark survival,
- civilization continuity,
- ecological/genetic diversity,
- settlement capability,
- Natural-Origin continuity.

HUMAN:1 is one preserved objective among several.

# 10. Emotional and thematic function

The most useful contrast is not:
`one real human vs fake people`.

It is:
- countless persons carry inherited/alternate histories,
- one old biological continuity has almost no lived biography yet,
- both are persons once conscious/lived,
- neither receives moral superiority from origin.

At arrival the question is not who is more human.
It is what kind of civilization will raise and live with a person who has no inherited social past.

Do not state this as an author sermon in prose.

# 11. Hostile checks

FAIL if:
1. exactly one was chosen at departure for mystical reasons.
2. one convenient accident kills every backup except the protagonistically useful specimen.
3. archive already contains hundreds of viable counted embryos but UI still says HUMAN:1.
4. HUMAN:1 can regenerate all genetic diversity alone.
5. citizens were built solely as caretakers for HUMAN:1.
6. the surviving embryo has administrator inheritance or sovereignty.
7. new children are legally inferior/superior based on whether they descend from HUMAN:1.

PASS if:
- original cohort was redundant,
- loss came from mixed real attrition,
- preservation priority rationally increases as redundancy declines,
- genetic archive has a different function,
- one is contingent history rather than destiny.
