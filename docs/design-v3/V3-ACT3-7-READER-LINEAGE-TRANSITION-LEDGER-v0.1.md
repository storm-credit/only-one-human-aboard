# V3 ACT3~7 READER-FACING LINEAGE TRANSITION LEDGER v0.1

Status: `NARRATIVE ONTOLOGY CONTROL / CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Goal:
The hidden civilization field may be astronomical, but the reader must not be forced to follow dozens of complete worlds.

Core decision candidate:
**Only one major protagonist continuity transfer is directly lived as a full apparent regression.**

After that, the scale of other histories is revealed mainly through:
- testimony,
- memory fragments,
- records,
- relationships,
- physical contradictions,
- institutional inheritances.

This directly matches the desired writing style: other-history morality is heard in people's mouths and seen in current behavior, not presented as a parade of alternate-world chapters.

---

# 1. Reader-facing history labels

These are author labels only.
Do not call them H-A/H-B in prose.

## H-A — Opening Meridian

Acts:
**Act1~Act2**.

Role:
The first home the reader learns and loses.

Protagonist:
His original lived continuity begins here.

Key facts:
- Arun officially died in protagonist's remembered past,
- protagonist/former-partner relationship has one baseline,
- family relationships establish emotional anchors,
- Act2 catastrophe occurs here.

End:
The protagonist's still-active continuity is misbound out of H-A during the Act2 climax.

Important:
H-A does not become meaningless after exit.
Deaths/losses there remain losses.

---

## H-B — Wrong-Return Meridian / Main Current After Act2

Acts:
**Act3 through the end of the series, unless a later deliberate design proves another transfer necessary.**

This is a major simplification and strength.

The protagonist does NOT hop worlds every arc.

H-B:
- shares deep ancestry with H-A,
- resembles an earlier civic period in some respects,
- already diverged before protagonist arrives,
- contains people whose current relationships do not match his memories,
- may contain Arun alive or an Arun history incompatible with H-A,
- has its own valid current people who are not replacements.

The protagonist then actually lives forward in H-B.
His choices become part of H-B's real causal history.

---

# 2. Why stay in H-B for Acts3~9

Benefits:
1. prevents repetitive reset mechanics,
2. preserves attachment to a stable current cast,
3. makes every later death matter,
4. avoids `which protagonist copy are we on?` fatigue,
5. lets the mystery widen through evidence rather than teleportation,
6. keeps astronomical ontology author-side while reader follows one continuing life.

Hard candidate:
**No second full protagonist regression merely to show another history.**

A second transfer requires a major narrative justification and should default to rejection.

---

# 3. Other histories as evidence families

The reader can learn several additional histories without living entire arcs inside them.

Use a bounded set of evidence families.

## E1 — Protagonist's H-A memory

Highest-confidence alternate history because he actually lived it.

Function:
- comparison baseline,
- grief,
- foreknowledge,
- wrong-return mystery.

Reliability:
high for autobiographical experience, not perfect for interpretation.

## E2 — Ivo-class coherent history

One strong independent rememberer has a coherent history inconsistent with both H-A and H-B.

Function:
proves protagonist's past is not the only alternate past.

Reader never needs a full 40-episode tour of E2.

## E3 — Narrow foreknowledge/economic history

Cassian-type case or equivalent.

Only fragments:
- asset movement,
- disaster timing,
- career outcomes.

May be partly wrong/ambiguous.

Function:
show that memory of another path can become social/economic power.

## E4 — Institutional catastrophe history

Evidence of a major civil conflict/disaster that H-B officially never experienced.

Appears through:
- laws with no local origin,
- emergency procedures,
- memorial fragments,
- design improvements,
- testimony.

Function:
prove civilizations inherit learning without ordinary conscious memory.

## E5 — Relationship/family history

A different lived lineage in which familiar persons had different family relations.

Possible delayed high-value evidence:
- protagonist/former partner married,
- child existed,
- someone remembers current stranger as parent/spouse.

Function:
make ontology emotionally personal.

Use one dominant family case, not repeated alternate children.

## E6 — Moral-reputation history

A current person is remembered by another witness/group as:
- oppressor,
- coward,
- murderer,
- hero,
- rescuer
for acts not committed in H-B.

Function:
show same disposition under different conditions without narrator moral lecture.

E4/E5/E6 may originate from the same hidden lineage or different ones; exact genealogy is author-side and should remain economical.

---

# 4. Reader memory budget

The ontology can be huge.
The reader-tracked evidence families cannot.

Recommended active cognitive budget:
- 1 current lived world: H-B,
- 1 fully remembered lost world: H-A,
- 3~5 additional evidence-history families at any given stage,
- no requirement to give every family a formal name or full chronology.

If the reader needs a chart to remember 17 alternate Meridians, design has failed.

# 5. Act3 ledger

Current world:
H-B.

Reader hypothesis progression:
`regression → wrong regression`.

Evidence:
- H-A foreknowledge initially works,
- H-B pre-existing differences prove protagonist did not cause all divergence,
- one other coherent memory case appears late.

Do NOT reveal astronomical lineage count.

Key emotional rule:
Protagonist initially treats H-B people as if they are restored H-A people.
This creates mistakes.

Example functions:
- assumes old intimacy that current person does not share,
- tries to prevent a death that is not on the same causal path,
- distrusts someone based on H-A behavior.

Evidence families expand:

# 7. Act5 ledger

Current world:
still H-B.

Reader hypothesis:
`the ship has histories that never happened here.`

Evidence:
- E4 institutional/disaster residue,
- Arun contradiction,
- one relationship/family contradiction can begin,
- HUMAN:1 current hard record.

Key shift:
Evidence moves from `people's minds` to **physical/institutional traces**.

By Act5 end the reader should know memory disorder alone cannot explain everything.

# 8. Act6 ledger

Current world:
still H-B.

Important decision:
Act6 `Devastated Meridian` should preferably be **H-B itself entering a real civilization-scale collapse**, not protagonist being sent to a convenient war-world.

Why stronger:
- current relationships now face consequences,
- no escape into `this was just another test branch`,
- emergent minor actor can genuinely reshape the society the reader has lived in since Act3,
- system cannot be blamed for every outcome.

Other-history evidence is used as comparison:
`someone remembers this crisis ending differently elsewhere.`

But H-B must make its own choices.

No full protagonist transfer at Act6 end by default.

---

# 9. Act7 ledger

Current world:
still H-B after surviving/transforming through Act6.

Act7 does NOT need a new `novel merged world` to create ontology shock.

Instead the shock is:
**H-B itself has never had a purely singular past.**

Discoveries:
- institutions inherited from absent histories,
- technologies whose triggering disaster never happened locally,
- hidden lineage genealogy,
- current persons descended from multiple modeling/reconciliation decisions,
- astronomical anchor namespace.

Thus the question changes:
`어느 세계로 돌아왔나?`
→
`애초에 하나의 원본 역사라는 게 있었나?`

This preserves one current world while destroying the notion of privileged originality.

---

# 10. Act8~9 implication

H-B remains the current society that confronts the truth and makes settlement decisions.

This is important:
The final ethical choice is made by people the reader has actually followed for hundreds of episodes, not by a newly generated merged cast.

Existing other D3 lineages still have rights/claims, but the story does not need to shift POV into all of them.

---

# 11. Counterpart-not-replacement proof cases

At least two should occur before/around Act7.

## Proof A — relationship refusal

Protagonist treats an H-B familiar person according to H-A intimacy/guilt.
The H-B person rejects the assumed relationship.

Function:
`same face/lineage ≠ same shared past`.

## Proof B — moral reputation contradiction

A current admirable person is feared by someone carrying evidence of another history.

Current person:
- did not commit those acts,
- cannot be punished as if they did,
- may still recognize that under those conditions they might have become that person.

Function:
separates responsibility from lineage resemblance.

Optional Proof C — family/child
Use once, late.
A family bond existed elsewhere but does not automatically bind current counterparts.

---

# 12. How to write moral variants

Preferred scene grammar:

1. current person acts normally,
2. another person reacts disproportionately,
3. protagonist/reader assumes prejudice or hidden crime,
4. testimony reveals an impossible past,
5. current person denies it honestly,
6. later independent evidence supports part of the testimony,
7. current person's present choice either echoes or rejects the remembered version.

Do not use narrator summary:
`In another timeline she was evil.`

---

# 13. False testimony requirement

Not every incompatible claim is true.

Sources can be:
- honest leaked memory,
- ordinary false memory,
- trauma reconstruction,
- fraud,
- political manipulation,
- mistaken identity,
- genuine other-lineage evidence.

Reason:
If every weird statement is an alternate history, mystery becomes trivial.

---

# 14. Death stakes across the ledger

H-A death remains H-A death.
H-B counterpart does not erase it.
H-B death remains H-B death even if another lineage counterpart exists elsewhere.

The protagonist cannot solve grief by searching for a replacement lineage.

By Act7 he must understand this behaviorally before the full ethics reveal.

---

# 15. Harness

FAIL if:
1. protagonist transfers worlds repeatedly for novelty.
2. every Act introduces a new labeled Meridian.
3. reader must track >5~7 detailed alternate histories simultaneously.
4. Act6 catastrophe is emotionally discounted as `not our world`.
5. current counterparts inherit criminal guilt automatically.
6. every strange testimony is true.
7. astronomical scale is shown through endless alternate-POV chapters instead of evidence convergence.

PASS if:
- H-A is the lost home,
- H-B is the long current home,
- one protagonist transfer drives the regression hook,
- additional histories widen mystery through bounded evidence families,
- Act7 reveals huge scale without requiring huge narrative sprawl.

# 16. Verdict

Selected reader-facing structure:

**H-A (Acts1~2) → one exceptional continuity transfer → H-B (Acts3~9).**

Other histories:
**evidence/testimony/residue families, not repeated protagonist world-hopping.**

This is compatible with an astronomical hidden civilization field while preserving character attachment and death stakes.
