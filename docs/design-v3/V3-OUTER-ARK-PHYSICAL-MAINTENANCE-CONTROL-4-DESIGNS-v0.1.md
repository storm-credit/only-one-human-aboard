## Problem discovered during IP/visual audit

Current v3 ontology says:
- there is one real physical Outer Ark,
- the ~300k reader-facing Meridian is an Experienced Meridian implemented by the ultradense substrate,
- HUMAN:1 is preserved biologically and has not lived ordinary civic life,
- ordinary D3 citizens do not possess default omnipotent access to outer hardware.

Therefore the old v2 intuition:
`hundreds of thousands of macroscopic citizens physically maintain the interstellar ship for centuries`

cannot be silently reused.

Need to define who maintains the **real physical Outer Ark** during the centuries before late reveal/settlement.

This is hidden author architecture. It should not become an early lore explanation.

---

# Design A — Fully autonomous omnipotent ship AI

Core:
one general AI diagnoses, fabricates, repairs and controls almost everything physically.

Pros:
- simple,
- explains centuries of maintenance.

Cons:
- makes `why didn't AI solve X?` dominate every failure,
- easily becomes secret god/captain,
- reduces physical deterioration and maintenance scarcity,
- conflicts with system-not-omniscient tone.

Verdict:
# `REJECT AS SINGLE-LAYER MODEL`

Retain only bounded automation.

---

# Design B — D3 citizens unknowingly operate real hardware through abstracted jobs

Core:
Experienced-Meridian engineers think they are repairing local civic systems, while some actions secretly map to physical Outer Ark hardware.

Pros:
- gives protagonist profession direct outer significance,
- elegant hidden coupling.

Cons:
- ethically manipulative at extreme level,
- creates implausible one-to-one mapping between simulated city and physical hardware,
- risks every maintenance clue being secretly outer-layer clue,
- citizens could accidentally control mission-critical real systems without informed access,
- violates current `no simple street = circuit board` rule.

Verdict:
# `REJECT AS DEFAULT`

Allow only indirect validated lesson transfer, never routine hidden teleoperation.

---

# Design C — Dedicated nonhuman maintenance population

Core:
a separate fleet/population of sophisticated maintenance agents operates physical ship.

Variants:
- robots with simple automation,
- high-level digital agents,
- synthetic persons.

Pros:
- separates outer maintenance from D3 civic worlds,
- gives craft/work-machine ecology.

Cons:
- if agents are persons, entire hidden society/personhood problem appears,
- if highly general but non-person, consciousness boundary becomes arbitrary,
- risks introducing a second unseen cast/civilization.

Verdict:
# `LIMIT / DO NOT CREATE SECRET PERSON SOCIETY`

Robotic equipment is useful; a separate hidden person-class is not.

---

# Design D — Layered Non-Person Automation + Bounded Planning + Human-Civilization Lesson Input

Core:
The physical Outer Ark is maintained by a **layered mission-maintenance stack**, not one god AI and not hidden citizen teleoperation.

Layers:

## D0 — Hard safety / local control
- dedicated controllers,
- fault protection,
- pumps/valves/power isolation,
- simple condition response,
- physical interlocks.

No personhood claim.

## D1 — Robotic maintenance fleet
- inspection pods,
- service tugs,
- crawlers,
- manipulators,
- fabrication/replaceable modules,
- radiator/shield service machinery.

Supervised by mission automation.

## D2 — Bounded non-conscious planning / diagnosis
- predicts degradation,
- schedules maintenance,
- selects from verified repair strategies,
- runs non-conscious engineering models,
- cannot freely redefine mission ethics/personhood/governance.

## D3-input — validated civilization outputs, not direct control
Deep lived civilizations may produce:
- robust engineering procedures,
- failure lessons,
- allocation heuristics,
- governance/safety insights.

Only validated/translated outputs may modify outer maintenance policy or design libraries.
Citizens do NOT unknowingly move real robot arms.

## Late transition
After Act7/8 reveals and rights/governance changes:
- selected current persons can receive **explicit bounded outer-world access**,
- first through remote/scout embodiment,
- later through continuity migration/local embodiment in Act9,
- authority is logged/domain-limited.

# Verdict

# **D = SELECTED LEADING ARCHITECTURE**

# Why the maintenance stack is not omnipotent

It is limited by:
- sensor access,
- model uncertainty,
- physical reach,
- spare stock,
- feedstock,
- fabrication throughput,
- thermal windows,
- radiation/particle damage,
- shielding erosion,
- propellant/reaction-mass constraints,
- actuator/craft loss,
- incompatible generations,
- communication/isolation faults,
- cumulative centuries-long degradation.

It can make conservative decisions but cannot conjure material or perfectly forecast novel coupled failures.

A physical component may remain unrepaired because:
- access risk is too high,
- replacement stock is reserved,
- fabricator is busy/degraded,
- repair requires shutdown incompatible with current mission phase,
- radiator geometry/safe orientation blocks work,
- diagnostic confidence is insufficient.

This keeps physical survival meaningful.

---

# Self-repair / fabrication law

Allowed:
- standardized spare fabrication,
- module swapping,
- remanufacturing,
- recycling,
- robotic inspection/service,
- limited reconfiguration.

Forbidden easy answer:
`nanobots repair anything from raw atoms instantly`.

Physical repair has:
- time,
- energy,
- heat,
- feedstock,
- access,
- tooling,
- certification
costs.

# Why Experienced Meridian includes engineering jobs

These jobs are NOT fake busywork.

Inside a D3 lived civilization:
- infrastructure failure is real to that society,
- safety, repair, power, transport and resource decisions have genuine consequences,
- people develop real expertise and institutions,
- those histories are part of civilization-resilience exploration.

Some robust lessons may later inform Outer Ark maintenance or settlement engineering.

Hard:
There is no one-to-one mapping like:
`protagonist repairs a simulated wall = a robot repairs the matching real outer hull panel`.

Therefore protagonist's job retains meaning without secret puppet-control logic.

# Physical maintenance fleet implications

The real Outer Ark can support non-person/robotic families such as:
- external inspection pod,
- outer service tug,
- radiator/shield maintenance carriage,
- robotic manipulator/crawler,
- deployment barge,
- fabrication/service modules.

Important distinction:
Some reader-facing Experienced-Meridian craft may share **engineering design ancestry/concepts** with outer machinery because simulation/civilization seeds inherit mission engineering knowledge.
They do NOT need to be literally the same machine.

This creates visual rhyme without secret direct mapping.

# Failure-source taxonomy

## P-Physical
Actual outer component degradation/damage.

## A-Automation
Sensor/controller/planner error or conservative shutdown.

## R-Resource
No adequate spare/feedstock/time/thermal access.

## C-Coupled
Multiple individually survivable problems interact.

## N-Novel
Condition outside validated repair/model envelope.

Act9 final crisis uses strong C + N behavior.

C4 protagonist misbinding remains an extraordinary substrate-continuity integrity failure, not ordinary maintenance-stack behavior.
