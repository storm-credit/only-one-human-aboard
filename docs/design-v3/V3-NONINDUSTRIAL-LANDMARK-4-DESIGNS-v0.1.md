# V3 NON-INDUSTRIAL LANDMARKS — 4 DESIGNS v0.1

Status: `IP/BACKGROUND BRAINSTORM / NOT CANON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-20
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Problem:
Old Works/machines naturally carry strong visual identity. The civilian city must be equally memorable or Meridian will feel like an industrial set with decorative homes.

Goal:
Select one HIGH-memory landmark each for Transfer Belt, Midring, Garden Commons and Med-University that:
- works in ordinary life,
- supports recurring character action,
- visually changes under crisis,
- does not exist merely to showcase worldbuilding.

Proper names remain C2.

---

# L1 — Transfer Belt landmark

## A. Grand departure hall
Huge central concourse.
Risk: airport cliché.

## B. Layered bridge interchange
Multiple passenger bridges cross above/beside partially visible freight channels, with food/service pockets suspended at transfer edges.
Strength:
movement in depth; lets reader see people/cargo systems interlock.

## C. Rotating route wall
Large dynamic route display landmark.
Risk: too UI-dependent, visually dated.

## D. Old station preserved inside new hub
Historic platform shell encased by expansion.
Strength:
material history.
Risk:
too heritage-forward if every zone does this.

### Selected candidate
# **B + small D layer — Layered Bridge Interchange**

Visual anchors:
- crossing bridges at different heights,
- glimpses of freight movement below/behind safety barriers,
- old structural seam/column surviving one renovation,
- crowded quick-meal/service edge.

Act transformations:
Act1 normal flow.
Act2 bridges close in sequence; stranded crowd changes vertical composition.
Act4 rumor creates self-rerouting/empty vs crowded asymmetry.
Act6 temporary corridor signage and aid stations overlay formal routes.

# L2 — Midring landmark

## A. Residential megatrium
Large housing atrium.
Risk: generic sci-fi apartment block.

## B. Layered neighborhood courtyard street
Several residential blocks open onto a shared pedestrian/service courtyard with school path, small food frontages, seating, child/play zone and visible upper balconies.
Strength:
home/social memory; many characters can cross without contrivance.

## C. Covered market spine
Strong social activity.
Risk: duplicates Juno venue/Transfer commerce.

## D. Community water garden
Visually attractive.
Risk: overlaps Garden Commons.

### Selected candidate
# **B — Layered Neighborhood Courtyard**

Visual anchors:
- balconies/windows showing private life without glass-tower luxury,
- one school route cutting through,
- local service storefronts,
- repaired floor/wall art accumulated over years,
- benches/table edges with visible local use.

Act transformations:
Act1 school/family/meal life.
Act2 temporary refuge/evacuation assembly.
Act3 one mundane H-B household/school difference.
Act6 ration/aid/private-access conflict while homes remain recognizable.
Act9 settlement housing borrows courtyard logic.

# L3 — Garden Commons landmark

## A. Giant forest dome
Risk: fake Earth theme park / biosphere cliché.

## B. Long planted transit promenade
Readable but not special enough.

## C. Lightwell terrace with visible engineered horizon
A tall planted civic/recreation chamber where lighting and layered terraces create openness, but one edge deliberately reveals structure/maintenance ribs so citizens never pretend it is Earth outdoors.
Strength:
unique closed-city identity; strong Act9 open-sky contrast.

## D. Agricultural canal/wetland gallery
Strong ecology link.
Risk: falsely implies scenic systems are primary life support.

### Selected candidate
# **C + limited D — Lightwell Terrace**

Visual anchors:
- strong simulated daylight from overhead/side lightwell,
- planted terraces and recreation paths,
- one visible structural rib/service edge,
- modest water/agriculture demonstration element, not life-support core.

Act transformations:
Act1 family/sport/leisure.
Act3 familiar-but-different social encounter.
Act6 some areas remain open deliberately as psychological/civic relief; others used for temporary support.
Act9 real external sky/light/weather produces strongest background completion payoff.

# L4 — Med-University landmark

## A. Monumental hospital lobby
Risk: sterile corporate hospital.

## B. Teaching amphitheater
Too academic, weak patient life.

## C. Rehab/learning promenade
A broad public corridor/commons linking rehab, clinics and teaching spaces; patients practicing movement, students crossing, families waiting, staff eating and ordinary life co-exist.
Strength:
shows medicine as recovery/life, not secret technology.

## D. Glass laboratory bridge
Risk: secret-lab visual and contemporary SF cliché.

### Selected candidate
# **C — Rehab / Learning Promenade**

Visual anchors:
- varied floor/handrail textures for rehab/accessibility,
- small practice zones integrated into public circulation,
- students and patients sharing space without spectacle,
- warm waiting/service pockets alongside clinical routes.

Act transformations:
Act2 casualty overflow expands into promenade.
Act3 protagonist memory evaluation occurs in familiar public-care institution.
Act6 rehab space converted partly to longer-term treatment/overflow.
Act8 D3/HUMAN care remains visually continuous with ordinary medicine.
Act9 settlement clinic compresses same logic into rougher small-scale form.

# Landmark rule

A landmark becomes HIGH-memory only if it appears in at least 3 different functional/emotional contexts.

No landmark may exist only for establishing shots.

Selected current set:
1. Transfer — Layered Bridge Interchange
2. Midring — Layered Neighborhood Courtyard
3. Garden — Lightwell Terrace
4. Med-University — Rehab/Learning Promenade

These complement existing strong anchors:
- Old Works structural junction/diagnostics base,
- Central old structural rib/public hall,
- Rim expansion edge,
- Legacy service pockets.
