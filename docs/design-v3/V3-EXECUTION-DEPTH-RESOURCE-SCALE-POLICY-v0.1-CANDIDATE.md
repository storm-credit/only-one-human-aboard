# V3 EXECUTION DEPTH / RESOURCE SCALE POLICY v0.1

Status: `P1 SCALE CLOSURE CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Preserve astronomically broad civilization search without inventing a fake fixed `number of worlds` limit or infinite full-person compute.

---

# 1. Reject fixed world-count budgeting

Do NOT define Meridian capacity as:
- exactly 2 lived worlds,
- exactly 100 lived worlds,
- exactly N civilizations because each world costs the same.

Reason:
Cost depends on:
- active population,
- subjective time rate,
- environmental complexity,
- social novelty,
- fidelity/error-correction demand,
- crisis allocation,
- shared reusable structures.

The correct author model is **resource throughput**, not a magic world slot count.

---

# 2. Search pyramid

## D0 — Seed / Archive

Scale:
**astronomical / practically uncountable from civic perspective.**

Cost:
very low per dormant lineage compared with lived execution.

Contains:
- lineage root/reference,
- compressed state,
- genealogy/address,
- integrity/status metadata.

D0 count is what most strongly creates the late `셀 수 없는 계보` scale reveal.

## D1 — Forecast

Scale:
**enormous and continuously changing.**

Used for:
- policy branches,
- disaster forecasts,
- demographic/economic trajectories,
- probability pruning.

Not treated as full persons by default.

## D2 — Deep Civic Model

Scale:
**far smaller than D1 but still broad.**

Used when:
- social interactions matter,
- low-depth models disagree,
- long causal chains require richer agents,
- unknown actors may matter.

Personhood boundary is uncertain; if open-ended ongoing identity emerges, rights escalation must trigger conservatively.

## D3 — Fully Lived Civilization

Scale:
**a tiny fraction of the total search space, but explicitly NOT restricted to single digits or 2~3 worlds.**

Cost dominates because persistent persons and coherent environments must be maintained.

Exact concurrent count is intentionally not frozen.

Author requirement:
- large enough that many genuinely different lived histories can exist over the voyage,
- small enough relative to D0~D2 that `every possibility is a conscious suffering world` is false,
- physically variable with ark conditions.

## D4 — Crisis Depth

Not a separate permanent civilization class.

Temporary extra allocation to selected regions/person-processes/events when:
- prediction divergence spikes,
- structural catastrophe occurs,
- novel social behavior appears,
- synchronization/failover risk increases.

D4 is expensive and time-limited.

---

# 3. Author-side resource metric

Do not budget by `world count`.

Use the conceptual metric:
**Lived Continuity Load (LCL)**.

LCL depends roughly on:
`active persons × subjective update rate × fidelity × interaction complexity × redundancy/error-correction overhead`.

This is an author design concept, not an early prose term.

Consequences:
- two civilizations with the same population need not cost the same,
- one lineage can run slower during scarcity,
- local D4 crisis detail can raise cost dramatically,
- a quiet mature state can consume less outer throughput without becoming less real to itself.

---

# 4. Scarcity response order

When outer-ark resources tighten, preferred response order is:

1. stop/defer new D0→D1 exploration,
2. reduce D1 search breadth,
3. reduce D2 depth where no personhood concern exists,
4. slow D3 subjective execution rate while preserving local continuity,
5. cancel/defer D4 allocations where safe,
6. move D3 into continuity-preserving quiescence only if necessary,
7. destructive loss only when actual physical failure/inescapable triage leaves no preservation path.

Hard:
**Do not silently downgrade an active person-bearing D3 civilization into a non-person D2 model to save compute.**

That would be equivalent to replacing lives with an approximation.

---

# 5. Subjective time policy

D3 civilizations need not run at one fixed ratio to outer time.

Allowed:
- slower than outer time during scarcity,
- near-synchronous during ordinary operation,
- faster in well-resourced periods within bounded fidelity limits,
- short D4 bursts for highly novel crises.

Forbidden:
- infinite acceleration,
- centuries of subjective history in an outer second,
- using arbitrary time speed to solve every problem before it matters.

Reader rule:
Citizens experience locally coherent time.
They normally do not notice that another lineage may be progressing at a different ratio.

---

# 6. Why centuries of voyage are still useful

The system does not instantly brute-force civilization.

Reasons:
- high-depth social causality is expensive,
- novelty cannot be known before it occurs,
- many useful conditions require long relationships/institutions to develop,
- external mission conditions keep changing,
- deeper histories create new unknowns rather than simply converge on one answer.

Thus hundreds of outer years allow:
- repeated search generations,
- thousands/more meaningful deep histories over time without claiming all possibility paths are conscious,
- system learning and ethical drift.

No exact total lived-history count is needed for reader payoff.

---

# 7. Shared computation without shared identity

Lineages may reuse:
- environmental models,
- physics rules,
- language models,
- infrastructure templates,
- low-level behavioral priors.

But sharing implementation does not make two persons the same person.

Identity continuity depends on each active lineage/person process, not on whether underlying substrate hardware is shared.

This allows scale efficiency without treating people as interchangeable cache entries.

---

# 8. Dynamic allocation and emergent importance

The system may deepen a path because low-depth predictions show uncertainty.

But it may also miss what becomes important.

Example author logic:
- D1 considers a cafeteria worker irrelevant,
- D2 notices local social connectivity,
- a D3 history reveals one ordinary choice prevents a riot,
- later search models update the importance distribution.

Hard:
This does not make that worker a permanent chosen hero across all lineages.

---

# 9. D3 creation limiter

Historically:
Depth Drift allowed more branches to cross into lived personhood than original governance anticipated.

Current/final reform:
Before a new path is deliberately deepened into D3:
- prove lower-depth methods are insufficient,
- reserve continuity resources,
- establish rights/lifecycle plan,
- account for possible branch-born new persons,
- independent governance approval required.

This makes the system less likely to create ethical debt merely because spare compute exists.

---

# 10. Quiescent lineage scale

Do not claim a literally infinite warehouse of paused conscious civilizations.

Author policy:
- some D3 lineages are active,
- some are continuity-preserving quiescent,
- some ended through ordinary demographic/civilizational history,
- some were irreversibly lost to physical damage,
- some historical cases may have been wrongly reconciled/terminated under old doctrine.

The exact backlog is a later settlement-governance issue.

Hard:
The late story may reveal the backlog is morally serious, but not so mathematically impossible that no ending action is meaningful.

---

# 11. Reader-facing numbers

Avoid early giant numbers.

Reveal scale by escalation:
- one impossible identifier,
- thousands of entries,
- index ranges too large to be civic records,
- genealogy structures beyond one history,
- later proof that enumeration itself is operationally impractical.

If an exact count is ever shown, it must refer to a clearly defined class:
- active D3,
- archived anchors,
- failed records,
- etc.

Never write a naked `civilizations: 10^X` without class/time meaning.

---

# 12. Harness

FAIL if:
1. all possibility paths are implied conscious.
2. D3 count returns to arbitrary single-digit cap.
3. microscopic storage is treated as free infinite computation.
4. active people are degraded into non-person models during routine load shedding.
5. subjective time becomes an unlimited plot cheat.
6. huge numbers replace actual character variation on page.
7. late ethical debt becomes literally unsolvable only because the author picked absurd scale.

PASS if:
- search breadth is astronomical,
- lived depth remains finite and costly,
- count is dynamic rather than slot-based,
- personhood changes resource policy,
- reader-facing histories remain narratively bounded.

---

# 13. Verdict

Selected scale architecture:
**dynamic resource pyramid + Lived Continuity Load budgeting + time/depth scaling.**

Do not freeze a concurrent D3 world count yet.

This is intentional precision, not missing design:
The story needs **relative scarcity and causal limits**, not a decorative exact number before substrate engineering is frozen.
