# V3 CIVILIAN FASHION / MATERIAL CULTURE BIBLE v0.1

Status: `IP/VISUAL/MATERIAL DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / NOT CANON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-20
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Ensure ordinary Meridian citizens, homes, shops and personal possessions look like a 450-year closed civilization rather than generic `future extras` around carefully designed protagonists.

## Core material-culture premise

Meridian is technologically advanced but materially finite.
Objects exist within mature systems of:
- manufacture,
- repair,
- replacement,
- recycling,
- customization,
- heritage,
- certification,
- secondhand transfer.

Therefore visual status is not simply:
`new = rich / repaired = poor`.

A repaired object can be:
- ordinary,
- trusted,
- sentimental,
- professionally certified,
- unfashionable,
- fashionable precisely because its shell/design is old,
- cheaper or more expensive depending provenance/service.

---

# 1. Clothing system

## Everyday textile baseline
Materials are future closed-loop fibers/composites with:
- high recyclability,
- replaceable high-wear sections,
- stain/odor/moisture management within reasonable SF bounds,
- easy resizing/alteration,
- limited embedded sensing only when useful.

Hard:
No universal self-cleaning morph-suit.
Clothes still wrinkle, wear, stain, tear, fit badly and go out of style.

## Common construction language
- layered garments rather than one-piece jumpsuits,
- replaceable cuffs/knees/seat/edge panels on practical clothing,
- closures designed for repair,
- inner comfort layer + outer social/weather/work layer,
- pockets/carry systems vary by job/generation.

Meridian citizens should NOT all look like crew members.

---

# 2. Four civilian style families — coexisting, not factions

## F1 — Clean Modular
- simple silhouettes,
- high fit precision,
- minimal visible repair,
- newer integrated closures/materials.

Common among:
people who prefer contemporary design, some professional/civic contexts.

Not synonymous with wealth.

## F2 — Layered Practical
- overshirts, short jackets, adaptable layers,
- visible replaceable wear zones,
- strongest everyday baseline.

Common across ages/jobs.

## F3 — Heritage/Reissue
- old Meridian/Earth-descended cuts reissued in current materials,
- historical detail chosen aesthetically,
- often mixed with modern pieces.

Hard:
not cosplay by default.

## F4 — Youth/Local Experimental
- asymmetric layering,
- personalized non-safety patches/fasteners,
- reused materials,
- temporary prints/textures,
- local scene/sport/art references.

Niko's civilian design can borrow here without making all youth identical.

---

# 3. Age / generation effect

Older citizens:
may retain preferred cuts/interfaces from earlier decades because garments can be remade in modern materials.

Middle-aged:
often mix durable familiar pieces with current replacements.

Youth:
more likely to remix proportions, temporary surfaces, accessories, local subculture references.

Hard:
no `old people wear 20th-century Earth clothes` shorthand.

---

# 4. Workwear integration

Workwear is a layer over civic identity.

Engineering:
abrasion, attachment, contamination/hazard modules.

Medical:
cleanability, free hands/forearms, local procedure layers.

Emergency:
role visibility, hazard protection, removable authority markers.

Logistics:
movement/visibility/load-handling layers in depots only.

Food/service:
cleanable removable outer layer.

Archives:
handling/protection only when required.

Security/investigation:
professional civilian base + evidence/hazard layer; not permanent police armor.

Hard:
profession cannot dictate entire off-duty wardrobe.

---

# 5. Formal / ceremony clothing

Meridian has ceremonies, weddings/partnership celebrations, memorials, school events, civic awards and performances.

Visual direction:
- more structured drape/fit,
- higher-quality surface texture,
- personal/family heritage references possible,
- jewelry/accessories more visible,
- garments remain recognizably descended from everyday clothing rather than a universal `space formal uniform`.

No one global ethnic costume.

---

# 6. Mourning / memorial material culture

No mandatory black-only rule.

Possible practices vary by family/community:
- quiet color/texture choices,
- temporary memorial pin/strip,
- printed/physical name card,
- food/community gathering object,
- digital remembrance combined with durable physical token.

This supports Act5 memorial contradictions without one monolithic funeral religion.

---

# 7. Jewelry / body modification / wearables

Common:
- rings/ear pieces/neck pieces,
- smart but non-magical health/access wearables,
- simple interface bands,
- tattoos/pigment/body art,
- cosmetic hair/skin treatments.

Less common but plausible:
- visible assistive/medical devices,
- sensory aids,
- prosthetic components,
- decorative printed accessories.

Hard:
No mandatory neural implant visible on everyone.
No cyberpunk chrome-body default.

---

# 8. Personal computing / interfaces

Do not make every surface holographic.

Mix:
- handheld/slate devices,
- wall/public displays,
- wearable access/health devices,
- tactile controls in safety-critical systems,
- projected/AR overlays where useful,
- physical labels/tags for degraded/offline safety.

Reason:
closed city needs redundancy, accessibility and maintainability.

Visual rule:
advanced tech often looks **boringly reliable** rather than glowing.

---

# 9. Homes

Homes are not identical capsule apartments.

Common layers:
- modular structural/service core,
- personalized furniture/surfaces,
- storage designed around limited space,
- repairable household equipment,
- local art/photos/prints,
- inherited/reissued objects,
- plants/pets where household allows,
- children's clutter,
- work carry zones near entrance.

Housing differences arise through:
- location,
- area,
- acoustic/privacy quality,
- view/public-space access,
- age/retrofit,
- household size,
- service convenience,
not only luxury materials.

---

# 10. Furniture / household object language

Furniture:
- modular enough for moving/repair,
- not all fold-out spaceship furniture,
- some deliberately heavy/permanent pieces because people live there for decades.

Common visible repair:
- replaced hinge/panel,
- resurfaced tabletop,
- reupholstered seat,
- mixed-generation appliance shell/core.

Household collectibility:
old design shells, local ceramics/composites, prints, toys, game pieces, transit memorabilia.

---

# 11. Foodware / packaging

Closed-loop material use means:
- reusable containers common,
- deposit/return systems plausible,
- lightweight disposables exist for medical/emergency/sanitary use but are controlled/recycled,
- local venues develop recognizable container/serving designs.

Juno's venue can have a recognizable reusable container family without becoming branded merch in-story.

---

# 12. Toys / childhood material culture

Children should not only hold educational holograms.

Use mix:
- miniature utility machines/transit vehicles,
- construction sets,
- physical/digital hybrid board games,
- sports objects,
- art materials,
- character/story figures from Meridian media,
- homemade/repaired toys,
- school project objects.

This strengthens the collection engine naturally:
**citizens themselves already miniaturize the machines they see every day.**

Hard:
no foreshadowing clue hidden in every toy.

---

# 13. Sports / fandom / entertainment objects

Possible material expressions:
- team patches/scarves/bands,
- event tickets/tokens/reproductions,
- signed physical items,
- cards/prints,
- miniature arenas/vehicles,
- fan-made objects.

Keep specific sport canon controlled by existing everyday bible; do not invent a giant league here solely for merchandise.

---

# 14. Music / art / physical media

Digital abundance does not eliminate physical art.

Plausible:
- limited physical print editions,
- object-based installations,
- tactile instruments,
- restored/reproduced Earth-descended instruments,
- local craft materials,
- signed scores/prints/objects.

Original vs reproduction matters culturally through provenance, but reproduction is not considered fake worthless culture.

---

# 15. Shops / commercial visual language

No giant holographic ad canyon by default.

Neighborhood shops/services use:
- readable signs,
- physical display objects where useful,
- transparent pricing/account access,
- digital reservation/order overlays,
- reusable-container return points,
- repair counters.

Transfer/commercial areas can be denser/brighter than residential zones without cyberpunk cliché.

---

# 16. Repair culture

Repair is normal but not sacred ideology.

Three broad attitudes coexist:
- maintain/repair as ordinary economics,
- replace/recycle when safer/cheaper,
- preserve old shell/object for sentimental/design/history reasons.

Professional certification matters for safety-critical objects.
DIY culture exists mostly in noncritical surfaces/devices unless authorized.

This supports protagonist/Gideon/Niko differences without making all citizens engineers.

---

# 17. Secondhand / inheritance

Objects can pass through:
- family,
- resale,
- community exchange,
- professional decommissioning,
- archive/museum donation,
- recycling/refabrication.

Inherited object does NOT automatically mean hidden clue.

Most inheritance is ordinary.

---

# 18. Status / wealth display

Status may appear through:
- larger/better-located housing,
- professional service convenience,
- high-quality tailoring/fit,
- rare authenticated heritage objects,
- access to time/space/privacy,
- custom work/art commissions,
not absurd jewel-encrusted scarcity goods.

Cassian's visual wealth uses this grammar.

---

# 19. Act transformation

Act1:
fullest normal diversity; important baseline.

Act2:
protective layers, borrowed clothing, temporary blankets/containers, school/family emergency material.

Act3:
small H-B life-history differences through objects/wardrobes only.

Act4:
merch/fraud around `prediction` may appear, but avoid turning into comedy shop montage.

Act5:
heritage/provenance objects become socially loaded; ordinary collecting culture makes disputed old objects believable.

Act6:
repair/reuse/secondhand intensifies; some fashion remains because people do not stop being people during crisis.

Act7~8:
new knowledge should not generate a sudden `lineage fashion` subculture across everyone within weeks.

Act9:
settlement clothing adapts to breathing/weather/work needs while retaining Meridian civilian style underneath.

---

# 20. Collectibility rules

Good:
- recognizable object family,
- generation differences,
- local markings,
- provenance,
- character wear,
- transformed reuse.

Bad:
- rarity colors,
- secret stat bonuses,
- `complete all seven relics`,
- every old object being important,
- expensive object = morally better owner.

# Background-extra design test

A random Midring crowd of 20 should include visible variation in:
- age,
- clothing family,
- work/off-duty state,
- repaired/new mix,
- assistive devices,
- bags/carry habits,
- family/child objects,
without looking like 20 separate fantasy factions.

A random Old Works shift should still show personal differences beneath job-safe layers.

A random Act9 outdoor group should share breathing/weather requirements but not one colonist uniform.
