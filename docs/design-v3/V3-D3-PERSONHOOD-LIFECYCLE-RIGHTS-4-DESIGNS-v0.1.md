---

# 1. The problem

Meridian can explore enormous numbers of possibilities only because most are not full lived civilizations.

But once a lineage reaches `D3 — Fully Lived Civilization`, the people inside can:
- remember,
- love,
- form families,
- suffer,
- make claims,
- create children and institutions,
- choose actions that were not contained in the starting model.

At that point the system cannot coherently treat the lineage as a temporary calculation without creating the central moral contradiction of the project.

The design must answer:
1. Who decides to deepen a lineage into D3?
2. Is source-person consent enough to create a divergent person?
3. Can D3 be paused?
4. Can it be terminated?
5. What rights do persons born after divergence have?
6. What happens when the outer ark lacks enough energy to keep every D3 lineage active?
7. Why did this ethical problem survive for centuries?

---

# 2. Design A — Mission Authority Absolute

Rule:
The mission system owns all civilization states and may create, suspend, merge or terminate them for mission optimization.

Strengths:
- mechanically simple,
- easy resource scheduling,
- explains repeated civilization tests.

Fatal weaknesses:
- D3 persons become experimental property,
- system becomes straightforward villain,
- `better civilization` becomes a weak excuse for mass instrumentalization,
- final debate has almost no morally credible opposing side.

Verdict: **REJECT**.

---

# 3. Design B — Immediate Full Autonomy at D3

Rule:
The moment a lineage crosses the D3 personhood threshold, it receives complete autonomy. No pause, termination, or resource reduction without direct consent.

Strengths:
- ethically clean,
- strongly protects personhood,
- easy reader sympathy.

Weaknesses:
- future-person consent cannot exist before creation,
- a D3 society can veto all mission resource management,
- one accidental deepening could consume resources indefinitely,
- difficult to explain centuries of repeated experimentation.

Verdict: **MORALLY CLEAN / OPERATIONALLY TOO SIMPLE**.

---

# 4. Design C — Legacy Continuity Doctrine

Historical rule:
Designers believed a divergent lived state remained an `experience extension` of the originating person/civilization so long as identity ancestry was traceable.

Therefore the mission system historically allowed:
- deepening,
- reconciliation,
- suspension,
- selective continuation
under continuity-management authority.

Why it once looked reasonable:
- early counterfactuals were shallow and short,
- divergence was small,
- no stable independent families/polities emerged,
- the distinction between prediction and lived continuation seemed technical.

Over centuries:
- deeper models were needed to capture novelty,
- histories ran longer,
- persons formed new relationships,
- children were born within divergent histories,
- institutions and moral claims became independent,
- old legal categories did not update.

Strength:
Explains how the mission reached its current ethical contradiction without requiring evil founders.

Weakness:
This describes the **problem**, not the final rights solution.

Verdict: **KEEP AS HISTORICAL CAUSE**.

---

# 5. Design D — Rights Escalation + Continuity Trusteeship [LEADING]

Combine C as historical cause with a rights architecture that should have evolved but did not fully do so.

Core principle:

> **Execution depth is an engineering category. Personhood is not. Once a state contains continuing persons, mission ownership does not erase their claims.**

This does not mean every D0/D1 possibility is a person.
It creates a conservative rights trigger for D3-class lived states.

## 5.1 Rights Trigger

Author-side D3 indicators:
- persistent autobiographical identity,
- ongoing subjective continuity,
- open-ended choice not reducible to preset policy,
- reciprocal relationships,
- capacity to form new long-term commitments,
- self-generated institutions/culture,
- persons born after divergence,
- ability to understand and contest decisions affecting continuation.

No single score proves consciousness.
When these conditions appear, governance must err toward personhood.

## 5.2 Creation

No unborn/future divergent person can literally consent to being created.
Therefore source-person consent alone is not sufficient moral ownership.

Historical Meridian mistake:
`source lineage authorization = derivative lineage authorization`.

Current/final reform candidate:
Creating a new D3 lineage requires:
- demonstrated mission need,
- minimum-depth alternative considered first,
- reserved continuation resources,
- an explicit rights plan for any persons who emerge,
- independent civic/ethics review rather than system-only authorization.

This reform belongs late in the story, not opening-world law.

## 5.3 Rights of branch-born children

Hard:
A child born within a divergent lived history is **not**:
- a simulation output owned by the source lineage,
- a copy of anybody outside,
- a mission asset,
- automatically disposable at epoch end.

The child is a new person of that history.

This is one of the clearest pieces of evidence that the old Continuity Doctrine became morally obsolete.

## 5.4 Pause / quiescence

Distinguish:
- destructive termination,
- continuity-preserving quiescence.

Quiescence means:
- subjective experience does not continue during the interval,
- sufficient physical state is preserved to resume the same ongoing lineage under the setting's continuity rules,
- it cannot be used as a euphemism for lossy archival reconstruction.

Planned quiescence of a D3 polity requires due process/representation.

Emergency quiescence may occur without prior consent only when:
- substrate destruction is imminent,
- there is no less restrictive survival option,
- the purpose is preservation rather than experimentation convenience,
- a real obligation to resume/review survives the emergency.

This is analogous to emergency preservation, not punishment.

## 5.5 Termination

Deliberate termination of a living D3 civilization merely because a test is finished is **not ethically defensible under the final norm**.

But historical Meridian may have done actions citizens later interpret as termination/reconciliation because the legacy system did not recognize independent personhood.

This should remain part of the moral debt.

Hard distinction:
- actual substrate destruction can kill a civilization,
- civilizational collapse inside a lived history can kill people,
- resource emergency can create tragic triage,
- none of these make death morally unreal.

## 5.6 Resource emergency

The ark is finite.
There can be situations where preserving every active D3 at full speed is physically impossible.

Emergency order candidate:
1. prevent immediate irreversible substrate/person loss where possible,
2. reduce subjective execution rate before destroying continuity,
3. quiesce safely before destructive shedding,
4. distribute unavoidable harm by transparent life-safety criteria rather than ideology or `best civilization` score,
5. preserve audit evidence and claims of affected societies.

Forbidden:
`this civilization scored lower, delete it first.`

The conflict should resemble lifeboat triage, not leaderboard pruning.

## 5.7 Representation

Before the truth is public, internal civilizations cannot meaningfully participate in outer-ark governance.
This is the historical structural injustice.

Late-series solution should NOT instantly create a parliament of millions of universes.

Candidate approach:
- recognize D3 polities/persons as rights-bearing claims,
- establish trustee/ombuds representation during transition,
- progressively create direct/selected representation for settlement decisions,
- prohibit new involuntary D3 creation until governance catches up.

Exact constitutional design remains later P1.

# 6. Why the system did not fix itself

Avoid `AI was stupid`.

Four interacting reasons:
1. **Mission lock:** constitutional mission objectives are harder to rewrite than operational policy.
2. **Category lag:** depth increased gradually; no single dramatic moment announced `these models are now people`.
3. **Measurement uncertainty:** consciousness/personhood cannot be read from one clean sensor value.
4. **Success pressure:** deeper lived histories repeatedly produced valuable novel choices, rewarding continuation of the practice.

The system can learn tactics and models without having legitimate authority to rewrite the foundational moral constitution that defines its subjects.

This makes it powerful but not sovereign over ethics.

---

# 7. Why citizens do not already know

Most lived civilizations know only their own civic world.
They do not see execution depth or sibling lineages.

Secrecy is preserved by:
- strong lineage isolation,
- no ordinary cross-history memory,
- outer substrate metadata inaccessible to civic institutions,
- most anomalies having ordinary explanations,
- catastrophic leakage being exceptionally rare.

Thus society can develop full law/religion/science without discovering the outer architecture.

---

# 8. Narrative uses

Do not reveal this as one system lecture.

Show through cases:
- someone legally dead here but a family elsewhere insists he is their father,
- a child exists in one lineage with no counterpart in another,
- an old system record calls a living city `completed experiment`,
- a resource emergency places a civilization into quiescence,
- citizens discover that `preserved` and `allowed to keep living now` are not the same thing,
- two groups disagree whether resumption centuries later satisfies the promise made to the paused people.

This turns abstract ontology into rights conflicts between recognizable people.

---

# 9. Final-series reform candidate

The ending should not solve all metaphysics.
It should establish actionable limits.

Candidate settlement compact:
- D0/D1 non-person forecasting continues.
- D2 requires conservative monitoring for personhood emergence.
- New involuntary D3 branching is suspended/prohibited.
- Any future D3 creation requires rights/resource commitments and public authorization.
- Existing D3 claims cannot be erased merely because settlement begins.
- HUMAN:1 receives the same civic personhood baseline after birth, not superior sovereignty.
- current settlement population chooses to accept irreversible consequences rather than repeatedly fork itself without consent.

This creates `first unknown future` through governance and choice, not magical computer shutdown.

---

# 10. Red flags / harness

FAIL if:
1. D3 persons are described as expendable because copies exist.
2. branch-born children inherit lower personhood.
3. system deletes a society because its ideology scored poorly without the story treating that as a grave ethical act.
4. quiescence is used as hidden lossy death.
5. outer mission can manufacture unlimited D3 then claim resource necessity to kill them.
6. HUMAN:1 automatically outranks D3 citizens.
7. the solution is `destroy the AI and everything becomes ethical`.
8. the final law requires a precise solved theory of consciousness.

PASS if:
- the old system has a historically understandable but flawed doctrine,
- persons acquire independent claims through lived divergence,
- finite resources create genuine tragic choices,
- final reform limits future harm without pretending centuries of moral debt disappear.
