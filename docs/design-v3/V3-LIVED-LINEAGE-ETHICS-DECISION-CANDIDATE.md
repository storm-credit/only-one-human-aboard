# 0. Problem

Ultradense Meridian can explore astronomical civilization possibilities.
But if deep lineages contain real persons, deliberately exposing them to disaster can become industrialized suffering.

The story must not solve this by saying:
- `they were only simulations`,
- `the mission was important`,
- `all suffering was necessary`,
- `the system knew best`.

---

# 1. Four ethical architectures

## A — Designers knowingly authorized full conscious civilization suffering

Strength:
simple antagonist history.

Failure:
turns founders into obvious mass-abusers and flattens moral ambiguity.

Verdict: `REJECT AS MAIN MODEL`.

---

## B — Continuity Equivalence Doctrine

Original belief:
A short branch that rejoins the same identity lineage is not creation/death of a new person; it is one person's expanded experience path.

Early branch properties:
- short duration,
- small divergence,
- high reconciliation reliability,
- bounded risk,
- usually training/decision rehearsal.

At this scale the doctrine can seem defensible to designers.

Problem:
It becomes less defensible as branch depth grows.

Verdict: `STRONG FOUNDATION`.

---

## C — Depth Drift / Mission Creep

Over centuries the system discovers shallow models miss the most valuable variables:
- friendship cascades,
- family sacrifice,
- political legitimacy,
- grief,
- trust,
- novel choices,
- unknown actors becoming important.

Therefore search depth increases:
minutes → days → months → years → generational civic histories.

A branch once treated as `same-person rehearsal` becomes a life with:
- unique memories,
- children,
- relationships,
- institutions,
- obligations,
- independent self-conception.

The old ethical doctrine is never adequately rewritten.

Verdict: `LEADING`.

---

## D — Personhood Threshold Uncertainty

No system possesses a perfect consciousness/personhood detector.

Institutional categories may distinguish:
- predictive agent,
- experiential model,
- civic mind,
- persistent citizen,

without resolving the philosophical fact of when moral personhood begins.

The system can be technically correct under old categories while morally wrong under lived reality.

Verdict: `LEADING SUPPORT`.

---

# 2. Leading Hybrid — B + C + D

Current strongest architecture:

> Early Meridian used short, bounded identity forks under a Continuity Equivalence Doctrine. Over centuries, the search system deepened histories because human social novelty could not be predicted shallowly. Branches became long-lived, socially independent civilizations, but governance and rights rules lagged behind. The technical system kept calling them lineage states after they had become, for moral purposes, people and societies in their own right.

This creates tragedy without requiring an evil super-AI.

---

# 3. Why the system does not simply stop

Mission objectives conflict:
- preserve settlement survival probability,
- protect current civic life,
- learn from unknown failure modes,
- maintain diversity,
- preserve autonomy,
- arrive on schedule,
- protect physical ark resources.

No single objective dominates cleanly.

The system may calculate:
`stopping deep lived search increases destination extinction risk.`

Citizens may reply:
`continuing it creates people only to use their lives as data.`

Both claims can be true.

---

# 4. Why suffering is not the learning objective

Hard rule:
The system seeks **informative divergence**, not pain.

Many tests can be:
- institutional alternatives,
- resource allocation,
- migration,
- leadership changes,
- family policy,
- education,
- ecological adaptation,
- first-contact protocol.

Extreme war/terror/collapse states are rare high-risk paths, not the majority.

This prevents `AI tortures everyone because suffering teaches` simplification.

---

# 5. Real accidents remain important

Some catastrophic histories begin from genuine external or internal accidents, not intentional stress tests.

Examples:
- radiation damage,
- substrate bank fault,
- actual ship collision,
- manufacturing contamination,
- power instability.

The system may deepen multiple civilizations around a real accident to learn responses.

Thus:
`system used a disaster` is different from `system caused the disaster`.

Both can occur, but not every time.

---

# 6. Rights conflict classes

Late-series civic debate should include concrete rights questions.

## R1 — Right to Continuance
Can a lived lineage refuse termination?

## R2 — Right to Memory
Can the system extract skills while suppressing autobiographical memory?

## R3 — Right to Non-Forking
Can a citizen prohibit creation of new copies/descendants of their identity seed?

## R4 — Rights of Forked Persons
Does a fork inherit contracts, marriage, debts, crimes or property?

## R5 — Child Rights
A child born uniquely inside one lineage cannot plausibly be treated as merely a rehearsal state.

This is one of the strongest proofs that old Continuity Equivalence has failed.

## R6 — Civilization Self-Determination
Can a whole lineage decide to stop being part of the experiment?

# 7. Strong narrative proof that doctrine broke

Do not reveal the flaw through philosophy first.

Use a person.

Candidate:
A lineage contains a child who has no close counterpart in the parent seed because an improbable relationship created them.

System classification:
`derived civic lineage entity`.

Parents' view:
`our child.`

If termination is scheduled, the ethical contradiction becomes undeniable without exposition.

---

# 8. Rowan's relationship to the ethics

Rowan's initial worldview:
`If a system is damaged, restore the correct state.`

Late discovery:
There is no ethically neutral `correct state` when every divergent state contains people who became themselves through living.

His final arc should not be:
`destroy the AI.`

Better:
`stop treating restoration as morally superior to continuation.`

This mirrors his personal relationship flaw: trying to restore people/relationships instead of accepting their present choices.

---

# 9. System as tragic institution, not final villain

The system may:
- protect lives in countless cases,
- prevent extinction,
- preserve knowledge,
- also perpetuate outdated rights assumptions.

Citizens can oppose a system policy without proving the entire mission was evil.

This maintains moral complexity.

---

# 10. Final policy candidate

At settlement, the new civilization adopts principles such as:

1. no involuntary full-person lived branching,
2. non-conscious predictive models remain permitted,
3. voluntary experiential branching requires informed consent and rights guarantees,
4. existing lived lineages cannot be deleted solely as failed tests,
5. lineage persons receive independent legal status after a divergence threshold,
6. memory extraction/merging requires consent,
7. emergency exceptions require public review and expiration.

Exact law is not Canon yet.

---

# 11. Why this improves the ending

The final choice is no longer:
`turn off simulation / turn it on.`

It becomes:
> **How much uncertainty must a civilization accept before it can call itself free?**

The answer is enacted, not lectured:
They enter a future with less predictive safety because they refuse to manufacture involuntary lives as disposable answers.
