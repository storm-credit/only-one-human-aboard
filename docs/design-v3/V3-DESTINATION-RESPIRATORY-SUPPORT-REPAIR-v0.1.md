# 1. Science boundary

Current destination premise:
- near-Earth-order total pressure,
- oxygen partial pressure below safe routine human-normal outdoor respiration,
- possible aerosols/trace contaminants require monitoring/filtration.

Hard distinction:
**filtering and oxygen supply solve different problems.**

Air-purifying respirators remove contaminants from ambient air but do not create missing oxygen.
Therefore a filter-only mask is NOT sufficient for the selected destination atmosphere.

Reader-facing consequence:
Outdoor people need some form of **supplemental breathing gas / oxygen enrichment** in addition to any contaminant filtration that may be required.

---

# 2. Four equipment directions

## A — Filter-only face mask
Concept:
ambient air passes through filters.

Problem:
does not correct low oxygen.

Verdict:
# **REJECT**.

Allowed only in already oxygen-safe processed indoor environments when particulate/chemical filtration is the actual task.

---

## B — Open nasal/loose oxygen assist
Concept:
small oxygen flow supplements ambient breathing with minimal face coverage.

Strength:
lightweight and visually ordinary.

Weakness:
- concentration is sensitive to exertion/wind/leakage,
- poor contamination isolation,
- less suited to rough field work.

Verdict:
`LIMITED LOW-EXERTION / CONTROLLED-AREA OPTION`, not default work kit.

---

## C — Demand-regulated compact supplemental-gas face unit
Concept:
A close-fitting but non-pressure-suit face unit supplies oxygen-enriched breathing gas on demand while external atmosphere supplies the surrounding pressure environment.

Possible system layers:
- compact oxygen / enriched-gas reservoir or local supply connection,
- demand regulator / mixing control,
- exhalation management,
- independent environment sensor,
- optional contamination filter stage where chemistry requires it.

Strength:
- directly solves oxygen deficiency,
- far less bulky than a full pressure suit,
- easy visual distinction between breathing support and weather PPE,
- can support work/exertion variants.

Weakness:
- finite gas duration,
- face fit/seal/maintenance matter,
- exact future hardware remains speculative.

Verdict:
# **SELECTED DEFAULT DIRECTION**.

---

## D — Compact semi-closed rebreather
Concept:
part of exhaled gas is recirculated with CO2 removal and oxygen addition.

Strength:
- longer autonomy,
- less gas consumption,
- good for remote field teams.

Weakness:
- more complexity,
- scrubber/thermal/moisture management,
- can make every outdoor scene look like specialist expedition gear.

Verdict:
`SPECIALIST / LONG-RANGE OPTION`, not default citizen outdoor kit.

---

# 3. Selected hierarchy

## Normal short/medium outdoor work
**C — demand-regulated compact supplemental breathing-gas unit**.

## Low-exertion controlled exterior area
B-like minimal assist may exist after atmosphere is well characterised and medical/operational limits are known.

## Long-duration remote or contaminated work
D-like semi-closed system or larger C supply pack.

## Indoors
processed oxygen-compatible civic atmosphere; no breathing mask by default.

---

# 4. Visual design rule

The breathing system must NOT become one generic `cool sci-fi mask`.

Separate visually/functionally:
1. breathing-gas delivery,
2. environment sensor,
3. contaminant filtration if required,
4. eye/weather protection,
5. communication,
6. work PPE.

This allows different jobs to assemble different kits.

Examples:
- logistics worker: compact breathing unit + weather shell + route/comm gear,
- damage-analysis technician: breathing unit + eye protection + sensor/tools,
- medic: breathing unit + patient-compatible emergency gas interface,
- short public transfer: lighter civic unit,
- long remote survey: larger semi-closed pack.

No universal helmet silhouette.

---

# 5. Architecture implication

Near-Earth total pressure still matters.

Buildings do not need to resist vacuum-level pressure differentials by default, but they DO need:
- processed/clean indoor air,
- oxygen enrichment,
- contamination control,
- monitoring,
- doors/vestibules appropriate to biosecurity/weather rather than full airlock logic everywhere.

Thus the destination remains materially easier to inhabit than Mars/vacuum while not being freely breathable.

---

# 6. Vehicle implication

Long-duration surface vehicles can provide processed breathable cabin atmosphere.

Therefore:
- workers may remove face units inside a verified sealed cabin,
- boarding interfaces include quick gas/health/environment checks rather than full decompression cycle,
- rescue vehicles carry emergency supplemental breathing supply.

This creates practical scene variation.

---

# 7. HUMAN:1 implication

Natural-Origin infant/child never receives `native-atmosphere adaptation privilege`.

HUMAN care remains:
- processed indoor atmosphere,
- controlled exposure,
- ordinary medical welfare thresholds.

Outdoor access later uses age/health-appropriate support.

---

# 8. Failure modes allowed in story texture

Use sparingly:
- supply duration miscalculation,
- damaged line/regulator,
- face-fit problem,
- sensor disagreement,
- contamination filter saturation,
- moisture/fogging,
- cold/wind affecting comfort or equipment,
- emergency sharing/transfer.

Do NOT create repeated `mask ripped off = instant death` scenes.
Low oxygen is dangerous but this is not vacuum.

# 10. Source boundary used for this repair

Primary technical principles checked against:
- NIOSH guidance: air-purifying respirators do not supply oxygen and cannot be used for oxygen-deficient atmospheres,
- NASA human-system atmosphere/spacesuit standards: oxygen partial pressure, total pressure and breathing support must be treated together.

These constrain the logic; they do not dictate far-future industrial design.
