# 1. Naming-system choice — 4 designs

## A. Modern Earth names preserved almost unchanged
Pros:
- instantly readable,
- low terminology burden,
- easy emotional attachment.

Cons:
- can make centuries-later Meridian feel too contemporary if every combination is culturally predictable.

## B. Recognizable Earth-origin names recombined across centuries
Pros:
- readable,
- naturally multicultural,
- future feeling comes from recombination rather than invented syllables,
- does not require nationality exposition.

Cons:
- combinations can feel artificially `international` if overdesigned.

## C. New future-Meridian invented names
Pros:
- distinctive setting identity.

Cons:
- high risk of fantasy/game-character sound,
- weak read-aloud familiarity,
- unnecessary cognitive burden.

## D. Registry name + short call-name system
Pros:
- plausible future bureaucracy.

Cons:
- doubles labels,
- solves no current narrative problem.

# Decision

# **B — recognizable Earth-origin names, freely recombined over generations = SELECTED CANDIDATE**

Hard:
- no invented `future syllables` merely to signal SF,
- no assumption that given-name language determines surname ancestry,
- no nationality exposition unless story-relevant,
- names should sound like names people could actually call across hundreds of episodes.

# 2. Protagonist name criteria

Must satisfy:
1. Korean reader pronounces immediately.
2. Given name remains comfortable when repeated alone in dialogue.
3. Full name has asymmetrical or at least non-choppy rhythm.
4. Fits male ~36~38.
5. Fits ordinary field/technical professional.
6. Not strongly hero-coded, aristocratic or fantasy-coded.
7. Not so generic that it disappears.
8. Does not depend on `한` or Korean ancestry.
9. Does not sound like an obvious codename.
10. Works beside Mira Solano / Arun Kalev / Niko Osman / Selene Adebayo without feeling from a different genre.

Preferred Korean rhythm:
- 3+3,
- 3+2,
- 4+3,
- occasionally 2+3 if the full name still flows.

Avoid making 2+2 the default.

# 5. Family naming rule

Once protagonist surname is selected:
- parents share or plausibly retain their own surnames depending family structure,
- older sister does not need a different surname merely because married/partnered,
- child naming can reflect current household convention,
- avoid using family names to signal hidden ancestry/destiny.

No family name may become a clue by default.

# 6. District naming calibration

Four options:

## A. Fully invented district proper names
REJECT as default.
Too much terminology for a mystery already carrying many concepts.

## B. Functional ship-city names only
Examples:
Spine / Transfer Belt / Midring / Garden Commons / Old Works / Med-University / Rim.

Very readable and institutionally plausible.

## C. Functional official name + lived nickname
Example:
`Transfer Belt` officially, residents simply say `벨트`.
`Old Works` officially/historically, workers say `올드웍스` or a local shorter nickname.

## D. Historical Earth-city-inspired proper names
Risk of irrelevant Earth nostalgia/worldbuilding burden.

# Decision

# **C — functional official names + natural resident shorthand = SELECTED CANDIDATE**

Reason:
The city should feel lived-in without forcing readers to memorize eight decorative proper nouns.

Current shorthand candidates:
- Central/Spine → `스파인`
- Transfer Belt → `벨트`
- Midring → `미드링`
- Garden Commons → `가든`
- Old Works → `올드웍스`
- Med-University → `메드` / `메드클러스터` depending context
- Rim/Expansion → `림`
- Legacy Service Layer → official technical term; no cute nickname required

These are surface candidates, not final Canon labels.

# 7. Currency/payment calibration

Options:

## A. Invent a unique currency name
REJECT by default.
No narrative payoff justifies another term.

## B. `크레딧` for discretionary exchange + separate nonmarket strategic allocation
SELECTED CANDIDATE.

Reader-facing rule:
- basic survival services are rights,
- ordinary discretionary goods/services use account credits,
- strategic power/medical/industrial capacity cannot simply be bought with credits and is allocated/scheduled separately.

Everyday speech:
`크레딧`, `잔액`, `결제`, `배정`, `슬롯` as context requires.

Hard:
Do not explain monetary theory unless a scene needs it.
