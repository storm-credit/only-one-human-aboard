# V3 ENSEMBLE POV ARCHITECTURE v0.1 — CANDIDATE

Status: `DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Define how the series can remain anchored to one protagonist without making him the causal, informational, or dramatic center of every event.

---

# 0. Core rule

This is an **ensemble civilization story with one primary reader anchor**, not a story in which the protagonist must be present for every important event.

The protagonist:
- is the strongest long-term emotional/mystery anchor,
- does not witness every important event,
- does not cause every major change,
- does not own every solution,
- does not have privileged access to every institution,
- may learn major events only after other characters have already lived through them.

Hard:
**important events are allowed to begin, escalate, resolve, or fail while the protagonist is absent.**

---

# 1. Recommended narrative mode

Leading candidate:
**close third-person limited with rotating POV ownership.**

Reason:
- keeps each scene emotionally grounded,
- allows protagonist-absent stories,
- supports contradictory testimony across lineages,
- avoids omniscient explanation of hidden ontology,
- makes institutions and ordinary citizens causally real.

Default:
one clear POV owner per scene.

Episode can contain more than one scene/POV only when transition is explicit and necessary.

Avoid rapid head-hopping inside one continuous scene.

---

# 2. POV share bands — candidate, not quota

Series-wide target:
- protagonist direct POV: roughly **45–55%**
- core/major recurring cast POV: roughly **30–40%**
- ordinary/emergent/institutional POV: roughly **10–20%**

These are diagnostic bands, not mathematical quotas.

Act tendency:
- Act1: protagonist-heavy to establish attachment and profession
- Act2: protagonist still anchor, but survival relay expands
- Act3: protagonist-heavy again because apparent regression must be felt directly
- Act4~6: ensemble share rises substantially
- Act7~9: civilization-scale relay; protagonist remains anchor but no longer owns majority of consequential events

FAIL if protagonist remains >70% of meaningful causal scenes after Act4.
FAIL if protagonist falls so low that the central emotional line disappears.

---

# 3. Types of protagonist-absent story

## A. Independent causal chain
An event starts before the protagonist knows about it.

Examples:
- transit dispatcher reroutes one line,
- school staff decide shelter policy,
- district council delays evacuation,
- sanitation team finds contamination,
- a family hides a missing person,
- security unit misidentifies a threat.

The protagonist may encounter only the consequence later.

## B. Core-character private life
Show a major character as a person independent of protagonist.

Examples:
- Mira with family/work colleagues,
- Selene making command decisions without protagonist input,
- Rafi at hospital/triage,
- Sora investigating someone unrelated to protagonist,
- Juno running community network,
- Niko making a choice the protagonist cannot supervise.

Hard:
Do not make every private scene secretly about what they think of protagonist.

## C. Institutional viewpoint
A system-wide decision occurs in the institution that actually owns it.

Examples:
- emergency command,
- hospital allocation,
- court/review board,
- transport control,
- archive office,
- resource allocation,
- school/community management.

Purpose:
prevent protagonist from becoming implausibly omnipresent.

## D. Ordinary-person viewpoint
A non-core citizen experiences the cost of a policy/disaster.

Use for:
- evacuation queue,
- housing loss,
- memorial/funeral contradiction,
- ration disruption,
- school closure,
- rumor/prophecy panic,
- arrival/settlement access.

Hard:
ordinary POV needs a concrete local desire, not merely exposition delivery.

## E. Emergent-critical actor
A previously minor person becomes important because of circumstances.

Narrative sequence:
`ordinary life → small choice → consequence elsewhere → social cascade → later recognition of importance`

Do not announce that the person is an unknown variable.

## F. Antagonistic/morally conflicting viewpoint
Show why a disliked actor's decision makes sense from inside their constraints.

Use sparingly.
Do not erase accountability by giving everyone a sympathetic POV.

---

# 4. Off-screen causality rule

The universe must continue when the protagonist leaves a room.

For every major arc, require:
1. at least two important causal developments while protagonist is absent,
2. at least one decision he learns about after it is irreversible,
3. at least one problem solved by someone else,
4. at least one problem made worse by someone else without his participation,
5. at least one relationship/institutional change he does not control.

This extends the existing Rowan causal-centrality gate in the Cast/Incident Density Harness.

---

# 5. Information asymmetry

Different POVs may know different pieces of reality.

Important:
- protagonist is not the only mystery detector,
- protagonist is not always first to find evidence,
- other characters may hide evidence from him,
- reader may occasionally know something protagonist does not,
- protagonist may know something a POV character does not.

This creates suspense beyond `what will protagonist discover next?`.

Guardrail:
Do not reveal author-only ultradense ontology merely because POV changed.
Every POV remains bounded by that person's actual access/knowledge.

---

# 6. Cross-lineage testimony use

Other-history morality should often be delivered outside protagonist POV.

Example pattern:
1. current Selene saves civilians in her own POV or another witness POV,
2. elsewhere a person refuses to trust her because of an incompatible remembered history,
3. protagonist hears only part of this later,
4. evidence arrives much later.

This makes contradictory histories social facts, not protagonist-only hallucinations.

---

# 7. Protagonist absence as emotional tool

Sometimes the strongest use of protagonist absence is to show the effect he cannot see.

Examples:
- after he seals a sector, remain with the people on the other side,
- after he makes a technical recommendation, show the family affected by the decision,
- after he assumes a relationship can be repaired, show the other person's private refusal,
- after he is praised as a hero, show a household that hates him for the same choice.

This prevents moral framing from being monopolized by protagonist self-interpretation.

---

# 8. Act-level ensemble profile

## Act1
Anchor first.
Protagonist dominant, but introduce 3~5 recurring non-protagonist POV lenses by behavior rather than profile chapters.

## Act2
Survival relay.
Use Selene/Rafi/Mira/Juno/ordinary trapped citizens so geography and human cost exceed protagonist's location.

## Act3
Protagonist temporarily regains dominance because the apparent regression must be experienced through his certainty.
Still show at least one H-B life that clearly continued without him.

## Act4
Social spread.
POV expansion strongly recommended: security, medicine, business, media, ordinary believers/scam victims.

## Act5
Investigation + relationship contradiction.
Use Hana/Sora/Arun-side or family witnesses so historical evidence is not protagonist-owned.

## Act6
Maximum ensemble pressure.
At least one major causal chain should proceed for multiple episodes with protagonist peripheral or absent.
Emergent minor actor may become decisive.

## Act7
Scale inference.
Use institutional/ordinary perspectives to prove the revelation affects society, not only protagonist philosophy.

## Act8
Rights of many.
D3 person/family/child case must have its own viewpoint ownership; protagonist cannot argue their humanity on their behalf as sole voice.

## Act9
Settlement relay.
Multiple groups make independent choices. Final viewpoint may return to protagonist for emotional closure, but the settlement outcome must already have been co-authored by many characters.

---

# 9. Anti-anthology guardrail

Multi-POV must not become disconnected anthology fiction.

Each non-protagonist POV episode/scene must connect to at least one of:
- existing relationship,
- current incident chain,
- institutional consequence,
- later causal pivot,
- ongoing world-state change,
- active mystery evidence.

A one-off POV that exists only to explain lore should be cut or merged.

Recurring secondary POVs are preferred over constantly inventing new viewpoint characters.

---

# 10. POV introduction rule

Before giving a named character a full POV, preferably seed them in another person's scene first unless surprise itself is the purpose.

Reader recognition sequence:
`see person → understand role → later enter POV`

This reduces cognitive load.

Emergent actors should usually appear ordinarily before their critical POV.

---

# 11. Protagonist causal-centrality harness extension

PASS per major Act if:
- protagonist is absent from at least one consequential sequence,
- another known character owns a decision with irreversible consequences,
- at least one ordinary/non-core viewpoint changes reader understanding of the crisis,
- protagonist cannot undo all consequences,
- no institution waits for protagonist to function.

FAIL if:
- every major decision requires protagonist arrival,
- all other POVs only react to protagonist,
- every mystery clue is found by protagonist,
- every moral judgment is settled by protagonist narration,
- protagonist is physically present at every climax.

---

# 12. Current verdict

**ENSEMBLE POV ARCHITECTURE = LEADING CANDIDATE.**

The intended series shape is:

`one primary emotional anchor`
+
`multiple independent people`
+
`multiple concurrent causal chains`
+
`institutions that act without the protagonist`
+
`ordinary people who can become historically important`.

The protagonist matters because the reader follows his long transformation, not because the universe waits for him.

Exact episode-by-episode POV allocation remains open and should be built only after World/Character Bible v3 consolidation and the Act/incident map are stable.
