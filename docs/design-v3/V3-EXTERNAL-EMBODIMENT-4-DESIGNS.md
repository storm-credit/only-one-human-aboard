# V3 EXTERNAL SETTLEMENT EMBODIMENT — 4 DESIGNS

Status: `P0 OPTIONS / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

---

# 0. Problem

At destination, citizens must interact with macroscopic physical reality.
If their body is merely a remote avatar, physical danger becomes cheap.
If their mind is copied into a body, continuity becomes ambiguous.

Need a model that preserves:
- same-person continuity,
- physical stakes,
- staged settlement,
- substrate identity history,
- no easy resurrection.

---

# A — Remote Avatar

Mind remains entirely in Meridian substrate.
External body is a drone/avatar.

Pros:
- technically clean,
- easy staged settlement,
- safe.

Cons:
- external death is hardware loss,
- final survival stakes weak,
- `first irreversible future` loses force.

Verdict: `REJECT AS PRIMARY`.
Can exist for preliminary scouting only.

---

# B — Copy Into Macro Body

A full cognitive state is copied into a biological/synthetic body.

Pros:
- external citizen seems physically independent.

Cons:
- original remains unless deleted,
- copy identity problem becomes central,
- forced deletion looks arbitrary,
- same-person continuity not guaranteed.

Verdict: `NOT PREFERRED`.
Useful for controversial alternate process.

---

# C — Continuity Anchor Migration [LEADING]

A citizen's active continuity process is physically transferred/extended from ultradense substrate infrastructure into a settlement-grade local embodiment system.

Key:
- no stop-copy-delete step,
- causal process remains continuously active through migration,
- settlement body/local neural substrate becomes part of the same ongoing process,
- original ship-side execution allocation is relinquished as migration completes.

This resembles moving an active process through continuously connected hardware rather than restoring a stored snapshot.

Pros:
- strongest same-person continuity,
- external physical stakes,
- compatible with continuity-anchor model,
- staged transition possible.

Cons:
- speculative engineering,
- need temporary redundancy rules during migration.

Verdict: `LEADING`.

---

# D — Distributed Continuity Mesh

Person remains continuously distributed across:
- body,
- local settlement substrate,
- possibly ship infrastructure.

Pros:
- robust,
- plausible for advanced society.

Cons:
- death becomes difficult to define,
- physical body damage may be cheap,
- can weaken final survival tension.

Verdict: `USE ONLY AS LIMITED FAULT-TOLERANCE LAYER`.

---

# 1. Leading Hybrid — C with bounded D

Current strongest architecture:

> Citizens migrate active continuity into external settlement embodiment while retaining only short-range fault tolerance that preserves an uninterrupted active process. Once continuity is truly broken, snapshots cannot prove resurrection.

This keeps:
- ordinary technical redundancy,
- meaningful death,
- no magical soul token,
- no trivial respawn.

---

# 2. Settlement body candidate

Exact body technology remains open.

Possible:
- synthetic-organic humanlike body,
- engineered biological body,
- hybrid bio-synthetic body.

Current preference:
**synthetic-organic / biological hybrid sufficiently humanlike to support ordinary sensory/social life.**

Reason:
The story's final emotional payoff benefits if Rowan can physically:
- breathe destination air after verification,
- feel gravity/weather,
- touch soil/material,
- be injured,
- carry someone,
- repair real settlement infrastructure.

Avoid turning final act into robot-body spectacle.

---

# 3. Migration stages

## E0 — Scout phase
Non-person drones / remote avatars.
No major identity stakes.

## E1 — Tethered embodiment test
Citizen controls/inhabits body while primary continuity remains ship-side.
Used cautiously; not final settlement status.

## E2 — Continuous migration
Active continuity extends through a verified channel into settlement substrate/body.

## E3 — Local continuity dominant
Person's active center is now local settlement embodiment.
Ship-side source allocation withdraws without interrupting the stream.

## E4 — Independent citizen
Can survive loss of ship link as long as local active continuity remains intact.

---

# 4. Why final danger becomes real

After E3/E4:
- destroying the body/local continuity system can kill the current person,
- another archived snapshot may create successor but not restore this stream,
- another lineage counterpart is a different person,
- ship-side source is no longer a live duplicate.

Thus final destination disaster has real stakes.

---

# 5. Temporary redundancy guardrail

During migration, redundancy is allowed only if it preserves one synchronized causal stream.

If two streams become independently conscious/divergent, they become two persons.
The system cannot later call one `redundant copy` and discard it without rights implications.

This is a major late ethical rule.

---

# 6. Relationship payoff

External embodiment lets earlier character themes become physical:
- Mira chooses where to live rather than which simulated state to restore.
- Selene must enforce safety rules without old branch safety net.
- Rafi treats injury knowing no alternate version substitutes for patient.
- Rowan repairs infrastructure that cannot be rolled back into another civic configuration.

---

# 7. HUMAN:1 relation

HUMAN:1 does not need migration because its continuity is already macroscopic biological continuity.

But it may still require:
- completion of development,
- adaptation support,
- microbiome/ecological protection,
- ordinary care.

This creates contrast without superiority:
- citizens move outward from ultradense substrate continuity,
- HUMAN:1 begins outward life from preserved biological continuity.

Both enter the same uncertain settlement future.

---

# 8. Physical-ark dependency

Migration/embodiment requires massive physical resources:
- manufacturing,
- biological feedstock,
- power,
- local habitat construction,
- transport,
- environmental verification,
- continuity-channel integrity.

Therefore destination arrival does not instantly embody 300k citizens.
Transition can occur in waves.

This provides final-act logistics and stakes.

---

# 9. Failure modes

- channel interruption during migration,
- body fabrication defect,
- local substrate radiation damage,
- two synchronized streams diverge unexpectedly,
- identity-rights dispute over duplicated active stream,
- settlement disaster forces choice between more bodies and habitat infrastructure.

Use selectively; do not turn finale into pure cybernetics.
