# 1. Design goal

The protagonist must be:
- ordinary enough to preserve survival/mystery identification,
- professionally useful in dangerous infrastructure scenes,
- unusual enough that the job feels native to a centuries-old ark civilization,
- unable to solve the hidden ontology merely by job title,
- not military,
- not police detective,
- not system architect,
- not AI administrator,
- not HUMAN:1,
- not selected by destiny.

---

# 2. Occupation — leading candidate

Working Korean title:
**선체 손상분석관**

Field shorthand used by colleagues:
- `손상분석`
- `진단팀`
- `구조진단`

Exact bureaucratic title is not frozen.

Author-side English concept only:
`Hull Damage / Structural Failure Analyst`

Avoid using `Hull Pathologist` as an in-story official title unless later language design supports it.

## Core job function

He investigates what physically happened to a structure after abnormal stress.

Typical duties:
- fracture pattern interpretation,
- pressure/decompression damage assessment,
- heat/fire deformation analysis,
- fatigue and repeated-repair history,
- material replacement/rejuvenation trace,
- load-path failure,
- sealing/bulkhead integrity,
- safe-to-open / safe-to-reoccupy recommendation,
- emergency temporary stabilization advice,
- accident-sequence reconstruction from physical evidence.

He is not the person who knows every subsystem.
He often works with:
- maintenance technicians,
- life-support engineers,
- crisis response,
- medical teams,
- security investigators,
- materials labs.

---

# 3. Why this job is better than generic facilities engineer

## Narrative advantages

1. He is routinely sent to places after something has gone wrong.
2. He sees old compartments, repair layers and records without being a detective.
3. He can distinguish `what records say` from `what matter says`.
4. He can join survival scenes without becoming a combat specialist.
5. His professional instinct naturally creates mystery questions without giving answers.
6. He has a credible reason to revisit the Arun incident.

## Critical guardrail

His job must not become a magical truth detector.

Physical traces can be:
- incomplete,
- deliberately repaired,
- contaminated,
- ambiguous,
- inconsistent with available records,
- impossible to date precisely,
- different across civilization lineages.

He should often know only:
> `이 기록대로라면 이런 손상은 나올 수 없다.`

He does not know why.

# 4. Relationship to the ultradense-world truth

The protagonist works **inside the reader-facing experienced Meridian**.
He does not begin the story servicing the outer ark or microscopic substrate banks.

Most failures he analyzes are ordinary causal failures within his civilization:
- aging,
- bad maintenance,
- fire,
- pressure,
- human error,
- sabotage,
- design compromise.

Only rare anomalies reflect hidden substrate/lineage integrity problems.

This prevents:
`all engineering = fake simulation artifact`.

Late-stage escalation can reveal:
- maintenance identifiers that do not belong to his civic history,
- repair patterns inconsistent with local time,
- material/version metadata associated with another lineage,
- impossible `already repaired` structures,
- sections whose physical history is older/different than public history.

# 5. Career path

Recommended baseline:

## Early 20s
Starts as ordinary maintenance/structural inspection worker or technical trainee.

Not elite.
Not top academy prodigy.
Strong at:
- spatial reasoning,
- reading physical wear,
- remembering routes and repair histories,
- staying functional during emergencies.

## Late 20s — Arun incident
A major compartment accident occurs.

He is still junior enough that:
- he does not command the response,
- but he participates in a structural/bulkhead decision,
- his assessment contributes to sealing a section.

Arun is trapped on the wrong side.

The decision prevents a wider cascading failure.
Arun is officially killed.

The protagonist is not convicted of wrongdoing.
He also does not emotionally accept `규정상 문제없음` as a satisfying answer.

## After Arun
He specializes further into failure/damage analysis.

Motivation is not:
`I will uncover the secret conspiracy.`

It is closer to:
`다음에는 왜 망가졌는지도 모른 채 결정을 내리고 싶지 않다.`

This is practical guilt, not heroic destiny.

## Series start
Age center remains late 30s, around 36~38 candidate.
Experienced but not managerial elite.
Can argue with supervisors because of field credibility, but cannot command society.

# 6. Did he regress before the series?

**No full regression before the story.**

This is a hard leading recommendation.

If he had already experienced a full death/return before Act3:
- the first major transition loses shock,
- he becomes genre-savvy too early,
- the reader cannot discover the rule with him,
- he starts feeling selected.

## Allowed earlier anomaly
One small, unresolved autobiographical mismatch linked to the Arun incident.

Examples — choose only one later:
- he remembers one radio sentence absent from the recording,
- he remembers Arun standing in a location the official map says was inaccessible,
- he remembers a tool/door state inconsistent with evidence,
- another witness remembers him making a different call.

At the time he attributes it to:
- shock,
- bad memory,
- recording loss,
- ordinary accident confusion.

This is NOT enough for him to believe in another history.

# 7. First clear apparent regression

The story-era Act2 → Act3 transition is his **first unmistakable experience**.

Leading mechanism remains:
**Emergency Continuity Failover Misbinding**.

He experiences:
- catastrophic survival event,
- continuity-threatening failure,
- interruption,
- waking/continuing in a closely related civilization lineage whose civic state resembles an earlier period.

He initially believes:
`I went back.`

The audience is allowed to believe the same.

No prior knowledge gives him the correct answer.

# 8. Personal-life tone

The protagonist should NOT have a `tragedy checklist` biography.

Avoid stacking:
- orphanhood,
- abusive parents,
- dead spouse,
- dead child,
- dead sibling,
- terminal illness,
- war veteran trauma,
- secret royal/system lineage.

His life before the series should feel mostly ordinary.

Core asymmetry:
> **His biography is not extraordinarily cursed. The story makes his ordinary life impossible.**

The Arun accident is the one deep pre-series wound.

# 9. Family — leading baseline candidate

## Parents
Both alive at series opening candidate.

Relationship:
- functional,
- affectionate without melodrama,
- occasional friction,
- not estranged,
- not living together.

They are ordinary citizens, not secret founders or high officials.

Recommended occupational contrast:
- one parent from logistics/food/distribution/service operations,
- one parent from education/community administration/care support.

Avoid making either parent an engineer/system scientist; protagonist expertise should not become inherited destiny.

## One sibling
Recommend one sibling, preferably a sister, near the protagonist's age.

Functions:
- ordinary family anchor,
- alternative view of the protagonist's controlling/protective habits,
- connection to a household outside his work circle,
- child/family-life window through a niece/nephew if needed.

The sibling is not automatically a recurring hostage.

## Niece/nephew — optional but useful
One school-age child can provide:
- ordinary culture/education scenes,
- emotional stakes without giving protagonist his own child at start,
- strong cross-lineage contrast later.

Do not repeatedly endanger the child for cheap tension.

# 10. Romantic history — leading candidate

The protagonist has **no spouse and no child in the opening lineage**.

Mira-role candidate remains useful as:
- serious former partner,
- long relationship rather than brief fling,
- breakup not caused by cheating or villainy.

Core conflict:
- protagonist: when frightened, tries to fix/control the situation for others,
- partner: values autonomy and wants her decisions respected.

They cared for each other but their coping styles became incompatible.

No mandatory reunion romance.

---

# 11. Cross-lineage family/romance variation

This is one of the strongest uses of the civilization-field model.

Across related lived histories:
- parents may have different careers/health outcomes,
- sibling may live in a different district,
- one relationship may never begin,
- protagonist and former partner may marry,
- they may separate earlier,
- they may have a child in one lineage and none in another,
- the protagonist may die before any of this happens.

Critical writing rule:
Do not summarize these differences in author exposition.

Reveal through:
- someone using the wrong family term,
- a photograph that means nothing to him,
- a stranger treating him as an in-law,
- a child drawing/remembering a person who is not family here,
- an old friend reacting to someone as if they committed an unforgivable act,
- testimony that contradicts the current person's behavior.

---

# 12. Alternate child — high-value but delayed device

Candidate only; do not freeze yet.

In one other lived lineage, the protagonist and his former partner may have had a child.

Why powerful:
- transforms lineage theory into personal loss,
- asks whether a family relationship from another life can ethically bind the current person,
- reinforces `counterpart != replacement`,
- gives the protagonist a reason to stop treating alternate histories as recoverable originals.

Timing recommendation:
**Act5 or later.**

Too early becomes melodrama and overshadows the mystery.

Use once, not as a repeated shock generator.

# 13. Arun reveal ladder

## Stage 1 — early Act1
Only behavioral residue.
- reluctance around certain bulkhead decisions,
- discomfort with one old incident class,
- coworkers know there was `an old accident`.

No long flashback.

## Stage 2 — Act2 first half
Arun's name appears.
Reader learns protagonist once helped seal a dangerous compartment.

## Stage 3 — Act2 late
Near-complete conventional version:
- Arun trapped,
- seal decision made,
- many saved,
- Arun officially died.

This should feel like a complete personal tragedy at first.

## Stage 4 — Act3
A small fact contradicts the old story.
Possibly someone knows Arun differently.
Do not immediately bring a living Arun on page unless pacing earns it.

## Stage 5 — Act4/5
A living Arun-lineage counterpart or strong evidence becomes unavoidable.

The important question changes from:
`Did Arun actually survive?`

to:
`Which Arun are you talking about?`

## Stage 6 — late series
The incident becomes evidence of older lineage-integrity failures and the protagonist's own earlier small memory mismatch.

# 14. Character flaw / arc

Working core belief:
`망가진 건 원인을 찾으면 고칠 수 있다.`

Dangerous extension:
`고치면 원래대로 돌아갈 수 있다.`

This works particularly well with damage analysis.

But do NOT phrase it repeatedly as a literary slogan in prose.
It should emerge from behavior:
- rechecking,
- reopening cases,
- trying to restore relationships,
- refusing to accept an irreversible loss,
- treating people as problems he can stabilize.

Final movement:
He learns that:
- causes matter,
- repair matters,
- but there may be no single original state to restore,
- a present person has rights independent of a counterpart's past,
- irreversible choice is not identical to failure.
