# V3 EXTERNAL CRAFT / VEHICLE FLEET v0.1

Status: `IP/VISUAL DEEP-DESIGN BRAINSTORM CANDIDATE / NOT CANON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-20
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Give Meridian a memorable non-military craft/vehicle ecology with model-family recognition and collection appeal, while keeping the novel's center on mystery/survival/civilization rather than ship battles.

## Fleet philosophy

Meridian's craft are descendants of centuries of utility use.
They are closer to:
- workboats,
- rescue craft,
- harbor tugs,
- cranes,
- survey vehicles,
- utility trucks
than starfighters.

Hard:
- no standing fighter wing required,
- no ace-pilot progression,
- no secret warship,
- no protagonist signature craft,
- no craft class created only for merchandise.

---

# Four fleet art directions

## F-A — Sleek unified fleet
All craft share smooth municipal design language.
Pros: clean brand identity.
Cons: variants blur together; too generic.
Verdict: `LIMITED CURRENT-GENERATION USE`.

## F-B — Rugged exposed industrial fleet
Open trusses, tool booms, tanks, hardpoints.
Pros: strong model-kit appeal.
Cons: can make everything NASA-industrial and visually same.
Verdict: `USE FOR EXTERNAL WORK / LEGACY UNITS`.

## F-C — Layered generational harbor fleet
Different eras coexist; adapters/retrofits make lineage visible.
Pros: strongest history/collectibility, supports 450-year civilization.
Cons: needs model discipline.
Verdict: `SELECTED BASE`.

## F-D — Heroic named machines
Every recurring craft gets a strong proper name/livery.
Pros: maximum merch recognition.
Cons: mecha/anime squad drift.
Verdict: `REJECT AS BASE / ALLOW LOCAL NICKNAME FOR VERY FEW INDIVIDUAL UNITS`.

# Craft recognition grammar

Each major craft family is identified by:
1. **mass distribution silhouette**,
2. **tool/cargo interface geometry**,
3. **crew visibility/enclosure**, 
4. **anchor/thruster arrangement at visual level**, 
5. **one historical generation cue**.

Reader does NOT need thrust equations or full spec sheets.

---

# CF01 — External Inspection Pod

Role:
close hull survey, sensor placement, short external access work.

Scale:
1~2 trained operators or remote/supervised equivalent depending mission.

Silhouette:
- compact armored pressure capsule,
- broad forward observation/sensor face,
- two or three short articulated tool/anchor arms,
- thruster clusters recessed/protected,
- docking collar visibly oversized relative to cabin.

Character:
`beetle/submersible` feeling rather than fighter jet.

Generations:
Legacy:
- thicker hull, smaller window/sensor area,
- more external cable/tool routing,
- highly repairable.

Mature:
- common fleet baseline,
- improved sensor face/tool swapping.

Final Approach:
- better external navigation/local autonomy,
- settlement survey adapters.

Failure modes:
- thruster contamination,
- anchor arm jam,
- local sensor blindness,
- docking collar damage,
- operator isolation.

Story uses:
rare Act1/5 exterior context; stronger Act9 deployment/survey.

Collectibility:
clear era variants; transparent/cutaway model potential.

---

# CF02 — Outer Service Tug

Role:
move external modules, radiator/service components, sensor packages, staging cargo.

Scale:
small crew + heavy remote assistance; many operations can be supervised locally.

Silhouette:
- central compact crew/service block,
- **large open structural frame** behind/around it,
- standardized capture arms/collar,
- widely separated thruster/control pods for leverage.

Visual analogy:
harbor tug + crane + spacecraft service bus.

Key recognition:
looks almost `unfinished` when not carrying cargo because the frame exists to surround/grip loads.

Variants:
- general service,
- thermal/radiator handling,
- heavy module transfer.

Act6/9:
scarcity/settlement deployment makes these strategically valuable without turning them into combat craft.

Collectible variant pleasure:
load configuration changes silhouette dramatically while core tug remains recognizable.

---

# CF03 — External Rescue Cutter

Role:
reach disabled pods/work teams, recover suited workers, carry emergency isolation gear.

Name `cutter` is functional working term only; final C2 may change.

Silhouette:
- compact pressure cabin,
- **side rescue blister/cradle**, 
- forward docking/soft-capture nose,
- high-visibility rescue markings,
- redundant local life-support package visibly bulkier than inspection pod.

Difference from service tug:
rescue cutter prioritizes rapid personnel interface, not heavy load leverage.

Modules:
- suited-worker recovery,
- pressure casualty cradle,
- emergency seal/tool pack.

Hard:
not armed patrol boat.

Narrative:
one variant can appear as background before any major external rescue so it does not materialize for plot convenience.

---

# CF04 — Radiator / Shield Maintenance Carriage

Role:
slow specialized work along large exterior structures.

Mobility:
rail/track/anchor progression on prepared external infrastructure rather than free-flight where possible.

Silhouette:
- long low maintenance carriage,
- folding work booms,
- shielded crew/service nook,
- strong attachment/clamp geometry.

Why visually valuable:
shows scale of Meridian's external systems: human machine looks tiny against radiator/shield structure.

Collectibility:
less hero craft, more industrial diorama anchor.

Act9:
parts/standards can influence settlement maintenance rigs.

---

# CF05 — Deployment Barge / Bootstrap Carrier

Role:
late-voyage movement of packaged settlement/industrial infrastructure from Meridian staging to target orbital/surface transfer chain.

Silhouette:
- cargo dominates vehicle,
- propulsion/control package visibly attached around standardized pallet/frame,
- no sleek passenger fuselage.

Design principle:
**the thing being delivered is visually larger/more important than the craft.**

Variants:
- orbital depot package,
- substrate/utility package,
- surface transfer package depending destination engineering.

Story value:
Act9 makes the cost of deploying settlement physically visible: hardware leaves Meridian and does not magically duplicate.

Collectibility:
loadout-based diorama/variant appeal.

---

# CF06 — Descent / Surface Utility Lander Family

Status:
`CANDIDATE DEPENDENT ON FINAL DESTINATION ENVIRONMENT`.

Role:
move limited equipment/people/embodiment interfaces between orbital staging and surface where physically required.

Hard:
exact aerodynamic/propulsive shape MUST follow destination atmosphere/gravity science. Do not lock wings/engines from aesthetics first.

Visual design regardless of environment:
- utility cargo/landing geometry,
- wide stable base or mission-appropriate landing interface,
- modular cargo bay/pallet standard shared with deployment barge,
- repair access obvious,
- no fighter silhouette.

Collectibility:
can become the most `vehicle-like` late-series craft, but must be earned by science/design pass.

---

# CF07 — Surface / Settlement Utility Crawler

Role:
move people/tools/parts across unfinished settlement terrain before mature local transport exists.

Silhouette:
low multi-wheel/track/legged platform depending terrain, with same standardized cargo interfaces known from Meridian.

Critical completion rule:
reader should recognize its ancestry from **Utility Tug + Field Rig + cargo standards**, even if body is new.

No miraculous all-terrain rover.

# Individual craft naming policy

Most units:
functional fleet numbers/local short labels only.

A few old beloved units may acquire worker nicknames through decades of service.

Rules for a nickname:
- comes from appearance/incident/work culture,
- not mythic war name,
- may outlive original parts,
- should tell social history.

Candidate nickname types:
- `납작이`-like worker shorthand in Korean localization? Do not freeze; cultural language pass needed.
- geometry/behavior nicknames,
- old depot names.

Do NOT create 20 named hero craft.

# Fleet generations and collection appeal

## G0 Founding / very old lineage
Mostly museum/rare surviving interfaces; few complete active units.

Visual:
heavy, segmented, many exposed service points.

## G1 Early/Mid Voyage workhorse
Some long-lived chassis or design descendants remain.

Visual:
repair-friendly, standardized mechanical interfaces.

## G2 Mature Meridian
Most culturally familiar fleet.

Visual:
balanced, modular, known by workers/citizens.

## G3 Final Approach retrofit
Not all-new fleet; G2 frames with new navigation, isolation, settlement adapters.

Visual:
mixed old body/new modules = strongest generational recognition.

## G4 Settlement descendants
New machines built/reconfigured from inherited standards/materials.

Meaning:
not `ultimate version`, but **new environment branch of the same material culture**.

craft are mostly background/professional texture; avoid introducing fleet catalogue.

scarcity makes fleet availability/cannibalization visible.

Act8:
