# P1-E — Act8 D3 rights family seed

## Selected author-side case: `Rights Family A`

Do not present them as a symbolic family created for a court case.
They are first encountered as ordinary people inside another fully lived D3 lineage during late Act7 cross-lineage preservation/contact work.

### Household
- two adult caregivers/parents,
- one school-age child, roughly 6~8 candidate,
- child was born **after the lineage diverged/deepened**,
- child has no source counterpart in H-B or another source state.

At least one parent may have an identity-lineage counterpart elsewhere, but the family relationship itself is locally formed.

### Ordinary seed — Act7 EP320~327 band
Before any termination/quiescence threat dominates:
show one or two mundane facts through bounded contact/record/operational access:
- school routine,
- food preference,
- one parent arguing about housing/shift timing,
- child's annoyance at adult attention,
- family has a current plan for the next month.

Hard:
reader meets a life, not `Exhibit A for personhood`.

### Threat — Act8 EP343~350
A real preservation/resource transition puts the lineage at risk of:
- destructive termination under obsolete classification,
- unsafe emergency migration,
- or indefinite quiescence without representation/review.

Old Continuity Doctrine can treat parent lineages as derivative experience extensions.
The child breaks source-copy logic because there is no source version to claim ownership over.

### Why this does NOT settle all ethics
Adult D3 persons already have autonomy/continuity claims.
The child exposes the contradiction cleanly but is not the sole reason adults matter.

### Protagonist role
Protagonist may help identify physical preservation risk and argue from current-person experience.
He is **not the family's lawyer/savior/parental representative**.
Family and relevant D3 representatives speak for themselves.

### Payoff
Contributes to:
- termination/quiescence distinction,
- restriction of new involuntary D3 creation,
- cross-lineage preservation rights,
- temporary representation/continuity trusteeship.

No chosen child.
No HUMAN:1 connection.

# P1-F — Imperfect `Knowledge Without History` example

## Selected rule: Dual-Barrier Return-to-Service Protocol
Author shorthand: `DBR` only; reader-facing name can remain ordinary safety-language.

### Current H-B rule
A damaged pressure/service route cannot return to normal public use until:
1. two independent isolation boundaries are verified,
2. structural and environmental/operations checks independently sign off,
3. temporary bypass state is logged and time-limited.

### Why nobody knows the famous origin
The rule's exact trigger catastrophe is absent from H-B public history.
It exists as mature safety practice with several contradictory institutional stories about where it came from.

### Actual lineage value
A prior lived lineage experienced a cascade in which one repaired boundary failed during reopening because the supposedly independent second barrier was not actually independent.
A robust lesson migrated forward without ordinary autobiographical memory.

### Imperfection
H-B architecture has since changed.
The inherited rule can be:
- overcautious in some modern sections,
- poorly worded for new modular interfaces,
- used bureaucratically to delay decisions,
- circumvented by workers who see it as obsolete.

Therefore knowledge transfer is **not perfect wisdom**.

### Story use
Act1:
boring safety rule; protagonist/Niko may complain about practical burden.

Act2:
its logic genuinely prevents one premature route reopening, while a different informal bypass undermines safety elsewhere.

Act6:
strict application blocks a fast restoration option and becomes political/operational conflict.

Act7:
Hana/protagonist discover no H-B historical event sufficiently explains why the rule exists in this exact form.

Payoff:
institutional learning can survive without matching local history, but inherited lessons require present judgment.

Status: **P1-F CLOSED**.
