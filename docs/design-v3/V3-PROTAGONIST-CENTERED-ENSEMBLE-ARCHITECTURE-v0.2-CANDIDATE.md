# V3 PROTAGONIST-CENTERED ENSEMBLE ARCHITECTURE v0.2 — CANDIDATE

Status: `DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / SUPERSEDES v0.1 POV SHARE FOR CURRENT V3 WORK / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 0. Core correction

The series is **protagonist-centered**, not equal-share ensemble fiction.

The protagonist is:
- the primary emotional anchor,
- the primary mystery-following anchor,
- the character whose long transformation gives the series its spine,
- present in most episodes/scenes of consequence.

But the world does not wait for him.
Other people, families, institutions, districts and ordinary citizens continue to act when he is absent.

Core formula:

`strong protagonist-centered narrative`
+
`independent off-protagonist causality`
+
`selective secondary POV`
+
`world consequences the protagonist cannot fully control`

Hard distinction:
**story center != world center.**

---

## 1. POV / scene-share diagnostic bands

These are flexible diagnostics, not quotas.

Series-wide working band:
- protagonist-centered POV/scenes: **65~75%**
- core/major recurring cast: **15~25%**
- ordinary/emergent/institutional lenses: **5~15%**

Act tendency:
- Act1: protagonist 75~85%
- Act2: protagonist 65~75%, survival relay expands
- Act3: protagonist 75~85% because apparent regression must be lived through him
- Act4: protagonist 60~70%, social spread increases
- Act5: protagonist 60~70%, investigation + relational testimony
- Act6: protagonist 50~65%, maximum multi-causal pressure
- Act7: protagonist 60~70%, scale inference stays personally anchored
- Act8: protagonist 55~70%, rights cases require other voices
- Act9: protagonist 55~70%, settlement decisions are plural but emotional closure returns to him

Do not enforce mathematically during prose. Use only as drift detection.

FAIL if:
- protagonist is absent long enough that the reader wonders whose story this is,
- other POVs accumulate into a parallel co-protagonist structure without design reason,
- protagonist becomes only a witness to decisions made by everyone else.

---

## 2. Why protagonist-absent material exists

Non-protagonist material exists to prove at least one of these:
1. another person has an independent life,
2. an institution acts without protagonist permission,
3. a causal chain begins outside protagonist knowledge,
4. a decision becomes irreversible before protagonist learns of it,
5. protagonist action has consequences he cannot see,
6. a minor person becomes important through circumstance,
7. reader judgment of a character differs from protagonist judgment,
8. civilization-scale events exceed one person's field of view.

It does **not** exist merely to show lore the protagonist cannot know.

## 3. Blindspot audit

### B-01 — Ensemble dilution
Risk:
Too many secondary POVs weaken attachment to protagonist.

Repair:
- protagonist remains default lens,
- recurring secondary lenses preferred over new one-offs,
- no long secondary chain unless it changes a major incident or relationship,
- Act3 must strongly recenter protagonist after Act2 ensemble expansion.

Verdict: `HIGH / REPAIRED BY v0.2`.

### B-02 — Passive protagonist
Risk:
If the world keeps acting without him too often, protagonist looks unnecessary.

Repair:
Every Act requires protagonist to own at least:
- one consequential decision,
- one investigation/mystery advance,
- one irreversible relationship action,
- one competence payoff or failure that changes available choices.

Others may solve problems, but protagonist must continue to generate plot as well as receive it.

Verdict: `HIGH / HARD GATE`.

### B-03 — Secondary POV as exposition camera
Risk:
Switch POV only because author needs to reveal information.

Repair:
Every non-protagonist POV must have a concrete immediate want independent of exposition.
If removing the lore leaves no scene, cut the POV.

Verdict: `HIGH / HARD GATE`.

### B-04 — Reader knows too much
Risk:
Multiple viewpoints prematurely expose conspiracy/system truth.

Repair:
- every POV has an explicit knowledge fence in author ledger,
- no POV gets author-only ontology,
- reader-ahead suspense is allowed only on local event information,
- series-level ontology remains clue-bounded.

Verdict: `HIGH / HARD GATE`.

### B-05 — Repeated event retelling
Risk:
Show protagonist event, then replay same event from Selene/Mira/civilian for emotional completeness, slowing pace.

Repair:
Second lens must add a **new causal action or irreversible consequence**, not just a new opinion.
Do not replay entire scenes.
Enter later or leave earlier.

Verdict: `MEDIUM-HIGH`.

### B-06 — Anthology drift
Risk:
Ordinary-citizen chapters feel like disconnected short stories.

Repair:
Each off-protagonist thread connects to at least one active chain:
- current disaster,
- relationship network,
- institution,
- future causal pivot,
- mystery evidence,
- persistent district/world change.

A pure slice-of-life side story is allowed only when it strengthens a recurring place/person that later matters; density must remain low.

Verdict: `HIGH`.

### B-07 — Deus-ex-minor-character
Risk:
An unknown worker suddenly saves civilization to prove emergence.

Repair:
Civilization-scale emergent pivots: **2~4 maximum across series**.
Seed each person earlier in ordinary function.
The decisive action must use an already-visible skill, relationship or access condition.

Verdict: `HIGH / HARD GATE`.

### B-08 — Fake world independence
Risk:
Every secondary subplot eventually turns out to be secretly about protagonist.

Repair:
Each Act must contain at least one meaningful non-protagonist goal whose resolution would matter even if protagonist were removed from that subplot.

Examples:
- Mira's transport problem,
- Selene's command legitimacy,
- Rafi's patient decision,
- Juno's community network,
- sibling/family issue,
- school/district conflict.

Verdict: `HIGH`.

### B-09 — Protagonist omnipresence despite multi-POV
Risk:
Secondary characters act independently, but protagonist still physically appears at every climax and receives every report.

Repair:
Across the series, some local climaxes resolve entirely without protagonist.
But **major Act climaxes should usually retain protagonist emotional involvement**, even if the decisive causal action belongs to someone else.

Verdict: `MEDIUM-HIGH`.

### B-10 — Chronology/geography confusion
Risk:
Multiple concurrent incidents in a closed ship become hard to track.

Repair:
Author-side incident ledger tracks:
- time,
- district,
- POV owner,
- known information,
- active hazard,
- causal handoff.

Reader-facing prose uses concrete location/time transitions, not constant timestamp headers unless needed.

Verdict: `HIGH FOR ACT2/6`.

### B-11 — Too many named people
Risk:
300k-city realism causes cast inflation.

Repair:
- active important persons per Act target remains 8~14,
- new POV character should usually have prior seed,
- unnamed/role-labelled citizens may carry short causal functions,
- late Acts reuse > introduce.

Verdict: `HIGH / EXISTING HARNESS SUPPORTS`.

### B-12 — Secondary characters frozen when offscreen
Risk:
They appear independent only when POV camera visits them; otherwise their lives pause.

Repair:
Relationship/state ledger records offscreen changes between appearances:
- work shifts,
- family changes,
- alliances,
- injuries,
- reputation,
- decisions already made.

When protagonist meets them again, some change should occasionally predate his arrival.

Verdict: `HIGH / IMPORTANT FOR LIVING-WORLD EFFECT`.

### B-13 — Every offscreen change is dramatic
Risk:
To prove the world moves, every absence yields death/betrayal/promotion.

Repair:
Most offscreen changes are mundane:
- schedule changed,
- someone started dating,
- repair finished,
- child changed school group,
- restaurant closed,
- minor workplace conflict resolved.

Large changes remain rare.

Verdict: `MEDIUM / TONE GATE`.

### B-14 — Mystery clues monopolized by ensemble
Risk:
Other characters discover all important evidence first, weakening protagonist's mystery role.

Repair:
Use a split:
- protagonist owns the primary interpretive chain,
- other characters independently own some evidence,
- protagonist sometimes connects facts they do not,
- other characters sometimes correctly reject protagonist's interpretation.

Verdict: `HIGH`.

### B-15 — Everyone becomes morally ambiguous in the same way
Risk:
Multi-lineage + many POVs creates repetitive `good person was bad elsewhere` gimmick.

Repair:
Moral variation types must rotate:
- reputation contradiction,
- relationship contradiction,
- competence contradiction,
- institutional role reversal,
- same virtue causing harm,
- same flaw producing benefit,
- testimony proven false,
- behavior converging despite changed history.

Verdict: `HIGH / VARIETY GATE`.

### B-16 — Protagonist absence weakens emotional payoff
Risk:
Critical death or relationship rupture occurs without protagonist and reader misses his emotional spine.

Repair:
Some events may occur without him, but aftermath must often be processed through protagonist when it affects his long arc.
Do not require him to witness everything; require him to **live with consequences**.

Verdict: `HIGH`.

### B-17 — Offscreen causality becomes author convenience
Risk:
An unseen decision appears exactly when plot needs it.

Repair:
Every major offscreen causal turn requires at least two of:
- prior actor seed,
- prior motive,
- prior resource/access condition,
- prior institutional pressure,
- later traceable evidence.

Verdict: `HIGH / CAUSAL FAIRNESS GATE`.

### B-18 — Secondary POV spoils character mystery
Risk:
Entering Selene/Sora/Cassian POV can immediately reveal whether they are lying, guilty, remembering, etc.

Repair:
POV access does not mean full self-explanation.
Characters may misunderstand themselves, compartmentalize, omit irrelevant memories naturally, or simply not think about hidden material in that scene.
Do not artificially censor obvious thoughts solely to trick reader.
If honest close POV would spoil a required mystery, delay that character's direct POV.

Verdict: `HIGH / POV ELIGIBILITY GATE`.

### B-19 — Same-face alternate histories overwhelm present relationships
Risk:
Other-history spouses/children/enemies become more interesting than current relationships and present H-B feels provisional.

Repair:
H-B is the long current world from Act3~9.
Current relationships receive more scene time and irreversible choices than residue relationships.
Alternate histories recontextualize the present; they do not replace it.

Verdict: `HIGH`.

### B-20 — Protagonist role becomes too special through information intersection
Risk:
Even if he is not causal center, every unrelated thread eventually crosses him because he is the only person connecting them.

Repair:
Maintain multiple inter-character edges that do not pass through protagonist.
Examples:
- Mira ↔ Juno,
- Selene ↔ Rafi,
- Sora ↔ Hana,
- Niko ↔ Gideon,
- family ↔ local community,
- institution ↔ ordinary claimant.

At least one major solution should emerge from a network path that never requires protagonist as intermediary.

Verdict: `HIGH / NETWORK GATE`.

## 4. Act-level protagonist-center rule

### Act1
Reader bonds primarily to protagonist.
Other POV use is sparse and recurring.

### Act2
Expand field of survival because one location cannot show the entire catastrophe.
Protagonist still owns primary survival/investigation line.

### Act3
Strong recenter.
This is **his** impossible return experience.
Other POV proves H-B existed independently before his arrival.

### Act4
Other people exploit/believe/fear future information independently.
Protagonist follows one central chain, not every rumor.

### Act5
Other witnesses own incompatible histories.
Protagonist remains central because Arun/personal-history fracture lands on him.

### Act6
Largest ensemble expansion.
One significant sequence may run multiple episodes with protagonist peripheral.
But protagonist retains a consequential survival/relationship/mystery decision.

### Act7
Scale revelation returns strongly through protagonist interpretation and direct physical evidence.
Other POV shows civilization consequence.

### Act8
Rights cases must speak for themselves.
Protagonist participates but cannot become spokesperson for all persons.

### Act9
Settlement is co-authored by many people.
Protagonist remains emotional finishing anchor.

---

## 5. Revised hard harness

Per major Act, PASS only if all are true:
1. protagonist owns at least one irreversible decision,
2. protagonist owns at least one meaningful mystery/interpretive advance,
3. at least two causal developments happen outside his presence,
4. at least one other character solves or worsens a problem independently,
5. at least one important relationship/institution changes without his control,
6. at least one non-protagonist thread has a goal not reducible to protagonist,
7. at least one causal edge between secondary characters bypasses protagonist,
8. protagonist remains emotionally central to the Act's main promise,
9. no secondary POV exists only to explain lore,
10. no major offscreen turn lacks prior causal seed.

Series FAIL if:
- protagonist becomes omnipotent,
- protagonist becomes passive,
- ensemble becomes anthology,
- secondary characters exist only as mirrors of protagonist,
- every decisive event waits for protagonist,
- every decisive event occurs without protagonist.

## 6. Current decision

**Recommended narrative identity:**

> `주인공 중심 다중인과형 장편`

Not:
- pure single-POV hero story,
- equal-share ensemble,
- anthology,
- omniscient civilization chronicle.

v0.1's `45~55% protagonist` diagnostic is superseded for current v3 work.

Current working center:
**protagonist 65~75% overall, with Act-specific variation.**

Exact episode allocation remains downstream work after World/Character Bible v3 consolidation.
