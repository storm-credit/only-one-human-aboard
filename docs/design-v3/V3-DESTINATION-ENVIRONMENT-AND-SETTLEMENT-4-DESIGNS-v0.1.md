Status: `IP/BACKGROUND/SETTLEMENT DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / NOT CANON / NO PROSE`
Date: 2026-08-20
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Create a destination environment that pays off centuries of travel, supports Act9's irreversible physical settlement, gives craft/background art a coherent basis, and does not turn the finale into terraforming or alien-ecology fiction.

Important:
Old v2 Epsilon-Indi-specific numbers are **source pool only**. Current v3 does not need to freeze a real-star identity during this phase.

# Required narrative properties

The destination must:
1. feel genuinely worth reaching,
2. permit meaningful direct outdoor experience,
3. still require settlement infrastructure,
4. keep Meridian valuable during transition,
5. create real weather/environment uncertainty,
6. not require full pressure-suit life everywhere,
7. not be an Earth clone,
8. not introduce sentient/complex alien-life main plot,
9. support HUMAN infant/child only inside controlled safe environments initially,
10. support substrate-city / embodiment settlement rather than 300k instant biological migration.

---

# Design A — Breathable Earth Twin

Core:
~1g, ~1 bar, Earthlike oxygen, abundant water, mild climate.

Pros:
- maximum arrival emotion,
- easy outdoor scenes.

Cons:
- arrival solves too much,
- outdoor embodiment becomes objectively superior immediately,
- ship/substrate settlement seems unnecessary,
- weakens first-year infrastructure stakes.

Verdict:
# `REJECT`.

---

# Design B — Pressure-Compatible, Oxygen-Poor Temperate Water World

Core:
- near-Earth total atmospheric pressure,
- ~Earthlike gravity,
- abundant liquid water,
- cool-to-mild climate,
- oxygen partial pressure too low for routine unassisted human respiration,
- trace/CO2/aerosol uncertainty requires filtering/monitoring,
- outdoors needs breathing support but usually not a full pressure suit.

Pros:
- strong open-sky emotional payoff,
- still demands infrastructure,
- supports real weather/water/geology,
- pressure-compatible construction is easier than vacuum-world colonization,
- distinctive breathing gear/vehicle/architecture language,
- supports gradual embodiment/settlement.

Cons:
- must avoid `just give everyone an oxygen mask and move down tomorrow`,
- atmosphere physiology needs disciplined wording.

Verdict:
# `STRONGEST BASE`.

---

# Design C — Thin Cold World

Core:
low pressure, colder climate, abundant ice/water, selected temperate zones.

Pros:
- striking landscapes,
- strong engineering stakes.

Cons:
- too much pressure-suit/dome life,
- feels like another closed environment after 400 episodes,
- weaker emotional contrast with Meridian.

Verdict:
`REJECT AS PRIMARY`.

# Design D — Dense Warm Greenhouse World

Core:

Pros:

Cons:

# Selected hybrid — DEST-H1

# **Cool-Temperate Water-Rich Rocky World + Near-Earth Pressure + Oxygen-Poor Atmosphere**

This is the v3 leading environment.

---

# 1. Gravity / body experience

Working range:
**~0.94–1.02 g**.

Preferred story center:
`near 1g, slightly lower than Earth-descended design baseline`.

Reason:
- no superhuman effects,
- ordinary walking/carrying/falling remains intuitive,
- external embodiment does not require a new movement genre,
- HUMAN development can be managed without radical gravity premise.

Do not make gravity itself a major plot device.

---

# 2. Atmospheric pressure / respiration

Total surface pressure in first settlement region:
**roughly Earth-order, near enough that structures do not need vacuum-habitat pressure shells.**

Oxygen:
**too low for safe routine long-duration unassisted human-normal respiration.**

Operational result:
- outdoor workers use supplemental oxygen / lightweight breathing support,
- local atmosphere can serve as bulk pressure medium in appropriately designed buildings after filtering/isolation strategy,
- indoor civic spaces maintain clean human-compatible oxygen mix,
- accidental brief mask loss is dangerous but not instant explosive-decompression death,
- exertion makes low-O2 problem more serious.

Hard:
Do not freeze exact O2/CO2 percentages until physiology QA.

---

# 3. Environmental uncertainty

Even with pressure/temperature compatibility, settlement must verify/manage:
- oxygen partial pressure,
- CO2/trace gases,
- aerosols/mineral dust,
- possible bioactive chemistry,
- humidity/condensation,
- weather contamination transport.

Thus:
`habitable` ≠ `immediately breathable Earth`.

---

# 4. Water / land pattern

Selected visual world:
**multiple large seas/inland seas + broad continental/highland terrain**, not global ocean and not Earth-copy continents.

Water:
- abundant surface liquid water,
- rivers/lakes/coastal basins,
- permanent high-latitude/elevation ice,
- groundwater in settlement-relevant regions.

First settlement region:
**cool-temperate basin near a large inland sea / coastal-equivalent water body, with mineral-rich uplands within logistics range.**

Why:
- visually rich horizon,
- water access,
- construction/mineral diversity,
- storms/weather,
- flat enough deployment areas without `magic perfect valley`.

---

# 5. Climate / day-to-day weather

Global tone:
cool-temperate rather than tropical paradise.

First region supports:
- clear cold mornings,
- overcast/rain periods,
- strong frontal winds,
- fast weather shifts,
- occasional snow/sleet at colder season/elevation,
- coastal/inland-sea storm systems,
- mineral dust on dry days/works sites,
- lightning possible.

No permanent death storm.

Weather is useful because it creates uncertainty the simulation can model but never perfectly script at local construction scale.

---

# 6. Sky / light visual direction

Do NOT make `alien planet = orange sky` by default.

Leading visual:
- daylight broadly familiar enough to feel emotionally open,
- slightly different stellar spectral warmth is subtle rather than filter-like,
- atmosphere/cloud/aerosol state creates large local variation,
- clear sky may read pale/cool blue-family depending final atmospheric physics,
- low sun / storms produce unfamiliar colors naturally.

Most important visual difference is **unbounded real depth, weather and horizon**, not a fantasy sky color.

Act1 Garden Lightwell → Act9 sky payoff:
reader recognizes spaciousness, then realizes the surface sky has no engineered rib/ceiling at its edge.

---

# 7. Ground / geology visual language

First region:
- dark-to-mid silicate/basalt-like structural rock candidates,
- paler sediment/mineral bands around water systems,
- exposed gravel/scree in construction zones,
- wet stone/soil-like processed regolith textures,
- no lush native grass/forest.

Color must come from geology, water, sky, human construction and possible microbial/mineral films—not instant Earth vegetation.

---

# 8. Native life / biosecurity

Hard scope:
**no confirmed native complex/macroscopic ecology at series end.**

Allowed ambiguity:
- organic chemistry,
- atmospheric disequilibrium,
- microbial-biosignature candidates,
- mineral/chemical structures initially hard to classify.

Operational policy:
settlement treats uncertainty seriously:
- sampling,
- quarantine/isolation of environmental materials,
- processed water/air before civic use,
- controlled agriculture.

Do not pivot into parasite/alien-ruin reveal.

---

# 9. Agriculture / food

No instant farming in native soil.

Early agriculture:
- controlled-environment modules,
- processed local water/material inputs,
- imported/engineered microbiome only after validation,
- familiar Meridian food systems gradually relocated/expanded.

Long-term open/semi-open cultivation is future work, not series-end proof.

This keeps Garden Commons culture materially relevant after arrival.

---

# 10. Settlement architecture

## First phase
Not glass domes as default.

Because external pressure is near Earth-order, settlement structures can prioritize:
- oxygen enrichment / clean air,
- filtration/biosecurity,
- insulation/weather sealing,
- local power/data/continuity protection,
- radiation/particle shelter where needed,
- thermal control,
- repairability.

Visual language:
**low, modular, wind-conscious utility structures + local-control nodes + covered connector/service edges**, evolving into courtyards/commons rather than one monolithic dome.

## Civic design inheritance
Borrow from Meridian:
- Midring courtyard social logic,
- Transfer readable routing,
- Med rehab/care interfaces,
- Old Works repairability,
- Garden desire for open communal space.

But do NOT recreate Meridian one-to-one.

---

# 11. Substrate settlement

The first settlement is not 300k individual houses.

Major physical layers:
1. **protected local substrate/continuity infrastructure**,
2. power/thermal/control islands,
3. fabrication/repair workshops,
4. communications/navigation/weather sensing,
5. medical/embodiment care,
6. storage/feedstock,
7. limited human-scale civic/embodiment spaces,
8. surface transport links.

This fits the ultradense civilization premise.

People may remain substrate-resident, use remote embodiments, or undergo persistent continuity migration in stages.

---

# 12. HUMAN:1 environment

The Natural-Origin child does not become a symbolic first settler exposed outdoors.

Initial life:
- protected clean indoor air,
- ordinary neonatal/pediatric medical environment,
- controlled microbiome/exposure,
- no research-showcase tours,
- no obligation to adapt to native atmosphere.

Later outdoor access is a welfare/medical issue, not destiny.

---

# 13. Outdoor embodiment gear

Because pressure is compatible but oxygen is low:

Typical mature outdoor kit candidate:
- lightweight sealed/filtered breathing mask or compact face unit,
- small oxygen/scrubber/support pack depending body physiology,
- weather shell,
- eye protection for wind/dust/work,
- comm/navigation/environment monitor,
- task gloves/boots.

Not required everywhere:
full pressure suit.

This creates visually distinct settlement wear without turning Act9 into EVA-suit fiction.

---

# 14. Lander implication — CF06 repair

Selected environment favors a **utility atmospheric entry/landing craft**, not a vacuum-only vertical box and not a fighter.

Leading silhouette:
- broad/blunt lifting-body or drag-efficient cargo form,
- thermal-protection underside/forward surfaces,
- wide stable landing gear/base,
- rear/side cargo pallet access,
- engines/propulsion arranged for final approach/landing as required by future tech,
- cabin/cargo dominate over sleek nose.

Hard:
exact aerodynamic shape stays open until final vehicle science pass.

---

# 15. Surface utility crawler implication

Near-1g temperate terrain favors:
- robust wheeled/multi-axle utility chassis in first prepared zones,
- specialized tracked/legged systems only where terrain justifies them,
- enclosed breathable cabin optional for long work,
- open/partial operator configurations possible for short suited/breathing-supported work.

Do NOT make one magical rover that crosses every terrain.

---

# 16. Background landmarks — settlement candidates

## S1 — First Substrate Yard
Low protected utility volumes, local-control cabinets, cable/service trenches, field rigs, weather covers.

Visual payoff:
city-scale civilization begins from a physically small but incredibly consequential yard.

## S2 — First Human-Scale Commons
A modest clean-air courtyard/connector space deliberately inspired by Midring social design, not a grand founding plaza.

## S3 — Waterline / Inland-Sea Observation Point
Not tourist monument initially; work/survey route where characters first experience real horizon, wind and weather.

## S4 — Settlement Workshop
Descendant of Old Works material grammar: visible repair, adapters, local stone/metal use, familiar tool standards in new environment.

## S5 — Clinic / Care Node
Small descendant of Med-University care logic, becomes HUMAN neonatal and embodied-person care context without sacred imagery.
