# 0. Design goal

Preserve the user's core image:
> Meridian physically contains an incomprehensibly large number of civilization seeds/addresses at atomic, molecular or similarly microscopic scales.

But avoid the unnecessary claim:
> one ordinary atom literally stores every bit and physical event of a 300,000-person civilization by itself.

Leading engineering metaphor:
**Atomic-scale civilization anchors + distributed ultradense substrate lattice.**

---

# 1. Civilization Anchor vs Civilization State

## Anchor
Each civilization lineage has a tiny physical identity anchor/address/seed.
Its external footprint may be atomic/molecular-scale speculative engineered matter.

Anchor contains or identifies:
- lineage identity,
- cryptographic/physical continuity key,
- base-state reference,
- rights/status flags,
- integrity state,
- pointer into distributed execution/storage fabric.

## State
The full civilization state is distributed across shared physical substrate banks rather than confined to the single anchor.

Analogy only:
- anchor = inode/address/seed,
- substrate lattice = storage + compute + interconnect.

Do not use computer terminology in early prose.

---

# 2. Why this is better than one-atom-one-world literalism

It preserves:
- astronomical civilization count,
- microscopic physicality,
- one physical spaceship,
- physical failure modes.

It avoids:
- magical information density concentrated in one ordinary atom,
- requiring literal pocket universes,
- making every civilization equally expensive at all times.

# 3. Shared lattice architecture

Working author-side model:
Meridian contains massive ultradense engineered-matter banks with:
- distributed state storage,
- local computation,
- optical/field/other speculative interconnect,
- error correction,
- redundancy,
- thermal pathways,
- radiation isolation,
- repair nanofabrication.

Millions/billions/far-more civilization anchors may reference the same physical infrastructure while consuming different amounts of active execution depth.

---

# 4. Resource depth model

A civilization lineage is not simply `ON/OFF`.

Possible states:

## D0 — Seed/archive
Minimal active cost.
Contains enough state to reconstruct/continue the lineage if resources permit.

## D1 — Predictive low-depth
Compressed social/technical model.

## D2 — Deep civic model
High-detail social and identity computation.

## D3 — Fully lived civilization
Full subjective continuity sufficient for moral personhood.
Highest sustained cost.

## D4 — Crisis overdrive
Temporary high-resolution allocation during highly novel events.
Very expensive and physically stressful to substrate banks.

No exact counts frozen.

# 5. Astronomical count without infinite energy

The ship may contain an enormous number of anchors and archived lineages because dormant state is cheap relative to fully lived execution.

At any moment:
- enormous archive population,
- enormous low-depth possibility population,
- smaller but still potentially vast deep-model population,
- resource-limited fully lived population.

Important correction:
Do NOT reintroduce arbitrary `2~3 lived worlds` cap.

Also do NOT claim infinite simultaneous full-person civilizations.

Exact active count remains a later science/scale decision.

# 6. Physical limits

Meridian must remain vulnerable.

Hard constraints:
- energy generation,
- heat rejection,
- interconnect bandwidth,
- write/read integrity,
- radiation damage,
- manufacturing feedstock,
- repair capacity,
- synchronization,
- error-correction overhead,
- substrate aging,
- external travel mission requirements.

These constraints produce meaningful tradeoffs.

Example:
During a real reactor loss the system may have to choose between:
- propulsion reserve,
- physical life support for HUMAN:1,
- settlement manufacturing,
- preserving threatened civilization banks,
- maintaining deep lived histories.

No choice is morally trivial.

---

# 7. Physical catastrophe scale

Because many lineages share infrastructure, a tiny macroscopic ship fault can affect enormous numbers of internal civilizations.

Possible events:
- radiation strike corrupts one bank,
- thermal runaway forces depth reduction,
- interconnect failure isolates lineage clusters,
- repair process misaddresses identity anchors,
- emergency compaction creates rare cross-history memory collision.

This connects external ship survival to internal mystery.

---

# 8. Why memory leak can be a tiny bug with huge effect

Shared distributed infrastructure creates rare boundary failures.

A microscopic fault may:
- point one identity anchor at a wrong memory segment,
- reconcile data from the wrong related lineage,
- preserve an autobiographical fragment that should have been abstracted,
- expose a current person to another lineage's face/relationship memory.

Frequency remains extremely low because isolation/error correction normally works.

Impact can be enormous because one coherent leaked memory can reveal the existence of the hidden civilization field.

# 9. Internal physical experience

Citizens experience:
- human-scale bodies,
- streets,
- buildings,
- food,
- injury,
- weather/environment,
- pressure,
- engineering,
- biological life.

Their experienced world is generated/maintained by the physical substrate system but is not treated narratively as `fake`.

For a citizen, causal continuity and subjective embodiment are real.

The exact mapping from microscopic physical substrate to human-scale experienced geometry is intentionally speculative and should not be front-loaded in prose.

---

# 10. External physical interface

Real external events can become internal constraints through translated boundary conditions.

Examples:
- external power drop → internal energy rationing,
- radiation event → unexplained local corruption/failure,
- propulsion maneuver → scheduled infrastructure disturbance,
- real communication signal → contact scenario input,
- damaged manufacturing → scarcity across many civic worlds.

Some internal disasters are deliberate stress tests; others are reflections of genuine physical ark problems.

---

# 11. Subjective time

Do not assume arbitrary infinite acceleration.

Candidate:
- subjective time ratio can vary by depth/allocation,
- low-depth simulations can advance rapidly,
- fully lived histories have much tighter fidelity/time constraints,
- crisis overdrive can temporarily consume extreme resources.

This preserves centuries-long journey value and prevents instant solution of all civilization problems.

Do not reveal with microscope exposition.

2. maintenance energy maps do not match known population,
3. tiny physical support area has impossible civic importance,

5. later scale evidence implies incomprehensibly many lineage anchors.

Visual late reveal candidate:

The shocking fact is not its appearance but what the maintenance metadata says it contains.

# 13. Guardrails
