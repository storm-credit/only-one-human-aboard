# WORLD BIBLE v3 ADDENDUM — EVERYDAY CIVILIZATION

Status: `DEEP-DESIGN CANDIDATE / NOT CANON`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》
Scope: reader-facing ordinary Meridian civilization

Important:
- This does not modify official v2 Canon.
- This addendum assumes the v3 ultradense civilization-field candidate.
- Citizens do not know the outer-ark/substrate truth at series opening.
- The first job of this setting is to create a place worth losing before the mystery destroys certainty.

---

# 0. Core social promise

Reader-facing Meridian must not feel like:
- a laboratory,
- a military base,
- a sterile simulation,
- a constant emergency shelter,
- a utopian infographic.

It should feel like a **mature ordinary city that happens to be a closed interstellar civilization**.

People:
- go to work,
- complain about transit,
- date,
- divorce,
- raise children,
- waste time,
- support sports teams,
- argue politics,
- order food,
- gossip,
- study,
- worship or do not worship,
- care for pets/companions,
- buy unnecessary things,
- remember family anniversaries,
- ignore maintenance warnings until they become inconvenient.

This ordinary density is mandatory because later lineage contradictions only matter if the reader knows what `normal` looked like.

---

# 1. Population / civic scale

Working reader-facing population:
**~300,000 persons** per principal lived civilization state.

This is large enough for:
- anonymity,
- multiple districts,
- social classes,
- subcultures,
- specialized professions,
- political factions,
- universities/schools,
- hospitals,
- industries,
- organized crime,
- niche hobbies.

It is small enough that:
- major disasters become socially legible,
- prominent institutions recur,
- missing thousands of people changes the whole society,
- reputational networks can propagate rapidly.

Population is not exactly 300,000 in every lineage.
Birth/death/migration-policy analogues and historical divergence create variation.

---

# 2. Spatial grammar — experienced Meridian

Candidate reuse from older physical grammar:
- three large habitation/service rings or zones,
- central Spine / transfer backbone,
- Outer Works / industrial-maintenance zones,
- sealed/legacy/service spaces.

Exact geometry is not frozen yet under the ultradense ontology.

Reader-facing rule:
Citizens experience the city as physically coherent human-scale architecture.
They do not walk through `computer space` or see code.

District identity matters more than engineering diagrams.

Each district should have:
- housing texture,
- work mix,
- food/market character,
- school/community anchors,
- transit access,
- reputation,
- maintenance age,
- emergency vulnerabilities.

---

# 3. Housing

Most citizens live in apartments or compact family units, not crew bunks.

Housing types:
- standard public/cooperative apartments,
- privately leased units,
- multigenerational households,
- small studio units near work hubs,
- family-oriented courtyard blocks,
- older neighborhoods with irregular retrofits,
- higher-cost units with better location/view/quiet/access.

No hereditary Habitat caste.

Scarcity exists through:
- location,
- floor area,
- quiet,
- access to parks/schools/transit,
- maintenance quality,
- prestige,
- emergency resilience.

Baseline society guarantees minimum safe housing but not equal comfort.

This creates ordinary class/status differences without making the ship a feudal dystopia.

---

# 4. Economy

Recommended hybrid:
**Civic Floor + regulated market/cooperative economy.**

Guaranteed baseline:
- basic shelter,
- essential water/air,
- minimum food entitlement,
- core healthcare,
- compulsory education access,
- emergency service.

Market/discretionary layer:
- restaurants,
- entertainment,
- fashion,
- premium housing,
- hobby goods,
- travel convenience,
- private services,
- specialist food,
- collectibles,
- clubs,
- art,
- discretionary fabrication.

People still need jobs because:
- labor is needed,
- status/income differ,
- scarce goods/services remain scarce,
- social contribution matters,
- material/energy quotas exist.

Do not make currency/resource accounting a crypto-lore exposition engine.
Exact currency name remains C2/TBD.

# 5. Work culture

Meridian citizens are not all `crew`.

Major sectors:
- infrastructure,
- materials/repair,
- food/agriculture,
- medicine,
- education,
- transport/logistics,
- civic administration,
- safety/security,
- fabrication/manufacturing,
- software/automation,
- science,
- commerce/services,
- media/entertainment,
- arts,
- childcare/elder support,
- hospitality,
- sanitation/recycling.

Work identities matter socially.

Possible tensions:
- public vs contractor/co-op labor,
- frontline vs desk management,
- emergency-service prestige,
- old technical trades vs automated operations,
- high-status analytical work vs essential dirty work,
- schedule privilege,
- shift work.

The protagonist's damage-analysis job should feel like one specialized occupation among many, not the secret center of civilization.

# 6. Education

Children receive broad civic education plus later specialization.

Baseline stages candidate:
- early childhood/community care,
- general primary/secondary education,
- technical/apprenticeship routes,
- academic/professional routes,
- adult reskilling.

Education includes closed-habitat literacy:
- emergency behavior,
- pressure/fire response,
- resource awareness,
- basic systems safety.

But ordinary children are not miniature astronauts.
They still have:
- favorite subjects,
- disliked teachers,
- clubs,
- exams,
- friendships,
- bullying,
- sports,
- games.

Family status can affect opportunity through neighborhoods/networks even when formal access is equal.

---

# 7. Family / reproduction

Baseline society recognizes multiple household forms:
- married couples,
- unmarried partners,
- single parents,
- multigenerational households,
- child-free adults,
- communal care arrangements.

No bloodline legitimacy hierarchy.

Population stability matters, but baseline reproduction policy should not begin as coercive dystopia.

Recommended normal-state tools:
- voluntary birth planning,
- healthcare counseling,
- resource forecasting,
- waiting/spacing incentives during tight periods,
- assisted reproduction access,
- public childcare.

In alternate crisis histories, reproductive policy may become a major moral variable:
- authoritarian quota,
- pronatalist panic,
- district inequality,
- emergency restrictions.

Those are branch outcomes, not baseline truth.

---

# 8. Romance / sex / intimacy

The society should feel adult and ordinary.

Assume:
- dating is normal,
- premarital relationships are unremarkable,
- divorce exists,
- queer relationships are ordinary civic life,
- contraception/reproductive medicine is advanced,
- privacy and consent remain socially/legal important.

Closed-population living creates practical tensions:
- ex-partners remain geographically close,
- professional networks overlap,
- family reputations travel,
- long-term social memory matters.

Cross-lineage relationship contradictions later become powerful because opening relationships first feel mundane.

---

# 9. Children

Children must exist visibly in the world.

Why:
A civilization preparing for another world cannot feel like an adult-only workplace.

Use children to show:
- schools,
- family routines,
- future expectations,
- cultural continuity,
- ordinary joy,
- social policy consequences.

Important later mystery use:
A child may innocently remember a relationship that never existed locally.

But genuine cross-lineage child memory remains exceptionally rare.
Do not create a `special psychic children` class.

---

# 10. Aging / medicine

Medicine is advanced but not immortality.

Recommended tone:
- many cancers/infections/organ failures are much more treatable,
- prosthetics/regeneration are sophisticated,
- trauma medicine is strong,
- chronic illness/disability still exist,
- aging remains socially visible,
- catastrophic injury can still kill.

No easy mind restoration from backup.

Healthcare inequality should be limited at life-saving baseline but can exist in:
- convenience,
- elective procedures,
- recovery time,
- specialist access,
- private rooms,
- experimental care.

---

# 11. Death / funerals

Death must have ordinary cultural weight before lineage truth appears.

Possible practices:
- cremation or material-return options,
- memorial walls/gardens,
- family/private rites,
- religious rites,
- digital archives/messages,
- public remembrance after disasters.

Important:
A memorial is a social claim that **this person died here in this history**.

Therefore later discovery of:
- a memorial for a living person,
- an absent memorial someone remembers,
- two incompatible death dates

is especially strong.

Do not turn funerals into substrate exposition.

---

# 12. Food

Food should be culturally rich, not nutrient paste.

Sources can include:
- controlled agriculture,
- fermentation,
- cultured proteins,
- plant/animal-cell foods,
- preserved heritage ingredients,
- fabricated specialty textures.

Restaurants matter.
Family recipes matter.
District foods matter.

Food is useful for cross-lineage clues:
- a person remembers a spouse's favorite dish who is not their spouse here,
- a restaurant exists under another name,
- a commemorative food refers to an event that `never happened`.

But most meals are just meals.

---

# 13. Water / air / heat in everyday culture

Citizens normally enjoy comfortable utility service.

They do not live in permanent ration mode.

However closed-habitat realities shape habits:
- wasting water is socially frowned upon more than on old Earth,
- emergency doors are familiar,
- fire is treated seriously,
- ventilation outages create immediate public concern,
- heat rejection/power failures can become political issues.

Infrastructure is usually invisible until it fails.
That is exactly why the protagonist's profession exists.

Ordinary conflicts:

# 15. Government / politics

For v3 candidate, preserve the useful principle of a plural civic order rather than a permanent captain dictatorship.

Baseline direction:
- elected shipwide legislature,
- collective executive rather than singular absolute captain,
- local councils,
- independent judiciary,
- emergency powers with legal review.

Exact v2 institutional mechanics may be reused only after regression review.

Political conflict should include ordinary questions:
- housing,
- work conditions,
- district budgets,
- safety spending,
- school allocation,
- privacy,
- emergency authority,
- resource priorities.

Later lineage histories can show the same institutions bending differently under pressure.

---

# 16. Security / emergency response

Separate ordinary policing from high-risk crisis response.

Ordinary functions:
- violence/crime investigation,
- public order,
- fraud,
- access violations,
- domestic incidents.

Crisis response handles:
- decompression,
- major fire,
- toxic zones,
- hostage/high-risk violence,
- infrastructure rescue,
- strategic-site protection.

The protagonist works with them when structural access/safety is needed.
He is not secretly one of them.

---

# 17. Crime

A 300k city has real crime.

Candidate categories:
- fraud,
- theft,
- domestic violence,
- corruption,
- access credential abuse,
- contraband fabrication,
- resource theft,
- organized black markets,
- sabotage,
- extremist violence,
- data/reputation manipulation.

Later anomaly economy:
- fake prophets,
- people selling `future knowledge`,
- insurance/investment manipulation using leaked memories,
- black-market search for impossible records.

True memory leakage is rare; scams around it can become much more common once rumors spread.

---

# 18. Privacy / surveillance

Closed-city safety requires sensors, but total surveillance would kill many mysteries.

Baseline compromise:
- infrastructure sensors are dense,
- personal-space surveillance has legal limits,
- emergency access exists but is logged/reviewed,
- security cannot freely inspect every private memory/device,
- old systems and fragmented data create blind spots.

Important:
Infrastructure telemetry is not equivalent to perfect human truth.

People can still:
- lie,
- hide,
- forget,
- act in dead zones,
- exploit bureaucratic gaps.

---

# 19. Media / social networks

Meridian has local public media and social platforms.

Functions:
- news,
- entertainment,
- neighborhood groups,
- professional channels,
- political campaigns,
- rumor propagation,
- disaster coordination.

Because the population is finite:
- viral rumors can reach a large share quickly,
- reputations become sticky,
- old screenshots/records matter.

This becomes important when multiple people claim incompatible histories.

# 20. Religion / belief / superstition

Earth-derived faiths and secular philosophies persist in transformed forms.

Also present:
- astrology,
- tarot,
- saju-like traditions,
- physiognomy folklore,
- luck rituals,
- launch/voyage customs.

Hard:
No guaranteed supernatural mechanism.

Most practices are:
- culture,
- counseling,
- identity,
- entertainment,
- community ritual.

Rare memory-integrity events may later be interpreted through them.

# 21. Sports / games / leisure

Citizens need leisure not tied to mission survival.

Candidate:
- court/team sports,
- endurance sports,
- low-g specialty games where appropriate,
- simulation games,
- tabletop/strategy games,
- dance,
- amateur performance,

- gardening,

Sports rivalries can persist across lineages but produce different heroes.

# 22. Music / art / entertainment

Culture has centuries to evolve.

Use:
- remixed Earth genres,
- ship-born music traditions,
- local film/serial drama,
- oral/family stories,
- public art,
- memorial art,
- immersive entertainment.

Avoid making every cultural artifact about `we are on a spaceship`.
For residents, Meridian is home, not a novelty.

---

# 23. Fashion / appearance

Everyday clothing is diverse and practical rather than uniform jumpsuits.

Influences:
- recycled/smart fabrics,
- work-specific safety wear,
- district fashion,
- heritage dress,
- youth trends,
- status brands.

Only hazardous workers wear visibly technical protective gear routinely.

This helps prevent sterile-SF visual monotony.

---

# 24. Companion animals / synthetic ecology

Candidate, low priority but useful for attachment.

Possible:
- small engineered companion species,
- conventional Earth-lineage pets where ecological cost permits,
- robotic/synthetic companions,
- specialized sensory/support animals.

Hard:
Do not turn the series into creature-battling fiction.

Narrative uses:
- household texture,
- grief,
- animal recognition of someone from another lineage,
- a breed/species remembered but absent locally.

Keep density low.

---

# 25. Language / names

Centuries of closed multicultural life should affect naming.

Recommended:
- common civic lingua franca or highly mature translation support,
- heritage languages remain in families/religion/art,
- names cross cultural boundaries,
- mixed naming patterns become ordinary,
- ethnicity cannot always be inferred cleanly from name alone.

This supports a future naming pass where protagonist names are chosen for:
- natural Korean readability,
- memorability,
- non-game-character rhythm,
- multicultural plausibility.

Do not force every name into `two-syllable given + two-syllable surname` rhythm.

Keep a small set, not lore overload.

Candidate categories:
- departure/launch remembrance,
- civic-equality or charter day,
- disaster memorial days,
- district festivals,
- family/religious inherited holidays,
- approach/arrival preparation observances late in voyage.

Avoid founder worship.

# 27. Class / status

Formal civic equality does not eliminate informal hierarchy.

Status can derive from:
- profession,
- education,
- district,
- family network,
- political access,
- wealth,
- emergency-service prestige,
- media visibility,
- technical scarcity.

The closed city amplifies reputation.

But baseline society should have real mobility.
No immutable caste system unless an alternate crisis history produces one.

# 28. What varies across civilization lineages

Stable-ish starting grammar:
- humans perceive human-scale bodies,
- closed-city infrastructure exists,
- basic family/social institutions exist,
- Meridian mission identity exists.

High-elastic variables:
- who marries whom,
- where people live,
- election outcomes,
- leadership,
- wealth distribution,
- crime networks,
- disasters,
- policy responses,
- school friendships,
- occupations,
- cultural trends,
- which unknown person becomes important.

Medium-elastic:
- district institutions,
- laws,
- family policy,
- emergency authority,
- economic rules.

Deep divergence may change all of these substantially.

# 30. Hidden-history clue channels embedded in normal life

Use sparingly:
- a memorial name mismatch,
- an old recipe attached to the wrong family,
- a teacher who remembers a student who never attended,
- a sports championship nobody else remembers,
- a photo with an extra spouse/child,
- a building repair date older than the public district,
- a closed station name absent from maps,
- a pension/employment record for a job never held,
- an animal reacting to the `wrong` person,
- a family elder calling someone by an unfamiliar relationship title.

Writer rule:
**ordinary detail must greatly outnumber anomaly detail.**

---

# 31. Failure-state variations

Alternate histories can push ordinary institutions into different moral shapes.

Examples:
- housing allocation → emergency confiscation,
- public health → coercive quarantine,
- civil defense → indefinite emergency rule,
- resource budgeting → starvation hierarchy,
- neighborhood solidarity → exclusionary militia,
- family protection → nepotism,
- privacy → dangerous blind spot,
- surveillance → authoritarian control.

No institution is inherently good or evil across every condition.

---

# 32. Worldbuilding guardrails

1. Meridian is home before it is mystery.
2. Citizens are not all engineers/scientists.
3. No permanent uniforms-and-rations aesthetic.
4. Do not explain substrate truth through everyday school textbooks.
5. Normal people do not know they are one civilization lineage among many.
6. Most daily events are not system tests.
7. Families/romance/friendship are causal variables, not decorative side plots.
8. Children and elders must visibly exist.
9. Crime and politics are human phenomena, not always system manipulation.
10. Culture must have non-mission content.
11. Cross-history anomaly frequency stays extremely low before the major break.
12. Every alternate history should change ordinary life as well as catastrophe outcomes.
