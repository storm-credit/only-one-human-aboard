# WORLD BIBLE v3 ADDENDUM — ECONOMY / LABOR / RESOURCE v0.1

Status: `OFFICIAL ACTIVE WORLD CANON — v3 PROMOTED 2026-08-21 (CHG-055)`
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose:
Make Meridian's economy scene-usable without turning the novel into economic exposition or pretending a closed city has infinite post-scarcity abundance.

---

# 0. Core model

Selected direction:
**Civic Floor + regulated market/cooperative discretionary economy + hard strategic-resource governance.**

Three layers:

1. **Civic Floor** — rights/basic service floor.
2. **Discretionary Economy** — work income/credits, businesses, co-ops, optional goods/services.
3. **Strategic Resource Layer** — air/water/heat/power/critical materials/medical/emergency stock governed outside ordinary consumer market logic.

Hard:
No citizen can buy unlimited life-support priority simply by being rich.

---

# 1. Civic Floor

Guaranteed under normal law:
- minimum safe housing,
- baseline water/air/heat service,
- staple nutrition access,
- core healthcare,
- compulsory education,
- basic local/transit mobility,
- emergency shelter/response,
- baseline communications/civic access.

This is not luxury.
Quality and convenience still vary.

Civic Floor exists because letting people become homeless or lose air/medical access is not a viable closed-civilization market outcome.

---

# 2. Currency / everyday payment

Final currency name: `TBD at G10`.

Working fiction rule:
People use a normal digital civic currency/credit system for discretionary transactions.

Used for:
- restaurants,
- premium food,
- clothing/fashion,
- entertainment,
- hobby fabrication,
- better housing/location premiums,
- private services,
- travel convenience,
- collectibles,
- elective upgrades.

Not used to auction emergency oxygen or life-saving priority.

Hard:
Do not explain blockchain/ledger architecture unless an incident specifically requires transaction provenance.

---

# 3. Work and income

People work because:
- society still needs labor,
- specialized skills are scarce,
- discretionary income/status differ,
- work access affects housing/location/network opportunity,
- meaningful social contribution is culturally important but not mandatory ideology.

Working normal schedule bands:
- many civilian jobs: roughly 30~38 hours/week equivalent,
- shift sectors vary,
- emergency/medical/industrial jobs use rotations,
- overtime exists and is politically contested.

Do not freeze exact universal workweek; sectors differ.

---

# 4. Employment forms

Common forms:
- public/civic service,
- regulated private enterprise,
- worker/cooperative organizations,
- contract specialist work,
- self-employed small business,
- apprenticeship/credential routes,
- academic/research employment,
- temporary emergency assignment.

Essential infrastructure cannot simply stop operating because a private firm fails.
Continuity obligations and takeover/reassignment law exist.

---

# 5. Business

Normal businesses can include:
- restaurants/cafes,
- repair shops,
- media/entertainment,
- clothing/design,
- fabrication services,
- software/services,
- logistics contractors,
- clubs/gyms,
- childcare support,
- private professional services,
- specialty agriculture/food.

Large strategic sectors are more regulated:
- power,
- water,
- core food production,
- medical emergency supply,
- structural life-safety infrastructure,
- major transit.

No single corporation owns the ship.

---

# 6. Housing economy

Selected direction:
**baseline occupancy right + long-term lease/co-op/private premium layer.**

Citizens are not sleeping in crew bunks.

Baseline:
- safe compact unit available through civic housing system.

Competition occurs around:
- floor area,
- private rooms,
- quiet,
- school/community access,
- commute,
- green/public space,
- building age/maintenance,
- prestige,
- storage/workspace.

Housing can be:
- civic rental,
- cooperative occupancy,
- long-term transferable lease rights,
- premium/private lease.

Avoid Earth-style land speculation copy-pasted literally; there is no unlimited territorial expansion inside the lived city.

---

# 7. Class / status

Meridian has inequality but not simple hereditary caste.

Status dimensions:
- professional credential,
- schedule autonomy,
- housing convenience,
- private space,
- political/social network,
- education access/network,
- control over discretionary resources,
- reputation,
- legacy family/community standing.

Important:
A respected senior technician may have more social capital than a wealthier trader in some districts.

A person can be economically comfortable but politically weak, or vice versa.

---

# 8. Fabrication and consumer scarcity

Advanced fabrication reduces scarcity but does not eliminate it.

Easy/cheap:
- common household objects,
- basic clothing,
- standard tools,
- generic replacement parts,
- ordinary digital goods.

Scarcer/more expensive:
- rare elements,
- high-performance industrial materials,
- medical-grade components,
- custom precision parts,
- heritage physical objects,
- premium biological foods,
- large-volume private space,
- high-energy hobby fabrication.

Story benefit:
People can live comfortably without turning every material problem into `printer makes it instantly`.

---

# 9. Resource visibility

Citizens know the civilization is closed and resource accounting matters.

They routinely see:
- maintenance notices,
- power-demand advisories,
- water use norms,
- fabrication surcharges,
- transit capacity,
- emergency stock discussions.

They do NOT normally see:
- D0~D4 substrate execution depth,
- outer lineage budgets,
- hidden CCAL resource allocation.

Early C3 effects must therefore look like ordinary civic capacity pressure.

---

# 10. Emergency economy

During declared emergencies, strategic resource priorities can temporarily override normal market allocation.

Possible actions:
- industrial power curtailment,
- transport priority lanes,
- fabrication reservation,
- medical stock control,
- shelter conversion,
- temporary price controls/ration rules,
- work reassignment under narrow legal conditions.

Hard:
Emergency authority is reviewable and cannot become permanent without political conflict.

Act2:
localized emergency controls.

Act6:
sustained resource crisis makes these rules politically explosive.

---

# 11. Rationing

Normal Meridian is NOT permanent ration fiction.

Baseline:
people have ordinary choice.

Rationing activates only for:
- strategic shortage,
- disaster,
- supply disruption,
- public health event,
- major energy/material constraint.

Ration forms may include:
- quantity caps,
- priority categories,
- temporary substitute goods,
- allocation windows,
- district delivery constraints.

Moral conflict:
who defines `essential`, who gets priority, and how exceptions work.

---

# 12. Labor politics

Normal conflict can include:
- shift schedules,
- overtime,
- safety standards,
- automation displacement,
- credential barriers,
- contractor vs civic workforce,
- union/co-op bargaining,
- emergency-service minimum coverage,
- whistleblower protection.

This supports ordinary world texture and Act2 maintenance-failure causes without requiring sabotage.

Candidate Act2 human-amplification cause:
a deferred maintenance item can result from budget/schedule conflict, but avoid one evil manager as sole explanation.

---

# 13. Protagonist economics

The protagonist should be:
- stable middle-income skilled technical worker,
- not rich,
- not poor enough that every plot becomes money survival,
- able to afford ordinary food/leisure/hobby,
- sensitive to housing/commute/shift tradeoffs.

His profession gives professional respect but little political authority.

This prevents `ordinary` from meaning economically desperate hero by default.

---

# 14. Mira economics

Mira's work must expose actual tradeoffs:
- moving medicine means delaying something else,
- rerouting passenger capacity affects freight,
- emergency reserve cannot be spent twice,
- formal fairness can conflict with local urgency.

She is not merely someone who presses a logistics dashboard.

Act6:
Mira becomes one of the strongest lenses on how scarcity turns technical allocation into political power.

---

# 15. Juno economics

Juno's community/food/co-op role operates at the edge between market and social trust.

Possible normal functions:
- restaurant/community kitchen,
- cooperative bulk purchasing,
- neighborhood mutual-aid account,
- informal information network.

Risk under crisis:
community priority can become insider allocation.

This creates a real Mira↔Juno conflict without making either a villain.

---

# 16. Cassian economics

Cassian's advantage is plausible only if:
- he used a narrow information anomaly early,
- then ordinary compounding/network effects built his position,
- not every successful trade is supernatural foreknowledge.

He may control meaningful commercial assets but cannot buy command over strategic life support.

Act4 investigation can examine:
- unusual pre-event asset moves,
- insurance/contract positions,
- relocation timing,
- warehouse/resource placement.

---

# 17. Economic inequality limits

Avoid two bad extremes:
A. utopia where money/status never matter,
B. dystopia where poor citizens lose oxygen.

Selected middle:
- survival floor protected,
- comfort/opportunity unequal,
- networks matter,
- emergency scarcity stresses fairness,
- corruption exists but is contestable.

---

# 18. Scene grammar

Show economy through ordinary decisions:
- rent/lease renewal,
- commute choice,
- restaurant bill,
- overtime refusal,
- school location,
- buying a specialty part,
- waiting for discretionary fabrication,
- a co-op vote,

Do not stop story to explain macroeconomics.

---

# 19. Cross-history variation

Different lineages may vary:
- market regulation,
- housing allocation,
- emergency ration law,
- union strength,
- public/private balance.

But avoid creating one libertarian world, one communist world, one capitalist world as ideology laboratory caricatures.

Variation should emerge from historical pressure and institutional choices.
